System 1 • Domain Expansion

Make contact inevitable—not resistance impossible.

A complete Domain remains a fight-defining commitment. Its imbued technique becomes oppressive, but defenses, barrier damage, anti-domain techniques, and opposing Domains all create real counterplay.

Complete Domain • Baseline

Expanding the innate domain

  • Activation: full-turn action and the required hands or signs.
  • Cost: 20 CE.
  • Area: 60-foot-radius enclosed space centered on you.
  • Duration: 1 minute, or until it collapses.
  • Targets: choose perceived creatures in the area when it opens. Every other creature is moved harmlessly to the nearest space outside.

A technique-specific Domain may change its action, cost, shape, range, target rule, or duration. Its explicit wording wins.

Domain Stability

Supreme, but not concentration-proof

Opening a Domain ends your current Technique Focus, and the Domain occupies that Focus slot. It does not use ordinary Focus checks. Instead, if one damage source deals at least 20% of your hit point maximum to you, make a Constitution save with DC 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher. On a failure, the Domain collapses.

It also collapses if you are incapacitated, die, dismiss it without an action on your turn, its barrier or anchor reaches 0 HP, or another rule breaks it.

A Domain ending for any reason causes Technique Burnout.
Signature Sure-Hit Event

One Domain-specific effect resolves at initiative 20

Every complete lethal Domain names one self-contained Sure-Hit Event. At initiative count 20 each round—after Domain clash checks—the event resolves once against every eligible chosen target still inside. It is a manifestation of the Domain itself, not an activation of the user’s ordinary attack or technique.

  • An unprotected chosen target receives the event’s complete printed result without an attack roll or initial saving throw.
  • A creature protected by an anti-domain defense uses the fallback attack or saving throw printed in that Domain. If none is printed, use the most relevant save; success halves damage and prevents movement, conditions, and secondary riders.
  • Every attack, summon attack, and saving-throw feature used outside that named event resolves normally, even when it shares the Domain’s technique.
Event timing

One inevitable moment per round

The Sure-Hit Event resolves once at initiative count 20 and loses ties to other initiative-20 effects. A creature entering after that count waits until the next round. The event is not a critical hit and cannot trigger Black Flash unless its own entry explicitly creates a rolled critical.

Ordinary activations

The rest of the character sheet still rolls

The Sure-Hit Event costs no extra action or CE unless its entry says otherwise. Separately used techniques retain their printed actions, CE costs, attack rolls, and saving throws. Domain terrain, origins, summons, and movement benefits can still modify how those ordinary features are used.

Anti-domain protection

Suppress the guarantee

Simple Domain, Hollow Wicker Basket, and similar defenses suppress sure-hit against protected creatures. They do not end the Domain, erase its terrain, prevent ordinary attacks, or automatically negate its separate environmental rules.

Lethal thresholds

The named event governs its own ceiling

A Sure-Hit Event applies its full printed effect, including control, confiscation, soul alteration, limb loss, maximum-HP damage, or death when that event expressly says so. The Domain cannot invent an effect absent from its entry, but there is no universal damage ceiling or low-HP gate.

0.2-second expansion • Tier 4

One impossible instant

As an action, spend 10 CE to expand until the end of the current turn and immediately resolve the Domain’s Sure-Hit Event once against every eligible creature in its area, including allies. Reactions cannot be used after the expansion begins but may still be used against its announced activation if their trigger permits.

When it ends, suffer your normal Tier 4 Burnout. This option cannot be extended or made open-barrier.

Non-lethal Domain

A disclosed rule, a fair procedure

A non-lethal Domain replaces sure-hit with a specific rule, trigger, consequence, and completion condition established by its feature. The rule is understood by every trapped creature when the Domain opens. A creature that knowingly violates it makes the listed save; on a success it avoids that consequence until the start of its next turn.

Its procedure must offer meaningful choices. Without explicit text, it cannot cause instant death, permanent resource loss, or unavoidable violence. It clashes and burns out normally.

Enclosed barrier

Statistics and weak points

AC: 12 + proficiency bonus
HP: 5 × character level + 10 × refinement tier

The barrier is immune to psychic and poison damage. By default it resists damage from inside and is vulnerable to damage from outside. It automatically fails saving throws and is immune to conditions.

From inside, a creature must first use an action and succeed on Wisdom (Perception) against the creator’s CE save DC. Success exposes a weak point to that creature until the end of its next turn.

Barrier configuration • Tier 3

Choose where it is vulnerable

  • Standard: resistance inside, vulnerability outside.
  • Inverted: vulnerability inside, resistance outside.
  • Balanced (+5 CE): neither resistance nor vulnerability on either side.

A closed barrier blocks physical passage and teleportation across it unless an effect expressly crosses planar barriers. Destroying it collapses the Domain; ordinary damage does not leave a lasting hole.

Open-barrier Domain • Tier 5

A divine technique with an escape route

When expanding, you may omit the outer barrier and choose a visible point on the ground as the Domain’s anchor. The Domain has a 120-foot radius and lasts 1 minute. Creatures can enter or leave normally, but an eligible creature inside remains subject to its effect.

The anchor has AC equal to your CE save DC, HP equal to 5 × your character level, immunity to psychic and poison damage, and no vulnerability. Destroying it collapses the Domain. During a clash with a closed Domain, the open Domain attacks the closed barrier from outside at initiative 20, dealing 4d10 + CE modifier force damage before outside vulnerability is applied. Two open Domains do not make this barrier attack.

Technique Burnout

The technique goes dark—not the entire character

During Burnout you cannot activate an Innate Technique feature, Cursed Technique Reversal, Maximum Output, Maximum Technique, or Domain Expansion. You may still move, attack with weapons, use class features independent of your Innate Technique, reinforce yourself with generic CE, use anti-domain techniques, and use generic RCT.

Existing manifestations remain and may receive ordinary commands, but cannot activate a burned-out technique through the creator. Burnout is not exhaustion, cannot be removed by ordinary condition cleansing, and no longer ends when Black Flash occurs.

Burnout Recovery

Destroy and rebuild

At Tier 4 with RCT, use an action and spend 8 CE to make a DC 15 Constitution save. Success ends Burnout. Failure deals 6d6 psychic damage that cannot be reduced, leaves Burnout intact, and prevents actions and reactions until the start of your next turn.

Tier 4 permits one attempt per long rest. At Tier 5 you may retry until a failure; each prior success since your long rest raises the DC by 5.

Refinement is a milestone, not a currency. The GM awards a tier after a meaningful breakthrough, a decisive clash, sustained narrative training, or technique evolution. Repeatedly defeating trivial creatures, spending unused CE before bed, or staging friendly clashes never generates refinement.

Domain procedure

Clashes resolve through momentum.

Refinement matters, but it does not automatically erase a close rival. Each round creates visible progress and gives both sorcerers time to change the battle.

Unequal mastery

A head start, not an auto-win

If one Domain’s refinement tier exceeds another by 3 or more, the stronger Domain begins with 1 Dominance against the weaker. A tie on a Clash check awards no Dominance.

Breaking or withdrawing

Burnout follows

A defeated or voluntarily dismissed Domain collapses immediately and its creator suffers Burnout. The winner’s sure-hit resumes at the next initiative count 20, not during the roll that broke the rival.

Keep fighting

The casters are still targets

Creators act normally during the clash. Damage can trigger Domain Stability, barriers and open anchors can be attacked, and anti-domain techniques still suppress sure-hit locally.

Three or more Domains

Compare every rival

All creators roll once. Compare every pair and track Dominance separately. A creator can gain against one rival while losing to another. When any Domain collapses, erase only its tracks; the remaining Domains continue clashing.

Non-lethal and open Domains

One common engine

Non-lethal Domains use the same Clash check. Open Domains also use it and make their barrier attack at initiative 20 after Clash checks. An open Domain can lose by Dominance even if its anchor remains intact.

System 2 • Reverse Cursed Technique

Regeneration needs a visible limit.

RCT is powerful emergency healing, not a second infinite health bar. CE controls burst speed; the Healing Reserve controls how much tissue can be rebuilt before a long rest.

Unlocking RCT

A feature or genuine breakthrough

You gain RCT from a class or technique feature, a feat, or a GM-awarded breakthrough after a near-death or exceptional CE insight. Remove the random d100 roll. Surviving danger can create the opportunity, but never guarantees the ability.

Healing Reserve maximum = 10 × total character level.
Reserve

One pool, restored on a long rest

Your Healing Reserve is separate from HP and CE. It returns to its maximum when you finish a long rest, not a short rest. RCT has no automatic healing pulse. If a feature increases the reserve, apply that increase once to this formula.

Reserve spent on healing or repairs does not return until the long rest, even if CE is restored sooner.

Self-healing

Ten hit points per CE

Once per round while conscious, use a bonus action—or a reaction immediately after taking damage—to spend from 1 CE up to your CE ability modifier. Heal up to 10 HP per CE spent, limited by your missing HP and remaining Healing Reserve. Deduct only the HP actually restored from the reserve.

If the triggering damage reduced you to 0 HP, you cannot use the reaction unless a feature expressly permits RCT while unconscious or at 0 HP. Unspent healing from an activation is lost; CE cannot be refunded.

Positive Energy Output

Others require mastery

Base RCT targets only you. A feature granting RCT Output lets you touch another creature as an action, spending up to your CE ability modifier at a rate of 2 CE per 10 HP restored and deducting the healing from your reserve. A class feature may improve this rate or action.

What RCT cannot do

Clear boundaries

RCT cannot restore CE, revive a dead creature, recreate a destroyed brain or torso, remove Technique Burnout, repair soul damage, or end a curse or supernatural condition unless a feature expressly says it can.

Positive damage

Not part of basic healing

You do not automatically weaponize positive energy. If RCT Output lets you harm Cursed Spirits, Undead, or another listed creature, use that feature’s revised damage or the legacy conversion below.

System 3 • Critical Injury

Injuries come from catastrophic hits.

The original Constitution modifier × proficiency formula could become 0 and did not say whether damage accumulated. This version keys injury severity to the victim’s actual durability and checks one hit at a time.

Qualify the attackA limb-tagged feature, declared targeted strike, or catastrophic hazard can cause an injury. An ordinary targeted strike is declared before rolling and has disadvantage.
Measure one hitUse damage actually lost after resistance and temporary HP. Never accumulate damage across attacks.
Compare thresholdsBreak at 25% of maximum HP; sever at 50%. Round both thresholds up.
Make the saveRoll Constitution against the attacker’s CE save DC, or 8 + PB + attack ability modifier for a non-technique attack.
Threshold result

Break, sever, or endure

  • Below Break: no lasting injury.
  • Break to below Sever: failed save breaks the body part; success prevents the injury.
  • At or above Sever: failed save severs a limb; success breaks it instead.

A broken limb does not escalate from repeated lesser hits. A later attack must independently meet the Sever threshold to remove it.

Legacy automatic injuries

Still respect durability

A feature that says it automatically breaks or severs a body part must still deal at least the Break threshold in one hit. If it lists no damage, a failed save can break the part only. It can sever without damage only if it expressly says so and the target is at or below 25% of its hit point maximum.

A Black Flash can meet a threshold, but never severs merely because it is a Black Flash.

Fatal head or torso damage

Only at 0 HP

If an attack that meets the Sever threshold reduces a creature to 0 HP and the failed injury save would destroy its head or torso, the creature dies unless it can survive without that body part. A creature with Legendary Resistance may spend one use to suffer the Major Wound and begin death saves instead.

Recovery

Healing HP is not repair

Ordinary healing restores HP but does not end an injury. With appropriate medical treatment, a broken limb heals after 7 days of downtime and a Major Wound after 14 days. A severed limb or destroyed eye does not naturally regrow. RCT uses the repair table above.

System 4 • Maximum Output

Overcharge one dimension.

Maximum Output is the universal act of forcing an ordinary technique above its safe operating level. It is not a named Maximum Technique and no longer turns a normal attack bonus into an accuracy-breaking number.

Prerequisite and activation

20 CE ability score, level 11

You must have a CE ability score of 20 and either a feature or breakthrough that unlocks Maximum Output. As a bonus action, declare one eligible technique you will activate before the end of the current turn and pay its Output surcharge. You pay and activate the technique normally afterward.

If you do not activate it before the turn ends, the surcharge is lost. The base technique must be legal at your Output Rank; the surcharge itself does not make an otherwise legal technique exceed that rank.

Output surcharge

Double the base—not every option

The surcharge equals the technique’s printed fixed base CE cost, minimum 2 CE. A variable-cost technique uses the X you choose as its surcharge. Optional overcharge, extra targets, or other add-on CE is paid once and is not doubled.

Total CE = base cost + Output surcharge + optional CE.

A normally free technique has a 2 CE surcharge. Cost reduction applies to the base activation only, not the surcharge.

Eligible technique

What can be overcharged

Maximum Output can enhance one activation of an Innate Technique that has a CE cost or a measurable attack, save, damage, range, or duration. A technique-specific Maximum Output feature replaces one or more modes only where it explicitly differs.

Excluded systems

No recursive pinnacle stacking

It cannot enhance Domain Expansion, RCT, anti-domain techniques, Binding Vows, a named Maximum Technique, Black Flash, or a manifestation’s stat block unless a specific feature says otherwise. It cannot be combined with Chanting or another Maximum Output on the same activation.

Worked example: a technique has a 4 CE base and can spend 3 optional CE for extra dice. Maximum Output costs 4 CE as its surcharge. Activating at full overcharge costs 4 base + 4 surcharge + 3 optional = 11 CE. Power adds half of the original printed dice; the optional dice are not copied.

System 5 • Black Flash

The original miracle stays rare.

This campaign preserves Project V3's Black Flash procedure: first land a critical hit, then confirm the millionth-of-a-second alignment on a second d20. The Zone is revised separately so Black Flash never refills RCT or erases Technique Burnout.

Black Flash check

Critical hit, then a second d20

Whenever a creature capable of using cursed energy lands a critical hit with an attack, roll a second unmodified d20 before damage. On a natural 20, the attack becomes a Black Flash.

There is no universal once-per-turn or once-per-round limit on Black Flash attempts or successes. Every qualifying critical may attempt the confirmation roll. A saving throw, automatic hit, passive effect, initiative-20 Domain event, or manifestation attack does not qualify unless a specific feature explicitly creates a qualifying critical hit.

Forced and expanded criticals

A critical is a critical unless the feature says otherwise

A forced critical or expanded critical range qualifies if the triggering feature permits Black Flash. An explicit restriction such as Great Luck's non-qualifying critical still wins. A feature that says an attack is automatically a Black Flash skips the confirmation roll.

Declared riders

Finish building the critical first

Choose and pay any damage rider that must be declared before damage is rolled, then resolve the complete critical hit. Effects that trigger only after damage is dealt remain separate and are not multiplied.

Black Flash damage

Triple the completed critical

Roll the qualifying critical hit normally, including its critical dice, flat modifiers, and eligible declared riders. Multiply that completed critical-hit damage by 3.

Black Flash damage = 3 × completed critical-hit damage.

This is multiplication of the final critical damage, not one extra set of dice.

Defense and injury

Bypass resistance, not immunity

The complete Black Flash hit ignores resistance to its damage types. Immunity still applies unless another feature separately says it ignores immunity. Use final HP lost from the full Black Flash when checking the revised injury thresholds.

Blessed by Black Sparks

Born close to the sparks

After any qualifying critical hit, your Black Flash confirmation succeeds on 18–20. The Zone cannot improve this feat beyond 18–20.

Natural versus forced

Only genuine timing deepens the Zone

An automatic or forced Black Flash deals the normal Project V3 Black Flash damage and grants Stage 1 if you are outside the Zone. If already in the Zone, it only resets the current stage's duration. It does not advance the Zone or restore CE.

What the Zone never does

No free regeneration or Burnout reset

Black Flash and the Zone never restore Healing Reserve, never end Technique Burnout, never remove exhaustion or injuries by themselves, and never create a second Technique Focus slot. A separate explicit feature may still create its own exception.

Breakthroughs

The sparks can still teach

The first meaningful natural Black Flash may justify a GM-awarded breakthrough, including eligibility to learn RCT, Domain refinement, or a technique evolution. A breakthrough changes what the character understands; it does not refill spent resources unless the granted feature says so.
