Part I

Character Foundations

Ancestries, normalized class tables, and Heavenly Restriction as a standard player option.

Shared character rules

One package from each layer.

Choose an ancestry, one ancestry option, a background, and a starting class. Class score increases are class features and remain separate from ancestry increases.

Ancestry ASI

Apply it exactly once

Choose one ancestry option and apply only its listed Ability Score Increase. A Human heritage replaces the ordinary Human increase; it does not stack on top of +1 to every score.

Ability maximum

Specific caps win

An ancestry increase cannot raise a score above 20 unless that ancestry expressly changes the maximum. Heavenly Restriction’s physical maximum is a class exception described below.

Terminology

Ancestry and option

“Ancestry” replaces legacy “race.” Human heritage, Vessel incarnation, and Cursed Corpse frame are ancestry options. Alignment is always the character’s choice, not an ancestry restriction. “PB” means proficiency bonus; “CE die” means the reserve die gained at that class level.

Class progression

Innate Technique levels

Every sorcerer class gains its Innate Technique at level 1 and major technique feature slots at class levels 3, 6, 11, and 18. These use the selected technique’s own feature text. A completed technique framework may also print automatic internal unlocks at other levels; those are part of that technique's progression and do not consume an additional class feature slot. Heavenly Restriction never gains an Innate Technique.

Empty feature row

Not a missing level

A dash means the source grants no new named class feature at that level. The character still gains HP, PB when shown, a CE Reserve die if applicable, and any scaling already written into an earlier feature.

Rule priority: a revised class or ancestry feature comes first, then this guide, then the CE Reserve guide and Core Glossary, then unaltered Project V3 text, then ordinary 5e.

Normalized ancestries

Three origins, seven complete options.

The original concepts and unusual bonuses remain. The rewrite identifies what is shared, what replaces a shared trait, and what every incomplete sentence means at the table.

Human

Shared traits: Humanoid; Medium; 30-foot walking speed; adulthood in the teens and a lifespan near one century; Common and one other language. Choose one heritage below.

Ordinary Human

Ability Score Increase. Each of your six ability scores increases by 1.

Jujutsu Sorcerer

Ability Score Increase. Increase one score by 2 and a different score by 1.

Martial Training. You gain proficiency with martial weapons.

Beginner Cursed Energy. Gain one Cursed Energy feat for which you meet the prerequisites.

Superhuman

Ability Score Increase. Strength and Constitution each increase by 2. Your speed becomes 35 feet.

Gain Athletics proficiency, double your jumping distance, and count as one size larger for carrying, pushing, dragging, and lifting.

Superhuman: Uncanny Endurance

When you take damage, use your reaction to reduce it by 1d12 + Constitution modifier. After using this trait, finish a short or long rest before using it again.

Cursed Vessel

Shared traits: Humanoid; Small or Medium, matching the host body; 30-foot walking speed unless the host form clearly requires another ordinary speed; the host body’s remaining natural lifespan; Common and one other language. Choose one incarnation below.

This Body Is Mine

You have advantage on saving throws against effects that would unwillingly transform your body or replace your physical form. This does not grant general advantage against possession, charm, or soul damage unless the effect also transforms your body.

Incomplete and complete states

These are narrative states, not additional ancestry options—the PDF supplies no separate mechanics. Choose Reincarnated or Death Painting for statistics. A host’s remaining will may be represented through roleplay, a background, or a campaign complication without granting another trait package.

Reincarnated: Ability Scores

Increase one ability score by 2 and each of your other five ability scores by 1. This deliberately retains the unusually large increase written in Project V3.

Reincarnated: Life Experience

Gain proficiency in two skills, or one skill and one tool, of your choice.

Reincarnated: Cling to Life

When reduced to 0 HP without dying outright, you may drop to 1 HP instead. Once used, this trait returns after a long rest.

Death Painting: Ability Scores

Your Charisma increases by 3, to a maximum of 20.

Death Painting: Durable Body

Your HP maximum increases by 2 at level 1 and by 2 whenever your total character level increases. This equals 2 × character level.

Death Painting: Poisonous Blood

When a creature within 5 feet deals piercing or slashing damage to you, use your reaction to splash it or another creature within 5 feet. The target takes poison damage equal to PB and makes a Constitution save against 8 + PB + Constitution modifier. On a failure, it is poisoned until the end of your next turn.

Cursed Corpse

Shared traits: Construct; Common; 35-foot walking speed; no natural maximum lifespan while repaired; choose Small, Medium, or Large; choose a Softbody or Hard Shell frame.

Purpose-Built

One ability score of your choice increases by 4, to a maximum of 20.

Constructed Size

A Large corpse occupies a 10-foot space but gains no automatic reach or larger weapon dice. It squeezes through spaces normally and uses its actual size for grappling and carrying.

Unnatural Nature

You do not need to breathe, eat, drink, or sleep, and magic cannot put you to sleep. You must still complete rests normally. Hit Dice, ordinary healing, and RCT can restore your HP unless an effect expressly excludes Constructs; describe rest recovery as repair materials and maintenance.

Softbody: Tailored Appearance

Choose Intimidation, Persuasion, or Performance. You gain proficiency in the chosen skill and double PB for checks made with it.

Softbody: Surprise

After posing as a mundane object or nonsentient body, use an action to make Charisma (Performance) against a creature’s passive Insight. On a success, reveal yourself and choose that creature within 30 feet if it can see you. It makes a Wisdom save against 10 + PB + Charisma modifier; on a failure it is stunned until the end of your next turn. Once used, finish a long rest before using it again.

Hard Shell: Integrated Armor

You gain a +1 bonus to AC. You may wear only armor with which you are proficient. Incorporating or removing armor takes 1 hour in contact with it, and may occur during a rest. Integrated armor cannot be removed against your will while you live. The +1 is a flat bonus and applies after the chosen AC calculation, including worn armor and a shield.

Heavenly Restriction ruling

Standard option. Calamity potential.

The class does not require a separate “calamity campaign” permission tier. Calamity-grade describes what a particular character may become, not what a player is allowed to select at level 1.

Campaign classificationStandard player class

Available under the same campaign approval as Juggernaut, Scout, or any Innate Technique. Its ceiling remains intentionally exceptional.

The trade

Pure Body

You have no CE ability, CE Reserve, Innate Technique, Domain, RCT, Maximum Output, or Black Flash. CE drain and recovery do nothing to you. You may multiclass normally if you meet the prerequisites, but any gained feature that requires or supplies CE, spellcasting, RCT, a Cursed Technique, or a Domain remains unavailable while this Heavenly Restriction exists.

A campaign-scale event may replace the Restriction with a different power source, but it is never lost merely by choosing a class. Physical, tactical, skill, and cursed-tool features function normally.

Ability maximum

24 + class level

At level 1, each ability score maximum becomes 25. It rises by 1 with every later Heavenly Restriction level, reaching 44 at level 20. The class’s automatic score increases still apply only to Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution.

Score increases

They intentionally stack

Supernatural Body’s +2 at level 1; +1 at levels 5, 9, 13, and 17; Improved Body’s +4 at level 7; God of War’s +4 at level 20; ancestry increases; and ASIs all stack, subject to the current physical maximum.

Encounter label

Build, not permission

An optimized high-level Heavenly Restriction character may be treated as calamity-grade when designing encounters. A lower-stat or utility-focused character need not be. The label never removes player access or rewrites the class.

Body points

Begin at level 2

At class level 2 you have 2 Body Points. Thereafter your total equals your Heavenly Restriction level. Spend points only on improvements whose prerequisites you meet; choices are permanent. Any improvement that lists level 1 as a selection gate instead uses level 2.

Feat exception

Body and Taijutsu only

When an HR ASI offers a “Cursed Feat,” read it as a Body or Taijutsu feat that does not grant CE, a technique, spellcasting, or another mystical resource. This is an exception to the label, not to Pure Body.

Cursed perception

Fight what the campaign contains

Supernatural Vision lets you perceive Cursed Spirits and cursed phenomena through heightened mundane senses. You still register as having no CE to features that detect, count, drain, or target CE.

Cursed tools

Weapons remain usable

You may wield cursed tools and benefit from passive weapon properties. You cannot fuel a tool with CE or activate a property whose stated cost or prerequisite requires CE unless the tool supplies its own independent charge.

Domains

Target definition matters

You are ineligible for a sure-hit that targets only creatures with CE. A Domain that targets objects, terrain, every creature, or creatures without CE can affect you normally. Celestial Body does not end a Domain or protect allies.

Celestial Body

Pass, do not dispel

At level 10, barriers do not impede your movement and do not harm you merely for crossing them. You do not dispel the barrier, create an opening for another creature, or ignore a separate physical obstruction occupying the same space.

Improved Might

Additional dice, not die size

Monstrous Might and Godly Might each add one additional Martial Arts die when you replace an eligible attack’s normal damage with Martial Arts. With both and a d12 Martial Arts die, the replacement damage is 3d12 before modifiers.

Demigod’s Figure

Twelve-step exhaustion

At level 15, track up to 12 exhaustion levels. Apply the ordinary effect for level 1 at HR exhaustion 2, level 2 at 4, and so on; death occurs at 12. Removing one ordinary exhaustion level removes 2 HR exhaustion levels.

Ancestry interaction

No hidden replacement

Ancestry and HR physical bonuses stack normally. A Jujutsu Sorcerer Human’s Beginner Cursed Energy feat becomes unusable under Pure Body and grants no replacement; Superhuman or Ordinary Human is usually the cleaner mechanical choice.

Source corrections

What the new tables resolve.

These are editorial rulings rather than broad balance changes. They make the table and feature text point to the same rule.

Names

Consistent feature labels

  • Juggernaut “Focus” becomes Battle Focus, distinct from Technique Focus.
  • Scout uses Unparalleled Movement and Swift Movement.
  • Warrior uses Unerring Blows.
  • Every “Unarmored Defence” entry uses Unarmored Defense.
Copied chassis text

Correct class references

  • Support Specialist higher-level HP says Support Specialist level, not Strategist level.
  • Shikigami User Martial Arts refers to Shikigami User weapons, dice, and levels—not Monk.
  • Explicit save proficiencies remain unchanged even when unconventional: Scout keeps Strength and Constitution; Shikigami User keeps Intelligence and Wisdom.
Shikigami mismatch

Swift Escape is level 15

The table lists undefined “Close Bond” at 15 and Swift Escape at 17, while the only written feature says Swift Escape begins at 15. The normalized table follows the written rule: Swift Escape at 15; level 17 has no new named feature. “Close Bond” grants nothing until rules text is designed.

Extra Attack

Total attacks, not multipliers

Every Extra Attack row now states the total attacks made with the Attack action. Warrior reaches 2/3/4/5 attacks at levels 5/11/17/20. Heavenly Restriction reaches 2/3/4/5 at 5/11/18/20.

CE columns

Reserve dice replace totals

Old formulas such as “level + CE mod + PB” are removed. Every sorcerer table shows the class die and fixed value already established by the CE Reserve guide. An empty feature level still grants that die.

ASI cadence

Unusual schedules remain

Strategist keeps ASIs at 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16, and 19. Support Specialist keeps 4, 8, 12, 14, 16, and 19. Heavenly Restriction keeps 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, and 19. These are intentional class advantages, not normalization errors.

Part II

Cursed Energy

HP-like reserve growth, one multiclass pool, and full player-controlled output.

Core definitions

These terms replace the repeated Cursed Energy paragraph printed beneath every Innate Technique.

Ability

Cursed Energy ability

When you first gain Cursed Energy, choose Wisdom or Charisma as your CE ability unless your class explicitly names another score. The choice applies to every class, technique, feat, and item you use.

Save DC

Technique resistance

CE save DC = 8 + your proficiency bonus + your CE ability modifier.

Attack

Technique accuracy

CE attack modifier = your proficiency bonus + your CE ability modifier.

One pool

Maximum and current CE

Your maximum CE is your total capacity. Current CE is what remains available. Spending CE never lowers your maximum, and current CE cannot fall below 0 or rise above your maximum unless a feature grants temporary CE.

Recovery

Restoring the reserve

You regain all spent CE when you finish a long rest. You do not normally recover CE during combat or on a short rest unless a class feature, technique, feat, vow, or item expressly says that you do.

Ability-score changes: Your CE ability modifier is added to the reserve only once, not once per level. If the modifier changes, adjust both maximum and current CE by the same amount, to a minimum current CE of 0.

Reserve Dice by class

The die represents how much raw Cursed Energy that class develops with each level. It is rolled only when gaining a level, never during combat.

Multiclass reserves

Multiclassing changes the dice that build the reserve; it never creates several independent CE pools.

Starting die

Maximum once

At character level 1, take the maximum value of your starting class’s Reserve Die. Every later class level adds a roll or fixed gain from the class taken at that level.

Modifier

Add it once

Your CE ability modifier is added only once to the combined reserve. Taking a new class never adds that modifier again and never allows you to select a second CE ability.

Example

Warrior 5 / Support Specialist 3

With a +4 CE ability modifier and fixed gains: start with 6 CE from the Warrior d6, add 16 CE for four later Warrior levels, add 15 CE for three Support levels, then add the modifier once. The character has 41 maximum CE.

Spend what the feature permits

A large reserve is allowed to create a large moment. There is no universal Output Rank, level-based fixed-cost gate, proficiency-bonus overcharge cap, or +2 accuracy ceiling.

Fixed cost

Pay the listed amount

If a feature costs 6 CE, pay 6 CE. Once the class or technique grants the feature, the amount is not a hidden level prerequisite.

Variable input

X can use the reserve

If the user chooses X and the feature gives no ceiling, X may be any positive amount the user can currently pay. A printed limit such as CE modifier, PB, or 10 CE still applies.

Additional CE

No universal overcharge cap

When a feature permits additional CE without a ceiling, spend any available amount. Each point produces only the benefit that feature actually assigns to it.

Accuracy

Use the printed bonus

A feature that adds CE to an attack roll or save DC may reach its printed value. There is no universal accuracy ceiling; the feature’s own cost and limit are the procedure.

Specific rules

Named limits still matter

A limit written in the Project feature or in a repair needed to stop the feature from triggering itself remains binding. A limit added only because a perfect build might be strong does not.

Availability: if a class, technique, feat, ritual, or breakthrough grants an ability, the character may use it whenever they can pay its cost and meet its stated trigger—even if that creates exceptional output earlier than expected.

Cost arithmetic

Calculate the full activation, then pay it once. Specific discounts, multipliers, and free uses apply exactly as written.

Order

Base, additions, multiplier, reduction

Start with base cost, add optional CE and extra targets, apply a multiplier such as Maximum Output, then apply cost reduction. Round only at the end unless the feature says otherwise.

Halves

Round up

Halved CE costs round up. A result of 0 is allowed only when the feature expressly makes the activation free.

Failure

CE is committed

CE is spent when the feature is activated. A miss, successful enemy save, broken Focus, counter, or suppression does not refund it unless a feature says so.

Insufficient CE

No partial payment

If you cannot pay the calculated cost, the activation does not begin. You may choose a lower legal X or fewer optional additions before committing.

Temporary Cursed Energy

Temporary CE is borrowed output, not a permanent expansion of the character’s natural reserve.

Above maximum

A separate amount

Temporary CE can raise current CE above maximum but never changes the maximum itself.

Spend first

Unstable energy burns away

When paying a CE cost, temporary CE is spent before normal CE unless the granting feature expressly says otherwise.

No restoration

Borrowed power cannot heal

Temporary CE cannot be restored. It disappears when its source ends, even if it was never spent.

Stacking

Same-source CE does not stack

Repeated uses of the same feature do not stack temporary CE; use the greater amount. Temporary CE from different sources stacks only when a source expressly permits stacking.

Restrictions

No permanent purchases

Temporary CE may power techniques but cannot pay a permanent creation cost, a permanent Binding Vow cost, or any cost explicitly requiring the user’s natural CE.

Single-class reserve calculator

Use fixed gains for fast character creation. Multiclass characters add each later class level’s own fixed gain as explained above.

Maximum CE—

Legacy CE conversion

Replace fixed class totals with Reserve Dice. Do not add an Output Rank or new spending ceiling.

CE quick reference

The reserve behaves like HP: it grows level by level, belongs to the whole character, and is spent according to the feature being used.

Maximum CE = starting die maximum + later Reserve gains + CE modifier once + permanent bonuses

At the table: confirm the feature is known, calculate its full cost, pay from current CE, resolve it, and apply only the feature’s own caps.

Actions and timing

How turns, triggers, technique activations, reactions, and legacy action terms resolve.

Rule priority

Specific revised text wins

Use the newest revised feature first, then this glossary, then unaltered Project V3 wording, then ordinary 5e. Within the same source, a specific exception beats a general rule only when it clearly says “instead,” “even if,” or otherwise identifies the exception.

Turn

Normal turn resources

A creature normally has movement up to its speed, one action, one bonus action if a feature provides one, one object interaction, and access to its reaction. Movement may be split around actions and attacks.

Reaction

One trigger, one response

You regain your reaction at the start of your turn. One creature can use only one reaction against a single triggering event, even if several features share that trigger. Resolve the reaction immediately after its stated trigger unless it says it interrupts.

Additional reactions

Distinct sources stack as written

A feature granting another reaction gives another reaction. Several distinct features can stack unless one says otherwise. Each reaction still needs a legal trigger. The same event can support more than one reaction when every reaction has a valid trigger; resolve them one at a time, and do not treat a reaction as a new occurrence of the triggering event.

Legacy “free action”

No action and no shared slot

A legacy free action requires no action. Use it whenever its written trigger occurs, or during your turn if it has no trigger. It has no added once-per-turn limit; its cost, target requirements, duration, same-name stacking rule, and any frequency printed in the feature still apply.

Full-turn action

Your complete commitment

You must begin the turn before moving or using an action or bonus action. The activation consumes your action, bonus action, object interaction, and all movement for that turn. It does not consume your reaction unless the feature says so. “Full action” by itself means a normal action unless the text expressly defines a full-turn action.

As an attack

Replace one attack

When legacy text activates a technique “as an attack,” it replaces one attack made as part of the Attack action. It is not an additional attack and cannot replace an opportunity attack or bonus-action attack unless expressly stated. “As part of an attack” modifies that one attack rather than creating another.

Additional action

Distinct sources stack

A creature can benefit from every distinct feature granting an additional action. Each action follows the restrictions, duration, and purpose written in its own source.

Additional action

It is a full action unless restricted

An additional action can take any normal action, including a CE feature or Domain, unless the feature granting that action supplies a restricted list. A full-turn action still consumes the turn unless an explicit calamity feature permits otherwise.

Frequency

Turn versus round

“Once per turn” resets at the beginning of every creature’s turn. “Once on your turn” can occur only during your turn. “Once per round” resets at the start of your next turn.

Simultaneous effects

Active creature decides

If several effects controlled by one creature resolve simultaneously, that creature orders them. Otherwise, the creature whose turn it is orders player-controlled effects and the GM orders hostile effects.

Trigger protection

An effect cannot trigger itself

Damage, movement, CE recovery, attacks, or conditions created by a feature cannot trigger that same feature again unless it expressly says it can. A trigger resolves only after the triggering event finishes, preventing recursive damage, movement, healing, and CE loops.

Durations and saves

Safe defaults for effects whose original wording omitted an end point, Focus requirement, or resistance method.

Time

Rounds and minutes

A round is approximately 6 seconds. One minute is 10 rounds. An effect lasting “until your next turn” ends at the start of that turn unless it specifically says the end.

Reapplication

Refresh, do not multiply

Applying the same named effect again refreshes its duration. It does not stack its condition, bonus, penalty, recurring damage, or other repeated benefit unless expressly stated.

Dismissal

End your own effect

If an effect says you may dismiss it at will but gives no timing, you may end it during your turn without an action. Ending it does not refund CE or another expended resource.

Technique Focus

One sustained technique

Technique Focus functions like spell concentration. You can maintain only one Focus effect at a time. Taking damage requires a Constitution saving throw with DC 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher. Focus ends if you are incapacitated, die, voluntarily end it, or begin another Focus effect.

Default Focus

When legacy text is silent

An ongoing technique requires Focus when it repeatedly damages or controls an area, can be moved or triggered again on later turns, grants a major combat transformation, or maintains hard control over more than one target. Passive class features and manifested creatures do not require Focus unless stated.

Successful save

Damage or condition

On a successful save against damage, take half damage unless stated otherwise. On a successful save against a non-damaging condition or forced action, suffer no effect.

Repeat save

Use the printed escape

A hard-control effect uses its printed duration and repeating save. Add an end-of-turn repeat save only when the source omitted every ending procedure; do not add one to a feature that deliberately states a fixed duration or no repeat.

Repeat immunity

Only when the feature grants it

Succeeding on a save does not create universal long-rest immunity. Apply immunity, escalating advantage, or retry limits only when the feature itself supplies them.

Hostile displacement

Unwilling creatures resist

If a technique would teleport or exchange an unwilling creature without listing a save, it makes a Charisma save against the user’s CE save DC. An unwilling creature pushed, pulled, grappled, or physically repositioned instead uses the physical save or contest stated by the feature.

Exhaustion

Use the feature’s actual rate

Exhaustion accumulates whenever its source says it does. There is no universal once-per-round ceiling. If a feature omits a required save or trigger, its individual ruling supplies one.

Accuracy

No universal ceiling

Attack and save-DC bonuses apply at their printed value. If one feature both raises a DC and imposes disadvantage, both apply unless that feature or a specific subsystem says otherwise.

Sheet 3 of 4

Summons and controlled creatures

One shared framework for shikigami, puppets, spirits, clones, swarms, and other created combatants.

Manifestation

What counts

A manifestation is one companion, summon, clone, puppet, spirit, or group created and controlled as one unit by a feature. Several creatures produced by one activation count as one manifestation only when their feature treats them as a swarm, pair, or group.

Statistics required

No stat block, no summon

A combat summon must have a complete stat block or an identified published stat block before it can be used. Appearance alone never supplies AC, HP, attacks, saves, movement, traits, or actions. The GM may approve a temporary stat block until formal errata exists.

Initiative

Immediately after the creator

A manifestation shares its creator’s initiative and takes its turn immediately after the creator. It moves independently and uses only the reactions its stat block permits.

Command

One command for one technique roster

Unless autonomous, a manifestation Dodges without a command. One bonus action gives the same simple command to every active creature created by one technique; an action may give different or complex orders. A specific technique can be more permissive.

Creature turns

Every creature keeps its stat block

Each summoned creature moves and takes the actions and reactions in its stat block. A swarm, pair, or group acts as one only when its feature or shared stat block says so.

Active roster

No universal manifestation limit

If a technique gives no active limit, it has no added CE-modifier cap. Summoning costs, available stored creatures, physical space, duration, and commands remain the practical limits.

Command range

Use the technique’s bond

Use the range printed by the technique. If none is listed, a supernatural command reaches any manifestation the creator can perceive on the same plane; a standing order continues when perception is lost.

Incapacitated creator

Defensive instinct

Without a specific rule, a manifestation whose creator is incapacitated may move, Dodge, protect itself, and approach its creator, but cannot use offensive or complex actions.

Exact statistics

The stat block is the permission

A manifestation gains everything in its own stat block and the creating feature, including legendary, mythic, or lair actions when actually listed. It does not copy the creator’s CE pool, feats, Domain, inventory, or class features unless expressly granted.

Split forms and clones

Resources divide; they do not refresh

Creating a clone divides current HP, current CE, limited uses, and other resources exactly as the feature states. Recombining adds the remaining divided resources together up to the original maximum and never restores what either body spent or lost.

At 0 HP

Disappear, do not refill

A manifestation reduced to 0 HP disappears unless its feature says it dies. Resummoning does not restore HP early; it returns with the HP it had unless the listed recovery condition has occurred.

Equipment

No copied inventory

Worn or carried objects are not duplicated when a summon or clone appears. Equipment supplied by a stat block disappears with it and cannot be sold, stored, or permanently transferred.

Ability improvements

Use the feature’s ceiling

Permanent and temporary ability improvements apply at their printed value. There is no universal score-20 ceiling for manifestations; a feature or stat block may impose one.

Specific techniques

Revised summon guides override this sheet

The revised Ten Shadows manifestation limits, paired Divine Dogs, grouped Gama, Mahoraga capacity, recovery, and autonomous behavior apply instead of these defaults wherever they differ. Other technique-specific summon guides do the same once revised.

Stacking and multiclass CE

How overlapping bonuses, damage amplifiers, defenses, triggers, and mixed class levels interact.

Same named effect

Use the strongest instance

Two instances of the same feature, technique, condition, or item effect do not stack. Apply the strongest numerical instance and the longest remaining duration.

Advantage

Ordinary 5e cancellation

Multiple sources of advantage or disadvantage still count once. If both apply, the roll is normal regardless of how many sources exist.

AC and calculations

Choose one formula

A creature uses only one AC calculation, such as armor or Unarmored Defense. Distinct flat bonuses may apply afterward; repeated copies of the same bonus do not.

Temporary HP

Never adds together

Temporary hit points do not stack. When receiving new temporary HP, keep the current amount or replace it with the new amount.

Damage amplifiers

Distinct effects can combine

Critical hits, maximization, vulnerability, Maximum Output, Chanting, Black Flash, and other distinct effects can apply to the same attack unless one of them expressly forbids the combination. Same-name effects still do not stack.

Declare additions → roll or maximize critical damage → apply Black Flash → apply resistance, immunity, or vulnerability.
Resistance penetration

Read the exact verb

“Bypasses” or “ignores resistance” treats resistance as normal damage but does not affect immunity. “Treats immunity as resistance” moves only immunity. “Ignores resistance and immunity” bypasses both. Distinct penetration effects can combine when their wording covers different steps.

Resistance and vulnerability

No repeated multiplication

Multiple resistances do not stack, and multiple vulnerabilities do not stack. If both apply to the same damage, they cancel and the target takes normal damage.

Conditions

One condition, one penalty

Repeated applications of the same condition do not intensify it. Different conditions apply normally unless one makes another redundant.

Maximum HP reduction

Same-source reductions refresh

Repeated maximum-HP reduction from the same named feature does not stack unless expressly stated. It uses the greatest reduction and the longest recovery condition.

Once per attack

Applied after all hits are known

An effect limited to once per attack applies once even if that attack deals several damage types or creates secondary damage. Once-per-turn additions apply to only one qualifying hit.

One reserve

All class dice build one CE pool

At character level 1, take the maximum Reserve Die of the starting class. Every later class level adds that new class’s rolled or fixed Reserve Die. Add the CE ability modifier once to the combined total, then add permanent bonuses.

One CE ability

The first choice remains

Choose Wisdom or Charisma when first gaining CE unless the starting class expressly names another score. Multiclassing never permits a second CE ability, a second CE pool, or another copy of the modifier.

Character level

Shared scaling

Total character level determines proficiency bonus. It does not combine class levels for class-specific features, and there is no universal Output Rank.

Class level

Feature progression

A class feature uses levels in that class unless it expressly says character level. Recovery features restore the one shared reserve but use their granting class’s level.

Innate Technique

One technique progression

Multiclassing does not grant a second Innate Technique. For a standard one-technique character, technique milestones use the highest individual level in a class that grants that technique, not the sum of all class levels.

Dual Technique Variant: a GM-approved character who explicitly possesses two Innate Techniques advances both by total character level. Both techniques still share one CE Reserve, one CE ability, one action economy, one Technique Focus slot, one reaction pool, and the normal Domain/Burnout limits. Gaining a second technique never creates duplicate CE, extra turns, a second Focus slot, or simultaneous Domains unless a specific calamity feature says otherwise.

Duplicate class features

No double recovery or defense

Identically named features do not stack or gain extra uses through multiclassing unless their multiclass rule expressly says they do. Extra Attack and AC calculations follow ordinary 5e multiclass rules.

Example

Warrior 5 / Support Specialist 3

With fixed gains and a +4 CE modifier: 6 CE from the starting Warrior d6 + 16 CE from four later Warrior levels + 15 CE from three Support d8 levels + the modifier once = 41 maximum CE. The character uses total level 8 for PB, but Warrior 5 and Support 3 for their respective class features.

Heavenly Restriction: it gains no Reserve Dice and cannot activate CE-dependent features, but it may multiclass normally. Physical, tactical, skill, and item features from those levels remain available.

Part IV

Advanced Sorcery

Domains, clashes, RCT, injuries, Maximum Output, Black Flash, and the Zone.

System 1 • Domain Expansion

Make contact inevitable—not resistance impossible.

A complete Domain remains a fight-defining commitment. Its imbued technique becomes oppressive, but defenses, barrier damage, anti-domain techniques, and opposing Domains all create real counterplay.

Complete Domain • Baseline

Expanding the innate domain

  • Activation: full-turn action and the required hands or signs.
  • Cost: 20 CE.
  • Area: 60-foot-radius enclosed space centered on you.
  • Duration: 1 minute, or until it collapses.
  • Targets: choose perceived creatures in the area when it opens. Every other creature is moved harmlessly to the nearest space outside.

A technique-specific Domain may change its action, cost, shape, range, target rule, or duration. Its explicit wording wins.

Domain Stability

Supreme, but not concentration-proof

Opening a Domain ends your current Technique Focus, and the Domain occupies that Focus slot. It does not use ordinary Focus checks. Instead, if one damage source deals at least 20% of your hit point maximum to you, make a Constitution save with DC 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher. On a failure, the Domain collapses.

It also collapses if you are incapacitated, die, dismiss it without an action on your turn, its barrier or anchor reaches 0 HP, or another rule breaks it.

A Domain ending for any reason causes Technique Burnout.
Signature Sure-Hit Event

One Domain-specific effect resolves at initiative 20

Every complete lethal Domain names one self-contained Sure-Hit Event. At initiative count 20 each round—after Domain clash checks—the event resolves once against every eligible chosen target still inside. It is a manifestation of the Domain itself, not an activation of the user’s ordinary attack or technique.

  • An unprotected chosen target receives the event’s complete printed result without an attack roll or initial saving throw.
  • A creature protected by an anti-domain defense uses the fallback attack or saving throw printed in that Domain. If none is printed, use the most relevant save; success halves damage and prevents movement, conditions, and secondary riders.
  • Every attack, summon attack, and saving-throw feature used outside that named event resolves normally, even when it shares the Domain’s technique.
Event timing

One inevitable moment per round

The Sure-Hit Event resolves once at initiative count 20 and loses ties to other initiative-20 effects. A creature entering after that count waits until the next round. The event is not a critical hit and cannot trigger Black Flash unless its own entry explicitly creates a rolled critical.

Ordinary activations

The rest of the character sheet still rolls

The Sure-Hit Event costs no extra action or CE unless its entry says otherwise. Separately used techniques retain their printed actions, CE costs, attack rolls, and saving throws. Domain terrain, origins, summons, and movement benefits can still modify how those ordinary features are used.

Anti-domain protection

Suppress the guarantee

Simple Domain, Hollow Wicker Basket, and similar defenses suppress sure-hit against protected creatures. They do not end the Domain, erase its terrain, prevent ordinary attacks, or automatically negate its separate environmental rules.

Lethal thresholds

The named event governs its own ceiling

A Sure-Hit Event applies its full printed effect, including control, confiscation, soul alteration, limb loss, maximum-HP damage, or death when that event expressly says so. The Domain cannot invent an effect absent from its entry, but there is no universal damage ceiling or low-HP gate.

Non-lethal Domain

A disclosed rule, a fair procedure

A non-lethal Domain replaces sure-hit with a specific rule, trigger, consequence, and completion condition established by its feature. The rule is understood by every trapped creature when the Domain opens. A creature that knowingly violates it makes the listed save; on a success it avoids that consequence until the start of its next turn.

Its procedure must offer meaningful choices. Without explicit text, it cannot cause instant death, permanent resource loss, or unavoidable violence. It clashes and burns out normally.

Enclosed barrier

Statistics and weak points

AC: 12 + proficiency bonus
HP: 5 × character level + 10 × refinement tier

The barrier is immune to psychic and poison damage. By default it resists damage from inside and is vulnerable to damage from outside. It automatically fails saving throws and is immune to conditions.

From inside, a creature must first use an action and succeed on Wisdom (Perception) against the creator’s CE save DC. Success exposes a weak point to that creature until the end of its next turn.

Barrier configuration • Tier 3

Choose where it is vulnerable

  • Standard: resistance inside, vulnerability outside.
  • Inverted: vulnerability inside, resistance outside.
  • Balanced (+5 CE): neither resistance nor vulnerability on either side.

A closed barrier blocks physical passage and teleportation across it unless an effect expressly crosses planar barriers. Destroying it collapses the Domain; ordinary damage does not leave a lasting hole.

Open-barrier Domain • Tier 5

A divine technique with an escape route

When expanding, you may omit the outer barrier and choose a visible point on the ground as the Domain’s anchor. The Domain has a 120-foot radius and lasts 1 minute. Creatures can enter or leave normally, but an eligible creature inside remains subject to its effect.

The anchor has AC equal to your CE save DC, HP equal to 5 × your character level, immunity to psychic and poison damage, and no vulnerability. Destroying it collapses the Domain. During a clash with a closed Domain, the open Domain attacks the closed barrier from outside at initiative 20, dealing 4d10 + CE modifier force damage before outside vulnerability is applied. Two open Domains do not make this barrier attack.

Technique Burnout

The technique goes dark—not the entire character

During Burnout you cannot activate an Innate Technique feature, Cursed Technique Reversal, Maximum Output, Maximum Technique, or Domain Expansion. You may still move, attack with weapons, use class features independent of your Innate Technique, reinforce yourself with generic CE, use anti-domain techniques, and use generic RCT.

Existing manifestations remain and may receive ordinary commands, but cannot activate a burned-out technique through the creator. Burnout is not exhaustion, cannot be removed by ordinary condition cleansing, and no longer ends when Black Flash occurs.

Burnout Recovery

Destroy and rebuild

At Tier 4 with RCT, use an action and spend 8 CE to make a DC 15 Constitution save. Success ends Burnout. Failure deals 6d6 psychic damage that cannot be reduced, leaves Burnout intact, and prevents actions and reactions until the start of your next turn.

Tier 4 permits one attempt per long rest. At Tier 5 you may retry until a failure; each prior success since your long rest raises the DC by 5.

Refinement is a milestone, not a currency. The GM awards a tier after a meaningful breakthrough, a decisive clash, sustained narrative training, or technique evolution. Repeatedly defeating trivial creatures, spending unused CE before bed, or staging friendly clashes never generates refinement.

Domain procedure

Clashes resolve through momentum.

Refinement matters, but it does not automatically erase a close rival. Each round creates visible progress and gives both sorcerers time to change the battle.

One common engine

System 2 • Reverse Cursed Technique

Regeneration needs a visible limit.

RCT is powerful emergency healing, not a second infinite health bar. CE controls burst speed; the Healing Reserve controls how much tissue can be rebuilt before a long rest.

Unlocking RCT

A feature or genuine breakthrough

You gain RCT from a class or technique feature, a feat, or a GM-awarded breakthrough after a near-death or exceptional CE insight. Remove the random d100 roll. Surviving danger can create the opportunity, but never guarantees the ability.

Healing Reserve maximum = 10 × total character level.
Reserve

One pool, restored on a long rest

Your Healing Reserve is separate from HP and CE. It returns to its maximum when you finish a long rest, not a short rest. RCT has no automatic healing pulse. If a feature increases the reserve, apply that increase once to this formula.

Reserve spent on healing or repairs does not return until the long rest, even if CE is restored sooner.

Self-healing

Ten hit points per CE

Once per round while conscious, use a bonus action—or a reaction immediately after taking damage—to spend from 1 CE up to your CE ability modifier. Heal up to 10 HP per CE spent, limited by your missing HP and remaining Healing Reserve. Deduct only the HP actually restored from the reserve.

If the triggering damage reduced you to 0 HP, you cannot use the reaction unless a feature expressly permits RCT while unconscious or at 0 HP. Unspent healing from an activation is lost; CE cannot be refunded.

Positive Energy Output

Others require mastery

Base RCT targets only you. A feature granting RCT Output lets you touch another creature as an action, spending up to your CE ability modifier at a rate of 2 CE per 10 HP restored and deducting the healing from your reserve. A class feature may improve this rate or action.

What RCT cannot do

Clear boundaries

RCT cannot restore CE, revive a dead creature, recreate a destroyed brain or torso, remove Technique Burnout, repair soul damage, or end a curse or supernatural condition unless a feature expressly says it can.

Positive damage

Not part of basic healing

You do not automatically weaponize positive energy. If RCT Output lets you harm Cursed Spirits, Undead, or another listed creature, use that feature’s revised damage or the legacy conversion below.

System 3 • Critical Injury

Injuries come from catastrophic hits.

The original Constitution modifier × proficiency formula could become 0 and did not say whether damage accumulated. This version keys injury severity to the victim’s actual durability and checks one hit at a time.

Break, sever, or endure

  • Below Break: no lasting injury.

Legacy automatic injuries

Still respect durability

A feature that says it automatically breaks or severs a body part must still deal at least the Break threshold in one hit. If it lists no damage, a failed save can break the part only. It can sever without damage only if it expressly says so and the target is at or below 25% of its hit point maximum.

System 4 • Maximum Output

Overcharge one dimension.

Maximum Output is the universal act of forcing an ordinary technique above its safe operating level. It is not a named Maximum Technique and no longer turns a normal attack bonus into an accuracy-breaking number.

Prerequisite and activation

20 CE ability score, level 11

You must have a CE ability score of 20 and either a feature or breakthrough that unlocks Maximum Output. As a bonus action, declare one eligible technique you will activate before the end of the current turn and pay its Output surcharge. You pay and activate the technique normally afterward.

If you do not activate it before the turn ends, the surcharge is lost. The base technique must be legal at your Output Rank; the surcharge itself does not make an otherwise legal technique exceed that rank.

Output surcharge

Double the base—not every option

The surcharge equals the technique’s printed fixed base CE cost, minimum 2 CE. A variable-cost technique uses the X you choose as its surcharge. Optional overcharge, extra targets, or other add-on CE is paid once and is not doubled.

Total CE = base cost + Output surcharge + optional CE.

A normally free technique has a 2 CE surcharge. Cost reduction applies to the base activation only, not the surcharge.

System 5 • Black Flash

The original miracle stays rare.

This campaign preserves Project V3's Black Flash procedure: first land a critical hit, then confirm the millionth-of-a-second alignment on a second d20. The Zone is revised separately so Black Flash never refills RCT or erases Technique Burnout.

Black Flash check

Critical hit, then a second d20

Whenever a creature capable of using cursed energy lands a critical hit with an attack, roll a second unmodified d20 before damage. On a natural 20, the attack becomes a Black Flash.

There is no universal once-per-turn or once-per-round limit on Black Flash attempts or successes. Every qualifying critical may attempt the confirmation roll. A saving throw, automatic hit, passive effect, initiative-20 Domain event, or manifestation attack does not qualify unless a specific feature explicitly creates a qualifying critical hit.

Forced and expanded criticals

A critical is a critical unless the feature says otherwise

A forced critical or expanded critical range qualifies if the triggering feature permits Black Flash. An explicit restriction such as Great Luck's non-qualifying critical still wins. A feature that says an attack is automatically a Black Flash skips the confirmation roll.

Declared riders

Finish building the critical first

Choose and pay any damage rider that must be declared before damage is rolled, then resolve the complete critical hit. Effects that trigger only after damage is dealt remain separate and are not multiplied.

Black Flash damage

Triple the completed critical

Roll the qualifying critical hit normally, including its critical dice, flat modifiers, and eligible declared riders. Multiply that completed critical-hit damage by 3.

Black Flash damage = 3 × completed critical-hit damage.

This is multiplication of the final critical damage, not one extra set of dice.

Defense and injury

Bypass resistance, not immunity

The complete Black Flash hit ignores resistance to its damage types. Immunity still applies unless another feature separately says it ignores immunity. Use final HP lost from the full Black Flash when checking the revised injury thresholds.

Part V

Innate Techniques

All 35 Project techniques, the separate X Tier exception, descriptive power tiers, complete procedures, and summon profiles.

Five tiers and one exception

Power tiers without a ban list

A technique’s tier is based on its normal encounter influence, narrative reach, rules load, and late-game volatility. It is not the user’s grade and does not predict the quality of the player’s build.

TIER I

Focused

A narrow, dependable combat identity with little off-sheet bookkeeping. Strong when used well, easy to prepare around.

TIER II

Versatile

Multiple tactical vectors, mobility, control, support, or a contained summon package. It can reshape a fight without rewriting the campaign.

TIER III

Apex

Consistent scene control, unusually high output, early Domain access, or party-wide swing potential. Encounters should anticipate its core loop.

TIER IV

Special Grade

Permanent acquisition, armies, copying, nullification, body or soul rewriting, or a bespoke rules engine. It asks for active GM preparation.

TIER V

Calamity

A campaign-defining endpoint whose full release can invalidate normal encounter assumptions. Still a standard player choice; the label sets expectations.

X TIER

Outside Classification

Reserved for Dimensional Authorship: a player-facing technique whose normal progression edits dice faces, turns, dimensions, local physics, and other rules used to measure techniques. It is not Tier VI, a character grade, or shorthand for higher damage.

Tier movement is descriptive. A table that seldom uses downtime may experience Body Manipulation below its listed tier. A clever Boogie Woogie user may play above Tier II. Do not move a character between tiers mid-campaign as a reward or punishment; the label belongs to the technique’s rules footprint. X Tier is likewise an authored exception, not a promotion for an optimized Tier V technique: modest play does not demote X, and extreme play does not promote another technique into X Tier.

Shared technique language

Core math

One CE ability

CE attack = PB + CE modifier. CE save DC = 8 + PB + CE modifier.

Spending

No added universal cap

If the Project feature allows any amount of CE, the user may spend any current CE. Ignore a PB or Output Rank ceiling added only by an earlier repair; printed limits and anti-recursion limits remain.

Actions

Extra means extra

Extra actions and reactions stack from distinct sources and can perform any action unless their granting feature lists a restriction. A legacy free action uses no action and has no added once-per-turn limit.

Stacking

Distinct effects combine

Same-name effects do not stack. Critical hits, vulnerability, maximization, Chanting, Maximum Output, and Black Flash may combine unless a specific feature forbids it.

Summons

No invented active-roster cap

Stored, prepared, or tamed creatures may be active in any number the Project technique permits. Each still needs its summon cost and command procedure. A catalog cap survives only when the source or dedicated Ten Shadows guide provides it.

Domains

One signature event

The Domain chapter controls: each complete lethal Domain has one self-contained Sure-Hit Event at initiative count 20. All ordinary attacks, summon attacks, and saving-throw features inside resolve normally unless their own text says otherwise.

Control

Repeating saves only when needed

A condition uses the saves and duration printed by its feature. A repair adds a repeat save only when the source omitted every way to end continuing hard control.

Precedence

Specific fiction wins

Project feature text supplies identity and scale; this catalog fills missing actions, ranges, targets, and stat blocks. The combined guide overrides earlier global safety caps.

How to read the audit

Verdicts describe editing, not power.

The status beside an option tells you how much its wording changed. The separate impact signal only warns the GM how strongly it can alter a scene.

Adopted

Use the printed effect

The Project wording already supplies a usable procedure. Only the shared terminology on this page applies.

Clarified

Same effect, complete procedure

The power stays intact; this guide adds a missing trigger, action, target, duration, save, or stacking instruction.

Rebuilt

Contradiction or loop repaired

A recursive bonus, undefined resource, impossible prerequisite, or open-ended permission receives replacement text.

Superseded

Use the dedicated revised subsystem

The printed entry remains in the inventory, but a later guide has already replaced it. This avoids two competing versions of the same mechanic.

High impact / Pinnacle

Preparation signal, never a ban

These labels identify encounter- or campaign-shaping options. They add no permission gate, hidden prerequisite, or automatic nerf.

Shared option language

Procedures without safety rails.

Feats, mindsets, and cursed tools use their printed power. Clarifications supply timing and targets, not a lower ceiling.

Actions and CE

CE score means the ability score. A free action uses no action and follows its written trigger without an added frequency cap. Extra actions/reactions stack from distinct sources. Optional CE has no universal PB cap.

Damage and penetration

Distinct damage amplifiers can combine. “Bypasses resistance” ignores resistance but not immunity; “ignores resistance and immunity” does both. Black Flash uses the restored triple-critical rule.

Audit verdicts

Adopted, Clarified, Rebuilt, and Superseded describe editing work only. High Impact and Pinnacle are GM preparation signals, never permission gates.

Source-first ceiling

If an audit row added a once-per-turn, PB, active-roster, or one-amplifier ceiling solely to curb an optimized combination, ignore that ceiling. Limits that stop recursion or complete missing duration/target rules remain.

Nine complete options

Mindsets turn Inspiration into a climax.

Choose one mindset. You can hold no more than one Inspiration, the GM awards it as normal, and spending it requires no action at the listed trigger. A mindset changes only when the story, GM, or a level-up permits it.

Pinnacle strike

Honored One

After a critical hit qualifies—or after a Black Flash is confirmed—and before damage is rolled, spend Inspiration to maximize the entire attack’s damage. This includes critical dice, Black Flash damage, flat modifiers, and declared riders.

Recovery

Cog

On your turn, spend Inspiration to end every condition and injury affecting you and regain HP equal to one quarter of maximum HP, rounded down. Ongoing sources can reapply an effect; destroyed anatomy and death are not conditions.

Tempo

Professional

On your turn, spend Inspiration to gain haste for 1 minute without Technique Focus. The normal lethargy occurs when it ends, and its extra action follows the spell’s restrictions.

High impact

Vengeful

Choose one creature you can perceive and spend Inspiration. For 5 rounds, it is vulnerable to damage from your attacks. This vulnerability can combine with other distinct effects.

Escalation

Rush

Spend Inspiration when you hit. For 1 minute, every attack you land grants a cumulative +1 to your attack and damage rolls. The source provides no stack ceiling; all bonuses end together.

Party climax

Just

Choose up to five perceived creatures as villains and spend Inspiration. For 3 rounds, you and your allies have advantage on attacks against them and cannot be reduced below 1 HP while at least one designated villain remains conscious.

Shared fate

Exceptional

Choose one willing creature and spend Inspiration. Until either creature ends the bond, dies, or finishes a long rest, form one HP pool whose maximum and current value equal both totals. Damage and healing change the pool; conditions remain individual. When it ends, divide remaining HP as you choose, up to each maximum.

CE recovery

Limit Breaker

On your turn, spend Inspiration to regain CE equal to half your maximum CE, rounded down, without exceeding that maximum.

Action break

Free

Spend Inspiration to take one complete additional action immediately, or to regain and immediately use a reaction. The action can activate any feature you could normally use with an action, including a technique or Domain.
