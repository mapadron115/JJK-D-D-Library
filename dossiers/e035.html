Ten Shadows Technique

Ten Shadows Technique

Core law

Core manifestation rules

Revised Jujutsu Kaisen 5e guide for the Ten Shadows Technique, its rituals, shadow manipulation, Shadow Wearing, Maximum: Embodiment, fusions, four Innate Technique feats, and Domain Expansion. These rules apply to every tamed shadow unless a stat block explicitly overrides them.

Manifest

Call from shadow

As an action, spend the listed CE and manifest a tamed shikigami in an unoccupied space within 5 feet of you. It remains until reduced to 0 HP, dismissed without an action, or you die.

Initiative

Act as one

A shikigami shares your initiative and takes its turn immediately after yours. It moves and uses its reaction independently.

Command

One manifestation

As a bonus action, command one manifestation to take an action. Every uncommanded manifestation Dodges. A standing order guides behavior but never removes this recurring command cost.

Range

Telepathic bond

You can communicate with your shikigami within 120 feet. Beyond that range it follows its last instruction and prioritizes returning to you.

Scaling

Your cursed energy

A shadow uses the higher of its printed attack bonus or your CE attack bonus, and the higher of its printed save DC or your CE save DC. Its attacks count as cursed attacks.

Incapacitated

Instinct takes over

If you are incapacitated, each manifested shikigami may act normally without being commanded, protecting you according to its nature and last order.

Manifestation counting: The Divine Dogs together count as one manifestation and share one action and reaction. All active Gama likewise count collectively as one manifestation and share one action and reaction. Mahoraga consumes your entire normal manifestation capacity. A fusion counts as one manifestation, although every component remains unavailable while merged.

Core law

Death and Totality

Death is permanent—but a destroyed shikigami's strength is never wasted. It can be passed on through Totality.

Death

Reduced to 0 HP

A shikigami reduced to 0 HP dies and cannot be summoned again. There is no long-rest recovery for a destroyed shadow: the shadow is gone, and its loss is felt in the roster.

Totality ritual

Passing on the dead

During a short or long rest, spend 1 hour in ritual to pass one destroyed shikigami's strength into a different tamed shikigami, forging a permanent Totality. Rabbit Escape and Mahoraga cannot take part in a Totality ritual—as the destroyed shadow or as the recipient—and a Totality (including Divine Dog Totality) cannot receive another Totality.A Totality counts as one manifestation. Its destroyed donor is gone.

  • Ability scores: the Totality takes the higher of each ability score from the two, plus the smaller shikigami's ability modifier as a bonus.
  • Hit points: the two maximums are added together.
  • Speed: the two speeds are added together.
  • Identity: the Totality gains all features, traits, actions, reactions, skills, saves, resistances, and immunities from both shikigami, with bonus damage dice from any modifier differences.
  • Size: you choose either shikigami's size.
  • Manifestation cost: the higher of the two CE costs plus half the lower.
  • Challenge rating: the higher of the two CRs plus half the smaller's CR, rounded up.

Optional DM ruling

A kinder shadow

The DM may choose to ignore Totality entirely. If so, all of your shikigami return when you finish a long rest, and the Totality ritual is not used at this table.

Core law

Shikigami User interaction

This prevents the class from receiving an unrelated generic companion in addition to the full Ten Shadows roster.

If a Shikigami User selects Ten Shadows, the Divine Dogs replace the generic Shikigami Companion. Choose one currently available Ten Shadows manifestation as your Bonded Shadow after each long rest. Class features that specifically name “your companion” apply only to that Bonded Shadow, not the entire roster.

Full progression

Technique progression

The roster is earned through victory.

Level 1Divine Dogs

You begin with the paired Divine Dogs already tamed. Each costs 1 CE to manifest; together they count as one manifestation, sharing one action and reaction.

You may normally maintain one manifestation at 1st level.

Level 3Trial Unlock & Expanded Manifestation

Trial unlock. The taming ritual unlocks: you may now challenge any untamed shadow on the roster through the taming ritual. The full roster is challengeable from 3rd level — there is no higher level prerequisite; the listed CE cost, the no-outside-aid rule, and the creature waiting within the ritual are the gates.

Your normal manifestation capacity increases to two.

Beginning the trial. Taming trials unlock at 3rd level. Use an action and pay the shikigami’s listed CE cost. It appears at full HP in an unoccupied space within 30 feet. Roll initiative normally. It treats you as its primary enemy. Your already-tamed shikigami are part of your technique and may participate.

Victory and interference. Reducing the untamed shadow to 0 HP without outside aid permanently tames it. If another creature directly attacks, heals, restrains, buffs, or otherwise assists, the taming is invalidated. An interfering creature becomes a ritual participant and a valid target. The ritual continues until the shikigami or every ritual participant is defeated. CE is spent even if the ritual fails.

Mahoraga: The Eight-Handled Sword Divergent Sila Divine General uses its complete CR 25 stat block during and after taming. No diminished player version exists. Anyone capable of defeating it has earned the full measure of the Ten Shadows Technique.

Level 6Shadow Storage

Shadow Storage. Your shadow becomes an extradimensional inventory whose burden still rests on your body.

You may store or retrieve one object you are carrying as your normal object interaction. Stored objects continue to count against your carrying capacity. Only objects can be stored unless another feature explicitly permits a creature to enter. An object must fit through a 5-foot opening and cannot be actively worn or carried by an unwilling creature. For 1 CE, you may retrieve a stored object as a reaction or cause it to emerge from a visible shadow within 30 feet.

Level 8Shadow Wearing

Shadow Wearing. At 8th level, you learn to draw a tamed shikigami’s shadow over your own body rather than fully manifesting it. This internal-mastery route trades that shadow’s manifestation for a focused personal form.

Activation: As a bonus action, choose one tamed, available shikigami that is not currently manifested or contributing to a fusion.

Cost: Spend cursed energy equal to half that shikigami’s normal summon cost, rounded up. Minimum 1 CE.

Duration: 1 minute. It ends early if you become unconscious, dismiss it without an action, or activate another Shadow Wearing form.

Limit: You can wear only one shikigami at a time.

Source lock: While wearing a shikigami, you cannot manifest it or use it as the base or donor of either fusion system.

Other shadows: Wearing is not a manifestation and occupies no manifestation slot. You may manifest and command other shikigami normally.

Unavailable shadows: If that shikigami has died (reduced to 0 HP), you cannot wear it.

Attack and save rules. Shadow Wearing is a body-overlap technique, so the user supplies the accuracy, control, and cursed energy output. Any attack caused by Shadow Wearing uses your Cursed Energy attack bonus unless the DM rules a weapon attack is more appropriate. Any saving throw caused by Shadow Wearing uses your Cursed Energy save DC. Wearing abilities use your actions, bonus actions, and reactions as written; they require no shikigami command. An expended once-per-activation ability returns only when you pay to begin a new activation of that form. Shadow Wearing is your body bearing a shikigami’s function, not a second creature or a separate pool of hit points.

Level 11Wells Unknown Abyss

Wells Unknown Abyss. Merge two manifested shadows through one of fifteen stable, predetermined recipes. Your normal manifestation capacity increases to three.

As an action, merge two manifested shikigami through one of the fifteen recipes in the Fusion Catalog. No additional CE is required after both components are manifested. The recipe identifies the base and the exact donor package it gains.

The fusion occupies one manifestation slot. Neither component can exist separately while merged. Undo the fusion as a bonus action; neither component regains HP. If the fusion reaches 0 HP, both components die permanently and are eligible for the Totality ritual.

Level 17Wheel Wearing

Wheel Wearing. Prerequisite: 17th level, Shadow Wearing, and Mahoraga tamed and currently available.

As a bonus action, spend 10 CE to wear the shadow of the Eight-Handled Sword Divergent Sila Divine General Mahoraga. This form lasts for 1 minute, locks Mahoraga as its source shadow, and can be used only once per long rest.

Adaptive Wheel. When you take damage or fail a saving throw against a phenomenon, you may open an adaptation track for that specific damage type, attack, technique, condition, or environmental obstacle. You may maintain a number of tracks equal to your proficiency bonus.

At the end of your turn, advance one track by one turn if you were exposed to its phenomenon since the end of your previous turn. A track cannot advance this way more than once per round.

1 turn: you gain advantage on future saves/checks against that specific effect. 2 turns: you gain resistance to that specific damage or advantage on saves and checks against that effect; if neither applies, the DM removes one limited part of it. 3 turns: you become adapted — for the rest of Wheel Wearing, you are immune to that specific damage or condition, or can ignore or counteract that exact obstacle as the DM determines.

Wheel tools. Quick Adaptation: as a reaction when you are exposed to a phenomenon on an existing track, spend 3 CE to advance that track once immediately; you may do this a number of times equal to your proficiency bonus per long rest. General’s Recovery: at the start of your turn, regain hit points equal to your proficiency bonus + your CE modifier. Executioner’s Edge: once per turn when you hit a cursed spirit or undead creature with a melee attack, deal an extra 3d12 radiant damage. Strain of Adaptation: when Wheel Wearing ends, you cannot activate Shadow Wearing, manifest Mahoraga, or use either fusion system until the end of your next turn. Heavy Strain: if you completed three or more adaptation tracks, gain 1 level of exhaustion when the form ends.

Level 18Domain Expansion: Chimera Shadow Garden

Domain Expansion: Chimera Shadow Garden. Activation costs 20 CE; use the system’s normal lethal-domain activation, barrier, range, duration, clash, and burnout rules. Chimera Shadow Garden uses Shadow Submersion at initiative count 20 as its sole sure-hit event. Every shikigami attack and ability continues to resolve normally.

The interior becomes a bottomless garden of fluid shadow. Terrain is difficult for creatures of your choice, while you gain the benefits of freedom of movement.

Sole sure-hit event: Shadow Submersion. At initiative 20, chosen hostile creatures touching the Domain’s surfaces automatically become restrained until the next initiative 20. A creature protected from the sure-hit instead makes a Strength save, avoiding the restraint on a success. A restrained creature may use its action to attempt a Strength check against your CE save DC, ending the restraint early on a success.

Shadow Clones. As a bonus action, spend up to your CE modifier in CE to create that many clones. Each has your AC, 1 HP, your speed, and no attacks or class features. As a reaction before damage is dealt, swap with a clone within 60 feet and make it the attack’s target.

Shikigami Copies. Once per round when you manifest a non-Mahoraga shikigami, create one perfect temporary copy without additional CE. Copies ignore the normal manifestation limit but share the normal command economy.

Free manifestations. Shikigami manifested within the Domain cost 0 CE. Any manifestation or copy created for free disappears when the Domain ends; a shadow that was already active before expansion remains afterward.

Innate Technique Feats

Innate Technique feats

Divine Dog Totality, Shadow Skulker, Chimerafication, and Maximum: Embodiment are the complete optional feat list.

Prerequisite: Divine Dogs tamed, and one Divine Dog has died (reduced to 0 HP).

Divine Dog Totality

Inheritance. Manifest the surviving Dog as Divine Dog Totality. Totality is mutually exclusive with both original Dogs and uses the revised Totality stat block.

Shadow Skulker

Shadow movement. As an action for 2 CE, submerge into your shadow for consecutive turns equal to your Constitution modifier (minimum one). While submerged, you have total cover and perceive the space around your shadow; your shadow moves across connected surfaces at half your speed; you may summon, dismiss, and command Ten Shadows shikigami; you cannot personally attack, use RCT, expand a Domain, or activate other cursed techniques. Emerge within 5 feet as a bonus action. You are forcibly ejected when the duration ends — or at the end of your next turn if no manifested shikigami remain.

Prerequisite: Wells Unknown Abyss and a 20 CE ability score.

Chimerafication

Freeform fusion. Build one base plus as many as three donor additions under the Chimerafication rules: living shikigami combine — no shadow is sacrificed and none need be dead. Three ready-to-play presets — Zagroth, Hageshi, and Agito — appear in the Fusion Catalog.

Prerequisite: Chimera Shadow Garden (Domain Expansion).

Maximum: Embodiment

Maximum Technique. As an action, spend half the manifestation cost (minimum 1 CE) of one of your tamed shikigami (no Totality or Chimera). Rather than summoning it, you instead gain that shikigami's movement, actions, legendary actions, features, and one ability score of your choice for 1 hour. You can embody one shikigami at a time. Mahoraga: borrowing Mahoraga's Adaptation grants its Eight-Handled Wheel three-stage adaptation as described in this dossier's Mahoraga stat block (damage types, conditions, and obstacles each adapt through exposure → resistance → immunity or functional counter).

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Fusion systems

Chimerafication is the late-game freedom to build something unprecedented.

Chimerafication

Prerequisites: Wells Unknown Abyss and a 20 CE ability score.

Choose one tamed shikigami as the base and up to three different tamed shikigami as additions. The donors need not be manifested beforehand, but you pay the normal manifestation cost of every participant.

  • The base retains its complete stat block.
  • Each donor contributes one named trait, action, bonus action, reaction, or special movement speed.
  • Each donor adds one-quarter of its maximum HP, rounded down, to the base's maximum HP.
  • Ability scores, AC, saving throws, and wholesale stat blocks cannot be donated.
  • A transferred Multiattack works only with the attacks actually named in its wording.
  • The fusion counts as one manifestation. All contributors remain unavailable separately.
  • If reduced to 0 HP, every contributor dies permanently and is eligible for the Totality ritual.

Full freedom at the pinnacle: A tamed Mahoraga may serve as either the base or a donor. Conquering Mahoraga is already the required balancing event.

Eight-Handled Wheel

Track each distinct damage type or phenomenon separately through three adaptation stages. The first resolved exposure begins adaptation. The second grants resistance, or advantage against an effect that does not deal damage. The third grants immunity or a specific functional counter. Each later distinct hit or failed save from the same phenomenon advances it one stage after that instance resolves; multiple hits can accelerate adaptation.

Conditional Adaptation

When afflicted by a condition, Mahoraga recovers at the end of its next turn. Repeated applications advance that condition through the same three-stage process, eventually granting immunity for the remainder of the manifestation.

Obstacle Adaptation

Barriers, unreachable movement, spatial defenses, and other obstacles can be treated as phenomena. Upon completing adaptation, Mahoraga develops a DM-determined method of overcoming that exact obstacle.

Ten Shadows — Fixed Fusion

Agito

A fixed named chimera from the V.6 visual supplement, formed through chimerafication: Nue as the base, with Great Serpent, Mourn Tiger, and Round Deer as living donor additions — the same chassis as Zagroth and Hageshi below. All four contributing shadows must be tamed. No shadow is sacrificed and none need be dead: the four combine alive. While Agito is manifested (1 minute), its components cannot be summoned or used separately. If Agito is reduced to 0 HP, all four component shadows die permanently and are eligible for the Totality ritual. Agito cannot receive another Totality or chimera, and it counts as one manifestation.

Campaign pacing

Suggested Progression

Levels 1–5

Summon and command the Divine Dogs. Taming trials unlock at 3rd level—learn the danger of losing shadows, because a destroyed shadow is gone for good.

Level 6

Shadow Storage expands the technique’s utility and makes the user feel like the shadow is part of their body.

Level 8

Shadow Wearing unlocks for every tamed and available shadow. No additional roster gate is imposed.

Level 11

Wells Unknown Abyss begins the external fusion route, while Shadow Wearing remains internal mastery.

Level 17

A user who has tamed Mahoraga unlocks Wheel Wearing as a once-per-long-rest capstone.

Level 18

Chimera Shadow Garden completes the technique’s progression by turning the battlefield itself into living shadow.


Training Arc Advice

Begin with senses, movement, and defense. Heavy forms such as Mourn Tiger, Max Elephant, Round Deer, and Piercing Ox are not level-gated, but their costs and body-forward tactics make them natural later breakthroughs.

Balance Philosophy

Shadow Wearing should make the Ten Shadows user more versatile, not better at everything at once. The biggest limiter is choosing one form and giving up that shikigami as a summon during the duration.

Chimera Shadow: Wildebeast of Hell’s Savannah — Zagroth

Where Hageshi says: you shall not pass,

Lore

Zagroth is not shaped to defend territory. It is shaped to erase escape routes. Its four shadows knot together beneath a fleeing creature before the chimera erupts from the path ahead, as though the hunt had already decided where the prey would die.

1. Predator Core

Zagroth has advantage on attacks against a creature if the summoner or one of the summoner’s allies is within 5 feet of that creature.

Once per turn, if Zagroth hits a creature that has already taken damage this round, it deals an additional 2d6 slashing damage.

2. Oxbreaker Charge

If Zagroth moves at least 20 feet directly toward its target without turning during the same turn, its next melee hit deals an additional 1d12 piercing damage per 10 feet moved, capped at the summoner’s proficiency bonus in d12s. These dice disappear after the attack, hit or miss.

A Huge or smaller target must succeed on a Strength save against the summoner’s CE save DC or be knocked prone or pushed 20 feet, summoner’s choice.

3. Tiger Phase

Once per round, Zagroth can move through creatures or objects as difficult terrain. It takes 2d4 force damage if it ends its turn inside an object.

Once per turn, if it emerges from a creature’s space, shadow, or solid object before attacking, its next hit deals an additional 1d8 slashing damage.

4. Serpent Lash

Zagroth’s melee attacks have 15-foot reach.

Once per turn when Zagroth hits a Huge or smaller creature, that creature must succeed on a Strength save against the summoner’s CE save DC or become grappled. A prone target is restrained for the duration of the grapple instead. The escape DC equals the summoner’s CE save DC, and Zagroth can hold only one creature at a time.

5. Hell Savannah Pounce

Once per summon, as an action, Zagroth moves up to its speed without provoking opportunity attacks and may phase through one creature or solid obstacle during that movement. It then makes one melee attack with advantage.

On a hit, the attack gains Tiger Phase and Predator Core damage. It also gains Oxbreaker Charge dice based on the final uninterrupted straight-line distance Zagroth moved directly toward the target.

The target then makes one Strength save against the summoner’s CE save DC. On a failure, it is knocked prone, grappled, and restrained until the end of Zagroth’s next turn. On a success, it is pushed 10 feet and is not grappled.

Chimera Shadow: The Last Standing Guardian of the Forest — Hageshi

Base: Max Elephant

Additions: Round Deer + Nue + Rabbit Escape

Type: Advanced freeform Chimerafication preset

Defeat: If Hageshi reaches 0 HP, all four component shadows die permanently and are eligible for the Totality ritual.

Theme

Hageshi is the summoner’s “wall” chimera.

It is designed to:

  • absorb frontal pressure,
  • remain stable under impossible force,
  • self-correct and self-heal,
  • hold space for the summoner and their allies,
  • and punish anything that tries to break through it head-on.

It is not the technique’s best offensive chimera.

It is one of its strongest defensive answers.

Lore

Hageshi resembles a walking forest boundary: elephantine legs rooted like ancient trunks, antlers branching through a canopy of storm feathers, and pale rabbits continually scattering from and reforming its hide. Its arrival marks the point where the technique stops asking how to win and asks only what must still be standing afterward.

1. Guardian Frame

When summoned, Hageshi gains 25 temporary HP.

It has advantage on checks and saves made to resist being moved or knocked prone, and it counts as one size larger when determining whether it can be grappled, shoved, or physically overrun.

2. Round Deer Core

At the start of each of Hageshi’s turns, it regains HP equal to twice the summoner’s proficiency bonus.

Once per round, when an ally starts its turn within 10 feet of Hageshi, that ally regains HP equal to the summoner’s proficiency bonus. This feature cannot restore a creature above half its maximum HP.

3. Rabbit Redistribution

Once per round, when Hageshi is hit, it can use its reaction before damage is rolled to partially disperse its structure and choose one benefit:

  • gain +4 AC against the triggering attack, potentially causing it to miss, or
  • gain resistance to the triggering attack’s damage.

After the attack resolves, Hageshi may move up to 10 feet without provoking opportunity attacks. The resistance does not stack with resistance from another source.

4. Elephant Bulwark

As a bonus action, Hageshi roots itself and chooses a facing until the start of its next turn.

While rooted:

  • its speed is 0, and it cannot be forcibly moved or knocked prone,
  • it has resistance to damage from attacks and effects originating in its frontal half,
  • allies directly behind it have three-quarters cover,
  • and when a creature moves at least 20 feet toward Hageshi and enters a space within 5 feet of it, that creature must make a Strength save against the summoner’s CE save DC. On a failure, it takes 4d6 bludgeoning damage and its speed becomes 0 for the turn; on a success, it takes half damage and can continue moving. A creature can trigger this save only once per turn.

5. Storm-Tusk Intercept

When a hostile creature Hageshi can see within 30 feet moves toward the summoner or one of the summoner’s allies, Hageshi can use its reaction to move up to half its speed without provoking opportunity attacks and make one Gore attack against that creature.

On a hit, the attack deals an additional 2d6 lightning damage, and the target must succeed on a Strength save against the summoner’s CE save DC or be pushed 10 feet and have its speed reduced to 0 for the rest of the turn.

Storm-Tusk Intercept and Rabbit Redistribution compete for the same reaction.

6. Last Standing Wall

Once per summon, as an action, Hageshi establishes a 20-foot-wide guard line across spaces it occupies or can reach and roots itself until the start of its next turn.

Until then:

  • the line counts as blocked by Hageshi’s pressure,
  • allies behind the line have three-quarters cover,
  • a hostile creature attempting to cross the line must make a Strength save against the summoner’s CE save DC,
  • on a failure, its speed becomes 0 and it takes 4d6 bludgeoning or piercing damage,
  • and on a success, it takes no damage but treats the line as difficult terrain.

A creature makes this save only once during this use, even if it attempts to cross the line more than once.
