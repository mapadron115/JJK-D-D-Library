Core law

Completed rules

Adopted inversion profile:

the source’s prose and example contradict each other on the below-threshold formula; the campaign adopts the worked-example profile. After ordinary immunity and saving-throw adjustments, let D be the nonimmune damage event before flat reduction. If D = 0, take 0 and do not manufacture damage. If 0 < D < T, replace with L − D. If D > T, replace with D − T. If D = T, leave D unchanged. A negative proposed result leaves D unchanged. L is a low-hit inversion parameter, not a separately enforced maximum incoming-damage cap. Determine high/low classification once from original D; never reclassify after replacement. For mixed types preserve their proportions using remainder allocation. Then apply the Extreme Inverse level adjustment if learned, ordinary flat reduction, resistance/vulnerability, temporary HP and HP.

Extreme Inverse:

when you take damage, if the damage exceeds your threshold, you reduce it by an amount equal to your level. However, if the damage is below your threshold, you increase the damage taken by an amount equal to your level. Applied as one flat adjustment after the inversion replacement and before normal resistance/vulnerability. Clamp reduction to zero. Equal T receives no adjustment.

Inverted Field:

as a bonus action for 6 cursed energy, you cause all objects and creatures within 30 ft of you to be affected by your Inverse technique for 1 minute. A creature or object is no longer affected by Inverse if they leave the range of the field. You can deactivate this as a bonus action. The field uses the owner’s frozen L/T and grants only base Inversion, not every feat, extra reaction, Domain or independent CE ability of the owner. Owner’s own active Inverse and Field do not apply twice to the owner. All occupants including allies and objects are affected while in range; reentry reapplies until the field expires. The field follows the caster. It does not require a willing target or invent a save. Damage still respects real immunity before determining D.

Maximum: Antithesis:

define within threshold as D ≤ T and below as D < T using the pre-inversion D. Choose one Antithesis option per damage event. When you take damage within your threshold, you can spend 4 CE as a reaction to reflect half of that damage back to the attacker—retaliation equal to floor(final HP-or-tempHP damage actually suffered/2), not prevention; damage becomes force or necrotic, your choice; attacker within 30 ft. When you take damage below your threshold, you can instead spend 1 CE as a free action to transfer the pain: choose a creature within 30 ft to take force or necrotic damage (your choice) equal to twice your CE modifier, once per triggering event. Neither retaliation triggers further reflection/transfer effects, preventing loops.

Synesthesia initiative 20:

choose eligible victims, roll d4 per victim (1: taste, 2: smell, 3: touch, 4: sight); lost senses accumulate only if different senses are rolled. End-turn Cursed Energy ability check versus the caster’s CE DC restores one chosen lost sense; all return when the Domain ends. Sight loss uses blinded; smell/taste loss prevents checks/actions explicitly requiring that sense; touch loss is not automatic paralysis—prohibit touch-dependent sensory discrimination, but retain movement and attacks. Unlimited free activation inside Synesthesia grants activation, not unlimited free deactivation or repeated refunds; Quick Inversion still governs deactivation timing. Infinite activation is idempotent while already active; it does not grant infinite durations after leaving the Domain. Anti-domain protection prevents new sensory application.

Full progression

Feature map

1Inversion.

As a bonus action, spend 2 cursed energy to activate Inverse for 1 minute; spend 1 cursed energy to extend it by another minute. While active, damage you take is inverted around a damage limit (L = your proficiency bonus × your Cursed Energy score) and a threshold (T = your proficiency bonus × your Cursed Energy modifier), per the adopted profile: damage above the threshold inverts down, damage below inverts up, and a negative result leaves the damage unchanged. For example, with limit 100 and threshold 20: 90 damage becomes 70 (90 − 20), and 10 damage becomes 90 (100 − 10). Voluntary deactivation is a bonus action. Before level 11, the bonus-action activation cannot interrupt incoming damage.

3Extreme Opposites.

If you take damage below your threshold, you are considered vulnerable to that damage. If the damage exceeds your threshold, you gain resistance to it, even if that damage would normally bypass resistances or immunities. For example, with threshold 15: 10 damage is treated as vulnerability, but 18 damage is halved by resistance even if that damage type would normally ignore resistances. At PB 5, score 20, modifier 5 (L 100, T 25): D 10 → 90, then vulnerability 180; D 90 → 65, then resistance 32; D 25 remains 25.

6Break the Strong.

When you are forced to make a saving throw against an effect that deals damage: if the damage is above your threshold, you have advantage on the saving throw, regardless of damage type; if it is below your threshold, you have disadvantage, as weaker attacks become more dangerous to you. Roll the damaging effect once before the saving throw, use its pre-save nonimmune damage to decide advantage or disadvantage, and retain that damage roll after the save; damage equal to the threshold gives neither. For Inversion’s eventual damage transformation, classify the actual post-save damage, which may differ. The explicit high-hit resistance-bypass exception is preserved. Damage-dealing effects with different success/failure damage need their failure packet rolled first.

11Quick Inversion.

You can now use your technique as a reaction to damage, and you can deactivate your technique as a reaction. A reaction to an incoming damage event may activate the technique for the normal 2 CE before damage mitigation, or deactivate it before mitigation. You also gain additional reactions equal to half your proficiency bonus (rounded up) that can be used only for the inverse technique; at 18th level this becomes a number of reactions equal to your proficiency bonus. Refresh these technique-only extra reactions at the start of your turn; one reaction per single trigger even if several remain.

18Synesthesia.

Domain Expansion. Activate Inverse inside as a free action any number of times for no cost. At initiative 20, chosen creatures roll a d4 and lose a sense (taste, smell, touch, sight) until they regain it with an end-of-turn CE check or the Domain ends.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

Maximum — Antithesis: while your Inverse is active: when you take damage within your threshold (D ≤ T), you can spend 4 CE as a reaction to reflect half of that damage back to the attacker (damage becomes force or necrotic, your choice) if they are within 30 ft. When you take damage below your threshold (D < T), you can instead spend 1 CE as a free action to transfer the pain: choose a creature within 30 ft to take force or necrotic damage (your choice) equal to twice your CE modifier. Choose one option per damage event; neither retaliation triggers further reflection or transfer effects.

Domain Expansion — Synesthesia: 18 — within your domain, you can activate Inverse as a free action an infinite number of times for no cost. At initiative 20, creatures of your choice have their senses inverted: each rolls a d4 (1: taste, 2: smell, 3: touch, 4: sight), losing that sense until regained. A creature can make a Cursed Energy check at the end of their turn (versus your CE DC) to regain one chosen lost sense; all return when the Domain ends.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier III: Inverse is the campaign’s purest tank engine—near-invulnerable to heavy blows while weak strikes become perilous—but it generates no offense of its own beyond Antithesis retaliation, keeping it at Apex.
Source contradiction adopted: the V6 below-threshold prose and its worked example disagree; the campaign follows the example-led profile (0 < D < T → L − D; D > T → D − T). These extreme discontinuities are intentional consequences of the example-led draft, not evidence the source is mathematically consistent.
No arbitrary extra actions: Quick Inversion’s restricted reactions never grant extra actions; one reaction per single trigger even if several remain.

Inversion
 Starting at 1st level, you gain the ability to invert the damage you
take. As a bonus action, you can spend 2 cursed energy to activate
Inverse for 1 minute. While Inverse is active, when you take damage,
it is modified based on a damage limit and threshold. The damage
limit equals your proficiency bonus times your Cursed Energy score,
and the threshold is equal to your proficiency bonus times your
Cursed Energy modifier.
 When you take damage that exceeds your threshold, you subtract the
threshold from the damage and take the remaining difference as
damage. However, when the damage is below your threshold, you
subtract the threshold from the damage and take the resulting number
as damage instead. For example, if your set limit is 100 and you take
90 damage, you would subtract the damage from your limit if it
exceeds the threshold (e.g., 90 - 20 = 70), and take the remaining 70
damage. Conversely, if the damage is below the threshold (e.g., 10
damage), you subtract that amount from your set limit (100 - 10 =
90), and take 90 damage instead. If the calculation results in a
negative number, the damage remains unchanged.
 You can extend the duration of Inverse by spending 1 cursed energy
to maintain the effect for an additional minute.

Extreme Opposites
 At 3rd level, you’ve learned to further manipulate the damage you
take based on its intensity. If you take damage that is below your
threshold, you are considered vulnerable to the damage dealt.
However, if the damage exceeds your threshold, you gain resistance to
the damage, even if it would normally bypass resistances or
immunities.
 For example, if your threshold is 15 and you take 10 damage, you
treat that as vulnerability. But if you take 18 damage, you instead
halve it due to your resistance, even if that type of damage would
normally ignore resistances.

Break the Strong
 At 6th level, you’ve become adept at manipulating damage based on
its intensity, even when making saving throws. When you are forced to
make a saving throw against an effect that deals damage, the
difficulty of the save depends on whether the damage is above or
below your threshold.
If the damage is above your threshold, you have advantage on the
saving throw, regardless of the damage type. If the damage is below
your threshold, you have disadvantage on the saving throw, as weaker
attacks become more dangerous to you.
For example, if your threshold is 15 and you would take 18 damage
from a fireball, you gain advantage on the saving throw. However, if
you take 10 damage from the same attack, you would roll with
disadvantage instead.

Quick Inversion
 At 11th level, staying with your technique active for long periods
of time can become a disadvantage at times, so you have trained to
have easier access to your technique. You can now use your technique
as a reaction to damage and can deactivate your technique as a
reaction.
In addition, you now have additional reactions equal to half of your
proficiency bonus(rounded up) that can be used only for the inverse
technique. At 18th level, this becomes a number of reactions equal to
your proficiency bonus.

Domain Expansion: Synesthesia
 At 18th level, you have mastered your technique allowing you to open

action an infinite number of times for no cost. At initiative 20,

rolls a d4 (1: taste, 2: smell, 3: touch, 4: sight) having a sense
inverted and unable to be used. A creature can make a Cursed Energy
check at the end of their turn to regain their lost sense.
