Nine Knots

Bind motion, distance, wounds, and relationships with cursed cord. The technique is simple to learn and frighteningly flexible once several knots overlap.

RoleController / Tactical Support
Power tierExceptional
Main resourceActive Knots

Field notes. Subject: the cord is the whole argument. Three truths govern her. First: one knot is a nuisance; many knots are a sentence, each Knot levies its small tax (slowed, tethered, wounds shared across a Stitch), but two different Knots on one body compound into extra damage and the Gordian Binding restrains outright. Second: the cord is laid cheaply and cut cheaply, one CE per Knot as a bonus action, capacity rising with mastery, yet ten points of cord-damage severs the whole affair. Third: the Knot is wherever she says it is, Hitch pins pairs together, Snare freezes the doubly-knotted, and by the Domain every hostile in the barrier wakes bound. Assess her correctly: she does not chase; she converts your position into your prison. Maxim: the rope that holds everything needs only one more loop.

Full progression

Level Progression

1Cursed Cord, Anchor Knot, Hitch Knot, Stitch Knot

Cursed Cord

The instrument is humble, thirty feet of cord that is nothing until she ties it, and everything after. You manifest cursed cord from your hands. It has 30-foot reach for technique purposes, cannot be used as mundane rope by another creature, and vanishes when detached from all your Knots.

Knot Die

Her measure of tightening: a die that grows with mastery, because a sailor's grip only strengthens with years. Your Knot Die is a d6.

Knot Capacity

The economy of the craft: capacity bounded by skill and resolve, each Knot a single coin of CE spent as a bonus action. You can maintain active Knots equal to your proficiency bonus + CE modifier. A creature may bear no more than 3 of your Knots before 11th level. Each Knot normally costs 1 CE to tie as a bonus action after you touch the target or hit it with a weapon/cursed attack this turn. Each Knot lasts 1 minute, ends if you dismiss it (no action), if the cord is cut (AC 12, 10 HP), or if the bearer leaves 120 feet of you (invented).

Anchor Knot

The mooring knot: the vessel may still drift, but it cannot flee, and against forced movement it holds fast. A creature’s speed is reduced by 10 feet. It adds your Knot Die to checks/saves made to resist forced movement, but it cannot willingly teleport more than 30 feet at once without first succeeding on a CE save.

Hitch Knot

Two vessels lashed together; neither may sail beyond the length of the rope. Tie two creatures/objects within 30 feet of one another. Until the knot ends, they cannot willingly move more than 30 feet apart. A creature attempting to exceed the tether makes a Strength save; on a failure its movement ends at the boundary.

Stitch Knot

The cruelest seam: when one of the pair would bleed, the cord pulls taut and the other bleeds instead. Tie two willing creatures. Reaction • 1 CE • once per round per Stitch Knot: when one creature of the pair would take damage, the cord pulls taut, the other creature takes the damage instead (same amount, same type; the original takes none). A single damage instance can travel a Stitch Knot only once (no loops).

3Snare & Slip —

With two knots on one body she holds the leash itself, Snare freezes the quarry; Slip repositions it like a chess piece. Snare: if a hostile creature bears 2 of your Knots, spend 2 CE as a bonus action; it makes a Strength save or its speed becomes 0 until the end of its next turn. Slip: reaction when a knotted creature moves; spend 1 CE to shift it 5 feet to an unoccupied space it could normally occupy (Dexterity save negates if hostile).

6Compound Knot —

Two different Knots on one body are not two taxes but one sentence, and now the cord may be tied in the rhythm of the attack. a creature can bear 4 Knots. When two different Knot types affect the same creature, your first damaging hit against it each turn deals one Knot Die extra force damage. You may also tie or remove one Knot as part of the Attack action once per turn.

Knot Die

Your Knot Die becomes a d8.

11Gordian Binding —

The fabled solution, inverted: she does not cut the knot; she completes it, and the bound one is restrained. a creature may bear 5 Knots. As an action, spend 4 CE and consume 3 Knots on one creature. It makes a Strength save; failure restrains it for 1 minute. It repeats the save at the end of each turn, ending the effect on success. On a success against the initial save, its speed is merely halved until the end of its next turn.

Knot Die

Your Knot Die becomes a d10.

18Domain Expansion: Temple of the Nine Ties —

A shrine courtyard at dawn, ropes coiled on every post and rail like sleeping snakes. Paper talismans flutter from lines strung overhead, and the wooden floor is worn smooth where generations of feet learned the knots. The air is still, the way air goes still before a knot is pulled tight.

The shrine of the craft: at initiative count twenty, every hostile in the barrier wakes already bound, the sure-hit is the morning the ropes were laid. 10 CE. At initiative count 20, choose one ephemeral Knot effect for every hostile creature in the Domain: Anchor, Hitch (to a fixed point), or Snare. This is the Domain’s sure-hit and lasts until the next initiative count 20. Your ordinary cord, attacks, and Gordian Binding still resolve normally.

Knot Die

Your Knot Die becomes a d12.

Feats

Technique Feats

Sailor’s Hands

The veteran's trick: splice through an old Knot to lay the new one farther than the arm can reach, around corners the eye cannot see. You can splice your cords through existing Knots: when you tie a Knot, you may route it through another creature or object already bearing one of your Knots, extending your tying range to 60 feet for that Knot and ignoring cover between the relay and the target.

Quick Release

She never holds a knot she cannot let go of, and the letting-go carries her five feet toward the next opportunity. When one of your Knots ends, move up to 5 feet without provoking opportunity attacks. Once per turn.

Executioner’s Knot

The noose is the cord's oldest argument. Three Knots deep, the quarry learns what the hangman always knew. As an action for 3 CE, you can tie a hangman's noose around a creature bearing 3+ of your Knots within 30 feet: it makes a Constitution save or is restrained until the end of your next turn, unable to speak or use verbal components while the noose holds.

Rules integration

Table Rulings

Knots are technique effects; cutting visible cord does not automatically end them unless an ability specifically suppresses cursed techniques.
A creature can be affected by only one instance of each named Knot from you at a time.
Forced movement created by Slip cannot place a creature inside solid matter or into a space it cannot physically occupy.
