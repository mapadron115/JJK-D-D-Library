Core law

Completed rules

Miracle triggers:

“rolls the maximum amount on a roll” includes damage rolls and saves, not just d20s, per the plain reading. “Two equal numbers one after the other” means two consecutive rolls by the same roller showing the same face. The $1,000 Treasure! threshold is kept as printed; if the campaign’s Wealth system renames currency, the threshold converts 1:1 to the equivalent value—no mechanical change.

Blind Luck:

the once-per-round limit applies to the free (non-reaction) use; the reaction use costs the reaction normally.

Fated for Fortune:

the exclusion (no Black Flash, no Miracle generation) is kept exactly—it protects the campaign’s Black Flash economy.

Luck of the Draw conversion:

initiative-20 timing matches the modern system; clashes use the deterministic point system (the reaction-expansion option is kept as a legal reaction trigger under the clash rules); barrier per D007; burnout per D010. The 2d20 variable expansion cost and 1d4-minute duration are kept as the technique’s printed specific rules (V.6: “All domains cost 20 Cursed Energy unless specified”—this one specifies).

Blessing of Fortune:

when you or a creature you can see fails a death saving throw, you can spend 1 Miracle and 1 CE as a reaction. The roll is now considered a natural 20. You cannot experience a breakthrough or gain a Miracle in this way. The no-breakthrough/no-Miracle clause is kept to protect the Miracle economy.

Innate Luck:

you roll all dice generated by your cursed technique twice, picking the result of the higher one. Additionally, you can make a miracle die roll its maximum amount once per long rest. The twice-rolled technique dice half is playable now; the maximum-die half is on HOLD—the source never defines which dice are “miracle dice” (Blind Luck’s Xd4/d6? Miraculous Outcome’s d6/d8/d10?). The die must be defined before play; do not invent it.

The fiction. The prevailing theory holds that Miracle is a gambler's gratitude made manifest: the user's cursed energy learned to hoard luck the way a miser hoards coin, taxing the good fortune it witnesses and spending the store only on the moments that matter. It does not create luck — it *collects* it, which is why the Domain mints and the Miracles ward, flip, and cheat death rather than striking. This is energy that watched fortune save everyone but itself, and decided to keep a ledger.

Full progression

Feature map

1Little Miracles & Blind Luck.

Little Miracles: when you witness good fortune, you gain a Miracle, stored as marks somewhere on your body; you can hold up to 6 Miracles at once (2 Miracles per witness at 11th level, 3 at 18th). The four fortunes are: Lucky! — you or an ally you can see rolls the maximum amount on a roll; Equal Numbers! — you or an ally you can see rolls two equal numbers one after the other; Treasure! — you or an ally you can see finds an item worth $1,000 or more; Overcoming Hardship — you or a creature you can see succeeds on a roll they had disadvantage on. Blind Luck: once per round when you take damage, spend a Miracle to reduce the incoming damage by Xd4 (X = your CE modifier) + your proficiency bonus; or as a reaction for a Miracle + 1 CE, roll d6s instead and add your proficiency bonus twice.

3Miraculous Outcome.

reaction to failing a roll: spend 1 Miracle + 1 CE to add a d6 (d8 at 6th, d10 at 11th), potentially turning failure into success.

6Deadly Miracle.

when hit or failing a save that would drop you to 0 HP or kill you outright: spend 2 Miracles + 2 CE to instead succeed the save or survive with no damage/effects. Death saves at advantage while holding at least 1 Miracle. At 11th: usable against effects that would drop you to half HP or below.

11Fated for Fortune.

rolling 20+ on a Miraculous Outcome die counts as a critical success as if a natural 20, but cannot trigger Black Flash rolls or gain a Miracle.

18Luck of the Draw.

Domain Expansion. Costs 2d20 CE to expand, lasts 1d4 minutes; reaction-expansion costs 20 CE or a 2d20 roll. At initiative 20, gain 3 Miracles with no maximum. Inside the Domain, spend a Miracle on self or another creature making an attack or using a technique to grant it the guaranteed hit.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

Domain Expansion — Luck of the Draw: 18 — your domain costs 2d20 CE to expand and lasts 1d4 minutes. If you lack the Cursed Energy, you must reattempt on your next turn and reroll the 2d20. If you expand your domain as a reaction to another Domain Expansion, you can choose to spend 20 CE or to roll 2d20. At initiative 20, you gain 3 Miracles and have no limit on your max amount of Miracles. Within your domain, you can spend a Miracle on yourself or another creature when they make an attack or use a technique to imbue it with your guaranteed hit.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it.

Prerequisite: Must have Little Miracles

Beginner’s Luck

Your luck is generous to those you favor. As a bonus action for 1 CE, you can press one of your stored Miracles into the hand of a willing creature you touch — they hold it as one of their own and can spend it using your CE modifier where the technique calls for one. You cannot exceed your normal maximum Miracles this way.

Prerequisite: Must have Miraculous Outcome

Blessing of Fortune

When you or a creature you can see fails a death saving throw, you can spend 1 Miracle and 1 CE as a reaction. The roll is now considered a natural 20. You cannot experience a breakthrough or gain a Miracle in this way.

Prerequisite: Must have Fated for Fortune

Innate Luck

Your fortune casts a shadow. When you score a critical success through Fated for Fortune, you can spend 1 CE to unbalance someone else's luck — one enemy you can see within 30 ft has disadvantage on its next d20 roll made within 1 minute, as your stolen fortune tips the scales against them.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier III: Miracle is a support engine of stored luck—damage wards, flipped failures, death cheats, and a luck-minting Domain—without direct offense or battlefield control; hard to kill, but Apex, not calamity.
Miracle economy: Fated for Fortune and Blessing of Fortune both refuse to generate Miracles or trigger breakthroughs; those exclusions are load-bearing and kept exactly.
Appendix

Additional material.

Cursed Energy
 Starting at 1st level, you can harness your negative feelings into a
usable source of energy, called cursed energy. These feelings can be
harnessed into attacks or abilities. The number of cursed energy you
possess is based on your class, some classes use a modifier that
being charisma or wisdom (Player’s choice)
Your cursed energy DC is 8 + user cursed energy ability modifier +
proficiency bonus.

Little Miracles
 Starting at 1st level, your technique brings you strength when you
witness good fortune. When you witness this, you gain a Miracle. You
can have up to six Miracles at once, which are stored as marks
somewhere on your body. You gain 2 Miracles for every miracle you
witness at 11th level and 3 Miracles at 18th level.

Lucky! — Everyone loves a Jackpot! When you or an ally you can see rolls the maximum amount on a roll, you gain a Miracle.
Equal Numbers! — Equal Numbers are supposed to be lucky, right? When you or an ally you can see rolls two equal numbers one after the other, you gain a Miracle.
Treasure! — Finding free stuff is always cool! When you or an ally you can see find an item worth $1,000 or more, you gain a Miracle.
Overcoming Hardship — Overcoming hardship is something rare in this harsh world, so every chance we get is a miracle. When you or a creature you can see succeeds on a roll they had disadvantage on, you gain a Miracle.

Blind Luck
 Once per round when you take damage, you can spend a Miracle to
reduce the incoming damage by Xd4 equal to your CE modifier. You add
your proficiency bonus to the rolled amount and reduce the damage by
that much. You can also do this as a reaction for a Miracle and 1 CE.
 When used this way, the d4 increases to a d6 and you add your
proficiency bonus twice.

Miraculous Outcome
 At 3rd level, you can self-consciously alter fate. As a reaction to
failing a roll you can spend 1 Miracle and 1 CE to add a d6 to the
roll, potentially turning it into a success. This increases to a d8
at 6th level and a d10 at 11th level.

Deadly Miracle
 At 6th level, you have learned that out of difficulties, miracles
grow. Your technique lets you fend off death by some miracle. When
you are hit by an attack or fail a saving throw that would reduce you
to 0 hit points or kill you outright, you can spend 2 Miracles and 2
CE to instead succeed the save or survive the attack and take no
damage or additional effects. Completely escaping harm by seemingly
dumb luck.
While you have at least 1 Miracle, you make death saving throws at
advantage.
 At 11th level, you can use this feature against an attack or effect
that would reduce you to 1/2 or below your hit point maximum.

Fated for Fortune
 At 11th level you realize there is no bad future for you, only a
path to fortune paved with miracles! If you roll 20 or higher using
Miraculous Outcome, it is considered a critical success as if you
had rolled a natural 20 (cannot roll for Black Flash or gain Miracle).

Domain Expansion: Luck of the Draw
 Finally at 18th level you have mastered your technique allowing you
to expand your domain. Your domain costs 2d20 CE to expand and lasts
1d4 minutes. If you lack the Cursed Energy, you must reattempt on
your next turn and reroll the 2d20. If you expand your domain as a
reaction to another Domain Expansion, you can choose to spend 20 CE
or to roll 2d20.
 At initiative 20, you gain 3 Miracles and have no limit on your max
amount of Miracles. Within your domain, you can spend a Miracle on
yourself or another creature when they make an attack or use a
technique to imbue it with your guaranteed hit.

Beginner’s Luck
Prerequisite: Must have Little Miracles
You have learned to more efficiently store up luck. You can now store
an additional number of Miracles equal to your CE modifier.

Blessing of Fortune
Prerequisite: Must have Miraculous Outcome
 When you or a creature you can see fails a death saving throw, you
can spend 1 Miracle and 1 CE as a reaction. The roll is now
considered a natural 20. You cannot experience a breakthrough or gain
a Miracle in this way.

Innate Luck
Prerequisite: Must have Fated for Fortune
 Some have asked you: "Are you lucky because you have miracles by
your side, or do you have miracles by your side because you're
lucky?" The answer was obvious, you are naturally lucky and your
technique merely reflects that. You roll all dice generated by your
cursed technique twice, picking the result of the higher one.
Additionally, you can make a miracle die roll its maximum amount once
per long rest.
