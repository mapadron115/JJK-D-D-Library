Synovia Manipulation

The technique curdles the oil in the hinge and melts the hinge itself.

Power tierTier II · Controller
Combat roleController
Core engineBone liquefaction and marrow mites
Domain—

Field notes. Subject: the architecture of the body, betrayed from within. Three truths govern Synovia Manipulation. First: every fighter is a building, and every building has joints. Synovial fluid is the oil in the hinge; this technique curdles the oil and softens the hinge, and a softened frame cannot brace against anything. Second: the damage is honest — 4d12 necrotic where the bone gives way — but the weakness is the weapon. A liquefied body takes five more damage from every source, because structure is what turns a blow aside, and structure is what the technique removes. Third: the mites are the long game. They do not kill the limb. They eat what makes it a limb, and a limb without marrow is a limb in name only. Consider: armor is a confession that the body was already a siege target. The technique simply accepts the confession.

Full progression

Feature map

1Synovia Manipulation.

Synovia Manipulation

The founding liquefaction. For four CE, a skeleton softens, and every blow afterward lands deeper. As an action for 4 CE, force a creature within 30 feet that has bones to make a Constitution saving throw against your CE save DC. On a failure, the target's bones begin to liquefy: it takes 4d12 necrotic damage and is weakened — it takes an additional 5 damage from any source until the end of your next turn, as its body cannot brace. On a success, it takes half damage and is not weakened. This technique has no effect on creatures without bones, such as constructs, oozes, or cursed corpses.

3Devour the Marrow.

Devour the Marrow

The long game, planted with a touch. Cursed mites ride your strike into a chosen limb and wait for the bone to break. When you hit a creature within 5 feet with an unarmed strike or melee weapon attack, you may spend 3 CE to force the target to make a Constitution saving throw against your CE save DC. On a failed save, your cursed mites burrow into one of the target's limbs of your choice. If you then deal the damage required on the Limb Damage Table to that limb, the mites devour the limb's bone marrow and the limb gains the effects of a Broken Limb until the target finishes a long rest. The limb choice is made when the mites burrow, before the save is resolved.

6Bone Broth.

Bone Broth

The broth is served to the whole table. One action, and every skeleton in thirty feet softens at once. As an action for 10 CE, force every creature with bones within 30 feet of you to make a Constitution saving throw against your CE save DC. Each creature that fails suffers the base effects of Synovia Manipulation: 4d12 necrotic damage and weakened until the end of your next turn. Creatures that succeed take half damage and are not weakened.

11Softened Frame.

Softened Frame

The weakness deepens past the skin. A softened body cannot brace, cannot dodge, cannot hold its stance. While a creature is weakened by your technique, it has disadvantage on Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and the additional damage it takes from any source becomes twice your proficiency bonus instead of 5.

18Marrow Brood.

Marrow Brood

The mites join the liquefaction; the technique learns to strike twice at once. A creature that fails its saving throw against Devour the Marrow is also weakened as if by Synovia Manipulation, with no additional action required. Additionally, when you use Synovia Manipulation, you may target one additional creature within range for 2 CE per extra target; each target makes its own saving throw.

20Total Liquefaction.

Total Liquefaction

Once per long rest, the architecture fails completely. When a creature fails its saving throw against your Synovia Manipulation or Bone Broth, you may collapse its skeleton entirely: it falls prone and is restrained as its bones turn to slurry. At the end of each of its turns it may repeat the Constitution saving throw, ending the effect on a success as its bones re-harden.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it.

Prerequisite: Must have Synovia Manipulation

Ossuary Sense

The skeleton, felt through stone. You can feel for the architecture of bodies the way others listen for footsteps. As an action, you detect every creature with bones within 60 feet of you, even through walls and total cover, and learn roughly how many there are. You also instantly know whether any creature you can see has bones — no more wasted liquefactions on the boneless.

Prerequisite: Must have Synovia Manipulation

Joint Lock

Seize the softened hinge mid-stride. When a creature weakened by your technique moves, you may use your reaction and spend 2 CE to seize its liquefied joints: its speed becomes 0 until the end of its current turn.

Prerequisite: Must have Bone Broth

Broth of the Fallen

Drink what the technique made. When you reduce a creature weakened by your technique to 0 hit points, you may use a bonus action to draw its liquefied marrow into yourself, gaining 2d6 + your CE modifier temporary hit points.

Prerequisite: Must have Devour the Marrow

Marrow Hound

The mites can be taught a gentler hunger. Over 1 minute of contact with a corpse or an unattended skeleton, you may seed it with your cursed mites; for 1 hour the mites puppet it as a crude servant. You may use your action to command it to move, carry, hold, or stay — simple commands only. It cannot attack. The GM determines its statistics (a skeleton or zombie stat block is appropriate).

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier II: the liquefaction is honest damage with a party-wide damage amplifier stapled to it, the AoE arrives early, and the mites threaten permanent-until-rest limb loss — but every effect is Constitution-gated, bone-gated, and the technique does nothing at all to the boneless (clarified).
Boneless creatures: Synovia Manipulation and Bone Broth have no effect on creatures without bones — constructs, oozes, cursed corpses, and similar. Devour the Marrow's mites likewise need a limbed, boned target. The GM decides edge cases (skeletal undead count as boned).
Limb Damage Table: Devour the Marrow's marrow-devour trigger uses the Limb Damage Table (V6 §7.4), adopted in the rules injury section. The limb is chosen when the mites burrow, before the save is resolved; the Broken Limb condition lasts until the target finishes a long rest, per the source.
CE save: V6's 'Cursed Energy saving throw' is a saving throw against your CE save DC (clarified).

On a failure, the target's bones begin to liquefy and they take 4d12 necrotic damage and they take an additional 5 damage from any source as their body weakens. When you make an unarmed strike or melee weapon attack against a creature within 5 ft, you can spend 3 cursed energy to force the target to make a Constitution saving throw on a hit. On a failed save, you force cursed mites to burrow into one of their limbs of your choice.
