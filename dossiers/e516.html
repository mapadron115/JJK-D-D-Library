Synovia Manipulation

The technique curdles the oil in the hinge and melts the hinge itself.

Power tierTier II · Controller
Combat roleController
Core engineBone liquefaction and marrow mites
Domain—

Field notes. Subject: the architecture of the body, betrayed from within. Three truths govern Synovia Manipulation. First: every fighter is a building, and every building has joints. Synovial fluid is the oil in the hinge; this technique curdles the oil and softens the hinge, and a softened frame cannot brace against anything. Second: the damage is honest — where the bone gives way, the flesh follows — but the weakness is the weapon. A liquefied body takes every blow deeper, because structure is what turns a blow aside, and structure is what the technique removes. Third: the mites are the long game. They do not kill the limb. They eat what makes it a limb, and a limb without marrow is a limb in name only. Consider: armor is a confession that the body was already a siege target. The technique simply accepts the confession.

1Synovia Manipulation.

Synovia Manipulation

Action · 4 CE · One boned creature within 30 ft: Con save vs. 4d12 necrotic and weakened (+5 damage from any source until the end of your next turn); half damage and no weakened on success · No effect on the boneless.

The founding liquefaction. A skeleton softens, and every blow afterward lands deeper.

3Devour the Marrow.

Devour the Marrow

On a hit with an unarmed strike or melee weapon attack (within 5 ft) · 3 CE · Target makes a Con save; on a failure, cursed mites burrow into a limb of your choice — deal the Limb Damage Table threshold to that limb and the mites devour its marrow: Broken Limb until the target finishes a long rest · Limb chosen before the save.

The long game, planted with a touch. Cursed mites ride your strike into a chosen limb and wait for the bone to break.

6Bone Broth.

Bone Broth

Action · 10 CE · Every boned creature within 30 ft: Con save vs. the base effects of Synovia Manipulation (4d12 necrotic + weakened); half damage and no weakened on success.

The broth is served to the whole table. One action, and every skeleton in thirty feet softens at once.

11Softened Frame.

Softened Frame

Passive · While a creature is weakened by your technique, it has disadvantage on Strength and Dexterity saves, and the bonus damage it takes from any source becomes twice your PB instead of 5.

The weakness deepens past the skin. A softened body cannot brace, cannot dodge, cannot hold its stance.

18Marrow Brood.

Marrow Brood

Passive · A creature that fails its save vs. Devour the Marrow is also weakened as if by Synovia Manipulation · When you use Synovia Manipulation, target one additional creature in range for 2 CE per extra target (each saves separately).

The mites join the liquefaction; the technique learns to strike twice at once.

20Total Liquefaction.

Total Liquefaction

Once per long rest · When a creature fails its save vs. your Synovia Manipulation or Bone Broth, collapse its skeleton: it falls prone and is restrained · It repeats the Con save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success.

Once per long rest, the architecture fails completely.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier II: the liquefaction is honest damage with a party-wide damage amplifier stapled to it, the AoE arrives early, and the mites threaten permanent-until-rest limb loss — but every effect is Constitution-gated, bone-gated, and the technique does nothing at all to the boneless (clarified).
Boneless creatures: Synovia Manipulation and Bone Broth have no effect on creatures without bones — constructs, oozes, cursed corpses, and similar. Devour the Marrow's mites likewise need a limbed, boned target. The GM decides edge cases (skeletal undead count as boned).
Limb Damage Table: Devour the Marrow's marrow-devour trigger uses the Limb Damage Table (V6 §7.4), adopted in the rules injury section. The limb is chosen when the mites burrow, before the save is resolved; the Broken Limb condition lasts until the target finishes a long rest, per the source.
CE save: V6's 'Cursed Energy saving throw' is a saving throw against your CE save DC (clarified).
Appendix

Additional material.

Synovia Manipulation
Major Innate Technique
As an action for 4 cursed energy, you can force a creature within 30 ft to make a Constitution saving throw. On a failure, the target's bones begin to liquefy and they take 4d12 necrotic damage and they take an additional 5 damage from any source as their body weakens. This technique doesn't work on creatures without bones such as a Cursed Corpse.

Bone Broth
As an action for 10 cursed energy, you can force all creatures within 30 ft to make a Constitution saving throw or suffer the base effects of Synovia Manipulation.

Devour the Marrow
When you make an unarmed strike or melee weapon attack against a creature within 5 ft, you can spend 3 cursed energy to force the target to make a Constitution saving throw on a hit. On a failed save, you force cursed mites to burrow into one of their limbs of your choice. If you deal the damage required on the Limb Damage Table, your cursed mites devour the limb's bone marrow and they gain the effects of the Broken Limb until they finish a long rest.
Source: Creeps and Curses v3.1 text extraction, Ch.2 §2.2, 2026-10-02.
