Keening Before Death

Core law

Character engine

When an ally falls to 0 HP, the warning wail fires on its own — no storage, no choice to bank. The keen arrives first, and it always costs the banshee. The banshee does not decide to keen; the keen decides.

Technique save DC: 8 + PB + chosen CE ability modifier. Technique attack: PB + chosen CE ability modifier.

Field notes. Subject: grief that fires before thought. Three truths govern the Keening. First: the keen decides. When an ally within sight drops to 0 HP, the wail fires immediately — no action, no reaction, no CE, no choice, no banking for later; it cannot be held back because it was never the sorcerer's to hold. Second: the cost is the proof. That it costs the bearer every time is the registry's evidence that this is not a weapon but an apology — the CE making amends on behalf of the one who failed to be there. Third: the keen arrives first. At mastery it cannot be surprised by the dying, it grants 1 HP instead of merely a saved death throw, and the Domain never lets it wait — the witness range becomes the whole barrier. I have watched the wail steady a falling ally and unnerve every hostile in thirty feet, and understood that the technique's true target was never the enemy. Mark this: the banshee is not a mourner. She is a sentry who arrived one moment too late, once, and refuses to be late again.

Full progression

Level progression

1Warning Keen

The technique's whole confession in one feature. Ally at 0 HP within sixty feet: the keen fires — no action, no reaction, no CE, no choice — the falling ally's first death save succeeds, and hostiles within thirty feet must hold against fear. Once per short or long rest. It cannot be saved for later. Grief, here, is not a resource. It is a reflex. The Keen (automatic): whenever an ally you can see within 60 feet drops to 0 HP, the keen fires immediately — no action, no reaction, no CE, and no choice. You cannot hold it back, and it cannot be saved for later. The falling ally automatically succeeds on its first death saving throw (death heard it coming). Each hostile creature within 30 feet of you that can hear must succeed on a Wisdom save against your technique DC or be frightened of you until the end of your next turn. Once the keen fires, it cannot fire again until you finish a short or long rest.

3Piercing Wail

The wail, aimed. Thirty-foot cone, 2d8 thunder, deafened and disadvantaged on failure — and the dying roll their save at disadvantage, because the technique is crueler around the dying. This is the answer to a quiet battlefield: the keen is a warning, but the wail is a weapon, and the bearer carries both. Action, 2 CE: each creature in a 30-foot cone makes a Constitution save — 2d8 thunder damage (invented: the die is a d8) on failure, half on success; a creature that fails is deafened and has disadvantage on its next attack. A creature currently at 0 HP has disadvantage on this save — the wail is crueler around the dying. This is the technique’s answer to a quiet battlefield: the wail is a weapon even when no one is falling.

6Mourning Veil

The vigil, made into weather. Twenty-foot aura for a minute: allies inside hold advantage on death saves, and each successful save lets the bearer wail back at one hostile — thunder and deafness on a failed Wisdom. The veil does not prevent the fall. It makes the fall expensive to witness. Action, 2 CE, Focus: a 20-foot aura for 1 minute. Allies inside have advantage on death saving throws. When an ally inside succeeds on a death save, you may wail as a reaction: one hostile creature within 30 feet of you that can hear must succeed on a Wisdom save against your technique DC or take 1 die thunder damage and be deafened until the end of its next turn.

11The Cry Arrives First

The sentry's promotion. Initiative is rolled and the bearer simply knows — no surprise from any visible creature below half HP — and the keen now grants 1 HP instead of a mere saved throw. The technique's apology is getting better at arriving in time. One HP is not much. It is everything. When initiative is rolled, you hear every coming death: you cannot be surprised by any creature you can see that is below half HP. The keen sharpens — when it fires, the falling ally regains 1 HP instead of merely succeeding on a death save.

18Domain Expansion: House That Heard the Cry

The house where no fall goes unannounced. The keen never waits inside — it fires whenever any ally drops, even unrecharged, across the whole Domain — and one chosen creature receives Forewarning: +2 AC, +2 to saves. The Domain is the technique's final form: a room where death is always heard coming, and always answered. Full-turn action, 20 CE, enclosed 60-foot radius centered on you, up to 1 minute, Technique Focus. This is an omen-support Domain. While it stands, the keen never waits: whenever any ally inside drops to 0 HP, the keen fires immediately even if it has not recharged, and its witness range extends across the whole Domain. When the Domain forms, choose one creature inside — it receives Forewarning: +2 AC and +2 to saving throws, but it has disadvantage on attacks against you. This is not mind control and causes no automatic damage. If the chosen creature is unwilling and protected by an anti-domain defense, it may make a Wisdom save to reject Forewarning entirely.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Family Wail

The watch extends to the chosen. PB allies named after each long rest: the keen's witness range reaches ninety feet for them. Mark this: the technique lets the bearer decide, once, who matters most. Everything after that is the keen's decision, not hers. Choose PB allies after each long rest; your keen’s witness range extends to 90 feet for them.

Glass-Shattering Keen

The wail's other talent: fragile things break. Double damage to glass, ceramics, thin wood — windows and pottery shattering on failed saves, carried or worn objects exemptable. The keen is not only grief; it is force, and force has opinions about windows. Your Piercing Wail deals double damage to fragile mundane objects — glass, ceramics, thin wood, and the like — shattering windows and pottery in its cone on a failed save regardless of their remaining hit points. You may exempt objects carried or worn by creatures from the cone's destructive resonance. As a finer application, you may spend 1 CE to narrow the wail to a 15-foot cone that affects only objects, keening the room's glass to shards while leaving every creature untouched.

Professional Mourner

The mourner's ledger. Spend a minute keening over an ally the keen could not save: learn the direction and distance of whatever dealt the killing blow, within a mile, and be unsurprisable by it for a day. The apology has a second half, and it is called reckoning. When an ally you keened for dies, you may spend 1 minute keening over the body: you learn the exact direction and distance of whatever dealt the killing blow (if within 1 mile), and you cannot be surprised by it for the next 24 hours.

Second Cry (11+)

The echo, shaped. When the keen fires, 2 CE turns its echo into a warning for one ally within sixty feet — +2 AC until the next turn, because they heard death coming. The first cry was for the fallen. The second is for the living, and it is the kinder of the two. When the keen fires, you may spend 2 CE to shape its echo into a warning for one ally within 60 feet: that ally gains +2 AC until the start of your next turn, having heard death coming.

Rules integration

Domain procedure

This entry uses the repaired campaign Domain rules. The level-18 feature above is complete: its printed action, CE cost, radius, duration, omen law, fallback protection, and counterplay replace any legacy blanket auto-hit wording.
Rules integration

Counterplay

Keep the banshee’s allies healthy — the keen only fires at 0 HP. Silence or deafen the banshee to suppress the frighten (the omen itself is supernatural and still steadies the fallen). Strike the banshee after the keen fires, while it recharges.
