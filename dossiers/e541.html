Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core Engine, the Deck. You manifest a deck of fifty-two cursed cards and play them as your technique. Your hand is five cards; the suit chooses the school — Spades for sharpening, Hearts for mending, Clubs for control, Diamonds for warding — and the number is the magnitude, always the number itself, never dice. At the start of each encounter you declare Stacked (you choose the cards, but numbers count as half, rounded down) or Shuffled (a blind draw, but numbers count as +2). The declaration applies to every draw in that encounter. At the end of your turn you discard down to two cards for free; holding more costs 1 CE per card. CE also buys a 2-CE extra draw and a 3-CE mulligan — discard the hand, draw five fresh, using the encounter's declaration.

Field notes. Subject: a player, not a gambler. Three truths govern the deck. First: the cards are honest — a seven does what a seven does, and no amount of wanting makes it a ten; the technique respects arithmetic the way other techniques respect physics. Second: the deck punishes control and rewards faith — stack it and it taxes you, trust it and it pays you, and every practitioner eventually learns the same lesson at the worst possible moment. Third: the hand is a conversation across turns — holding a king for the flush that never comes is the technique's oldest tragedy, and playing it early is its oldest wisdom. Mark the economy: cursed energy never changes what a card is, only how many chances you get to draw a better one.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1Opening Hand

Free · Manifest the deck. At the start of combat, declare Stacked or Shuffled, then draw five cards.

The deck arrives the way all cursed tools arrive: already in your hands, like it was always there.

Free · Draw step. At the start of each of your turns, discard any number of cards, then draw back up to five (using the encounter's declaration).

Every turn is a new deal. The deck does not remember your luck, which is either a mercy or an insult depending on the morning.

Action · Play a Spade: a creature within 60 feet gains a sharpened edge — the next time they deal damage with an attack or technique before the end of their next turn, they deal additional damage equal to the card's number. If they have multiple Spade bonuses, each applies to a separate damage roll.

The blade suit. A spade does not strike — it sharpens. It lends its number to someone else's blade, and the damage arrives with their name on it.

Action · Play a Heart: a creature within 30 feet regains HP equal to the card's number. If the number is 8 or higher, also end one condition on them.

The suit that puts things back together. Hearts do not hurry — an eight or better takes the time to pull the poison out too.

Action · Play a Club: a creature within 60 feet takes bludgeoning damage equal to the card's number and must make a Strength save or be restrained until the end of your next turn.

The suit that holds the board down. Clubs do not ask permission; they wrap around ankles and arguments with equal enthusiasm.

Bonus action · Play a Diamond: a creature within 30 feet gains temporary HP equal to the card's number.

The suit that holds the line. Diamonds are quick — warding is the one thing the deck lets you do while doing something else.

Passive · Face values are Jack 11, Queen 12, King 13, Ace 14 for magnitude. Their special riders unlock at L6.

The court outranks the numbers the way nobility outranks arithmetic: by simply being worth more.

2 CE · Draw one extra card beyond five. 3 CE · Mulligan: discard your hand, draw five fresh, using the encounter's declaration. 1 CE per card · Hold beyond the free two at end of turn.

Cursed energy cannot change a card. It can only buy more chances, better timing, and the right to keep what you've drawn.

3Card Sharp

Action · Play a poker combination as a single action: choose one of the played cards' suits and resolve its effect with a total equal to the sum of the numbers × the combination multiplier (round down). Pair ×1, two pair ×1.5, three of a kind ×1.5, straight ×1.5, flush ×2, full house ×2.5, four of a kind ×3. Face-card riders do not trigger inside combinations.

The deck's oldest lesson: one card is a statement, five cards in agreement are an argument. A pair is just efficiency — two cards, one action — but a flush is the deck leaning in and doubling whatever you said.

6Face Value

Passive · Face cards gain riders when played singly (not in combinations). Jack: teleport up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space you can see as part of the play. Queen: one creature within 60 feet makes a WIS save or obeys a one-word command until the end of your next turn. King: one creature within 60 feet makes a WIS save or is frightened of you for 1 minute (save ends). Ace: draw a replacement card immediately after playing it.

Jacks cheat position, Queens command, Kings dominate, and Aces — the deck's apex — reward themselves: play one and the deck deals you another, because apex plays deserve an audience.

11The Turn

Passive · Your draw step draws seven cards; choose five to keep and discard two.

Named for poker's fourth street: the moment the hand gets honest. Seven cards and the discipline to throw two away is where players become professionals.

18The Long Game

Passive · You hold your entire hand for free — no discard-down, no CE. Once per combat (bonus action) · Cold Deck: re-declare your Stacked/Shuffled choice for the rest of the encounter.

At the end, the deck stops taxing patience. And once per fight, the professional does what professionals do: changes the terms mid-hand, and dares the table to prove it.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

Pinnacle, Royal Flush (action, 10 CE): requires holding 10, Jack, Queen, King, and Ace of a single suit, all drawn while the encounter's declaration is Shuffled — never stacked, never arranged. Everything at once: each hostile creature within 60 feet takes 50 piercing damage (Dexterity save for half) and is restrained until the end of your next turn on a failed save; each ally within 60 feet regains 50 HP and gains 50 temporary HP. The hand is discarded afterward. It only ever happens shuffled, because nobody wastes five perfect cards at half power.

The barrier falls like a velvet curtain and the world goes green-felt and gold. Chandeliers that were never built light a floor that was never laid; somewhere a deck shuffles itself with the sound of distant thunder, and every card in the air turns to face you, the dealer. The exits are still there. They are simply no longer accepting bets.

Domain Expansion, The House Always Wins (action, 20 CE, 1 minute, 90-foot radius): the arena becomes a casino floor and you are the House. The Game: at the start of each hostile creature's turn inside, they draw a card (number 2–14; use a real deck or GM-assigned numbers) and you reveal your house card for them. Higher number wins; ties go to the House. House collects (you win): the creature is stunted until the end of their turn — action or bonus action, not both, and movement halved. House rakes (they win): they act normally, but you gain 3 CE — the house takes its cut from every pot. Rigged Deck: your shuffled draws are +4 instead of +2, your stacked draws have no penalty, and your Spade bonuses inside the Domain are doubled.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Card Counter

You have memorized the deck. When you draw shuffled, peek at the top two cards and choose which one to take; the other goes to the bottom. The house respects a good memory, even when it's yours.

Marked Cards

Once per combat, when you draw shuffled, you may name one card and draw it anyway — it still counts as shuffled (+2 applies). The deck is random. Your memory of it isn't.

Sleight of Hand

Once per turn, when you discard a card, you may play it as if it were in your hand (it is discarded afterward regardless). Nothing is ever truly thrown away — the deck just lets you think so.

The River

Once per long rest, before a shuffled draw, name one card. If it remains in the deck, you draw it; it still counts as shuffled (+2 applies). Fate owes everyone one card. Professionals collect.

Red or Black

When you play a red card (Hearts or Diamonds), if it targets an ally, they may make one weapon attack as a reaction. When you play a black card (Spades or Clubs), if it targets an ally, they gain +10 feet of movement speed until the end of their next turn. A poker combination counts as one card play for this feat. The deck has two colors. Both of them pay out.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: No suit effect is a rolled attack, so none can use the campaign's Black Flash procedure. Card-draw effects, heals, wards, buffs, and Domain card comparisons cannot.
Generic Maximum Output applies only to ordinary features, never to Royal Flush or The House Always Wins.
Random draws use a physical 52-card deck at the table, or GM-assigned numbers 2–14 (J 11, Q 12, K 13, A 14). Stacked draws are player-chosen and public.
Combinations resolve with number values only; face-card riders trigger only on singly played face cards.
