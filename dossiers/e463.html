Features & procedures

Properties & techniques

Exact player-facing procedures

Warded shell

The armor's manners are constant: +2 to AC always, resistance to necrotic always, whatever shape the armor takes. The ward is woven into the wearing, not the steel. While wearing this armor, you gain a +2 bonus to AC and resistance to necrotic damage. It can be any armor type.

Curse gradient

The gradient answers only when struck, a reaction against necrotic damage or a cursed technique, rolling 4d4 to blunt the blow. Resistance halves first; the gradient trims what remains. As a reaction to being hit by necrotic damage or a cursed technique, you can roll 4d4 and reduce the damage by that amount.

Field notes. Subject: armor woven with a ward that fades like dusk. Two truths. First: the shell is layered, +2 to armor class against all comers, and resistance to necrotic damage besides, so the rot arrives already halved. Second: the gradient is reactive, not passive, when necrotic damage or a cursed technique lands, the wearer may spend a reaction to shave 4d4 off the wound, after resistance has done its work. Mark the order of operations: halve first, then subtract, the ward respects the armor beneath it and does not double-count. Assess it as dusk, not dawn: it does not prevent the night, it dims it. Against the right curse, dimming is enough.

Campaign canon notes

Note: Curse gradient reduces damage after resistance applies (halve first, then subtract the 4d4). It triggers on the hit, so it works against damage that also carries other riders.
