Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core Engine, Time Counters. Time Counters are the whole economy: a maximum of his proficiency, minted when a Readied action triggers, when he makes an enemy waste an action or reaction, or when he opens his turn maintaining a delayed effect. They buy manipulation of timing, never free extra turns. The ledger is strict; the treasurer is stricter. Max PB. Gain 1 when you Ready an action that successfully triggers, cause an enemy to waste an action/reaction, or begin your turn maintaining a delayed effect. Time Counters manipulate timing but do not grant unrestricted extra turns.

Field notes. Subject: a treasurer of seconds. Three truths govern him. First, readiness is minted: Time Counters accrue when a Readied action triggers true, when an enemy wastes an action or reaction, or when he begins his turn nursing a delayed effect, patience, converted to currency, capped at his proficiency. Second, time is spent, never taken: his manipulations postpone, phase, and delay, but the technique's oldest law holds, no unrestricted extra turns. Third, the patient arrive first: he sidesteps attacks into the near future, delays enemy techniques until their next turn, and in his Slow Sunset forces hostile actions to the end of their turns. Mark this: he fights the way a miser spends, one second at a time, and only when it buys the battle.

The sidestep into the near future. As a reaction, two energy buys three armor against one attack, and if it misses, he phases ten feet without reprisal. He postpones the question. 9Time Raveler

He unweaves the enemy's readiness. Three energy and one Counter: a failed Wisdom save strips a creature's reactions and forbids the Ready action until its next turn ends. To disarm a man's future is to leave him only his present. For 3 CE + 1 Time Counter, target one creature; it makes a Wisdom save. On a failure it loses its reactions and cannot take the Ready action until the end of its next turn.

The second chance, offered at a price. The held breath. Six energy: a failed Constitution save phases a creature out until just before its next turn, unable to affect or be affected by anything. 18Domain, The Slow Sunset

Initiative 20 · place three temporal markers among creatures · hostile marker: Dex save, on failure its action is delayed to the end of its turn (after bonus action and movement) · ally marker: immediately move up to half speed · never grants extra full turns.

At the twentieth count the evening lengthens. Three temporal markers are placed: a hostile marked creature that fails its Dexterity save acts at the end of its turn, after movement and bonus action; an allied one may immediately move half its speed. The day does not end. It is merely extended. At initiative 20, place three temporal markers among creatures. A creature with an enemy marker makes a Dexterity save; on a failure its action is delayed, it acts at the end of its turn, after its bonus action and movement. A creature with an ally marker may immediately move up to half its speed. This never grants extra full turns.

Once per long rest, after completing your turn, you may spend 6 Time Counters to take a Temporal Window: move and take one action, which cannot be a Domain, a Maximum, or another turn-granting effect.
