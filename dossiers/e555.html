Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the librarian's ledger. Everything you have witnessed — every technique seen, every maneuver, every moment — is shelved in your mind like a library. You may lend these volumes. Lending (bonus action · 1 CE): lend one book to a willing creature within 60 feet; choose its volume from the Lending Menu. You may have up to PB books lent at once. Due date: 3 rounds — the book is due at the start of your turn, 3 rounds after lending. The holder may return it as a free action once per turn on their turn. Returned on time: nothing happens; the library is satisfied. Overdue: each round past due, the holder accrues a fine — a stacking −1 to attack rolls, ability checks, and saving throws. Payback: when an overdue book is finally returned or recalled, the holder takes 1d6 psychic damage per overdue round. The fine is the mechanism; the payback is the point.

Field notes. Three truths from the stacks. First: every library is a confidence trick — it lends you something valuable, trusts you to bring it back, and profits exactly when you don't; the technique simply removed the trust and kept the profit. Second: the due date is the weapon, not the book — a buff with a deadline is a leash, and nobody notices the leash until the fines start; lend generously, date strictly, collect always. Third: the cruelest thing a librarian can do is lend to someone who never asked — they get the buff, they get the deadline, and they never agreed to either; the stacks do not require consent, only a borrower. Mark the economy: the book is theirs. The fines are yours.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1The Lending Desk

Bonus action · 1 CE · Lend one book to a willing creature within 60 feet (up to PB books lent at once). Choose the volume: Field Manual (+1 to attack rolls), Ward Folio (resistance to one damage type, chosen at lending), Atlas (+10 ft movement speed). Due in 3 rounds; the holder may return it as a free action once per turn. Returned on time: nothing. Each overdue round: stacking −1 to attack rolls, ability checks, and saving throws. Payback on return: 1d6 psychic damage per overdue round.

The bread and butter: lend, date, collect. You don't choose between supporting and acting — lending is a bonus action, folded into whatever you were already doing. The buff is real and the deadline is real; the library's genius is that both are true at once. Lend generously. Date strictly.

3Force Lend

Action · 2 CE · Force-lend a book to an unwilling creature within 60 feet: Wisdom save negates (DC = your CE save DC). Creatures with Intelligence 3 or lower cannot be force-lent — they cannot comprehend the loan. The book's buff applies in full; it is a genuine volume. Due in 2 rounds, and the holder cannot return it as a free action — returning costs their action. You may recall any lent book as a bonus action (no CE).

The new verb: L1 lends to friends; L3 lends to everyone. The technique was blind to enemies — now the stacks lend indiscriminately. Note the honesty of it: the buff is real, the deadline is real, and they never asked for either. The library does not require consent. Only a borrower.

6The Fine Schedule

Passive · You may lend two books with a single bonus action (same or different creatures). The Lending Menu expands: Second Wind (once while held, the holder may use a bonus action to regain 2d8 HP), Clarity (advantage on saving throws against the charmed and frightened conditions). The fines escalate: payback is now 1d8 psychic damage per overdue round. Renewal Denied: once per round, when a creature holding your book attempts to return it, you may deny the return (no action required) — they keep the book.

The system deepens and the foundation sharpens with it: two books at once, stronger volumes, and the fines grow teeth at the same level the lending grows arms. Renewal Denied is the engine of the offense — lend to an enemy, deny every return, and let the fines do the talking. The technique doesn't abandon its first patrons; it just learns harsher policies.

11Special Collections

Action · 8 CE · Once per short rest · Lend a volume from Special Collections to a creature within 60 feet (willing, or unwilling via Force Lend's save): the holder gains +2 to AC, advantage on all saving throws, and +2d6 to attack rolls. Due in 2 rounds. Fines accrue at double rate (−2 per overdue round instead of −1). If the book is 3 or more rounds overdue when returned or recalled, the holder suffers technique burnout after the payback: they cannot use technique features until the end of their next turn.

High power, not a finisher: the limiter is the short rest, the 8 CE, and the catastrophic risk — you do not lend from Special Collections lightly, because the fines can end a technique. It doesn't end the fight. It puts the most valuable volume in the stacks into play, and dares everyone to meet the deadline.

18The Library Never Closes

Passive · Lending becomes a free action once per turn (still 1 CE per book). You may have up to twice your PB in books lent at once. Books lent to willing allies never go overdue — the library waives fines for its friends. Force Lend's save DC increases by 2.

Enablement, not a nuke: the technique stops taxing its own existence and starts running the field. Your allies carry permanent buffs with no deadlines; your enemies carry deadlines with no mercy. You are very difficult to handle now — not because you hit harder, but because every book on the field has your stamp on the due-date slip.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

You close the ledger with a sound like a book slamming shut, and every borrower on the field feels it — the due-date slip in their chest, the fine schedule behind their eyes. The library is calling in every loan at once. Those who returned on time feel a warmth, like a librarian's nod. Those who didn't feel the full weight of the stacks.

Maximum, The Audit (action, 10 CE): every book you have lent is called in simultaneously — no save, no refusal. For each book held by a willing ally and within its due date: they regain 2d8 HP and gain +2d6 to their next attack roll, ability check, or saving throw (their choice) — the library rewards its patrons. For each overdue book: the holder takes payback immediately at double the normal rate (2d8 psychic per overdue round instead of 1d8), and force-lent books are always audited as overdue — the library does not recognize unwilling borrowers as patrons. Books are then returned to the stacks.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Storytime

Action · 3 CE · Read aloud from a shelved volume: every ally within 30 feet gains the buff of one Lending Menu volume of your choice until the start of your next turn. No lending, no due date, no fines — it is a reading, not a loan. Lending, but as a chorus.

Marginalia

When you lend a book, you may inscribe a note in its margins (1 CE): choose a kind note or a barbed note. When the book is returned or recalled — on time or overdue — the note triggers: a kind note restores 2d8 HP to the returner; a barbed note deals 2d8 psychic damage to the returner. The handwriting is unmistakably yours. Lending, but as a trap.

The Quiet Floor

Action · 2 CE · Designate a 20-foot cube within 120 feet as the Quiet Floor for 1 minute: no sound can be created within or pass through the area (as the silence effect). The library demands quiet, and the building enforces it. The stacks, but as silence.

Card Catalog

Bonus action · 1 CE · Catalog a creature within 60 feet: you learn its damage resistances, immunities, and vulnerabilities, its current conditions, and whether it is under the effect of a technique. The reference desk answers every question — for a price. The stacks, but as intelligence.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: Direct rolled technique attacks can use the campaign's Black Flash procedure when otherwise eligible. Save-effect damage (The Audit's payback) cannot critically hit or Black Flash.
Generic Maximum Output applies only to ordinary features, never to The Audit.
Lent books are not objects: they cannot be stolen, destroyed, dispelled, or transferred — only returned (by the holder) or recalled (by you). A book's buff ends immediately when it is returned or recalled. Overdue fines end when the book is returned. If a holder dies while holding a book, the book returns to the stacks with no payback — the library writes off dead accounts. While you are unconscious, lent books remain out and fines continue to accrue, but you cannot recall books or deny renewals.
A creature may use its action to return a book held by a willing ally adjacent to it (interlibrary courtesy). Force Lend cannot target creatures with Intelligence 3 or lower. A creature that has resisted your Force Lend before has advantage on the Wisdom save against it for 24 hours — a borrower only refuses the stacks once.
