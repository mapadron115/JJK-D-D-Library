Djinn & Metal Vessels

Dungeons are permanent living Domains containing ancient beings called Djinn. Conquer a Dungeon, earn its ruler’s recognition, and it may bind itself to an object you carry—granting you a Metal Vessel that grows from supernatural weapon, to Full Djinn Equip, to battlefield-shattering Extreme Magic. Djinn are partners rather than possessions, and they choose their Kings regardless of bloodline, cursed technique, or status.

What a Djinn Is

A Djinn is neither human nor curse. It is a soul converted into a self-sustaining barrier intelligence — a new branch of JJK metaphysics.

The other endpoint

Long before modern jujutsu, possibly even before the Heian era, a group of ancient sorcerers discovered another endpoint for the relationship between body, soul, barriers, and cursed energy. Most sorcerers die. Some become vengeful cursed spirits. Some become cursed objects. The beings who became Djinn did something different: they constructed artificial innate domains capable of surviving independently of their original bodies, and transferred the stable structure of their souls into those barrier systems.

A new branch

Djinn fit into JJK metaphysics as another branch alongside cursed spirits, cursed objects, shikigami, reincarnated sorcerers, and evolved beings like Tengen. A Djinn is essentially a soul converted into a self-sustaining barrier intelligence — ancient, opinionated, and very much alive in the way that matters.

A Metal Vessel is not an Innate Technique. It is a separate supernatural contract system — closer to an extremely advanced cursed object + binding vow + spirit contract. This matters because it lets different JJK character types interact with Djinn without becoming mechanically identical.

What Is a Dungeon?

A Dungeon is the Djinn’s externalized innate domain made permanent. Unlike Domain Expansion, which a sorcerer projects temporarily, a Dungeon is a lasting world — the interior architecture of the Djinn’s soul.

A living world

Because a Dungeon is a soul’s interior made real, it can contain impossible architecture, ecosystems, monsters, environmental rules, treasure, trials, and alternate spatial geometry. It is not just a building. Brute force alone should not always clear one — the Djinn at the center is judging potential Kings.

The interview

A Dungeon is effectively an interview conducted by an ancient living Domain. Different Dungeons test different things: courage, ambition, compassion, intelligence, leadership, restraint, strength, willingness to sacrifice, ideology. Reaching the treasure room proves capability; the trial determines whom the Djinn is willing to follow.

The seventy-two and why they are back

The seventy-two Dungeons were never destroyed. They were sealed outside ordinary barrier space, and for centuries Tengen’s barrier infrastructure — or some ancient sealing network — incidentally kept them isolated. Then something disrupts the system:

Tengen dies or evolves

The keystone of Japan’s barrier network changes state, and sealed coordinates reconnect.

A barrier ritual fails

A massive working collapses and the isolation it maintained collapses with it.

A Culling Game-like event

Enormous barrier violence tears holes in the old order.

Deliberate sabotage

Someone who wants the Dungeons open breaks the seals on purpose.

A global CE surge

Cursed energy rises worldwide and old locks stop holding.

An ancient seal expires

The simplest answer: the wards were always going to fail, and now they have.

The coordinates reconnect. Dungeon One appears — perhaps in Tokyo Bay, perhaps replacing part of a mountain, perhaps overlapping an existing Domain. Then another. Then several at once. Eventually one appears outside Japan, proving this is a global issue. The jujutsu world realizes there are seventy-two total Dungeons, and every single one contains a potentially Special Grade-level power source waiting to choose a wielder. That is the campaign premise.

What Is a Metal Vessel?

A Djinn cannot safely manifest its complete existence in normal reality indefinitely. Its Dungeon is effectively its true body. To operate outside the Dungeon, it needs an anchor.

The address, not the house

When the Djinn accepts a King, it binds part of itself to a physical object connected to that person. That object becomes the Metal Vessel. The best way to understand it: the Metal Vessel does not contain the Dungeon — it contains the “address” to the Dungeon. Djinn abilities work by allowing increasing amounts of that permanent domain to manifest through the anchor. It does not literally need to be metal, though the term stays. A sword, ring, bracelet, staff, coin, spear, fan, musical instrument, prayer bead, chain, crown, or tool can all serve.

Progression as manifestation

This gives a clean metaphysical explanation for the whole progression: Weapon Equip manifests one conceptual piece of the Djinn’s domain; Full Djinn Equip overlays the King’s body with the Djinn’s domain; Extreme Magic briefly forces enough of the Djinn’s domain into reality to impose its highest principle on the world. Baal is not merely “casting lightning.” For a moment, Baal’s world is entering ours.

Why “King Vessel”?

“King” does not necessarily mean literal monarch. It is a modern shortening of an ancient barrier concept meaning something like “the person upon whom the domain recognizes final authority.”

A King Vessel is simply the individual the Djinn recognizes as worthy to carry its power outside the Dungeon. That lets very different people become Kings. A Djinn might choose a clan heir, a homeless teenager, a Grade 4 sorcerer, someone with no Innate Technique, a Heavenly Restriction user, a criminal, a pacifist, someone who defeated it — or someone who refused to kill when killing would have been easier. The strongest contestant does not have to win. That is important.

The Political Earthquake

This is one of the strongest reasons the Djinn system belongs in JJK. Jujutsu society has historically been heavily hereditary: power comes from bloodlines, inherited techniques, clans, and family influence. Djinn completely destabilize that model.

“We choose our own heirs.” A Djinn does not care whether someone inherited Limitless, Ten Shadows, Blood Manipulation, or nothing at all. It chooses whoever it believes is worthy. A sorcerer with cursed energy but no Innate Technique could enter a Dungeon as a nobody and emerge carrying a Metal Vessel capable of eventually threatening Special Grades. A Heavenly Restriction user could become a King. A civilian could theoretically become a King.

The system should constantly ask: what does power entitle you to? Different Kings answer differently — responsibility, authority, freedom, conquest, protection, service, destiny, superiority — and that creates natural rivalries between King Vessels even when their mechanics are completely different. Kouji Han’s answer was: strength exists for those who need it; power creates responsibility, not entitlement. Other Kings do not have to agree.

Djinn Are People

Djinn are people, not inventory. They have personalities, opinions, values, grudges, and agency — and the relationship is a partnership, not ownership.

They can disagree

A Djinn may argue with its King’s plans and withhold cooperation it considers dishonorable.

They can demand vows

Recognition can come with conditions. A Djinn may require binding vows before granting deeper power.

They can refuse

After a serious betrayal, a Djinn can refuse cooperation — including refusing an Extreme Magic — until trust is rebuilt.

They can steady you

A Djinn can support its King against the King’s own worst impulses. Power as a relationship cuts both ways.

They recognize Household

A King cannot hand out power by fiat. The Djinn itself must accept each Household member.

They remember

A Djinn that rejects every candidate remembers the party. A Dungeon can reappear with the same Djinn watching.

Core Mechanics: The Independent Djinn Reserve

Every Djinn has its own independent Reserve. All Djinn costs — Weapon Equip, Full Equip, abilities, reactions, Extreme Magic — spend that Djinn’s Reserve, never the wielder’s CE.

Djinn RankReserveNotes

B14Reliable mid-campaign power
A16Flexible or unusually potent
S18Rare conceptual gifts
S+20Soul, space-time, and similarly fundamental effects

Refresh

Each Djinn’s Reserve refreshes on a long rest. If one Djinn is exhausted, using another Djinn does not refill the first — each Djinn is its own resource economy.

Your numbers still drive it

Attacks and saves made through a Djinn use your CE attack modifier and your CE save DC unless the Djinn explicitly says otherwise. The energy is external; the skill is yours.

Why this is better

A Heavenly Restriction user can use Djinn because the energy is external. A technique-less sorcerer can spend their own CE on reinforcement or Black Flash while the Djinn runs its own pool. A normal sorcerer never has to choose between fueling their Innate Technique and fueling the Djinn. Collecting multiple Djinn gives versatility rather than one gigantic shared mana pool.

Progression

Djinn Die

d6 levels 1–4 • d8 5–10 • d10 11–16 • d12 17–20. All listed Djinn damage and healing uses the current Djinn Die unless a flat die is named.

Levels 1–4: Early contract

The Djinn is dormant or newly bound. Djinn Die d6. Minor utility manifestations only — the vessel is a promise, not yet a weapon.

Level 5: Weapon Equip

Bonus action, 2 Reserve, 10 minutes. The Metal Vessel transforms into the Djinn’s signature supernatural weapon or tool. Baal becomes a lightning sword; Dantalion becomes a spatial transfer fan; a gravity Djinn might manifest a royal arm that changes weight at impact. Weapon Equip should meaningfully alter how the character fights without being a full transformation. Only one Equip can normally be active at a time.

Level 9: Full Djinn Equip

Bonus action, 4 Reserve, 1 minute. The King synchronizes deeply and manifests supernatural armor, wings, markings, elemental structures, or other features of that Djinn. Full Equip grants major mobility, affinity-based defenses, a unique battlefield passive, and full access to the Djinn’s higher abilities. It should feel like a Special Grade transformation, not a stat buff.

Level 11: Deep Synchronization

Djinn Die becomes d10. Once per short rest, one Equip activation costs 1 Reserve less. A King who narratively masters a Djinn may use its minor utility manifestations outside combat without spending Reserve.

Level 13: Extreme Magic

Action, requires that Djinn’s Full Equip, 1/long rest per Djinn. Pay the listed Reserve cost from this Djinn’s Reserve. A Full Equip ends after resolution, and that Djinn cannot Equip again until a short rest. Extreme Magic is the Djinn’s pinnacle ability — a battlefield-scale or strategic trump card, not a normal spell.

Level 17+: King Vessel mastery

Djinn Die becomes d12. Switching directly from one Full Equip to another is a bonus action, paying the new Equip’s cost. Switching between Full Equips gets easier as mastery deepens.

Level 20: Extreme transition

Once per long rest, immediately transition from one Djinn’s Extreme directly into another Djinn’s Full Equip.

Djinn abilities are a second combat identity. Each Djinn carries a small package of distinct abilities tied to its affinity and personality — Dantalion handles teleportation and transfer circles, Baal handles lightning and reaction denial, Phenex handles healing and restoration, Belial handles souls and separation, Zagan handles life, plants, and battlefield control. Avoid the “small attack / medium attack / big attack” template; a Djinn should not feel like a magic item with extra steps.

Extreme Magic: Two Scales

Encounter scale. Against PCs and named bosses, use normal stat blocks, saves, damage, and conditions. Typical destructive Extremes spend roughly 15–18 Djinn Dice and affect areas measured in hundreds of feet. Extreme Magic should be powerful enough to alter battlefields and destroy major terrain without automatically deleting equal-tier bosses.

Anime / strategic scale. When an Extreme is used against empty terrain, armies, fortresses, mountains, or ships, let the fiction reflect its true scale instead of pretending everything fits on a 5-foot grid. Resolve the listed anime-scale result narratively; named combatants still use the encounter profile.

Multiple Djinn & Who Can Be a King

No hard cap

There is no hard lifetime limit on how many Djinn one person can acquire. The limiters are narrative and political: clearing Dungeons, whether each Djinn accepts the person, relationship conflicts, public consequences, and power balance. Only one Equip can be active at a time, so multiple Djinn mostly increase the number of answers available. One Djinn is already enough for a complete player build; two is exceptional; three or four is rare elite King territory; ten is campaign-end legendary status.

Tools, not replacements — unless you choose otherwise

The Reserve pools keep Metal Vessels as tools rather than entire replacements for power. But a Djinn-focused character can conquer many Dungeons and go that route for power instead of — or entirely without — an innate technique.

Who can be chosen

A sorcerer with no Innate Technique can still become a King Vessel. A normal sorcerer with an Innate Technique keeps their technique. A Heavenly Restriction user can also become a King Vessel, because the Djinn’s power is external to the user rather than produced by their own body or cursed technique.

Household Vessels

A King Vessel can allow their Djinn to recognize Household members — but the Djinn itself must accept them. The King cannot hand out powers by fiat.

What a Household member gets

One narrow passive and one active ability themed after that Djinn. Household members do not gain Full Equip or Extreme Magic unless they later become a true King Vessel themselves.

Why it matters

Household Vessels let Djinn create organizations, apprentices, rescue teams, political factions, succession systems, and rival schools of thought. A Phenex Household could become a humanitarian healing corps; a Dantalion Household could specialize in transport, evacuation, and spatial rescue; a Baal Household could become elite combatants; a Belial Household should stay tightly controlled due to soul manipulation; a Zagan Household could focus on medicine, agriculture, ecology, and restoration.

Running Dungeons at the Table

Use this generator when a Dungeon appears during a campaign. A Dungeon is not merely a monster-filled vault — it is a projected fragment of an older world shaped by its resident Djinn, and the Djinn can alter its difficulty.

1. Choose the Djinn

Pick a named or original Djinn first. Its affinity and personality determine the Dungeon’s visual language, hazards, monsters, and final trial. A gentle healing Djinn may build a terrifying Dungeon that tests whether a candidate protects others; a destructive Djinn may instead prize restraint.

2. Choose a Scale

Minor: 3 chambers + trial, 1–2 sessions. Standard: 5–7 chambers + trial, 2–4 sessions. Grand: 8–12 chambers, rival factions, safe rooms and multiple routes, 4+ sessions. Scale changes adventure length, not the eventual Djinn’s power.

3. Establish the Law

Every Dungeon gets one supernatural rule: gravity reverses at bells; spoken lies animate; fire heals monsters; doors remember who opened them; shadows move independently; water rises whenever CE is spent. Reveal the rule through play rather than a lore dump.

4. Three Pillars

Include at least one Traversal chamber, one Conflict chamber, and one Choice chamber. The Choice should expose character, not merely demand the highest skill modifier.

5. Dungeon Creatures

Reskin existing monsters around the Djinn’s affinity. Standard creatures should be expendable; elite guardians should teach the Dungeon’s law. Defeating everything is never required unless that particular Djinn values conquest.

6. Collapse

Once a Djinn selects a King Vessel, the Dungeon begins collapsing. Run a short escape challenge unless the story benefits from an immediate magical ejection. Treasure may be plentiful, but the Metal Vessel is the true prize.

Djinn Compatibility & Final Trials

The final trial is not an Athletics check. Reaching the treasure room proves capability; the trial determines whom the Djinn is willing to follow. A Djinn can consider multiple people present and choose only one. Score behavior across the whole Dungeon, not one die roll.

Interpretation: 12+ is an extraordinary match; 9–11 is worthy; 6–8 is uncertain and merits a direct trial; 5 or less is normally rejected. These are GM tools, not player-visible “relationship points.” A Djinn may knowingly choose the lower score if its personality gives it a compelling reason.

Trial of Strength

Defeat an avatar, survive a fixed number of rounds, or land one meaningful blow. Best for Barbatos, Leraje, Agares, and similarly martial personalities.

Trial of Choice

Two goals cannot both be saved. The answer matters more than “correctness.” Best for Belial, Phenex, Zagan, Zepar, or morally complicated Djinn.

Trial of Rule

The Djinn presents a game, riddle, negotiation, race, or deceptive contest. Best for Valefor, Dantalion, Shax, or cerebral Djinn.

Trial of Kingship

Rivals must convince the Djinn why they should lead without simply killing one another. Allies may advocate for another candidate, which itself can influence the result.

The Treasure Room & Contested Captures

When several PCs reach the Djinn together, do not default to PvP. Everyone present may be considered; the Djinn adjudicates its own contract.

Step 1 — Freeze Hostilities

The Djinn may suspend combat inside the treasure room long enough to speak. Attacking anyway is possible, but becomes evidence about the attacker rather than a way to “loot” the Djinn.

Step 2 — Hear Every Claim

Each interested candidate gets one uninterrupted statement or action demonstrating why they seek the power. Players can decline candidacy or openly support somebody else.

Step 3 — Resolve the Trial

Use accumulated compatibility plus the final trial. Only roll when uncertainty remains. A relevant check should modify the result, never replace characterization.

Step 4 — Recognition

The Djinn names one King Vessel. The contract cannot be stolen by grabbing the treasure first. The Djinn enters a metal object meaningful and familiar to its chosen bearer.

Deadlock

If two candidates are genuinely equal, the Djinn can demand a final duel, cooperative test, sacrifice, debate, or future deed. A Dungeon may remain open until that question is answered.

Refusal

A Djinn can reject everyone. The party may still escape with mundane treasure, knowledge, or artifacts, and the Dungeon can later reappear with the same Djinn remembering them.

Dark Metal Vessels

Separate corrupted system. Dark Metal Vessels do not house a Djinn — they consume Black Rukh and can transform users into Dark Djinn. Because there is no Djinn inside, there is no independent Reserve: their abilities draw on the bearer’s CE and are fueled by Depravity, not alternate skins for normal contracts.
Depravity

A Dark Vessel has 0–6 Depravity. Gain 1 when activating Dark Djinn Equip, voluntarily feeding the vessel intense hatred/despair, or using its Extreme Magic. At 3+, cosmetic/behavioral corruption becomes obvious. At 6, the vessel attempts to consume the bearer during the next transformation.

Dark Weapon

Attunement grants two 1–4 CE abilities. These are usually more aggressive than normal Metal Vessel powers and may ignore a resistance, impose a condition, or alter terrain.

Dark Djinn Equip • Level 7+

Bonus action, 3 CE and gain 1 Depravity; lasts 1 minute. Gain the vessel’s monstrous form, resistance/passive, and signature ability. Unlike normal Djinn Equip, this can physically distort the bearer.

Dark Extreme • Level 11+

Once/long rest, 7–10 CE and +1 Depravity. Target output roughly 8–10 Djinn Dice or equivalent control. A bearer at 5+ Depravity makes a Cha save afterward or remains transformed and hostile/compelled for a GM-defined scene.

A character can choose to embrace it; that is a character arc, not an automatic NPC conversion.

Use this when the campaign needs one of Solomon’s unnamed Djinn or an entirely original Dungeon. Build personality first and damage last — and give the new Djinn its own independent Reserve from the rank table above.
