Djinn & Metal Vessels

This rework keeps the modular JJK 5e framework, but moves the fantasy much closer to Magi: Weapon Equip is a real supernatural weapon, Full Djinn Equip is a landscape-changing transformation, and Extreme Magic is a battlefield or strategic catastrophe rather than a 20-foot fireball with a fancy name.

Reworked Metal Vessel Rules

Djinn Die

d6 levels 1–4 • d8 5–10 • d10 11–16 • d12 17–20. All listed damage/healing uses the current Djinn Die unless a flat die is named.

Weapon Equip • Level 5

Bonus action, 2 CE, 10 minutes. The Metal Vessel becomes the Djinn Weapon, unlocks its signature weapon identity, and counts as magical. One Equip at a time.

Full Djinn Equip • Level 9

Bonus action, 4 CE, 1 minute. Gain the entry’s Full Equip package. Full Equips are intentionally much stronger now: high-speed flight, affinity defense, and a unique battlefield passive.

Deep Synchronization • 11

Djinn Die becomes d10. Once per short rest reduce one Equip activation by 1 CE. A King who narratively masters a Djinn may use its minor utility manifestations outside combat without CE.

Extreme Magic • Level 13

Action, Full Equip only, 1/long rest per Djinn. Pay the listed cost. After resolution that Full Equip ends and that Djinn cannot Equip again until a short rest. Extreme Magic is supposed to be a trump card.

King Vessel • Level 17+

Djinn Die becomes d12. Full-to-Full switching is a bonus action by paying the new Equip cost. At level 20, once/long rest you may immediately Full Equip a different Djinn after an Extreme without paying its activation cost.

Encounter Scale. The numbers below are what you use when PCs, bosses, and initiative matter. Areas are now routinely 100–300+ feet, damage lives around 15–18 Djinn Dice for destructive Extremes, and terrain actually changes.

Strategic / Anime Scale. Against armies, empty terrain, ships, fortresses, or mountain-scale objectives, do not pretend a 5-foot grid is the whole fiction. Resolve the listed anime-scale result narratively. Named combatants still use the encounter profile.

Why the scale changed: Magi’s Full Djinn Equips are portrayed as landscape-changing weapons, while known feats include mountain-destroying wind, city-flooding water, mountain teleportation, tectonic manipulation, and Extremes that pierce gigantic supernatural targets. The old 20–40 ft areas dramatically undersold that fantasy.

King Vessels & Household Vessels

Collection

No hard lifetime cap. Dungeons, Djinn personalities, and political consequences are the real limiter. More Djinn mostly means more answers because only one Equip can be active at a time.

Household Vessel

A Djinn may recognize multiple Household members. Each receives one narrow passive and one active gift themed after that Djinn. A Household never gains Full Equip or Extreme Magic unless it later becomes a true King Vessel.

Djinn Are People

They can disagree, refuse an Extreme after a flagrant betrayal, demand vows, or support a King against their own worst impulses. Power is a relationship, not inventory.

Dungeon Generation

A Dungeon is not merely a monster-filled vault. It is a projected fragment of an older world shaped by its resident Djinn, and the Djinn can alter its difficulty. Use this generator when a Dungeon appears during a campaign.

1. Choose the Djinn

Pick a named or original Djinn first. Its affinity and personality determine the Dungeon's visual language, hazards, monsters, and final trial. A gentle healing Djinn may build a terrifying Dungeon that tests whether a candidate protects others; a destructive Djinn may instead prize restraint.

2. Choose a Scale

Minor: 3 chambers + trial, 1–2 sessions. Standard: 5–7 chambers + trial, 2–4 sessions. Grand: 8–12 chambers, rival factions, safe rooms and multiple routes, 4+ sessions. Scale changes adventure length, not the eventual Djinn's power.

3. Establish the Law

Every Dungeon gets one supernatural rule: gravity reverses at bells; spoken lies animate; fire heals monsters; doors remember who opened them; shadows move independently; water rises whenever CE is spent. Reveal the rule through play rather than a lore dump.

4. Three Pillars

Include at least one Traversal chamber, one Conflict chamber, and one Choice chamber. The Choice should expose character, not merely demand the highest skill modifier.

5. Dungeon Creatures

Reskin existing monsters around the Djinn's affinity. Standard creatures should be expendable; elite guardians should teach the Dungeon's law. Defeating everything is never required unless that particular Djinn values conquest.

6. Collapse

Once a Djinn selects a King Vessel, the Dungeon begins collapsing. Run a short escape challenge unless the story benefits from an immediate magical ejection. Treasure may be plentiful, but the Metal Vessel is the true prize.

Djinn Compatibility & Final Trials

The final trial is not an Athletics check. Reaching the treasure room proves capability; the trial determines whom the Djinn is willing to follow. In canon, a Djinn can consider multiple people present and choose only one. The GM should therefore score behavior across the whole Dungeon, not decide the winner from one die roll.

Interpretation: 12+ is an extraordinary match; 9–11 is worthy; 6–8 is uncertain and merits a direct trial; 5 or less is normally rejected. These are GM tools, not player-visible “relationship points.” A Djinn may knowingly choose the lower score if its personality gives it a compelling reason.

Trial of Strength

Defeat an avatar, survive a fixed number of rounds, or land one meaningful blow. Best for Barbatos, Leraje, Agares, and similarly martial personalities.

Trial of Choice

Two goals cannot both be saved. The answer matters more than “correctness.” Best for Belial, Phenex, Zagan, Zepar, or morally complicated Djinn.

Trial of Rule

The Djinn presents a game, riddle, negotiation, race, or deceptive contest. Best for Valefor, Dantalion, Shax, or cerebral Djinn.

Trial of Kingship

Rivals must convince the Djinn why they should lead without simply killing one another. Allies may advocate for another candidate, which itself can influence the result.

The Treasure Room & Contested Captures

When several PCs reach the Djinn together, do not default to PvP. Everyone present may be considered; the Djinn adjudicates its own contract.

Step 1 — Freeze Hostilities

The Djinn may suspend combat inside the treasure room long enough to speak. Attacking anyway is possible, but becomes evidence about the attacker rather than a way to “loot” the Djinn.

Step 2 — Hear Every Claim

Each interested candidate gets one uninterrupted statement or action demonstrating why they seek the power. Players can decline candidacy or openly support somebody else.

Step 3 — Resolve the Trial

Use accumulated compatibility plus the final trial. Only roll when uncertainty remains. A relevant check should modify the result, never replace characterization.

Step 4 — Recognition

The Djinn names one King Vessel. The contract cannot be stolen by grabbing the treasure first. The Djinn enters a metal object meaningful and familiar to its chosen bearer.

Deadlock

If two candidates are genuinely equal, the Djinn can demand a final duel, cooperative test, sacrifice, debate, or future deed. A Dungeon may remain open until that question is answered.

Refusal

A Djinn can reject everyone. The party may still escape with mundane treasure, knowledge, or artifacts, and the Dungeon can later reappear with the same Djinn remembering them.

Dark Metal Vessels

Separate corrupted system. Canon Dark Metal Vessels do not house Solomon's Djinn; they consume Black Rukh and can transform users into Dark Djinn. In this conversion they are powerful cursed tools fueled by Depravity, not alternate skins for normal contracts.
Depravity

A Dark Vessel has 0–6 Depravity. Gain 1 when activating Dark Djinn Equip, voluntarily feeding the vessel intense hatred/despair, or using its Extreme Magic. At 3+, cosmetic/behavioral corruption becomes obvious. At 6, the vessel attempts to consume the bearer during the next transformation.

Dark Weapon

Attunement grants two 1–4 CE abilities. These are usually more aggressive than normal Metal Vessel powers and may ignore a resistance, impose a condition, or alter terrain.

Dark Djinn Equip • Level 7+

Bonus action, 3 CE and gain 1 Depravity; lasts 1 minute. Gain the vessel's monstrous form, resistance/passive, and signature ability. Unlike normal Djinn Equip, this can physically distort the bearer.

Dark Extreme • Level 11+

Once/long rest, 7–10 CE and +1 Depravity. Target output roughly 8–10 Djinn Dice or equivalent control. A bearer at 5+ Depravity makes a Cha save afterward or remains transformed and hostile/compelled for a GM-defined scene.

A character can choose to embrace it; that is a character arc, not an automatic NPC conversion.

Use this when the campaign needs one of Solomon's unnamed Djinn or an entirely original Dungeon. Build personality first and damage last.

Rank C/B

Focused and dependable. Usually one strong combat lane plus utility. Excellent early or mid-campaign Dungeon rewards.

Rank A

Flexible or unusually potent. Strong movement/control plus respectable damage, or a particularly dangerous Extreme Magic.

Rank S

Rare conceptual gifts: healing, mind control, multi-element control, anti-healing, exceptional battlefield manipulation.

Rank S+

Belial/Dantalion territory. Soul, space-time, or similarly fundamental effects. Powerful but still bounded by saves, CE, one Equip, and action economy.

Do not balance by making every Djinn equal. Balance the campaign by acquisition difficulty, breadth limits, shared CE, one active Equip, and spotlight. A weaker Djinn with perfect compatibility should still be worth loving for twenty levels.
