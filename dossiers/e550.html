Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the sealed scroll. Your power arrives bound: seven seals, each holding back one judgment of the apocalypse. While every seal is intact you are warded — resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage; +2 to all saving throws; damage you take is reduced by your PB; advantage on death saving throws. Your bound power lends weight to your mundane blows. Sealed blows: trigger · 1 CE — after you hit with a weapon or unarmed attack, deal +1d8 force damage as the seals lend their weight to the strike.

Breaking seals. Bonus action, once per turn, break the next unbroken seal in order (1st through 7th). Breaking a seal is irreversible until you finish a long rest — there is no resealing mid-fight, no putting the judgment back. Each broken seal unleashes its judgment (an apocalyptic effect that stays active) and strips one protection from your ward (you become more fragile as you become more terrible). The seals broken are a count, not a currency — nothing is spent, nothing is tracked but the number.

The seven seals, in order. 1st — The White Horse, Conquest: judgment — your sealed blows deal +WIS force damage (the conqueror's weight behind every strike). Strips: resistance to bludgeoning. 2nd — The Red Horse, War: judgment — creatures hit by your sealed blows cannot take reactions until the end of their next turn (peace taken from the earth). Strips: resistance to piercing. 3rd — The Black Horse, Famine: judgment — hostile creatures within 30 ft pay +1 CE for technique features (the scales weigh every spending). Strips: resistance to slashing. 4th — The Pale Horse, Death: judgment — your sealed blows deal +1d8 necrotic (Death rides, and Hell follows). Strips: your +2 to saves becomes +1. 5th — The Martyrs: judgment — the slain cry out: for each creature that has died within 60 ft during this combat, your sealed blows deal +1 damage (grievances stack, maximum +PB). Strips: your remaining +1 to saves (now +0). 6th — Cosmic Upheaval: judgment — once per turn as an action (2 CE), call down a falling star: creatures in a 20-foot radius within 120 ft make a Dexterity save, taking 4d10 bludgeoning on a failure (half on success); the area becomes difficult terrain. Strips: damage you take is no longer reduced by PB. 7th — Silence in Heaven: judgment — when broken, silence falls: all sound is suppressed within 60 ft until the end of your next turn (verbal components fail, nothing can be heard). Then the trumpets. Strips: advantage on death saving throws — by the final seal, even death has no patience left for you.

Field notes. Three truths from the sealed scroll. First: the seals were never locks. They were mercy — every one you break is a mercy you have decided the world no longer deserves, starting with yourself. Second: count the cost before the first break, because there is no second counting. The ward does not come back. The skin does not re-thicken. Every judgment you loose is a piece of your own armor melted down for ammunition. Third: the seventh seal is not a weapon. It is a confession — that you were willing to stand before the end of the world with nothing left between you and it but your own nerve. Most who break it do not survive the trumpets. That is not a flaw in the technique. That is the technique, honestly priced.

Progression

Level 1 through 20.

1The Sealed Scroll

Trigger · 1 CE · After you hit with a weapon or unarmed attack, your sealed blows deal +1d8 force damage. While all seals are intact you are warded (resistance to bludgeoning/piercing/slashing, +2 to all saves, damage reduced by PB, advantage on death saves). Bonus action · once per turn · break the next seal in order (1st and 2nd available): unleash its judgment, lose its protection, irreversibly until a long rest.

The bread and butter: fight normally, and let the seals lend weight to every blow — never choose between attacking and technique-ing, because the technique is the weight behind your hands. At full ward you are a wall that hits back; at two seals broken you are a conqueror who bleeds. The choice of when to start breaking is the whole technique at level 1, and it never stops being the whole technique.

3Read the Scroll

Bonus action · 1 CE · Read the seals on a creature within 60 feet: you learn its current conditions, any binding vows or self-imposed restrictions affecting it, and its damage resistances, immunities, and vulnerabilities. A creature hiding a restriction reads as a sealed scroll with pages stuck together — you know something is bound, even if you cannot read the words.

The new verb: everyone is sealed about something. Vows, pacts, heavenly restrictions, suppressed techniques — the world is full of bound power, and you are the one soul who reads bindings the way others read faces. What the unsealing cannot do, the reading can: find the enemy's own seals, and plan around them.

6The Opening

Your sealed blows deal 2d8 force damage. The 3rd and 4th seals (Famine, Death) become breakable. When you break a seal, the next weapon or unarmed attack you hit with this turn deals sealed blow damage without spending CE — the unsealing carries its own momentum.

The foundation levels up with the player: the blows double while the deeper seals open, so the bread-and-butter is never abandoned for the judgments — your own hands are the delivery system the judgments ride on. Famine starves the enemy's CE; Death rides every strike. The scroll is half open now, and the ward is half gone. That is the trade, and it is working.

11The Woes

The 5th and 6th seals (Martyrs, Cosmic Upheaval) become breakable. When you break a seal, the judgment arrives twice — immediately invoke its effect a second time as a free action (a second star falls, the famine aura pulses, the martyrs cry out again). The limiter is the irreversibility: every seal broken this fight stays broken, every protection stays stripped. You cannot unbreak, and you cannot re-ward.

High power, honestly gated: the CE is never the limit at this tier — the limit is that each breaking is a door you kick off its hinges. Five seals broken means five judgments active and almost nothing left of the ward. The question is no longer whether you can afford to break, but whether you can afford to stop.

18The Seventh Seal

The 7th seal (Silence in Heaven) becomes breakable. Breaking it: silence falls within 60 ft until the end of your next turn, then the trumpets sound — every hostile creature within 60 ft makes a Wisdom save or is frightened of you for 1 minute (save ends). Your sealed blows deal 3d8 force damage. You are now entirely unwarded: no resistances, no save bonus, no damage reduction, no death-save advantage. What remains is seven judgments, one scroll, and you.

The peak is not a bigger number — it is the absence of everything that kept you alive. No other technique asks you to trade this much of yourself for its capstone. The seventh seal does not make you powerful. It makes you honest: this is what your power always cost. You just finally agreed to pay it.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

Silence. Half an hour of it, compressed into a single held breath — the heaven you carry inside you going quiet all at once. And then, from somewhere beyond the quiet, a trumpet. Not a sound so much as a verdict. The sky does not split. It is read aloud.

Maximum Technique, The Seventh Trumpet (action, 10 CE, once per long rest): sound the final trumpet. Every hostile creature within 120 feet makes a Constitution save, taking 2d10 thunder plus 2d10 radiant damage per seal you have broken on a failure (half damage on a success). If all seven seals are broken, creatures that fail the save are also stunned until the end of your next turn — the world stops to hear the verdict. This is setup-gated: the trumpet is only as apocalyptic as your willingness to stand unsealed before it. A fully sealed user sounding it deals nothing — there is nothing to sound with.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Worthy Is the Lamb

Once per long rest, when you break a seal, one ally within 30 feet gains its judgment until the end of your next turn — the conqueror's aim, the famine's scales, the starfall, lent, not given. You still lose the protection; the cost is yours alone. The judgment was never only for you. Seals, but shared.

The Intercession

Reaction · 2 CE · When an ally within 60 feet drops to 0 HP, the martyrs cry out on their behalf: the ally stabilizes at 1 HP and gains temporary HP equal to 5 × the number of seals you have broken. The slain do not only demand justice. Sometimes they plead for the living. Judgment, but as mercy.

Sound It Again

Once per short rest, as a bonus action, invoke one broken seal's judgment a second time — call down another falling star, pulse the famine aura, loose the conqueror's volley again. The seals were opened once. Nothing says the judgments only sound once. A seal, but as an echo.

The Sealed Scroll

Once per long rest, when damage would drop you to 0 HP, the next unbroken seal shatters on its own — you drop to 1 HP instead, immediately lose its protection, and gain its judgment. The scroll protects its reader the way scripture protects no one: by continuing. Seals, but as a last resort.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: Weapon attacks enhanced by sealed blows can use the campaign's Black Flash procedure when otherwise eligible (the blow is part of the attack). Save-effect damage (falling stars, The Seventh Trumpet) cannot critically hit or Black Flash.
Generic Maximum Output applies only to ordinary features, never to The Seventh Trumpet — the trumpet's scaling is the seals broken, not CE spent.
Seal order: Seals break strictly in order 1st through 7th; you cannot skip to a later seal. If an effect would break multiple seals, they break in order.
Between fights: All seals restore at the end of a long rest, and the ward returns in full. The judgments of a broken seal end when the seal is restored.
Antimagic: An intact ward is a technique effect and can be suppressed like one; broken seals cannot be "restored" by any means short of a long rest.
