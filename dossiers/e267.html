At-the-table character reference

Recovered printed values & campaign clarifications

Madness:

Madness Inducement: at 1st level, you can inflict Madness. As an action, you can spend 5 cursed energy to target any number of creatures that can see you within 120 feet. They must make a Wisdom saving throw against your cursed energy DC. On a success, they take no Madness and are immune to Madness Inducement for 24 hours. On a failure, they take 1d6 Madness. On a critical failure, they take 3d6 Madness. At the beginning of a creature's turn, they must make a Wisdom saving throw. On a success, they halve their Madness, on a failure, they take psychic damage equal to their Madness and add 1d6 to their Madness. They can choose to become blinded, deafened or restrained, reducing their Madness to 0 and being immune to Madness Inducement for 24 hours. If a creature reaches 50 Madness, they go berserk. Berserk creatures are blinded and deafened to everyone except their target. They can't use ranged attacks, have advantage on Strength saving throws, attack rolls and are immune to the frightened condition. Berserk creatures also have disadvantage on all saving throws except Strength. If a creature remains berserk for 3 turns, they suffer 1 level of exhaustion. A creature's Madness resets to 0 on a long rest. Madness Enhancement: at 3rd level, the Madness you inflict increases. They take 2d6 Madness instead of 1d6 and 5d6 instead of 3d6 on a critical failure. When a creature becomes berserk, they also gain 1 level of exhaustion. Obsession and Insanity: at 6th level, your madness becomes contagious. A creature that fails their Madness saving throw at the beginning of their turn also gives 1d6 Madness to all creatures within 30 feet. Additionally, berserk creatures gain 1 level of exhaustion every turn instead of every 3 turns. Maximum Rumination: at 11th level, your madness becomes unstoppable. As a bonus action, you can spend 5 cursed energy to gain a glimpse of the Faceless God, and inflict corruption on all creatures within 30 feet. For each point of corruption, a creature has disadvantage on all their saving throws. In addition, at the beginning of a creature's turn, they make a Charisma saving throw against your cursed energy DC. On a success, they take no damage and are immune to Corruption for 24 hours. On a failure, they take 1d10 psychic damage per 10 corruption and gain 1d6 corruption. This increases to 2d6 at 14th level, and 3d6 at 17th level. If a creature fails the saving throw three times, they become an aberration with your DM's approval. A long rest can be used to remove corruption. They must make a Wisdom saving throw against your cursed energy DC, removing all corruption on a success, and gaining half as much on a failure. However, on a failure, the corruption gained cannot exceed 25 corruption. Domain Expansion: Theia Mania: at 18th level, you have mastered your technique, allowing you to expand your domain. Within your domain, all creatures gain 2d6 Madness when entering your domain for the first time or starting their turn there, and they make all attack rolls and saving throws with a -2. If more than half of the creatures are berserk or corrupted, all creatures must make a Wisdom saving throw against your cursed energy DC. On a success, they take 10d10 psychic damage. On a failure, they take 20d10 psychic damage.

If a creature fails the saving throw three times, they become an aberration with your DM's approval. A long rest can be used to remove corruption. They must make a Wisdom saving throw against your cursed energy DC, removing all corruption on a success, and gaining half as much on a failure. However, on a failure, the corruption gained cannot exceed 25 corruption.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it.

Prerequisite: Must have Madness Inducement

Veil of the Faceless God

You have learned to see through the cracks your madness opens. When a creature gains Madness from your technique, you can spend 1 CE as a reaction to peer through the fracture — until the end of your next turn, you can see and hear through that creature's senses.

Prerequisite: Must have Madness Enhancement

Hands of Madness

You have learned to use your Madness to enhance your unarmed strikes. You gain 1d6 Madness when you hit a creature with an unarmed strike. Additionally, your unarmed strikes deal an additional 1d6 psychic damage for every 10 Madness the target has.

Prerequisite: Must have Obsession and Insanity

True Mania

You have learned to aim the madness you unleash. When a creature becomes berserk from your Madness, you can spend 2 CE to name its obsession — you choose which creature the berserk creature perceives as its target.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Activation targets: any number means up to CE modifier (campaign ruling); expose at most your CE modifier creatures. Exposure is visual — targets that can see you, not that hear you. Track once per-turn for each source separately. Berserk creatures are exempt from receiving new Madness.
Threshold penalties: thresholds at 10, 25, 40 (campaign fill: INT -2, INT and WIS -2, all ability checks -5) replace earlier values, not stack. Berserk ends at 50+. Madness loss is a reduction to current total, never below 0. Corruption is reduced by half separately.
Corruption floors: use floor((current corruption − 1) / 25) × 25, minimum 0. A long-rest success lowers the floor by 25. Only the Faceless God corruption from this technique uses the floor; Veil resistance is separate.
Rumination damage: floor(corruption/10)d10 psychic; success takes no damage. Success removes all corruption and grants 24 hours immunity to Corruption. A failed Rumination turn advances the transformation counter.
50% rule: "more than half" means 55%+, not 50% — per campaign convention for majority phrasing. Corrupted for the Domain's catastrophe check counts creatures with corruption greater than 0.
Domain penalty: the Domain's -2 on attacks and saves replaces the lesser Threshold penalties for the same roll; AC is separate. Madness from Domain and Obsession do not stack in the same trigger window.
Hands of Madness: both of your unarmed strikes share one corruption-gain-per-turn limit; a failed strike still carries damage. Your Madness gain from the hit is limited to the actual strike, not an area.
True Mania: adds one or two conditional current exhaustion levels when a creature (including you) becomes berserk — not repeated new levels. The 1d6 psychic is per attack roll.
