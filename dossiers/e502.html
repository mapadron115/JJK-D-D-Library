Cursed Spirit — Field Entry

Hell Hound

Field notes. Subject: the pursuit itself, given a body. Three truths govern the Hell Hound. First: its nose makes distance a rumor. Once it has your scent, invisibility, masking effects, and every mundane trick cease to function, it smells cursed energy itself, and the trail cannot be lost by ordinary means. Mark this: you do not outrun it. Second: it fights with the whole weight of the chase. The bite drags armored opponents down and pins them; the claw hurls its target fifteen feet; the pounce turns twenty feet of straight road into advantage. Third: it has no second form, no subtlety, no guile, which is precisely why it is never lured into one. Assess it correctly: the hound does not corner prey. The hound is the corner.

Classification & Risk

An Imaginary Cursed Spirit, Grade 2 (CR 4–7 band). Brutally direct in combat: fangs that drag armored opponents down, claws that launch targets meters through the air. Lacks subtlety; does not need it.

Stats

Ability scores & traits

Passive · Scent of Cursed Energy: advantage on Wisdom (Perception) smell checks; once it has a creature's scent, invisibility and masking don't hide it, and the trail can't be lost by mundane means.

STR 20 (+5) · DEX 16 (+3) · CON 18 (+4) · INT 6 (−2) · WIS 16 (+3) · CHA 10 (+0). HP 105 (12d10 + 36), AC 15, speed 50 ft. Skills Perception +7 (scent). Scent of Cursed Energy: advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks relying on smell; once it has a creature’s scent, invisibility and masking effects do not hide that creature from it, and it cannot lose the trail by mundane means.

Risk

Special

Hiding, masking one’s presence, or retreating through complex terrain offers little protection once it has locked on. Do not run to escape it — run to reach ground where you can fight it.

Campaign canon notes

Field rule — perception: At Grade 2 and above this spirit’s cursed energy is dense enough that no Invisibility trait is assigned; standard perception applies. It remains invisible to ordinary non-sorcerers.
