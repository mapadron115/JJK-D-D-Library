Completed rules

  • The gavel is your CE focus and counts as magical. When summoned, choose light hammer (1d4, light, thrown 20/60), maul (2d6, heavy, two-handed), or pole hammer (1d10, heavy, reach, two-handed). Change form as a bonus action. Its first summon after each long rest is free; later summons cost 1 CE.
  • Starting a trial: use the current Domain action and CE rules and choose one creature caught inside. No creature can make attacks, deal damage, force movement, or activate an effect intended to harm another while court is in session; an attempted offensive action simply fails. The trial resolves immediately, then the barrier ends.
  • The GM chooses one meaningful act the defendant actually committed and states the charge. If no such act exists, the verdict is innocent. Judgeman produces evidence; roll on the table below. Roll one evidence die normally, two at level 11, and three at level 18, choosing the result you prefer. The caster sees the evidence, while the defendant hears the charge but not the evidence.
  • The defendant chooses silence, denial, confession, or explanation and may briefly state a case. Then make a trial contest: you roll a CE ability check with PB; the defendant rolls Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) or Intelligence (Investigation), matching its argument. A knowing lie uses Deception. The evidence modifies the contest. Ties favor the defendant.
  • On acquittal, nothing is confiscated. A guilty verdict for a nonlethal offense imposes Confiscation. A deliberate killing, attempted mass killing, or comparably grave offense imposes Confiscation and the Death Penalty when you win by 5 or more; otherwise it imposes Confiscation.
  • Confiscation priority: Innate Technique; if none, CE use; if none, one attuned magic item; if none, one class feature chosen by Judgeman. The defendant cannot use the confiscated source until you dismiss the sentence, die, or a later trial acquits it. An item becomes mundane but remains physically present.
  • Death Penalty: your gavel becomes the Executioner’s Sword. A hit against the convicted creature kills it; against any other target it is a mundane longsword. It vanishes when the sentence ends or the convicted creature dies.
  • Gavel Improvements: summoning costs no action. Large form doubles the weapon’s base dice; Huge triples them. Once per turn, a Huge gavel attack can instead target a 10-foot square: creatures there make a Dexterity save, taking the weapon’s damage on failure or half on success.
  • Unstopping Justice: your first CE modifier uses per long rest require no CE, minimum one. Further uses pay the normal Domain cost. This does not bypass Domain Burnout.

Power identity

Why Tier IV: the technique is intentionally procedural and may permanently remove a core power or create an instant-kill weapon. The revised trial uses the fiction’s real facts instead of randomly inventing guilt.

Recovered character-build options

Innate Technique Feats 3

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it.

Tool Versatility

Standard

Prerequisite: Judge’s Gavel

As a bonus action, reshape the gavel into any melee weapon. You are proficient with it and it uses that weapon’s damage and properties; no ammunition, consumable, or attached special-grade property is created. It returns to gavel form when released.

Weight of Judgment

High impact

Prerequisite: Judge’s Gavel

Once per turn after a gavel hit, spend 2 CE to roll one additional set of the weapon’s base damage dice. The target also makes a Strength save, falling prone on failure. The added dice are not doubled by another amplifier.

Judgeman Release

Pinnacle

Prerequisite: Deadly Sentencing

Bonus action, 5 CE, Technique Focus for 1 minute: summon intangible Judgeman and designate one perceived opponent within 60 feet. Every time that opponent attacks, its cumulative assault penalty increases by 1. Subtract the full penalty from its attack rolls and saving throws until Judgeman leaves; there is no added charge cap.

At 1st level, once per long rest, you may summon a magical gavel for

Domain Expansion: Deadly Sentencing
Beginning at 3rd level, your prodigiousness allows you to expand
your domain expansion. While in this domain, no offensive actions can
be taken by either party. The creature has to make a persuasion check
against you to prove themselves not guilty. You gain a piece of
evidence, the quality of the evidence, and if the person is guilty is
determined by a d20 roll. The table for the roll is listed below:
Confiscation: The target loses either class abilities or any magic
items they have on them.
Death Penalty: Your Judge’s Gavel is replaced by the Executioner’s
Sword which kills the accused on hit.

Gavel Improvements
Starting at 6th level, your mastery over Judge’s Gavel has improved.
You can now re-summon your gavel as a free action, and increase the
size of the gavel up to huge, also increasing damage dice and the
area of effect.
Better Evidence
At 11th level, when rolling for evidence you may roll two dice and
pick the one you find more beneficial. At 18th level, this becomes 3
dice.
Unstopping Justice
Finally, at 18th level, you have reached the peak of your technique,
showing off to others that only with skill you can truly win. You can
now use Deadly Sentencing a number of times equal to your CE modifier
before needing to spend Cursed Energy.
