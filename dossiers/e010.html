Deadly Sentencing

A nonviolent trial Domain that judges an actual offense, confiscates power, and may issue an Executioner’s Sword.

The fiction. Jujutsu scholars classify Deadly Sentencing as a verdict made manifest, the user's cursed energy could not abide an unjust trial, so it built a courtroom out of itself. Registry notes emphasize that the Domain cannot deal damage because the technique is not an attack: it is one person's refusal to punish what has not been proven, enforced by an energy so certain of its procedure that violence simply fails inside it.

Field notes. Subject does not fight. He convenes. Within his domain no blade may be lifted, I watched two armed sorcerers halt mid-motion, as if the air itself had taken offense. His energy could not abide an unjust verdict, so it raised a courtroom from nothing. Understand: this is not an attack. It is a proceeding. And in a proceeding, the only blade is guilt.

Starting a trial

Domain action · Choose one creature caught inside the Domain · While court is in session: no creature can make attacks, deal damage, force movement, or activate an effect intended to harm another; attempted offensive actions simply fail · The trial resolves immediately, then the barrier ends.

He speaks, and the battlefield holds its breath. Blades, techniques, harm, refused. The court assembles; every raised fist becomes a statue. I have catalogued many shields. This is the only one that does not turn the spear aside, but persuades the arm never to throw it. Use the current Domain action and CE rules and choose one creature caught inside. The charge and evidence

Passive · The GM names one meaningful act the defendant actually committed as the charge; if none exists, the verdict is innocent · Judgeman produces evidence (roll on the table): the caster sees the evidence, the defendant hears only the charge.

Judgeman names what was truly done, not suspected, done, and lays its proof upon the table. He beholds the evidence; the accused hears only the charge, and must answer blind. One side has read the last page before the trial begins. That side never loses the opening. The GM chooses one meaningful act the defendant actually committed and states the charge. If no such act exists, the verdict is innocent. Judgeman produces evidence; roll on the evidence table in the Reference section below. The caster sees the evidence, while the defendant hears the charge but not the evidence.

The contest

Passive · The defendant chooses silence, denial, confession, or explanation and states a brief case · Trial contest: your CE ability check with PB vs the defendant's Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) or Intelligence (Investigation), matching its argument; the evidence modifies the contest · Ties favor the defendant.

Silence, denial, confession, explanation, the defendant chooses his ground, and then the trial is waged with words and will. Eloquence is armor here; guilt, a wound no bandage reaches. And if the scales hang level, the accused walks free: even this court bows to doubt. The defendant chooses silence, denial, confession, or explanation and may briefly state a case. Then make a trial contest: you roll a CE ability check with PB; the defendant rolls Charisma (Deception or Persuasion) or Intelligence (Investigation), matching its argument. A knowing lie uses Deception. The evidence modifies the contest. Ties favor the defendant.

Verdicts

Passive · Acquittal: nothing confiscated · Guilty, nonlethal offense: Confiscation · Deliberate killing, attempted mass killing, or comparably grave offense: Confiscation + Death Penalty if you win the contest by 5 or more, else Confiscation.

Judgeman knows neither mercy nor cruelty, only the turning of the wheel. The sentence follows the crime as night follows day: perfectly certain, and therefore perfectly terrible to the guilty. On acquittal, nothing is confiscated. A guilty verdict for a nonlethal offense imposes Confiscation. A deliberate killing, attempted mass killing, or comparably grave offense imposes Confiscation and the Death Penalty when you win by 5 or more; otherwise it imposes Confiscation.

Confiscation

Passive · Confiscation priority: Innate Technique, then CE use, then one attuned magic item, then one class feature chosen by Judgeman · The confiscated source cannot be used until you dismiss the sentence, die, or a later trial acquits it · An item becomes mundane but remains physically present.

The sentence strips the guilty of what they sinned with: technique, energy, relic, training. A sorcerer parted from his technique is a general without an army, titles, and nothing to command. The instructive punishment: it shows every witness what the power truly was, by showing what remains when it is gone. Priority: Innate Technique; if none, CE use; if none, one attuned magic item; if none, one class feature chosen by Judgeman. The defendant cannot use the confiscated source until you dismiss the sentence, die, or a later trial acquits it. An item becomes mundane but remains physically present.

Death Penalty

Passive · Your gavel becomes the Executioner's Sword: a hit against the convicted creature kills it; against any other target it is a mundane longsword · Vanishes when the sentence ends or the convicted creature dies.

For the grave verdict, the gavel lengthens into a sword that ends the condemned with a touch, and is a common longsword to all others. One true target. Against it there is no defense but innocence... which is also its only boundary. Your gavel becomes the Executioner’s Sword. A hit against the convicted creature kills it; against any other target it is a mundane longsword. It vanishes when the sentence ends or the convicted creature dies.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier IV: the technique is intentionally procedural and may permanently remove a core power or create an instant-kill weapon. The revised trial uses the fiction’s real facts instead of randomly inventing guilt.

Domain Expansion: Deadly Sentencing

A courtroom assembles from nothing, wood paneling and a judge's bench and a single harsh light that admits no shadow to hide in. The scales on the wall are balanced and will not stay that way, and the air carries the particular stillness of a room where someone's fate is being decided by strangers. There is no jury. There was never going to be a jury.
