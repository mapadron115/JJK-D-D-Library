What the technique actually does.

Character engine

Grow a finite Tail Magazine. Fire specialized quills at range, then close with the lion's maw to recover ammunition, creating a deliberate volley→feed→disengage rhythm.

Tail Magazine: holds up to 10 Spines; regain all expended Spines on a long rest.

Technique save DC: 8 + PB + chosen CE ability modifier. Technique attack: PB + chosen CE ability modifier.

Field notes. Subject: predatory thrift, weaponized. Three truths govern the Ballistics. First: ammunition is finite because the body is finite. The Tail Magazine holds ten Spines, regained on a long rest — the quill is not a weapon but a budget, and the technique is built around that honesty. Second: the rhythm is volley, feed, disengage. Fire the specialized quills at range — Barbed to slow, Heavy to push — then close with the Lion's Maw to recover Spines from the bitten, the dead, the incapacitated; a predator that cannot eat after the hunt does not hunt again. Third: the payloads are the vocabulary. At higher mastery, two-Spine loads — Venom to poison, Screamer to steal reactions, Tether to anchor movement — and the ricochet that ignores cover off hard surfaces. Maximum: the Sky Black With Quills — six to ten Spines spent in one convulsion, quills embedding three-deep on the failed, hazards seeded across the field. The registry calls it the most disciplined hunger in the codex. Mark this: every spine spent is a spine the sorcerer must earn back with his own teeth.

Full progression

Level progression

1Tail Volley

The budget, opened. Replace one attack with a ranged technique attack — 80/160, one Spine: 1 plus CE modifier piercing, the quill embeds, Barbed to slow or Heavy to push — and embedded quills tax speed until torn free. Bonus action, once per turn: Rattle the Barbs, and every quilled creature tests or stands frozen. The volley is not the hunt. It is the invoice. You can replace one of your attacks with a Tail Volley: a ranged technique attack (range 80/160) that spends 1 Spine; on a hit it deals 1 + your CE modifier piercing, the quill embeds (max 3 per creature), and choose a quill type: Barbed (the target’s speed is reduced by 10 while quilled) or Heavy (push the target 5 feet). Embedded quills: the target’s speed is reduced by 5 feet per embedded quill; a creature can use an action to tear free all its embedded quills, or remove them safely with an action and a healer’s kit (DC 15 Medicine). Rattle the Barbs: bonus action, once per turn, no CE — each creature holding your embedded quills makes a Constitution save vs your technique DC; failure: its speed is 0 until the end of its next turn; success: no effect.

3Lion's Maw

The collection. A melee technique attack costing no Spine — 2 plus CE modifier — with advantage if the target was quilled since the last turn, recovering 1 Spine on a hit; or forgo the recovery to Worry the barbs deeper and frighten. The maw is the technique's whole economy: the teeth that pay for the quills. Your Lion's Maw is a melee technique attack that costs no Spine; on a hit it deals 2 + your CE modifier piercing. If the target was hit by one of your Spines since your last turn, you have advantage on the attack; on a hit you recover 1 Spine. Worry: if the target holds embedded quills, you may forgo the Spine recovery — the target makes a Constitution save; failure: it is frightened of you until the end of its next turn as the maw works the barbs deeper.

6Three-Quill Load

The vocabulary expands. Two-Spine payloads: Venom poisons without damage, Screamer steals reactions, Tether anchors movement within fifteen feet until torn free. The manticore was always a scholar of the hunt; now it lectures in three dialects. You unlock 2-Spine payloads for your quills: Venom (the target makes a Constitution save; failure: it is poisoned until the end of your next turn, no damage), Screamer (the target cannot take reactions until the start of your next turn), and Tether (the target’s movement is anchored within 15 feet of the impact point until it uses an action to tear free; tearing free also removes its embedded quills).

11Predator's Geometry

The angle, weaponized. Once per turn, ricochet a quill off a hard surface to ignore cover — two traced segments within range — and a missed ricochet's Spine embeds in the surface, recoverable. The battlefield's walls have joined the magazine. Mark this: cover is a rumor the predator no longer believes. Once per turn, you may ricochet a quill off a hard surface to ignore cover, if two traced segments remain within range. A missed ricochet's Spine embeds in the surface and can be recovered.

18Maximum: Sky Black With Quills

The whole budget, spent at once. Action, 6 CE, once per long rest, six to ten Spines: every creature in thirty feet saves — failure embeds three quills and steals reactions, success one quill — and PB embedded-quill hazards seed the field. The sky goes black. The ledger is empty. The hunt, for one convulsion, has no throttle. Activating the Maximum costs an action (invented). You spend 6 CE (once per long rest) and consume 6–10 Spines: each creature in a 30-foot radius makes a Dexterity save; failure: 3 quills embed (up to the per-creature maximum) and it cannot take reactions until the end of your next turn; success: 1 quill embeds, no reaction loss. You then place a number of embedded-quill hazards equal to your PB in unoccupied 5-foot spaces within 30 feet of you; the hazards last 1 minute (invented); the first hostile to enter a hazard’s space has a quill embed in it.

Innate Technique Feats

Innate Technique Feats

4 optional build choices

Compound Payload

Two dialects in one quill. Spend 2 Spines on a single Tail Volley and combine two payload effects — the Barbed Venom quill, the Tethered Screamer. The manticore's thrift was never about spending less. It was about making every spending count twice. When you spend 2 Spines on a single Tail Volley attack, you may combine two payload effects on that attack, such as a Barbed Venom quill, instead of choosing only one.

Scorpion Tail

The venom learns the teeth. Deliver the Venom payload through the Lion's Maw — hit, spend 1 Spine, force the save. The bite was always the honest part of the hunt; now it carries the quill's subtler arguments. You may deliver your Venom payload through your Lion’s Maw instead of a quill. When you hit with Lion’s Maw, you may spend 1 Spine to force the target to make the Venom saving throw.

Quill Catcher

The magazine accepts donations. Once per round, when a ranged weapon attack misses: reaction, snatch it from the air, recover 1 Spine — and immediately fire it back as a Tail Volley without spending. The predator's thrift extends to the enemy's ammunition. Their misses are his resupply. Once per round, when a ranged weapon attack misses you, you may use your reaction to snatch the projectile from the air and add it to your Tail Magazine, recovering 1 Spine. If you do, you may immediately make a Tail Volley attack against a creature within range without spending a Spine, firing the caught missile back. This return shot follows the normal Tail Volley rules but cannot apply payload effects.

Hunter's Mouth

The carrion clause. Bite an object, the dead, or the incapacitated with the Maw: recover 2 Spines instead of 1. The technique makes no moral distinction about the meal. A spine is a spine, and the magazine does not ask where dinner came from. When you hit an object or a dead or incapacitated creature with Lion’s Maw, you recover 2 Spines instead of 1.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Counterplay Force the finite magazine empty, deny safe recovery with Lion's Maw, and exploit cover that cannot be solved by a simple ricochet.
