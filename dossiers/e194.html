Red Lioness Fever

Sekhmet inspiration. Consecutive damaging turns build Heat 0–5. Heat grants speed and damage but at 4+ also burns the user, creating a deliberate escalation/cooling decision.

Core law

Character engine

Consecutive damaging turns build Heat 0–5. Heat grants speed and damage but at 4+ also burns the user, creating a deliberate escalation/cooling decision.

Technique save DC: 8 + PB + chosen CE ability modifier. Technique attack: PB + chosen CE ability modifier.

Field notes. Subject: a berserker's fever that climbs with every consecutive blow and burns its own bearer. Three truths govern the Fever. First: escalation is a decision, not an accident. Heat builds 0 to 5 on consecutive damaging turns — speed, then fire on the first hit — but at 4+ the body begins to burn itself; every turn past that is a wager the sorcerer places against their own flesh. Second: the fire was taught mercy exactly once. The Blood-Red Draught — the red drink that once stopped the slaughter — still cools the Heat, still heals, still works; the technique never unlearned the taste. Third: the sun has two faces. Maximum: Lady of Slaughter locks Heat at 5 and doubles the self-burn for doubled fire; Lady of Healing locks it at 0 and turns the same energy to cleansing and mending. Mark this: the Fever's discipline is not restraint. It is knowing exactly what the fire costs, and paying it anyway.

Full progression

Level progression

1Lioness Heat

The engine and the wound, in one track. Consecutive damaging turns raise Heat 0 to 5 — speed at 1–2, fire on the first hit at 3, doubled at 5 — but at 4+ the bearer takes unavoidable damage each turn, coolable by bonus action. This is the technique's whole education: the fire feeds you until it feeds on you. Learn the difference between 3 and 4, or the lesson is written in your own skin. You track Heat from 0 to 5, built by consecutive damaging turns: gain 1 Heat at the end of each of your turns in which you dealt damage; if you end a turn without dealing damage, Heat resets to 0 (invented). At Heat 1–2 you gain +5 speed. At Heat 3 or higher, your first hit each turn deals +1d8 of fire damage (invented: the die is a d8); at Heat 5 this becomes +2d8 (invented). At Heat 4 or higher you take unavoidable damage equal to your proficiency bonus at the end of each of your turns; as a bonus action you may lower your Heat by 1.

3Breath of Pestilence

The plague, exhaled. Fifteen-foot cone, Constitution against it, fire or necrotic by choice — and the failed suffer healing reduced by PB. Mark this: the Fever's second weapon is not the flame but the denial. A wound that cannot be mended is worth two that can. As an action for 2 CE, you exhale a 15-foot cone of pestilence. Each creature in the cone makes a Constitution save, taking 2d8 of fire or necrotic damage (invented; your choice) on a failure. A creature that fails also has its healing reduced by your proficiency bonus until the end of your next turn.

6Blood-Red Draught

The one mercy the fire kept. One CE, one drink — Heat down by 3, a die of healing plus CE modifier, frightened ended; once per short rest. The registry's oldest story about this technique is that a berserker once had to be stopped by being handed something red — and the energy remembers the hand that gave it. Never underestimate a weapon that was taught gentleness. As an action (or a bonus action if you have a prepared red drink) for 1 CE, you drink to reduce your Heat by 3, regain 1d8 of hit points (invented) plus your CE modifier, and end the frightened condition on yourself. You can use this once per short rest.

11Eye of Ra

At Heat 5 the sun's gaze becomes absolute: attacks ignore fire resistance, and every turn the self-burn is taken, nearby enemies are tested by Dexterity or burned in turn. Consider — the Fever's peak is not immunity to its own cost but sharing it. The desert sun does not choose who it burns. While you are at Heat 5, your attacks ignore fire resistance. Whenever you take your Heat self-damage, each enemy within 10 feet of you makes a Dexterity save or takes 1 die of fire damage.

18Maximum: Lady of Slaughter, Lady of Healing

The sun's two faces, worn at will. Slaughter locks Heat at 5, doubles the self-damage, and doubles the fire on the first two hits — the furnace with the door torn off. Mercy locks Heat at 0 and turns the action to healing and cleansing — the same energy, the other face. Three rounds. One choice, made in the open, with the whole battlefield watching which lady arrives. As an action (invented), you spend 7 CE for a 3-round Maximum with two modes. Slaughter locks your Heat at 5 and doubles your Heat self-damage, and your first two hits each turn deal one additional die of fire damage (invented); Mercy locks your Heat at 0 and lets you use your action to heal 3d8 + your CE modifier (invented) and cleanse poison and fear. This healing is not Reverse Cursed Technique and cannot exceed the target’s normal maximum healing from technique sources per the campaign injury rules.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Lion's Pursuit

The hunt leaves fire behind. At Heat 3+, the Dash lays a five-foot trail of flame — the lioness's passage, still burning for the next turn's trespassers. I have watched prey realize too late that the road behind the predator is as dangerous as the predator. At Heat 3+, when you take the Dash action you may leave a 5-foot-wide trail of fire along your path: creatures that enter it before the start of your next turn take 1 fire die.

Red Beer Ritual

The draught, shared. Administer the Blood-Red Draught to an adjacent willing ally — they take the healing and the cooling of fear, while your Heat still falls by 3. The ritual's cooling passes through another's body. Mark this: the Fever's mercy was always meant to be handed to someone. You may administer your Blood-Red Draught to an adjacent willing ally instead of drinking it yourself: the ally gains the healing (1 die + your CE modifier) and the end of the frightened condition, while your Heat still falls by 3 as the ritual's cooling passes through another's body. A use spent this way counts against your once-per-short-rest limit, and the ally must drink as part of your action. You cannot split a single draught's effects between two creatures.

Fever Claw

The plague, delivered by hand. One CE on a melee hit and the target's healing is reduced as though it had breathed the pestilence. The claw is the technique's shorthand — no cone, no ceremony, just the fever passed skin to skin. When you hit a creature with a melee attack, you may spend 1 CE to deliver your plague by claw: the target's healing is reduced by your PB until the end of your next turn, as with Breath of Pestilence.

Two Faces of the Sun (11+)

The Maximum's hinge. Once during its three rounds, switch between Slaughter and Mercy as a bonus action — Heat snapping to 5 or 0 to match. The sun turns its other face mid-battle. I have seen the switch end fights: the enemy braces for the furnace and meets the healer, or braces for the healer and meets the furnace. Once during your Maximum's 3-round duration, you may switch between Slaughter and Mercy as a bonus action, the sun turning its other face. Your Heat immediately becomes 5 for Slaughter or 0 for Mercy to match the new mode, and the spent round still counts toward the duration. You cannot switch again for the rest of the Maximum.

Rules integration

Counterplay

Deny consecutive damaging turns and make the user choose between Heat momentum and self-preservation.
