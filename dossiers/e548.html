Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the accruing strike. When you take the Attack action, you may replace one attack with Principal: 1 CE — make a melee CE attack; on a hit, deal 1d8 bludgeoning damage plus your accrual. Accrual equals the number of combat rounds that have elapsed since this combat began (round 1: +0, round 2: +1, round 6: +5). It is not a pool — the table already counts rounds; the technique merely charges interest on them. When combat ends, the ledger closes and the accrual resets to +0.

Field notes. Three truths from the ledger. First: every fight is a loan the universe extends you, and the interest is quoted in bruises — the first round is always the cheapest and always the most expensive, because everything you survive early is principal for what comes later; the old fighters who carry this technique walk into every brawl already doing arithmetic. Second: your enemy can read the ledger too — anyone who has seen this technique once knows exactly what the tenth round looks like, which is why they will try to end you in the third; the technique's first lesson is that the most dangerous thing in the room is a patient man with a plan. Third: interest does not negotiate. It does not care about your stance, your guard, or your pride — it cares only that you are still standing when the round ticks over. Mark the economy: time is the only currency the technique accepts, and it accepts it from everyone.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1Principal

When you take the Attack action, you may replace one attack with Principal: 1 CE · melee CE attack, 1d8 bludgeoning + your accrual on a hit. Accrual = elapsed combat rounds (round 1: +0, round 6: +5, round 11: +10). The ledger closes when combat ends.

The bread and butter, folded into the attacks you were making anyway: weak at the bell, heavier every round. Round one you are a nuisance. Round six you are a problem. Round eleven you are the reason the fight is still going — or the reason it is about to stop.

3Draw It Out

Bonus action · 1 CE · Gain the benefits of the Dodge action until the start of your next turn. You are not fighting — you are billing hours.

The new verb: L1 is offense that needs time, L3 buys the time. The technique was blind to its own early weakness — every round you spend dodging is a round the ledger grows, and the enemy knows it, which is exactly why they cannot afford to let you. Ask what it couldn't do: survive itself. Now it can.

6Compounding

Passive · Principal now deals 2d8 bludgeoning + your accrual, and the accrual doubles: +2 per elapsed round instead of +1. Bonus action · 2 CE · Early Withdrawal: your next Principal strike this turn deals bonus damage equal to twice your current accrual — then the accrual resets to +0 for the rest of this combat. You spent the interest.

The system deepens and the foundation sharpens with it: harder principal, faster interest, and the first real decision the technique ever offers you — cash out now for certain damage, or let it ride toward something apocalyptic. The ledger does not advise. It only keeps score.

11Maturity

Action · 8 CE · Once per short rest · For 1 minute, every elapsed combat round counts as two for your accrual. At round 8 with Maturity active, your accrual is +32.

High power, not a finisher: the limiter is the short-rest gate, not the CE — you get one matured minute per rest, and it doesn't end the fight by itself. It changes the terms: every round is suddenly worth two, the ledger accelerates past what the enemy budgeted for, and the tenth round arrives wearing the eleventh's clothes.

18The Ledger Never Forgets

Passive · Your accrual no longer resets when you use Early Withdrawal — you keep the interest and spend it too. Additionally, the first round of every combat counts as three elapsed rounds for your accrual. You arrive already invested.

Enablement, not a nuke: the technique stops charging you for its own decisions. Withdraw early without penalty, open every fight with the ledger already warm — you are very difficult to handle now, not because any single blow is the biggest on the field, but because the arithmetic was finished before the enemy's first swing.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

You stop moving. The air goes still in the particular way it does before a debt comes due — every eye in the room does the same calculation at the same time, and every face arrives at the same number. You have been counting since the first bell. They have been bleeding since the second. The ledger is closed.

Maximum Technique, Total Liquidation (action, 10 CE): make a Principal strike. On a hit, it deals bonus damage equal to triple your current accrual — then the accrual resets to +0. The irreplaceable price is the investment itself: the whole fight's interest, spent in one blow. At round 10 with Compounding, that is +54 on top of the dice. Nothing about this is casual.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Living Off the Interest

Reaction · 2 CE · When you take damage, reduce it by your current accrual (minimum 1). The interest pays for your safety now — the principal can wait. Compounding, but as defense.

Short Sell

Bonus action · 2 CE · Mark one creature for 1 minute. The first time each round it deals damage, reduce that damage by half the elapsed combat rounds (rounded down, maximum your PB). You are betting against their power — and time is on your side, not theirs. Compounding, but inverted.

Compound Fracture

Passive · When your accrual is +5 or higher, creatures hit by your Principal strikes have their speed reduced by 10 feet until the end of their next turn. Everything gets heavier the longer it goes — including them. Accrual, but as control.

Trust Fund

Bonus action · 3 CE · One ally within 30 feet gains your current accrual as a bonus to their next damage roll before the end of your next turn. The interest was always going to be spent — it might as well be spent on family. Compounding, but for someone else.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: Direct rolled technique attacks (Principal) can use the campaign's Black Flash procedure when otherwise eligible. Total Liquidation's triple-accrual bonus is bonus damage and cannot critically hit or Black Flash beyond the base strike.
Generic Maximum Output applies only to ordinary features, never to Total Liquidation.
Accrual counts combat rounds as tracked by initiative — a round elapses when every combatant has acted. Rounds before you joined the combat do not count. The ledger closes when combat ends or all hostiles are defeated or fled.
Early Withdrawal and Total Liquidation both reset the accrual to +0 (L18's Ledger Never Forgets exempts Early Withdrawal only — Total Liquidation always closes the ledger).
