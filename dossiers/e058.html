Core law

What the technique actually does.

Rules are written to plug into the finalized campaign glossary: normal actions stay normal, direct attacks roll, and only named Domain events bypass the initial roll.

Calculated threshold

Solvency

For Hakoware, a creature’s Solvency equals maximum HP + 5 × maximum CE. A creature with no CE still uses maximum HP. Recalculate only when a long-duration effect changes those maxima, not every time HP/CE is spent.

On hit · 2 CE

A.P.R. — Potclean

After a direct melee hit, choose to deal no HP damage. Instead conjure A.P.R. on the target for 1 minute and add Debt equal to the damage the hit would have dealt. A.P.R. is intangible/indestructible except by technique removal or Domain rules.

Mutual conversion

Loan Combat

While A.P.R. is attached, direct damage you deal to the debtor increases Debt instead of reducing its HP. Direct damage the debtor deals to you reduces Debt instead of your HP until Debt reaches 0; excess damage after Debt hits 0 affects HP normally.

Initiative 20

Interest

At initiative 20 each round while debtor is within Hakoware range, increase Debt by 10%, rounded up. This is not damage and cannot be prevented by resistance.

Threshold

Bankruptcy

When Debt exceeds Solvency, A.P.R. becomes I.R.S. The target suffers Technique Burnout and cannot voluntarily spend CE for the listed duration. At low level: PB rounds. It can still use mundane/class features not requiring CE.

When the Domain opens, choose competitors. Each hostile automatically receives an A.P.R. with opening Debt equal 2 × your level + PB. Violence is not forbidden; it is financially reinterpreted by Loan Combat.

Pinnacle expression

Domain, Maximum, or equivalent.

Not every technique needs a Domain. When one exists, its sure-hit is a unique autonomous initiative-count-20 event unless the Domain is explicitly rule-based.

Bankruptcy Court — Chapter Seven

Level 3 · Action · 10 CE · 60-foot radius · up to 1 minute. A cartoonish financial tribunal overlays the battlefield. This is a rule-based nonlethal Domain: its procedure is the sure-hit equivalent.

On opening, chosen hostiles automatically receive A.P.R. and opening Debt equal 2 × level + PB. Every initiative 20, after clash checks, all active debts compound by 10% (or the level-11 aggressive rate). A creature whose Debt exceeds Solvency goes Bankrupt immediately.

Ordinary attacks remain possible, but Loan Combat converts creditor↔debtor damage until the debt is repaid. Anti-domain protection does not cancel the court automatically; it allows the protected creature to make a Charisma save when A.P.R. would first attach, avoiding opening Debt on success. It must still obey ordinary Loan Combat if later tagged by a normal hit.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

These rulings are designed around the revised Player Guide rather than the raw PDF where the two differ.

Debt is a contest number, not damage, so it does not interact with resistance, temp HP, criticals, or Black Flash.
Solvency uses maximum values to avoid recalculating every attack.
If the creditor becomes unconscious, A.P.R. ends and Debt disappears unless a pinnacle feature explicitly says otherwise.
Bankruptcy suppresses voluntary CE spending; passive non-CE ancestry/class traits continue.
Technique DC: 8 + proficiency bonus + your chosen Cursed Energy ability modifier (Wisdom or Charisma unless your class says otherwise).
Direct criticals: rolled weapon, unarmed, and CE attacks can critical normally. After a qualifying critical, roll the campaign's unmodified Black Flash confirmation d20; a natural 20 triples the completed critical damage. Autonomous Domain events, damage-over-time, hazards, and unrolled pulses cannot Black Flash.
Maximum Output: unless a feature says otherwise, double that feature's base CE cost and enhance one supported axis—damage, range, duration, or accuracy/DC—using the campaign's Maximum Output rules. Named Maximum Techniques and Domains do not receive generic Maximum Output.
Technique Focus: effects marked Focus occupy the shared Technique Focus slot. Opening a Domain ends other Focus effects unless the Domain expressly preserves them.
Two-technique characters: these guides assume each technique progresses from character/class level as agreed by the campaign. Multiclassing itself does not grant another Innate Technique.
