Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the answer. Full Counter is not a shield and it is not armor. It is a read, a swing, and a debt collected: an attack you can see coming can be caught on the blade and thrown back at the creature that sent it — harder than it arrived, because the Captain adds his own cursed energy to the return and calls the difference interest. The counter is a contest, not a certainty: your counter roll against their roll, your timing against their nerve. Read it right and their best attack becomes your best attack. Read it wrong and you take the hit like anyone else, two CE poorer for the lesson. Everything else in this technique is a variation on that single exchange: what can be returned is returned, what cannot be returned is erased, and what cannot be answered in the moment is kept — itemized, patient — until it can be spent all at once.

Field notes. Three truths govern the Captain's art. First: the swing is a read. Full Counter works on a sword cut and a fireball with the same motion, because the blade never cared what it was answering — only that the answer was timed. Second: interest is owed. A returned attack is never merely returned; it arrives carrying the Captain's own power on top of its own, and the stronger the attack he catches, the stronger the answer he can afford to give. Third: what he keeps, he spends. Every blow that gets through is written down somewhere behind that grin, and Revenge Counter is the grin opening the ledger. Mark the maxim: he does not fear strong opponents. Strong opponents are ammunition that walks toward him.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1Full Counter · Read the Blow

Reaction · 2 CE · When an attack roll made by a creature you can see would hit you, or a damaging effect you can perceive forces you to make a saving throw, you may counter it before it resolves: make a CE attack roll (your counter roll). Against an attack roll: if your counter roll equals or exceeds the attacker's roll, the attack is countered — you take no damage or effects from it, and the attacker takes the damage the attack would have dealt to you (rolled normally) plus amplification (below); any condition the attack would have inflicted on a hit is inflicted on the attacker instead if your counter roll would also hit the attacker's AC. Against a save-based effect: if your counter roll equals or exceeds the originator's CE save DC, the effect is countered — you are unaffected, and the originator makes the saving throw against its own effect (its own DC), taking the effect's damage plus amplification on a failure, or half that total damage and no condition on a success. If your counter roll fails, the attack or effect resolves against you normally (including your saving throw, if any); the CE is spent either way. Amplification: when you counter, you may spend up to your PB in additional CE; each additional CE adds 1d8 force damage to the returned damage. You cannot counter while surprised, incapacitated, or unable to perceive the attack or the effect's origin. Returned damage keeps its original type; the amplification dice are force.

The whole technique in one motion. Note what it is not: it is not automatic. The counter roll is the Captain's skill made mechanical — against a peer's best roll, the answer is a genuine contest, and a whiffed read costs CE and the hit. Note also what it covers: a blade and a blast are the same problem. If he can see it coming, he can send it home.

Read the Blow. Passive · You cannot be surprised while you are conscious. When a hidden or invisible attacker you are aware of makes an attack roll against you, you may still attempt Full Counter against it, making the counter roll with disadvantage.

The prerequisite of everything else: you cannot answer what you never saw. The Captain's eyes are the technique's true foundation — three thousand years of ambushes is an education, and graduating means the unseen knife is merely a harder question, not an automatic one.

3Counter Vanish · Second Helping

Counter Vanish. Action or reaction · 3 CE · As a reaction when a non-damaging cursed effect targets you, or as an action targeting one ongoing cursed effect within 30 feet, make a cursed energy ability check (Charisma + your PB) against the effect originator's CE save DC (DC 13 for an effect with no originator). On a success the effect is dissipated harmlessly — it is not returned; it simply never arrives. Counter Vanish cannot dissipate a Domain, a barrier, or a Domain's sure-hit.

The flat of the art. Some things should not be thrown back — a curse laid on a friend, a binding, a poison in the blood of the battlefield. Those he does not answer. He erases them, the way you wave away smoke: not returned, not endured, simply never arrived. It is the technique's one quiet move, and it is the one his enemies plan around least.

Second Helping. Passive · Once per round, after you use Full Counter, you may use Full Counter a second time before the start of your next turn without expending your reaction. You pay all of its costs normally.

One answer is a technique. Two is a personality. Armies learned this before single opponents did: the second attacker, the one who waited for the counter, discovers the Captain's hospitality does not run out after the first guest.

6Revenge Counter

Revenge Counter. Passive / Action · Whenever you take damage from a hostile creature's attack or effect you could perceive, add the damage taken to your Revenge total (maximum: your level × 10). You gain no Revenge from damage dealt by your own attacks, your own returned attacks, or your own effects. As an action, spend 3 CE to release it: make one melee weapon attack or melee CE attack against a creature in reach; on a hit, it deals the attack's normal damage plus force damage equal to your Revenge total. Hit or miss, your Revenge total becomes 0 after the release. It also resets to 0 when you finish a long rest.

The other half of the art, and the reason hitting him is a loan. What cannot be returned in the moment is kept — every cut, every burn, itemized behind the grin — until the moment he chooses to spend the whole account in a single swing. The gamble is real: the release empties hit or miss, and the ledger does not survive a night's sleep. Patience, here, is a resource with an expiry date.

11The Captain's Counter

The Captain's Counter. Passive / Reaction · You may use Full Counter against attacks and damaging effects targeting a creature within 30 feet of you, provided you can perceive the attack and its target; all costs and rolls are as normal. You interpose your blade, not your body: if the counter succeeds you take no damage; if it fails, the attack or effect resolves against its original target normally. Your amplification dice become 1d12 instead of 1d8. When your counter roll exceeds the triggering roll or DC by 10 or more, the return is critical: double all damage the counter returns. Second Helping improves: you may use Full Counter up to two additional times per round without expending your reaction.

The counter grows a perimeter. High power, deliberately not a finisher: three or four resourced answers in a round can genuinely endanger an attacker, because every swing at his friends is now also a swing at themselves, amplified, and sometimes — when the read is perfect — doubled. He named it honestly. It is not the Captain's defense. It is the Captain's counter, and the difference is who ends up bleeding.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

He stops smiling first — that is the warning, and it has never once been heeded in time. The blade comes up loose in his hand, almost lazy, and the next attack simply goes home wrong, and the next, and the next, each one answered in the same unhurried motion, until the battlefield understands it is no longer throwing attacks at a man. It is mailing them to itself.

Maximum: Absolute Counter. Bonus action · 15 CE · Once per long rest · 1 minute · While Absolute Counter lasts: Full Counter costs 1 CE; using it does not require your reaction (you may counter any number of times per round, paying CE and making a counter roll for each); your counter rolls have advantage; your amplification limit increases to twice your PB in additional CE; and Counter Vanish costs 1 CE. When Absolute Counter ends, you cannot use Full Counter until the end of your next turn — even the Captain's arm needs one breath to remember it is his.

The pinnacle is enablement, not a bigger number: for one minute the reaction economy stops applying to the one creature it was invented to restrain. He is not faster. He is simply finished deciding — every attack that enters his sight is already answered, and the only remaining question is whether his cursed energy outlasts their courage. Armies, historically, have run out first.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Prerequisite: The Captain's Counter

Cover the Sin

Your protective counters reach 60 feet instead of 30. When you successfully counter an attack or effect targeting another creature, that creature gains temporary hit points equal to your Charisma modifier + your proficiency bonus. The Captain has stood between his people and the sky for three thousand years. The sky has never once gotten through.

Prerequisite: Read the Blow

Read and Riposte

When a creature misses you with a melee attack, your next counter roll against that creature before the end of your next turn has advantage, and if that counter succeeds, the damage it returns ignores the attacker's resistances. A miss is a confession, read aloud.

Prerequisite: Revenge Counter

Interest on Pain

Your Revenge maximum becomes your level × 15, releasing Revenge Counter costs 1 less CE (minimum 1), and while your Revenge total is 50 or higher your speed increases by 10 feet. Pain, held long enough, becomes posture.

Prerequisite: Counter Vanish

Vanish Point

Once per long rest, when you use Counter Vanish against a non-damaging effect that would impose a condition on you, you may reflect it instead of dissipating it: the effect's originator makes the saving throw against its own effect (its own DC); on a failure, it suffers the condition as if it had targeted itself. What he cannot throw back, he files under 'later.'

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Ties answer. A counter roll that equals the triggering attack roll or CE save DC succeeds — meets it, beats it. The Captain's timing is the whole technique; a dead-even read goes to the reader.
One answer per attack. A single attack or effect can be countered only once, by one creature. If Full Counter and another counter effect (such as e509 Shattered Mirror's return) could both answer the same attack, the first declared counter resolves; the attack is then spent and cannot be answered again.
The Mirror is a different art. e509 Shattered Mirror returns ranged and cursed attacks automatically at their original strength and shatters against the physical. Full Counter is an active, contested swing: it can fail, it answers the physical and the magical alike, and it returns with interest. Neither is a stronger version of the other; they are opposite answers to the same question.
Lostvayne synergy. A clone created by e095 Demon Sword Lostvayne inherits this technique as it inherits any other. A clone's counter roll uses your CE attack bonus, but per Clone Output the damage a clone's counter returns — including amplification dice — is reduced to 25% (33% during Thousand Reflections), and a clone cannot activate Absolute Counter. Four Captains answer at quarter strength; the Captain primes at full.
Revenge bookkeeping. Revenge is gained only from damage dealt by creatures hostile to you, from attacks or effects you could perceive at the moment they dealt damage. Damage over time from an effect you perceived when it was applied counts once, when first applied — ongoing ticks do not keep feeding the ledger.
Absolute Counter and reactions. During Absolute Counter, Full Counter and Counter Vanish do not consume your reaction, but each use still costs its CE and requires its roll or check. Second Helping is redundant during the Maximum and grants no further benefit until it ends.
