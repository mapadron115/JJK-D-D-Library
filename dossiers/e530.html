The Dispensary

Name any mundane liquid and receive it, hot or cold, in a cup. The Office broke something in him getting here; his grief comes out as beverages.

The Dispensary

He does not remember the acquisition. He remembers the machine. He remembers being asked what he wanted, over and over, until wanting anything felt like the test. His technique makes beverages now. When the researchers ask why, he makes them coffee, and he cries while it dispenses, and the coffee is perfect. This is the saddest file the Office holds.

Core law

Beverage Preparation

Dispense.

Action · 1 CE · Name any mundane nonmagical liquid · A cup appears in your hand or on a surface within 5 ft · Thrown as an improvised weapon: 1d4 damage + the liquid's mundane effect (oil: a 5-ft square of difficult terrain for 1 minute)

Ask, and it is there — hot or cold, exactly as named. The file has stopped asking what ‘mundane’ excludes. The exclusions are in the rulings.

The Cup.

Passive · The cup is ceramic, exactly full, and the correct temperature · It has never once been wrong

The file has checked the cup against its own measurements. The file wishes it hadn't — the precision feels like an apology.

The Grief.

Trigger · At the GM's discretion (at most once per scene), when he is startled, saddened, or threatened: he dispenses without spending CE — usually something comforting, usually for someone else · Never targets enemies

He does not choose this, and the file does not bill it. It is always something comforting. It is always for someone else.

Rules integration

Table Rulings

‘Mundane nonmagical liquid’ excludes acids above household strength, poisons, and magical elixirs (until Pharmacist).
Dispensed liquids are real and permanent.
The involuntary grief-dispense never costs CE and never targets enemies.
The Grief Cup’s memory-taste is always accurate. The file does not explain how he knows. He does not explain either.

Subject file

Containment & observation record.

Containment Procedures

Machine access is scheduled. A beverage log is maintained. No caffeine after 1800 — a joke someone wrote in the file, and nobody erased, because everyone follows it.

He is not to be asked what he wants. This is the only rule the file prints in bold. The file learned it the way you learn a burn.

Grief-dispensing is logged, not billed. The log is the longest document in the file. Nobody reads the whole log in one sitting.

Research Log

Research log. 10/2024 — Acquisition: recovered with the machine. He was sitting beside it, making tea he did not drink. When asked what happened, he made coffee — two cups, perfect — and cried while it dispensed. Intake took four hours. Three were the coffee.

Research log. 04/2025 — The grief response is mapped and it is not voluntary: startled, saddened, or threatened, he dispenses — comforting things, for other people, without spending anything. His innate technique, engrained in his brain by whatever the Office did to get here, makes beverages. The file’s author has started bringing her own mug. She does not know why she does this. The file knows.

Research log. 12/2025 — The Regular’s Order confirmed: any remembered taste, exact. He reproduced a researcher’s grandmother’s soup from a description. The researcher did not finish it. The researcher sat with it for an hour. The file logged the hour as ‘testing.’

Research log. 07/2026 — The Grief Cup: a cup tasting of a specific memory, on request, once. The requester — the file withholds the name — drank it, sat down on the floor of the break room, and stayed there until shift change. No mechanical effect. The file’s author has requested reassignment twice over this entry. Both requests are pending. The file understands.

Incident Report — The Drill.

During a lockdown drill, unprompted and unbilled, he dispensed hot chocolate for the entire shift — forty-one cups, each exactly full, each the right temperature, each handed to the person who needed it most, in order. No one had told him there was a drill. When it ended, he sat down among the empty cups and apologized to them, quietly, for not having anything stronger. The file lists forty-one witnesses. The file lists zero complaints. The file does not know what to do with this incident, so it has filed it under ‘containment: adequate.’
