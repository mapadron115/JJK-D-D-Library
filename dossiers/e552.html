Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the priest's ledger. The Offering (bonus action · 1 CE): declare aloud your sacrifice and take 1d6 HP damage — this damage cannot be reduced or prevented by any means; the pain is the mechanism. Until the end of your next turn, your next attack roll, ability check, saving throw, or technique damage roll gains +1d8. The binding law: a tithe, once declared and accepted, cannot be refunded, dispelled, or countered — only fulfilled. The price is paid immediately. Spoken sacrifice cannot be unspoken; this is the oldest law the technique knows, and everything else is built on it.

Field notes. Three truths from the ledger. First: every religion ever invented discovered the same transaction — give something up, get something back, and the giving is what makes the getting real; the technique simply declines to launder this through a god, and deals direct. Second: the genius of the tithe was never the sacrifice, it was the plate — someone has to hold it, someone has to count it, and whoever holds the plate decides what counts; be the holder. Third: the best cons are the honest ones — the terms are real, the benefit is real, the price is real, and the mark thanks you for the privilege of paying; a priest who deals fair never runs out of congregants. Mark the economy: the sacrifice is theirs. The cut is yours.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1The Offering

Bonus action · 1 CE · Declare aloud your sacrifice and take 1d6 HP damage (cannot be reduced or prevented). Until the end of your next turn, your next attack roll, ability check, saving throw, or technique damage roll gains +1d8.

The bread and butter: pain, declared, converted. You don't choose between attacking and technique-ing — the Offering is a bonus action, folded into whatever you were already doing. The declaration matters as much as the blood: say it aloud, mean it, and the technique honors the oldest law. A spoken sacrifice cannot be unspoken.

3The Collection Plate

Action · 2 CE · Offer tithe terms to one creature within 60 feet: name a price from the Tithe Menu and a benefit. A willing creature accepts freely. An unwilling creature must be deceived — make a Charisma (Deception) check contested by their Wisdom (Insight); on a success they believe the terms are fair and accept. The price is paid immediately and cannot be refunded. House cut: whenever a creature other than you pays a tithe through you, your next technique feature costs 2 less CE (minimum 1 CE) — use it before the end of your next turn. Tithe Menu (L3) — Prices: Flesh (take 2d6 HP damage, cannot be reduced), Nerve (disadvantage on your next saving throw). Benefits: Might (+2d8 to attack rolls for 1 minute), Mend (regain 2d8 HP), Flow (restore 4 CE).

The new verb: L1 sacrifices your own flesh; L3 sacrifices anyone's. The technique was blind to other people's pain — now it isn't. Note the honesty of the con: the terms are real, the benefit is real, the price is real. You're not cheating them. You're just the house, and the house always takes its cut. A creature that has seen through your terms before has advantage on Insight against your Deception — a mark only falls for it once.

6The Altar

Passive · The Offering improves: sacrifice 2d6 HP for +2d8. The Tithe Menu expands — new prices: Sense (blinded or deafened until the end of your next turn), Blood (sacrifice one hit die: roll it and take that much damage, cannot be reduced); new benefits: Surge (+2d8 to attack rolls and saving throws for 1 minute), Restoration (regain 4d8 HP or restore 8 CE). Your congregation: creatures who have paid a tithe through you accept your terms without Deception when the terms are genuinely favorable (GM's call) — they remember the priest who dealt fair.

The system deepens and the foundation sharpens with it: bigger sacrifices, bigger payouts, and the menu becomes a real economy. Note the pairing — your own Offering grows teeth at the same level the Plate grows options. The technique doesn't abandon its first prayer; it just learns longer liturgies.

11The Revival

Action · 8 CE · Once per short rest · Preach the terms to every creature within 60 feet at once — each hears your offer individually and may accept (willing freely; unwilling via your Deception vs their Insight, one roll against all). Every accepter pays and benefits; you take the house cut from each (each cut applies to a separate future feature — they do not stack on one). The desperate tithe cheapest: a creature at half HP or fewer has disadvantage on Insight against your Deception for these terms.

High power, not a finisher: the limiter is the short rest and the social reality — you can only preach the revival so often, and the desperate are the easiest marks. It doesn't end the fight. It turns the battlefield into a congregation, and every prayer pays you.

18The Congregation

Passive · Your congregation is permanent: any creature that has ever paid a tithe through you counts. You may offer tithe terms to congregation members as a bonus action. Your house cut improves to 3 CE off your next feature (minimum 1 CE). Once per long rest — Call In the Debt: one congregation member within 120 feet must pay a price of your choice from the menu (Wisdom save negates, DC = your CE save DC); they receive no benefit. The house collects.

Enablement, not a nuke: the technique stops asking and starts collecting. Everyone who has ever paid at your altar is still in your ledger, and the ledger never closes. You are very difficult to handle now — not because you hit harder, but because every sacrifice on the field has your name on the receipt.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

You raise your hands, and the air goes still the way a chapel goes still. Every creature on the field hears the terms — not shouted, simply present, the way a debt is present. The altar is everywhere now. All that remains is for everyone to decide what they are willing to lose.

Maximum, The Grand Tithe (action, 10 CE): name your price — sacrifice 4d6 HP (cannot be reduced or prevented). Offer the grand terms to every creature within 120 feet: pay a grand price (6d6 HP, a sense until your next long rest, or three hit dice) and receive the miracle (regain all HP, end all conditions on yourself, restore all CE). For each creature that accepts, the congregation's faith strikes: deal 2d10 radiant damage to a hostile of your choice within 120 feet (Wisdom save for half, DC = your CE save DC); you distribute the strikes as you choose. The faithful pay. The faithless watch the faithful pay. Everyone remembers who held the plate.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Indulgences

Action · 2 CE · Another creature pays a tithe price from the menu; you or an ally within 30 feet receives the benefit instead of the payer. The payer still pays in full. Someone always pays for grace — it just doesn't have to be the one who receives it. Tithing, but as medicine.

Confession

Action · 3 CE · Sacrifice a memory: lose one skill proficiency until your next long rest (your choice). In exchange, name one creature you can see — learn one truth about them: their current HP, their greatest fear, their technique's weakness, or their immediate intent (your choice). The price is real; the truth is realer. Tithing, but as divination.

False Idol

Action · 4 CE · Designate an object or willing creature within 30 feet as a lesser altar for 1 hour. Tithes paid through the lesser altar grant you the house cut as normal, and you may offer terms through it (using your Deception) to creatures within 30 feet of it. The priest doesn't have to be present for the plate to pass. Tithing, but by proxy.

Scapegoat

Reaction · 2 CE · When an ally within 30 feet takes damage, take the damage instead — and count it as a tithe price you paid: gain the house cut immediately. The wound is the offering; the altar is your own body. Tithing, but as interception.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: Direct rolled technique attacks can use the campaign's Black Flash procedure when otherwise eligible. Save-effect damage (The Grand Tithe's strikes) cannot critically hit or Black Flash.
Generic Maximum Output applies only to ordinary features, never to The Grand Tithe.
Tithe prices that deal HP damage cannot be reduced or prevented by any means — the pain is the mechanism. Temporary HP does not absorb tithe damage. A tithe vow, once accepted, is binding until its stated duration ends: it cannot be dispelled, countered, or refunded — only fulfilled.
You cannot offer tithe terms to a creature that cannot hear or understand you. A creature that has seen through your terms before has advantage on Insight against your Deception, applied to every contest. The house cut must be used before the end of your next turn, applies to one feature only, and multiple cuts (e.g. from The Revival) apply to separate features.
