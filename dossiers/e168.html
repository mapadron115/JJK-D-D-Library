One Too Many

Make a group lose certainty about its own membership. Headcounts become cursed facts, and while the contradiction stands, Help actions and reactions fail against the uncertain table — passive corrosion, not currency.

Combat roleInfiltration / Coordination Disruption
TierTier III · Apex
Core engineContradiction field
Core law

Character fantasy

Make a group lose certainty about its own membership. Headcounts become cursed facts — and while the contradiction stands, the table corrodes on its own: Help goes to the wrong creature, reactions hesitate, certainty fails. Passive corrosion, not currency.

Combat loop

  1. Establish a Headcount on a group.
  2. Take your place among them as a False Member.
  3. While the contradiction stands, their teamwork fails by itself — no tokens, no spending.
  4. Stay believed: exposure collapses the field.

The fiction. Registry scholars describe it as doubt with mass: the user's cursed energy imprinted on a moment of absolute uncertainty — a headcount that did not come out right — and now reproduces that moment inside every group it joins. The prevailing theory holds that the technique never lies, which is what makes it cruel: the Headcount is a true fact, the False Member is truly there, and the contradiction between them is simply left to corrode everything the group believed about itself.

Full progression

Level progression

1False Member

Doubt Die

Your Doubt Die follows the shared d6 → d8 → d10 → d12 progression.

Headcount

Action, 2 CE: choose up to PB creatures you can see within 60 feet that regard themselves as one group — a shared camp, patrol, crew, or vehicle counts; the technique reads togetherness, not affection. For 10 minutes you know whenever one enters or leaves 60 feet of you, and the technique records the original count.

False Member

While your Headcount is active, you can make your appearance, voice, scent, and superficial CE signature read as “one member of the group” without copying a specific individual. A creature that spends its action inspecting you may make an Insight check against your CE save DC; on success it sees through you.

The Contradiction (automatic field)

While your Headcount is active and you are within 60 feet of at least two of its members, the contradiction stands. While it stands, no coordination within the group is certain: whenever a Headcount member uses the Help action on another Headcount member, or uses a reaction that benefits another Headcount member (a granted attack, a reaction buff or heal, a formation or flanking bonus), it must first succeed on an Insight check against your CE save DC. On a failure it hesitates — the Help, reaction, or bonus is wasted (the action or reaction is still spent). The creature feels the wrongness but does not learn why.

Exposure (fallback & counterplay)

The field is belief. A creature that spends its action looking for the impostor makes an Insight check against your CE save DC; on success it sees through your guise and the Headcount collapses entirely — spend another action and 2 CE to establish a new one. If the group never coordinates, the field has nothing to corrode — but the technique still works as infiltration: Headcount tracking, the False Member guise, and your decoy still bait attacks and waste enemy actions.

3There Were Six of Us

Bonus action, 2 CE: create a False Member decoy in an unoccupied space within 30 feet for 1 minute. It has your AC, 1 HP, no attacks, and can move 30 feet on your turn without an action. Maintain one decoy. A creature that wastes an attack or reaction on the decoy becomes certain of the wrong thing: it has disadvantage on Insight checks against your CE save DC until the end of your next turn.

6Wrong Voice in the Dark

Action, 2 CE: choose one Headcount creature within 60 feet. Until the start of your next turn, sounds you make may originate from its space, and your decoy may look specifically like it. While this holds, the dissonance is painful: a creature that fails a field Insight check takes 1 Doubt Die psychic damage as its certainty cracks.

11Someone Is Missing

Your Headcount can affect up to PB + CE modifier creatures. The corrosion deepens: single-target beneficial actions (buffs, heals) that a Headcount member targets on another Headcount member now also trigger the field check. On a failed check the action is delayed until the end of the creature’s turn, when it may retarget legally (resources are not spent unless it still resolves).

18Domain Expansion: Campfire of Seven

Full-turn action, 20 CE, 90-foot radius, 1 minute. Everyone appears as a dim silhouette around an endless forest camp. The Domain’s law is Verify Before You Trust — made absolute: hostile creatures inside cannot use the Help action on each other, cannot use reactions that benefit each other, and cannot target each other with single-target beneficial effects. No check is offered; the campfire’s law does not negotiate. Anti-domain protection lets a creature trust its own senses (it coordinates normally). Your False Member decoys cost 0 CE and you may maintain up to PB of them during the Domain. Clash bookkeeping: 500 clash points (Tier III). A 100+ point advantage collapses the weaker Domain; collapse deals no damage. Barrier AC 12 + PB; barrier HP = 10 × max(1, your CE modifier) + 500. Technique Burnout: 10 rounds.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Count Again

When a creature fails an Insight check to identify you or a decoy, it is left doubting every face: it has disadvantage on its next attack roll before the end of its next turn.

Campfire Voice

You can perfectly reproduce any voice heard for at least 1 minute, though this alone never grants a Headcount benefit.

Wrong Footprints

As an action and 1 CE, create false tracks, scent, and mundane evidence for one Headcount creature across a 60-foot trail.

Seventh Shadow

Prerequisite: There Were Six of Us. Your decoy may take the Help action once on your turn; doing so destroys it after the helped roll resolves.

Rules integration

Building the character

Strategist and Support Specialist fit best. It is intentionally much stronger against organized squads than lone monsters, and its combat power comes from ruining teamwork rather than controlling enemy turns wholesale.
Rules integration

Counterplay

Split the party deliberately, use predetermined passwords, rely on area effects instead of coordination, spend actions to look for the impostor — and when one member sees through the False Member, say so out loud: the contradiction collapses.
