The Hole

Everything lost goes somewhere. The Office spent decades theorizing about where. Then it recovered the place — a hole in a wall, dark past the wrist, patient as geology. It gives things back. It does not always give back yours.

Core law

Lost Property

The Hole.

Action, 2 CE, 1 minute: open a 2-foot dark hole on a surface. Reach in (action): retrieve one of YOUR lost objects — carried within the last 24 hours, unclaimed. Sometimes the hole gives someone else’s lost thing instead. The file does not control this. The file has stopped pretending to.

Someone Else’s.

When the hole substitutes, the GM chooses a lost object belonging to someone else — usually inconvenient, occasionally useful, never random.

Deposit.

Objects may be deposited for extradimensional storage; retrieve them as an action while the hole is open.

Living creatures (beyond L18’s willing Tiny) can be neither retrieved nor stored.
The hole is dark; nothing inside can be seen or heard until retrieved.

Subject file

Containment & observation record.

Containment Procedures

The hole is kept closed. The wall section is monitored. A retrieval log is maintained, and the log is audited against staff property quarterly.

Reaching is supervised, in pairs, with a spotter holding the reacher’s belt. This is not superstition. The file cites the work order.

Deposits are inventoried. The inventory has never balanced. The file no longer expects it to.

Research Log

Research log. 12/2024 — Acquisition: recovered from a wall in a demolished building. The demolition crew filled the hole. Their tools were later retrieved from the hole. The tools were not the same tools.

Research log. 05/2025 — The rule is mapped: lost, not stolen, not given — the hole is precise about the distinction in a way the file finds almost moral. Twenty-four hours, unclaimed, yours. Sometimes another’s. The substitutions are never random; the file has stopped calling them errors.

Research log. 11/2025 — Storage confirmed: 500 lb, safe, unreachable while closed. A wedding ring belonging to a staffer’s grandmother was retrieved during inventory — lost forty years, unclaimed, hers by every test the file could devise. She wept. The file logged it as a successful retrieval.

Research log. 08/2026 — The Deep Hole test: a lost Tiny willing creature (a mouse, volunteered by pest control, unlost within the hour) was pulled through unharmed. The 1-year window held. The memory-retrieval — something the subject didn’t know he’d lost — is logged but not described. The subject asked that it not be described.

Incident Report — The Belt.

A reacher’s spotter, following procedure, held the belt. The reacher retrieved a watch — not his. The spotter recognized it: his father’s, lost twenty years, never spoken of at work. Procedure held. The belt held. The spotter has requested transfer. The watch keeps perfect time. The file notes that the hole gives back what was lost, and that ‘lost’ has never meant only objects.
