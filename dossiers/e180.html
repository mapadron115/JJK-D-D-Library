Core law

Character fantasy

Earn Tails only when enemies actually waste meaningful resources on your deception, then spend those Tails on foxfire, substitutions, false terrain, and increasingly perfect lies.

Combat loop

  1. Create a deception that matters tactically.
  2. When an enemy genuinely wastes effort because of it, gain a Tail.
  3. Spend Tails on stronger tricks and foxfire.
  4. Escalate toward nine tails without relying on the GM simply declaring an NPC “fooled.”

Core law

Tails & Meaningful Deception

Tail cap:

level 1 = 1; level 3 = 3; level 6 = 5; level 11 = 7; level 18 = 9. Begin each encounter with 1 Tail, never above cap.

Gain Tail:

once per hostile creature per round when a technique-created deception causes it to waste an attack, action, bonus action, reaction, at least 10 feet of movement, or at least 1 CE on a target/location/effect that is not what it believed, gain 1 Tail. Pure dialogue misunderstandings grant a Tail only if they cause one of those concrete costs.

Foxfire:

ranged CE attack 60 feet, 1 Fox Die fire/psychic + CE modifier. Spend 1 Tail on hit to add one Fox Die and illuminate target until end of next turn.

Full progression

Level progression

1First Tail

Gain Tail engine and Foxfire. Paper Fox: action, 1 CE, create one visual/audio illusion occupying a 5-foot space within 30 feet for 1 minute; it may move 30 feet on your turn. It has no physical force. Investigation against CE DC identifies it after direct inspection.

3Three Tails

Tail cap 3. Substitution: reaction when attacked, spend 2 Tails before roll to swap with Paper Fox within 30 feet; the attack targets the illusion and destroys it. Fox Wedding: 2 CE, 15-foot cube of illusory rain, lanterns, or procession; lightly obscured and lasts 1 minute Focus.

6Five Tails

Tail cap 5. Maintain PB Paper Foxes. Spend 2 Tails to make one illusion tactile enough to provide half cover or difficult terrain in a 10-foot area until start of your next turn; it cannot restrain or deal damage. Foxfire becomes 2 Fox Dice before modifiers.

11Seven Tails

Tail cap 7. Bonus action, 3 Tails: choose one creature that can see an illusion; Wisdom save or until start of your next turn it treats one 15-foot cube of your illusion as fully real for movement and targeting, though taking damage from a contradiction gives an immediate new save. Once per round when you gain a Tail, move one illusion 30 feet without action.

18True Form — Nine Tails Ascendant

Level 18; replaces Domain. Bonus action, 10 CE, once per long rest, 1 minute. Tail cap becomes 9 and immediately rises to 9. Once per round, spend 1 Tail when someone treats an illusion as real; it becomes real for that interaction, blocks movement, and an attack against it causes 2 Fox Dice psychic backlash. Foxfire becomes 3 Fox Dice and is usable as a bonus action. Substitution costs no Tails. +10-ft speed; you leave no tracks and cannot be followed non-magically. This is not a Domain: it has no clash points, no barrier, and the Domain clash and Burnout rules do not apply.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Fox Bridegroom

Gain proficiency or expertise in Deception; harmless cosmetic shapechanging can alter hair, eyes, ears, tail, and clothing appearance at will.

Blue Foxfire

Foxfire may deal cold or lightning instead of fire/psychic; choose on attack.

Clever Enough to Believe Yourself

When you fail a save against frightened or charmed, spend 1 Tail to reroll it.

Thousand-Year Trick

Prerequisite: Seven Tails. A creature that successfully identifies one of your illusions does not automatically identify your other illusions.

Rules integration

Building the character

Charisma-heavy Strategist/Support Specialist or slippery Scout. It rewards players who create believable tactical lies, while Tail gain has objective triggers that keep it playable.
Technique die: Fox Die follows the shared d6 → d8 → d10 → d12 progression.
Rules integration

Counterplay

Probe illusions with low-cost actions, use area effects, communicate discoveries, and force the user to spend Tails defensively instead of accumulating them.
