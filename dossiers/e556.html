Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the mortician's procedure. There are four rites, and they are always in this order: the Washing, the Dressing, the Closing of the Eyes, the Words. Performed on a corpse, they settle it — it cannot become a curse, cannot be disturbed, cannot be used. Performed early, on the living, each rite loosens the world's grip on the target a little more. A creature undergoing the rites is Prepared: a simple condition, not a pool. Prepared lasts until you finish a long rest, until all four rites are complete, or until you release it. The rites must be performed in order — you cannot dress what you have not washed, cannot close eyes you have not dressed, cannot speak the Words over a face still open. Settling (passive): spend 1 minute preparing a corpse and it is settled permanently — it can never become a curse, be raised, be disturbed, or be used as a component. This is the daily work. Everything else is the work done early.

Field notes. Three truths from the preparation room. First: a funeral is not an event, it is a procedure — every culture that ever buried anyone discovered the same sequence independently, wash and dress and close and speak, because the dead are owed an order of operations and the living are owed witnesses; the technique simply noticed that the universe keeps the same paperwork. Second: the rites work on the living because the universe cannot tell the difference between a body being prepared and a body being prepared for — perform the steps correctly and reality signs the certificate in advance. Third: every mortician learns the same lesson sooner or later, usually at 3 a.m. over a body nobody claimed — the rites are not about the dead. The dead are past caring. The rites are about everyone still watching, and the universe is always watching. Do it right. Do it in order. Do not skip.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1The Washing

Bonus action · 1 CE · Begin the rites on a creature within 30 feet: it becomes Prepared. While Prepared, whenever the target takes damage from any source, it takes an additional 1 necrotic damage — the rites are already loosening its grip on life. Settling (passive): spend 1 minute preparing a corpse; it is settled permanently and can never become a curse, be raised, be disturbed, or be used.

The bread and butter: a bonus action, folded into whatever you were already doing, and the first line of the paperwork. The necrotic rider is small — one point, every time — but it never stops, and it is the sound of the world beginning to agree with you. Note the passive: settling the dead is not a combat tool. It is the job. The technique remembers what it is for.

3The Wake

You learn your Domain Expansion (see Pinnacle expression).

The Domain is not the capstone. It is the thesis — like the gambler's pachinko and the judge's courtroom, the funeral arrives early and everything after is learning to conduct it better. From here the technique is never really about the washing — it is about everything the washing was practicing for. Four rites, one room, no interruptions. That is the whole sermon.

6The Dressing

Passive · The Washing now reaches 60 feet, and the Prepared necrotic rider increases to 1d4. Action · 2 CE · Perform the second rite on a Prepared creature within 60 feet: until the rites end, it cannot regain hit points — the body is being readied, not healed. (A creature must have received the Washing before the Dressing; the order is the law.)

The system deepens and the foundation sharpens with it: longer reach, a hungrier rider, and the second line of the paperwork. Denying healing is the Dressing's quiet cruelty — nothing dramatic, just the slow administrative truth that a body being prepared for burial is not a body being kept alive. The order matters. It always matters.

11The Closing of the Eyes

Action · 3 CE · Once per short rest · Perform the third rite on a Prepared creature within 60 feet that has received the Dressing: it is blinded until the rites end, and its death saving throws are made with disadvantage. The world has nearly finished the paperwork — it barely sees the target as living anymore.

High power, not a finisher: the limiter is the short rest and the sequence — you cannot close eyes you have not dressed, and you cannot dress what you have not washed, which means this rite cost you three actions across the fight before it ever landed. It doesn't end anyone. It makes the ending legible. Blind, unhealing, one foot in the grave by every measure the universe keeps.

18The Words

Action · 5 CE · Speak the final rite over a creature within 60 feet bearing all three prior rites (Washing, Dressing, Closing of the Eyes), in order: the Last Word is spoken. The target drops to 0 hit points and dies outright — no death saving throws are made. This works anywhere; the funeral no longer needs the Domain.

Enablement, not a nuke: the technique stops needing the room and starts carrying the funeral with it. Four actions across the fight, every rite in order, uninterrupted — that was always the price, and at last you can pay it in the open air. The Words are short. They are always short. That is what makes them final.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

The light goes soft the way a viewing room goes soft. Rows of chairs assemble from nothing, all facing one empty bier at the center of the glass. Every candle in the room is already lit. There is no music, because the music hasn't started yet — it starts when you begin, and everyone present understands, the way mourners understand, that what happens next will be done properly.

Domain Expansion, The Wake (full-turn action, 20 CE, 1 minute): name one target within the Domain. You may then perform the four rites upon them in order — each rite takes your action and costs 2 CE, and each is sure-hit inside the Domain. If you take damage while the sequence is unbroken, make a Constitution saving throw (DC 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher) — on a failure the sequence breaks and must restart from the Washing. Complete all four rites in order without breaking the sequence: speak the Last Word as part of the fourth rite's action, and the target drops to 0 hit points and dies outright — no death saving throws are made. The counterplay is honest: hurt the mortician, break the order, or leave the room. Four uninterrupted actions is an eternity in a fight, and eternity is exactly what the funeral requires.

Feats

Build-defining options.

The Viewing

While preparing a corpse (settling), you may ask it three questions; it answers truthfully. The dead do not lie at their own viewing — the rites compel honesty the way the room compels quiet. The rite, but as divination.

Eulogy

Action · 2 CE · Perform an abbreviated rite over a willing living creature within 30 feet: it gains temporary hit points equal to your level + your WIS modifier and has advantage on death saving throws for 1 hour. The same hands, the same words, turned to keeping instead of releasing. The rite, but as blessing.

The Pall

Bonus action · 2 CE · Drape the funeral pall over a 20-foot-radius area within 60 feet for 1 minute: the area is heavily obscured and silenced. Mourners lower their voices; so does everything inside. The rite, but as control.

Cremation

Action · 3 CE · Reduce a corpse within 30 feet to ash instantly. It can never be raised, questioned, settled, or used — not by you, not by anyone. Some families ask for this. Some bodies require it. The rite, but as denial.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: Direct rolled technique attacks can use the campaign's Black Flash procedure when otherwise eligible. The Last Word's instant kill is not damage and cannot critically hit or Black Flash.
The Last Word kills outright: the target drops to 0 hit points and dies — no death saving throws are made, and effects that trigger on death saves do not trigger. Only true resurrection-tier magic, at the GM's discretion, can undo a properly performed Last Word; ordinary revival fails — the paperwork is filed.
The rites affect only creatures with souls. Constructs are unaffected. Undead are already dead: performing the rites on an undead creature settles it instead — complete any single rite on an undead target and it is destroyed (no save), its animating force dismissed like a mourner at closing time.
Prepared ends when you finish a long rest, when all four rites are complete, or when you release it (no action). Rites must be performed in order; performing a rite out of order does nothing and the CE is wasted. A rite performed inside The Wake does not require the target to have received prior rites outside it — the Domain is a complete funeral of its own.
