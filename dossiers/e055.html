Feature map

Level-by-level features

Field notes. Subject: a child’s game, played lethally. I have watched this technique and come away with three truths. First, it announces itself: the user must declare “Jajanken… Rock!” aloud, stand glowing and still, speed halved, attacks against him advantaged, and tell everyone exactly what is coming. Deception is not merely absent; it is structurally impossible. Second, honesty has three forms, and no more: Rock is weight charged into a fist, Paper is conviction thrown sixty feet, Scissors is aura honed to an edge, a complete doctrine with no fourth option. Third, and heaviest: there is no Domain here, and there was never going to be. When everything was on the line, the user spent his future instead. The technique’s true pinnacle is not a barrier but a vow, the future, spent. Hence the maxim: the honest fist needs no hiding place.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Campaign canon notes

Cursed Energy baseline: CE pool is based on class; CE ability is Charisma or Wisdom (player's choice). CE save DC = 8 + CE ability modifier + proficiency bonus. Regain all spent CE on a long rest.
The declaration is mandatory: Jajanken requires a spoken declaration, "Rock!", "Paper!", or "Scissors!", to charge or release. While silenced or unable to speak, you cannot charge or release. Enemies always know which form is coming; that is the point.
Charging economy: each action spent charging grants 1 charge (maximum 3; 6 from L18). While holding a charge, your speed is halved and attacks against you have advantage. Charges fizzle after 1 minute without charging or releasing. Rock's release is part of the Attack action.
Paper and Scissors are attacks: Paper is a ranged cursed-energy attack (magical); Scissors is a melee unarmed strike dealing slashing damage (magical). All three forms count as magical for overcoming resistances.
No Domain by design: Jajanken deliberately has no Domain Expansion, its pinnacle expression is the Forbidden Vow above, not a barrier technique. This is a standing principle: not every technique needs a Domain, and inventing one where the source fantasy provides a vow instead would be filler.
Maximum vs. Reversal: Jajanken's pinnacle is written as a Maximum Technique (All-In Rock), not a Reversal; it occupies the technique's pinnacle slot.
