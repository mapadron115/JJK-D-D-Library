Field notes. Subject: the argument of the falling wall. Sorcerers spend years learning to keep their footing against techniques that hurl, drag, and unmake, and then there is this: a cursed round that simply knocks a man flat. Two truths govern it. First, the effect is honest and narrow: on a hit, the target holds its footing against DC 10 or goes prone, no wound beyond the shot, no lingering curse, just the ground, arriving early. Second, each round is spent in the firing; the wall falls once per cartridge. Mark this: footing is the first technique and the last casualty. Every grand strategy in this archive assumes the fighter is standing up.

On hit

Walloping Impact

On hit · Target makes a DC 10 Strength save or is knocked prone · Round consumed when fired.

The whole of it: the round is consumed, the target saves against DC 10 or meets the ground. Prone is not damage, it is the denial of posture, and posture is everything. A creature hit by this ammunition must succeed on a DC 10 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone. Each round is consumed when fired.
