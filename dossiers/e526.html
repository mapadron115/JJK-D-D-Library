Take Two

The rule is always two. It was two in the recovery report, two in every test, two in the incident file. The file does not know why two. The file knows what happens at three, and the file has photographs.

Combat roleController
Core law

The Rule of Two

The Bowl.

Action, 2 CE, 1 minute: a spectral candy bowl manifests. Any creature may take exactly two candies as a free interaction; each heals 2d4+2. The bowl is generous. The bowl is exact.

The Third.

Taking a third candy: 4d8 slashing damage to the hands (Dexterity save vs your CE DC for half), and the bowl yields no more candy to that creature. The old punishment. It does not miss.

The Offering.

The taking must be voluntary — a creature chooses to take. Forced feeding does not count as taking, and the bowl knows the difference.

The Gift.

Offer the bowl to an unwilling creature as a gift. It chooses freely whether to take — the bowl enforces the rule regardless of the answer.

Rules integration

Table Rulings

The bowl’s candy is real while the bowl lasts; uneaten candy fades with it.
‘Taking’ requires a free, willing interaction; dominated or unconscious creatures cannot take.
The slashing punishment ignores resistance — it is the rule, not an attack.
Creatures that cannot eat gain no benefit but are still bound by the rule if they take.

Subject file

Containment & observation record.

Containment Procedures

The bowl is kept empty. Candy is counted in and counted out. The two-take rule is posted in four languages at every approach.

Staff take in pairs, witnessed. No one takes alone. The file does not explain why this matters. The file insists that it does.

Hands are the unit of account. Gloves are mandatory. The photographs are available on request.

Research Log

Research log. 09/2024 — Acquisition: recovered from a break room. The notice was already posted. The translations already agreed. No one at the site remembers posting it.

Research log. 02/2025 — The rule is mapped: exactly two, voluntary taking, 2d4+2 healing each. The third: 4d8 slashing to the hands, no resistance, no appeal. The file notes the punishment’s precision with professional admiration and personal unease.

Research log. 08/2025 — The Gift test: an unwilling subject was offered the bowl and chose to take. The rule enforced itself identically. Voluntariness, the file concludes, is about the taking, not the wanting.

Research log. 05/2026 — Mercy observed: the punisher — the file will not name it — accepted memory in place of flesh on one occasion. The subject could not remember taking. The hands were unmarked. The file has added ‘mercy’ to the vocabulary with visible reluctance.

Incident Report — Inventory.

During a routine count, a technician took three — quickly, casually, the way people take mints. The file lists what was lost: the use of both hands, six weeks of duties, and, per the technician’s own statement, ‘the memory of why I reached back.’ He remembers the bowl. He remembers the rule. He does not remember deciding. The count is now performed by pairs, witnessed, and no one takes alone.
