The Thirty Pieces of Silver

Metaphysical Law: A betrayal that accepts a price becomes a binding transaction.

FormCoin Set
EnginePrice of Betrayal
Attunement1 hour*

Field notes. Subject: a contract that collects its own penalty clause. Three truths govern the coins. First, betrayal must be priced and accepted: 1–10 coins offered for one stated act of betrayal, and the technique wakes only when the creature knowingly takes them. Consent is the trigger; the silver dominates no mind. Second, breach is invoiced without mercy: for 24 hours, acting against the purchased terms costs 2d10 psychic per coin accepted, 20d10 at most, plus 1 CE per two coins, irreducible. Third, the full set is a different instrument: all 30 coins, offered once per 30 days, and breaking it deals 30d10 psychic, carves the damage from maximum HP until a long rest, and locks the breaker's Domain Expansion for a day. Completed betrayals, by contrast, let the coins sleep until dawn and return them. The silver does not dominate minds. It merely makes treason enforceable.

Jujutsu origin

The individual coins are ordinary enough; the story attached to 'thirty pieces of silver' is not. Betrayal, price, guilt, and the idea that loyalty can be purchased condensed into the set as a contractual cursed technique.

Price of Betrayal

Passive · Offer 1–10 coins to a creature while stating a specific act of betrayal; the technique activates only if the creature knowingly and willingly accepts the coins.

Offer 1–10 coins to a creature while stating a specific act of betrayal, broken allegiance, or breach of duty. The technique only activates if the creature knowingly and willingly accepts the coins.

Purchased Treachery

Passive · For 24 hours: if the recipient knowingly acts against the purchased terms, it takes 2d10 psychic damage per coin accepted (maximum 20d10 per breach) and loses 1 CE per 2 coins accepted (rounded up); this damage cannot be reduced.

For 24 hours, if the recipient knowingly acts against the purchased terms, it takes 2d10 psychic damage per coin accepted (maximum 20d10 per breach) and loses 1 CE per 2 coins accepted, rounded up. This damage cannot be reduced.

Fulfilled Payment

Passive · If the recipient completes the purchased betrayal, the coins become inert until the next dawn, then return to the set.

If the recipient completes the purchased betrayal, the coins become inert until the next dawn and then return to the set.

Thirtyfold Purchase — all 30 coins, once per 30 days

Passive · Once per 30 days: if a creature knowingly accepts all 30 coins for one stated betrayal, breaking it deals 30d10 psychic damage, reduces its maximum HP by the damage taken until a long rest, and blocks its Domain Expansion for 24 hours.

If a creature knowingly accepts the complete set for one stated betrayal, breaking that bargain deals 30d10 psychic damage, reduces its maximum HP by the damage taken until a long rest, and prevents it from using a Domain Expansion for 24 hours.

Binding Vow — Consent Is the Trigger

The coins do not dominate minds. Their terrifying strength comes from the recipient voluntarily agreeing that loyalty has a price.

Designer’s note. The set is strongest in political, clan, and underworld play because it turns corrupt bargains into literal Binding Vows.

Rules integration

Cursed-tool attunement

Campaign canon

Cursed-tool attunement: you have PB attunement slots (2–6). This tool occupies one slot and requires a one-hour ritual. Attunement ends voluntarily over a short rest, on death, or when you replace it while over capacity. Distance does not break attunement. Cursed-tool attunement is separate from magic-item attunement.
