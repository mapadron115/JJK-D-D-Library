Idle Transfiguration

Direct soul damage, permanent alterations, stored transfigured creatures, a split-body clone, and soul fusion.

Field notes. Subject: one person’s boredom with the permanence of form, given hands. Three truths govern the sculptor. First, the touch is never a technique at all: his energy refuses to acknowledge any difference between a body and a soul, 1d6 necrotic on every touch-driven hit, 1 CE for a Constitution save against up to 5d10. Second, the dead are reworked, not left: what his touch reduces to 0 HP becomes a Transfigured Creature, maximum HP halved, stored, shaped during rests, fused permanently by Soul Multiplicity into effects like Body Repel. Third, even the self is clay: Body Clone divides HP and CE evenly between two bodies, and the Domain is the workshop with the doors locked, Self Embodiment of Perfection, a dimension of his own body parts from which no teleportation escapes, the sure-hit falling at every initiative 20. The clay cannot leave the hands.

Idle touch

Passive · Natural weapons, unarmed strikes, and deliberate touch attacks deal +1d6 necrotic · Action · 1 CE · One creature within your reach: Constitution save, 1d10 necrotic (2d10 at 5th, 3d10 at 9th, 4d10 at 13th, 5d10 at 17th); half on success · Constructs are immune to conversion, not to the damage.

The idle touch: 1d6 necrotic riding every natural weapon, unarmed strike, or deliberate touch, and for 1 CE, a Constitution save against up to 5d10. What the touch reduces to 0 HP does not die; it is stored, max HP halved, and may be shaped during rests. The sorcerer’s energy refuses to acknowledge any difference between a body and a soul. Whenever you hit a creature with a natural weapon, unarmed strike, or deliberate touch attack, you deal an additional 1d6 necrotic damage. As an action for 1 CE, force one creature within your reach to make a Constitution save: 1d10 necrotic damage on failure (2d10 at 5th level, 3d10 at 9th, 4d10 at 13th, 5d10 at 17th), half as much on success. Constructs are immune to conversion, not to the damage.

Transfigured Creatures

Passive · A creature reduced to 0 HP by your Idle Transfiguration becomes a Transfigured Creature instead of dying: maximum HP becomes half its former maximum (minimum 1) and it returns at full HP · Store up to your level × max(1, CE modifier); at most CE modifier (minimum 1) active at once · Bonus action: extract any number into open spaces within 10 feet, or store adjacent willing transfigured creatures.

A creature reduced to 0 HP by your Idle Transfiguration becomes a Transfigured Creature instead of dying. Its maximum HP becomes half its former maximum (minimum 1) and it returns at full HP. You may store up to your level × max(1, CE modifier); active creatures are limited to CE modifier (minimum one). A bonus action extracts any number up to that capacity into open spaces within 10 feet, or stores adjacent willing transfigured creatures.

Shaping

Passive · During a rest, customize a transfigured creature: choose Tiny through Huge (size changes space and reach only); set Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma to 4, then move the removed points among Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution (maximum 22 each); remove supernatural features, spellcasting, techniques, and proficiencies · AC 10 + Dexterity; attacks use your PB + Strength or Dexterity, 1d6 + modifier · Bonus action: one simple order to all active creatures; action: distinct or complex orders.

During a rest, customize a transfigured creature: choose Tiny through Huge (size changes space and natural reach only); set Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma to 4, then move the removed points among Strength, Dexterity, and Constitution, each to a maximum of 22; remove supernatural features, spellcasting, techniques, and proficiencies. AC is 10 + Dexterity modifier; attacks use your PB plus their Strength or Dexterity and deal 1d6 plus that modifier. A bonus action gives all active creatures one simple order; an action gives distinct or complex orders.

The clay-work, extended to the willing: permanent alterations during rests, up to PB per creature, and the sorcerer may reshape himself. The workshop takes commissions. 6Body Clone.

Body Clone

Action · 6 CE · Create a perfect copy of yourself: controlled by you, acts on your initiative, takes only the actions you grant it with your bonus action · Divide maximum HP and maximum CE evenly between bodies (odd points to the original); current pools drop to their new maxima · On recombination, restore maxima and add its remaining current HP/CE; if it dies, the reserved maxima return after your next long rest but its current resources are lost · It cannot create another clone.

6 CE for a perfect copy: HP and CE divided evenly between the bodies, the clone controlled by the sorcerer, seen through his eyes. The self, halved to be doubled. As an action for 6 CE, create a perfect copy of yourself. It is controlled by you, acts on your turn, and you can see through its eyes and hear through its ears. On creation, divide maximum HP and maximum CE evenly between bodies, assigning odd points to the original, and reduce current pools to their new maxima. The clone acts on your initiative and can take only the actions you grant it with your bonus action, and cannot create another clone. On recombination, restore maxima and add its remaining current HP/CE. If it dies, the reserved maxima return after your next long rest, but its remaining current resources are lost.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

Domain Expansion, Self Embodiment of Perfection:

The domain is a gallery of flesh, bodies in various states of becoming lining walls that breathe. Some are beautiful, some are monstrous, and the distinction feels arbitrary, a curatorial choice rather than a natural law. The air smells of antiseptic and something sweetly rotten, and every surface is warm to the touch. Perfection, it turns out, is not a shape. It is a process, and the process is ongoing.

A dimension of the sorcerer’s own body parts, and no teleportation or planar travel escapes it. At every initiative count 20, the sure-hit falls. The Domain is the workshop with the doors locked: the clay cannot leave the hands. 18: full-turn action, 20 CE; 60-foot-radius enclosed Domain centered on you; 1 minute; Burnout 10 rounds. The Domain is a dimension of your own body parts, and there is no escaping death within it: creatures cannot leave by teleportation or planar travel while it stands (invented). Sure-Hit Event, Self Embodiment of Perfection: at initiative count 20 each round, every chosen creature suffers twice your Transfiguration touch damage, with no attack roll and no initial save. A creature protected by an anti-domain defense makes a Constitution saving throw vs your CE save DC (invented); on a success it takes half damage.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

Prerequisite: Transfiguration

Remote Transfiguration

A bare-hand touch plants a detectable soul mark on a humanoid, indefinitely. Later, from the same plane, the sorcerer pays the normal cost and reshapes from afar. The clay remembers the hand. A deliberate bare-hand touch may place one detectable soul mark on a Humanoid indefinitely. While on the same plane and knowing its identity, use an action and pay the normal Transfiguration cost to affect it at any distance; it saves normally. The mark is consumed after the attempt and can be removed by Technique Extermination or explicit soul repair.

Prerequisite: Ally Alteration

Awakening

Altering a willing non-sorcerer may, DC 15, 10 CE, 1d4+1 days, wake a latent innate technique. The workshop does not only reshape. It opens. When altering a willing Non-Sorcerer, make one DC 15 CE ability check per creature. Success and 10 CE awaken a latent, lore-consistent innate technique after 1d4+1 days. The GM records the technique; it uses the target’s character level and does not grant class features or a second technique. The permanent change can reshape a campaign.

Prerequisite: Self-Embodiment of Perfection and CE score 20

Mastery of grotesque molds: two new ways to spend transfigured humans, repeatable. The idle hands find new idleness. Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier IV: soul rewriting and a permanent creature reserve are campaign systems, even when the player never pursues the strongest available bodies. The revision defines the system rather than pretending that potential makes it unusable.
