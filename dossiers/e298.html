Properties & techniques

Exact player-facing procedures

Relic profile

+4 to Charisma (Deception, Performance, Persuasion) checks · Advantage on saves against being charmed

Jujutsu origin

The necklace is remembered as an heirloom of impossible beauty that carries disaster from one generation to the next. Jujutsu society classifies it as a transfer-type cursed tool: the wearer is not spared consequence; the consequence is reassigned.

Perfected Appearance

While worn, the bearer does not visibly age and has advantage on Charisma checks made against a creature that can see them, in addition to the tool's +4 where applicable.

Transferred Calamity — Reaction, 2 CE

When you take damage or fail a saving throw, choose a Bonded creature within 120 ft. A willing Bonded creature takes the damage or suffers the failed-save effect instead. An unwilling Bonded creature makes a Charisma save against your Relic DC; on a success, the transfer fails and the CE is still spent.

Bonded

The necklace recognizes at most PB creatures with whom the wearer has a genuine ongoing personal bond. A bond cannot be created purely to feed the necklace.

Calamity Marks

Each time a creature receives Transferred Calamity, it gains 1 Calamity Mark, maximum 3. Each mark imposes a cumulative -1 to d20 rolls. All marks disappear after that creature finishes a long rest.

Binding Vow — Beauty Cannot Pay Its Own Price

The necklace's defensive power only functions by moving the cost to a relationship the wearer actually values. It refuses disposable substitutes.

At 3 Calamity Marks, a creature cannot be selected again until it finishes a long rest.

Rules integration

Cursed-tool attunement

Campaign canon

Cursed-tool attunement: you have PB attunement slots (2–6). This tool occupies one slot and requires a one-hour ritual. Attunement ends voluntarily over a short rest, on death, or when you replace it while over capacity. Distance does not break attunement. Cursed-tool attunement is separate from magic-item attunement.
