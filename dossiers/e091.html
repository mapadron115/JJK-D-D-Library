Sacred Treasure Framework

Design rule: Anime & Manga do not grant an entire second Innate Technique. They are exceptional cursed tools that increase a user's ability to express what they already do, while still providing enough independent function that finding one is exciting for the “wrong” build.

Field notes. Subject: the design rule itself. I have held this page longer than any treasure's, because it states the one doctrine that keeps these seven objects honest: a Sacred Treasure does not grant an entire second Innate Technique. Three truths govern them. First, they amplify, not bestow: exceptional cursed tools that deepen the expression of what the wielder already does, the steel remembers what a hand like yours once did with it and lends the memory back. Second, they are exciting for the 'wrong' build: each carries enough independent function that even an ill-matched wielder finds something worth finding. Third, the discipline is shared: Tool Dice scale with character level (d6 to d12), attunement costs one hour and one cursed-tool slot, and activated properties spend the wielder's own CE. Mark this: the treasures are mirrors, not doors. They show you more of yourself, never someone else.

Rules integration

Shared Rulings

Black Flash Direct melee attacks with a Sacred Treasure can Black Flash under the campaign's normal rules. Save-only Release effects cannot.
Maximum Output Activated tool abilities do not automatically qualify for Maximum Output. If an ability explicitly channels the wielder's Innate Technique, that underlying technique may use its normal Maximum Output rules.
Destruction These are legendary objects. Ordinary damage should not destroy them. A deliberate Special Grade effect, artifact-breaking ability, or major narrative event may damage one, creating a repair quest rather than deleting it casually.
Two Techniques Tool abilities are not Innate Techniques and therefore do not count against a character's CT limit. They still share actions, CE, concentration, and other normal resources.
