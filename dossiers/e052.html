Core law

What the technique actually does.

Rules are written to plug into the finalized campaign glossary: normal actions stay normal, direct attacks roll, and only named Domain events bypass the initial roll.

Passive

Blade Lifesteal

Passive: once on each direct melee hit that deals HP damage, regain HP equal to one Scarlet Die, maximum healing per turn equal to PB × Scarlet Die maximum. She cannot heal above half maximum HP from this feature.

Once on each direct melee hit that deals HP damage, regain HP equal to one Scarlet Die, maximum healing per turn equal to PB × Scarlet Die maximum. You cannot heal above half maximum HP from this feature.

On technique hit

Rot Mark

On technique hit: certain features add Rot Marks, maximum 3. At 3 marks the target contracts Scarlet Rot for 1 minute and marks reset to 0.

Certain features add Rot Marks, maximum 3. At 3 marks the target contracts Scarlet Rot for 1 minute and marks reset to 0.

Condition-like technique effect

Scarlet Rot

The condition-like technique effect: at the start of each turn, take 1 Scarlet Die + CE modifier necrotic/poison damage and healing received is halved. At the end of each turn make a Constitution save; two total successes end it. Fire damage grants advantage on the next save. A creature can suffer only one Scarlet Rot instance from her.

Name note: a final alias for Scarlet Rot is pending coordinator choice; “Scarlet Rot” remains the working name.

At start of each turn, take 1 Scarlet Die + CE modifier necrotic/poison damage and healing received is halved. When an ability lists two damage types, the user chooses which applies when the damage is dealt. At end of each turn make Constitution save; two total successes end it. Fire damage grants advantage on the next save. A creature can suffer only one Scarlet Rot instance from you.

Field notes. Subject: the warrior who would not fall, Scarlet Bloom is pride given a second body. Three truths govern her. First, every clean cut feeds the myth: Blade Lifesteal heals her on each direct melee hit, and her cursed energy learned its shape around a warrior who would not fall, so the myth is returned to her blade by blade. Second, the rot is the price, always intended: Rot Marks accumulate to three, and the third contracts Scarlet Rot, the price the technique meant to collect, finally collected. Third, the bloom is the apotheosis: the first scarlet aeonia, the goddess form at the edge of death, and the third bloom, when the myth outgrows the sorcerer. Mark it: it is one person’s refusal to lose, fermented, and the rot is not decay imposed from without.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

These rulings are designed around the revised Player Guide rather than the raw PDF where the two differ.

Damage-type chooser: when an ability lists two damage types, the user chooses which applies when the damage is dealt.
Lifesteal requires actual HP damage; hitting barriers, temp HP only, or immune targets does not heal unless HP is lost.
Scarlet Rot damage is ongoing and cannot Black Flash.
Goddess Form is not ordinary RCT and can function even if RCT is unavailable, unless the effect specifically prevents revival/transformation.
Bloom count is narrative-permanent and should not be farmed by staged self-knockouts.
Technique DC: 8 + proficiency bonus + your chosen Cursed Energy ability modifier (Wisdom or Charisma unless your class says otherwise).
Direct criticals: rolled weapon, unarmed, and CE attacks can critical normally. After a qualifying critical, roll the campaign's unmodified Black Flash confirmation d20; a natural 20 triples the completed critical damage. Autonomous Domain events, damage-over-time, hazards, and unrolled pulses cannot Black Flash.
Maximum Output: unless a feature says otherwise, double that feature's base CE cost and enhance one supported axis—damage, range, duration, or accuracy/DC—using the campaign's Maximum Output rules. Named Maximum Techniques and Domains do not receive generic Maximum Output.
Technique Focus: effects marked Focus occupy the shared Technique Focus slot. Opening a Domain ends other Focus effects unless the Domain expressly preserves them.
Two-technique characters: these guides assume each technique progresses from character/class level as agreed by the campaign. Multiclassing itself does not grant another Innate Technique.
