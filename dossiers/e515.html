Illusive Mirage

Your technique lets you curse people and make them see illusions.

Power tierTier II · Controller
Combat roleController
Core engineCurse-and-illusion warfare
DomainHouse of Mirrors (18th)

Field notes. Subject: the lie that eats the battlefield. Three truths govern Illusive Mirage. First: it never strikes the body — it strikes the witness. Its cursed energy curses the senses: faces distort, fists pass through doubles, the floor lies about being a floor. Being believed is stronger than being right. Second: every lie is save-gated. Charisma saves to resist the curse, Intelligence saves to see through it, Investigation or Perception to discern it — the technique dismantles stronger opponents through misdirection, never through statistics. Third: the House of Mirrors is the lie made inescapable. A non-lethal sure-hit of chosen illusions, with Mirror's Spite turning attackers against their own allies — disclosure being the Domain's only mercy. Consider: gaslighting, given cursed energy. Reality is whatever the cursed agree to see.

Full progression

Feature map

1Cursed Illusion.

Cursed Illusion

The founding curse. For two CE, a face warps — and with it, a mind. Confusion, disadvantage, broken concentration: the witness, not the body, is the target. As an action for 2 CE, curse a creature within 60 feet. The target makes a Charisma saving throw against your CE save DC. On a failure, the next person they see has a distorted face. The first time the cursed creature sees the distortion, it makes an Intelligence saving throw; on a failure it is confused and has disadvantage on attack rolls and on Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma checks until the end of its next turn. The cursed creature repeats the Charisma save at the end of each of its turns, ending the curse on a success.

Cursed tracking

A failed Charisma save is also a thread. Four hundred feet of general location, held until the curse breaks — she always knows where the lie found purchase. A creature that fails the Charisma save can be tracked: while cursed, you know its general location up to 400 feet away, and it keeps seeing the distorted face until it succeeds on the save or leaves your tracking range. At 6th level, you may target a number of creatures up to your CE modifier with one action, spending 2 CE for each; a creature that fails its save also has disadvantage on Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma saving throws and loses concentration on any technique or feature it is concentrating on.

3Self Distortion.

Phase Fist

A false weapon drawn beside the true one — miss and the real strike lands doubled, hit and it lands with advantage. The lie frames the truth; the truth does the cutting. As part of making a melee attack, spend 3 CE to create a duplicate of your weapon. Make a melee cursed-energy attack roll at disadvantage, then make your normal attack against the same target. If the phase attack misses, the real attack deals double the damage dice on a hit; if the phase attack hits, the real attack is instead made at advantage.

Blurring

Her body becomes a smear on the world's retina. Sight-dependent attackers swing at disadvantage — and what cannot see her cannot aim at her. As an action for 3 CE, your body becomes blurred, shifting and wavering to all who can see you for a number of rounds equal to your CE modifier. A creature that can see you has disadvantage on attack rolls against you. An attacker is immune to this effect if it does not rely on sight (such as blindsight) or can see through illusions (such as truesight).

Distorting Disguise

Self, revised. For hours equal to her CE modifier, she is whoever the witness agrees she is — until touch, or a careful eye, calls the bluff. For 3 CE, make yourself — including clothing, armor, weapons, and other belongings — look different for a number of hours equal to your CE modifier. The changes fail to hold up to physical inspection: objects pass through added features, and touch reveals the truth. To discern the disguise, a creature uses its action to inspect your appearance and must succeed on an Intelligence (Investigation) check against your CE save DC.

6Object Distortion.

Object Distortion

Objects, likewise, can lie — opacity, luster, value, even presence itself. A Medium object's invisibility costs three CE; a creature that runs into it learns the truth the hard way. As an action, choose an object within range of your Cursed Illusion that you can see and distort its appearance — opacity, luster, visible texture, or apparent value. A creature can discern the illusion with an Intelligence (Investigation) check against your CE save DC. You may spend an additional 3 CE to make the object invisible. A creature that runs into a Medium or larger invisible object takes 1d6 bludgeoning damage and loses its reaction. Cost depends on size: Tiny 1 CE, Small 2 CE, Medium 3 CE, Large 4 CE, Huge 6 CE, Gargantuan 10 CE.

11Illusive Terrain.

Illusive Terrain

The battlefield, rewritten. One hundred fifty feet of difficult ground, false walls, severed lines of sight — terrain that charges double movement for the privilege of walking on nothing. As an action for 10 CE, manipulate the terrain around you in a 150-foot radius. Rough Ground: the terrain appears difficult to traverse — a creature moving through the area spends twice as much movement per 5 feet of travel. Obstructing Illusion: as an action, create a false structure or wall within range; creatures convinced it is real cannot target you with features that require line of sight. A creature can see through the illusion with an Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Perception) check against your CE save DC — a Sorcerer makes the check with its CE modifier in place of the normal ability modifier. A creature with truesight or similar automatically succeeds.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

The inescapable version. Chosen illusions as a non-lethal sure-hit, and Mirror's Spite: attack her inside it and your own strike may be redirected to a friend. Disclosure is the Domain's only mercy. Domain Expansion — House of Mirrors: 18 — full-turn action, 20 CE; 60-foot-radius enclosed Domain centered on you; 1 minute; Burnout 10 rounds. Sure-Hit Event — House of Mirrors: at initiative count 20 each round, every creature of your choice inside the Domain falls under an illusion of your choice. This is a non-lethal sure-hit: the illusion is disclosed to each affected creature as it takes hold, and without explicit text it cannot cause instant death, permanent resource loss, or unavoidable violence. A creature protected by an anti-domain defense makes an Intelligence or Wisdom saving throw vs your CE save DC (invented); on a success it sees through the illusion until the start of its next turn. Standing law — Mirror's Spite: while the Domain stands, when a creature attacks you, you may use your reaction to trick it into targeting a random creature within range instead. Roll a die with one face per creature in range: on a 1 the attack still targets you; on any higher roll it targets the creature assigned to that face. If no other creature is in range, roll a d2: on a 2 the attack veers off in a random direction and misses you completely.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it.

Prerequisite: Must have Cursed Illusion

Funny Faces

The goofy variant. Two extra CE makes the distorted face so absurd the Intelligence save is made at disadvantage — comedy as a weapon of war. When a cursed creature sees a distorted face, you may spend 2 CE to force its Intelligence saving throw to be made at disadvantage by making the face look extra goofy. You may also spend 1 CE to change the distortion, forcing the creature to make an additional Intelligence save on its next turn if it sees a distorted face.

Prerequisite: Must have Self Distortion

Cursed Tracking

The mark, refined. Forgo the illusions and spend three more CE: a hidden 24-hour mark, precise location within three hundred feet plus proficiency — the curse as a leash. When a creature is cursed by your technique, you may spend an additional 3 CE to forgo the illusion effects and enhance the tracking instead. A mark appears somewhere on the target's body, out of sight and without its knowledge; it can become aware of the curse with an Intelligence (Investigation) check against your CE save DC, or if someone points it out. The mark lasts 24 hours, and while active you always know the target's precise location within 300 feet + 100 feet × your proficiency bonus. At the start of each day you may spend 5 CE to keep the mark active another 24 hours, provided the target is within range. If the target leaves your range, you cannot track it until it re-enters.

Prerequisite: Must have Illusive Terrain

Maximum: Absolute Perspective

The lie, total. All senses fooled for a full minute, the mind rationalizing every inconsistency — and once per turn, a harmful illusion that bites as psychic damage. For 10 CE, force a creature in range to make a CE save at disadvantage. On a failure, you completely fool all of its senses for 1 minute: you may freely create illusions around the target as if it were under your Cursed Illusion or Illusive Terrain, it automatically fails checks to determine the illusions are fake as its mind rationalizes inconsistencies, and once per turn when you create a harmful illusion the target takes psychic damage equal to your proficiency bonus in d6s.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier II: it can dismantle a much stronger opponent through misdirection, but every lie is save-gated and sight-dependent — its power comes from deception and setup, not raw statistics (invented).
CE save: V6's 'Cursed Energy saving throw' is a saving throw against your CE save DC, made at disadvantage where stated (invented clarification).
Discernment: Intelligence (Investigation) or Wisdom (Perception) checks to see through this technique's illusions are made against your CE save DC unless a feature states otherwise.

Appendix

Additional material.

Illusive Mirage
Your technique lets you curse people and make them see illusions.

Cursed Illusion — At 1st level, as an action for 2 cursed energy, you can curse a creature within 60 feet (Charisma save). On a failure, the next person they see has a distorted face; on first seeing it, Intelligence save or confused with disadvantage on attack rolls and Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma checks until the end of their next turn. The Charisma save repeats at the end of their turns. A failed save also lets you track the creature to 400 feet. At 6th level, target up to your CE modifier creatures (2 CE each); failures also impose disadvantage on Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma saves and break concentration.

Self Distortion — At 3rd level: Phase Fist (part of a melee attack, 3 CE: duplicate weapon, melee cursed attack at disadvantage, then normal attack; phase miss doubles the real attack's damage dice on a hit, phase hit grants advantage); Blurring (action, 3 CE: disadvantage on attacks against you for CE-modifier rounds; blindsight/truesight immune); Distorting Disguise (3 CE: changed appearance for CE-modifier hours; fails physical inspection; discern with action + Intelligence (Investigation) vs CE save DC).

Object Distortion — At 6th level, as an action distort a visible object's appearance (Investigation vs CE save DC to discern); +3 CE for invisibility; running into a Medium+ invisible object deals 1d6 bludgeoning and costs the reaction. Size costs: Tiny 1, Small 2, Medium 3, Large 4, Huge 6, Gargantuan 10.

Illusive Terrain — At 11th level, action for 10 CE, 150-ft radius: Rough Ground (double movement cost per 5 ft) and Obstructing Illusion (action: false structure/wall blocks line-of-sight targeting). See through with Investigation or Perception vs CE save DC (Sorcerers use CE modifier); truesight auto-succeeds.

Domain Expansion: House of Mirrors — At 18th level, at initiative 20 force all creatures to fall under an illusion of your choice; as a reaction, trick a creature that attacks you into targeting a random creature within range (die roll; on a 1 you are still targeted; no other creatures in range: d2, on a 2 the attack misses in a random direction).

Feats — Funny Faces (Cursed Illusion: 2 CE for disadvantage on the Intelligence save; 1 CE to change the distortion, forcing another Intelligence save next turn); Cursed Tracking (Self Distortion: +3 CE, forgo illusions for a hidden 24-hour mark; precise location within 300 ft + 100 ft × PB; 5 CE/day to extend); Maximum: Absolute Perspective (Illusive Terrain: 10 CE, CE save at disadvantage; all senses fooled 1 minute, auto-failed discernment, PB d6s psychic once per turn from harmful illusions).
