Fermentation

Core law, patience. You bottle cursed energy and let it age — the longer it sits, the stronger and more volatile it becomes. A fresh brew is a skirmish drink; a vintage aged across a long rest is a problem; a grand cru you have been cellaring for a week will level the block. Time is the only ingredient you cannot fake, and the table is already tracking it.

Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, patience. You seal cursed energy into bottles and let time do the work. A bottle's potency is its age — the table already tracks rounds, rests, and days, so there is no pool, no counter, no engine. Just the bottle and how long it has been waiting.

Aging ladder · Fresh (brewed this turn): 1d8 acid · Young (1+ rounds old): 2d8 acid · Mature (survived a short rest): 4d8 acid, 10-foot splash · Vintage (survived a long rest): 6d8 acid, 15-foot splash, lingering fumes · Grand Cru (aged 7+ days): 10d10 acid, 20-foot splash.

Volatility · Mature and older bottles are volatile: if you take damage while carrying one, roll a d20 — on a 1, a random bottle breaks at your feet. Vintage and older are unstable: they also break if you fall prone or critically fail a Dexterity save. You carry PB bottles safely in a padded bandolier; beyond that, every jostle risks the cellar.

Field notes. Subject: a brewer, not a brawler. Three truths govern the cellar. First: every bottle is a promise made to the future — the brewer fights today's battle with last week's patience. Second: volatility is the tax on power — the same age that makes a vintage devastating makes it treacherous, and a brewmaster who cannot carry their own cellar is just a bomb with legs. Third: the technique rewards the long game so completely that the clever brewer starts every campaign already aging. The war begins at the workbench, weeks before the first shot.

Progression

Levels 1–18.

1First Batch

Bonus action · 1 CE · Brew a fresh bottle. When you take the Attack action, you may replace one attack with a thrown bottle: ranged CE attack (60 ft), acid damage by the bottle's age tier on a hit. Bonus action · Drink a bottle: gain temporary HP equal to half its throw damage (rounded down).

The brew is barely a weapon. It is lunch, packed early, in a bottle that happens to hate everything it touches.

3The Palate

Bonus action · 1 CE · Taste the air, a liquid, or the residue on a creature within 30 feet. You learn a creature's approximate CE reserve (full, half, spent), whether it is concentrating on an effect, and you identify any poison, disease, potion, or brewed concoction by taste — including CE residue hidden in food or drink.

The brewer's second sense has nothing to do with throwing. The tongue knows what the eyes cannot: how much cursed energy is in the room, and exactly whose drink it is sitting in.

6Cask Finish

You gain a personal cask: one bottle at a time may age inside it at double rate — it counts as twice its actual age for potency. Passive · Brewing improves: you may brew a fresh bottle as a free action once per turn (instead of a bonus action), and fresh bottles deal 1d8 + WIS acid.

The foundation gets stronger while the new tool arrives. The quick brew was never abandoned — it just learned to keep up.

11Master's Cellar

You may hold one Grand Cru at a time — the cellar's pride. Brewing another requires uncorking the first. Action · 5 CE · Flash-age a bottle: advance it one age step instantly. Flash-aged bottles are unstable: they break if you roll a 1 on any d20 while carrying them, and on a natural 1 on the throw attack they detonate at your feet.

High power, honestly limited. The week of waiting cannot be bought with cursed energy — only cheated, and cheating has a blast radius.

18Ageless Cellar

Passive · Your bottles never spoil, never lose potency, and never go stale — age only ever moves in one direction. You carry twice as many safely (2 × PB). Brewing costs 1 less CE (minimum 1 CE).

Not a bigger bomb — the removal of every constraint the cellar ever had. The brewmaster stops managing risk and starts managing inventory.

Pinnacle

Maximum.

Maximum, The Perfect Vintage (action, 10 CE): uncork your oldest Vintage-or-better bottle — the patience of weeks, spent in a second. Thrown: the detonation deals the bottle's damage × 1.5 in a doubled radius, and the fumes linger for 1 minute (heavily obscured; 2d8 acid to creatures that start their turn inside). Drunk instead: the brewmaster's apotheosis — gain the bottle's throw damage as temporary HP, advantage on all saving throws, and your brews deal +1d8 for 1 minute. The setup is the limiter: the Maximum is only ever as devastating as your patience made it.

Feats

Unconventional applications.

Hair of the Dog

Action · 2 CE · Brew a restorative tonic instead of a weapon: the drinker ends one poison or disease, or gains advantage on saving throws against poison for 1 hour. Brewing, but as medicine — the same craft that burns throats can also close wounds.

Spiked

When a creature drinks one of your bottles — willingly, tricked, or slipped into their own cup (Sleight of Hand vs. their Perception) — you decide at serving time whether it nourishes or harms. The same bottle, your call, their surprise. Brewing, but as a trick.

Last Call

Bonus action · 1 CE · Crack a bottle without throwing it: a 15-foot radius of clinging vapor for 1 minute (heavily obscured; 1d8 acid to creatures that enter or start their turn inside). Brewing, but as a smokescreen — the bar closes, and nobody sees who left.

Mother Culture

You keep a living starter — the mother of every bottle you will ever brew. Once per long rest, brew a bottle without spending CE (the culture provides). Your bottles never go stale from age, and the starter remembers your recipes: you always know exactly how old every bottle you brewed is. Brewing, but as self-sufficiency.
