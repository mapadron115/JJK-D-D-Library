Original Codex

Fermentation

Core law, patience. You bottle cursed energy and let it age — the longer it sits, the stronger and more generous it becomes. A fresh brew is a skirmish drink; a vintage aged across two long rests is a lifeline; a grand cru you have been cellaring for two weeks will carry the whole party through the fight. Time is the only ingredient you cannot fake, and the table is already tracking it. Drinking is the technique. Throwing is the afterthought.

Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, patience. You seal cursed energy into bottles and let time do the work. A bottle's potency is its age — the table already tracks rounds, rests, and days, so there is no pool, no counter, no engine. Just the bottle and how long it has been waiting. Drinking is the technique: a bottle drunk restores and cleanses, and the older the bottle, the deeper the restoration. Throwing one is the afterthought: a flat splash of acid and a patch of control, the same at every age.

Aging ladder · Fresh (brewed this turn): drink → 1d8 temporary HP · Young (at least 2 rounds old): drink → 2d8 temporary HP · Mature (survived a short rest): drink → 4d8 temporary HP + end one poison or disease · Vintage (survived at least 2 long rests): drink → 6d8 temporary HP + end up to two poisons or diseases · Grand Cru (aged at least 14 days): drink → 10d10 temporary HP + end all poisons and diseases. Thrown at any age: 1d8 acid on a hit; Mature and older add a splash (Mature 10 ft, Vintage 15 ft, Grand Cru 20 ft), and Vintage and older leave lingering fumes (heavily obscured, 1 minute).

Volatility · Mature and older bottles are volatile: if you take damage while carrying one, roll a d20 — on a 1, a random bottle breaks at your feet. Vintage and older are unstable: they also break if you fall prone or critically fail a Dexterity save. You carry PB bottles safely in a padded bandolier; beyond that, every jostle risks the cellar. If you can safely store bottles in another container (such as a storage curse), they do not count against the bandolier's limit and are protected from volatility while stored; you may store up to that container's carrying capacity, provided the stored bottles' total weight does not exceed your carrying weight limit.

Field notes. Subject: a brewer, not a brawler. Three truths govern the cellar. First: every bottle is a promise made to the future — the brewer fights today's battle with last week's patience. Second: volatility is the tax on patience — the same age that makes a vintage a lifeline makes it treacherous, and a brewmaster who cannot carry their own cellar is just a walking spill. Third: the technique rewards the long game so completely that the clever brewer starts every campaign already aging. The war begins at the workbench, weeks before the first shot.

Progression

Levels 1–18.

1First Batch

Bonus action · 1 CE · Brew a fresh bottle. When you take the Attack action, you may replace one attack with a thrown bottle: ranged CE attack (60 ft), 1d8 acid damage on a hit (see Aging ladder for splash and fumes). Bonus action · Drink a bottle: gain temporary HP and cleansing by the bottle's age tier (see Aging ladder).

The brew is barely a weapon. It is lunch, packed early, in a bottle that happens to love everyone it touches.

3The Palate

Bonus action · 1 CE · Taste the air, a liquid, or the residue on a creature within 30 feet. You learn a creature's approximate CE reserve (full, half, spent), whether it is concentrating on an effect, and you identify any poison, disease, potion, or brewed concoction by taste — including CE residue hidden in food or drink.

The brewer's second sense has nothing to do with throwing. The tongue knows what the eyes cannot: how much cursed energy is in the room, and exactly whose drink it is sitting in.

Flavor Profiles · When you brew a bottle, choose its flavor profile: Bright, Bold, Smooth, or Fiery. When a creature drinks the bottle, they gain the profile's effect in addition to the bottle's other drink effects. Bright: +1 to their next attack roll. Bold: +1d4 to their next damage roll. Smooth: advantage on their next ability check. Fiery: resistance to fire damage until the end of their next turn, and the drinker's attacks deal fire damage until the end of their next turn. Unused profile effects expire at the end of the drinker's next turn.

Every bottle has a flavor, and every flavor has a job.

6Cask Finish

You gain a personal cask: one bottle at a time may age inside it at double rate — it counts as twice its actual age for potency. Passive · Brewing improves: you may brew a fresh bottle as a free action once per turn (instead of a bonus action). When a creature drinks one of your bottles, they gain advantage on the first d20 roll they make during their next turn. (At L11, this improves to advantage on all d20 rolls during their next turn.)

The foundation gets stronger while the new tool arrives. The quick brew was never abandoned — it just learned to keep up.

11Master's Cellar

You may hold up to four Grand Crus at a time — the cellar's pride. Brewing a fifth requires uncorking one. Action · 5 CE · Flash-age a bottle: advance it one age step instantly. Flash-aged bottles are unstable: they break if you roll a 1 on any d20 while carrying them, and on a natural 1 on the throw attack they detonate at your feet. When a creature drinks a Vintage bottle, they gain +2 to one ability score of their choice for 1 minute. When a creature drinks a Grand Cru bottle, they gain +2 to all ability scores and +1 to AC for 1 minute. The drink advantage improves: a creature drinking one of your bottles gains advantage on all d20 rolls they make during their next turn (instead of just the first).

High power, honestly limited. The two weeks of waiting cannot be bought with cursed energy — only cheated, and cheating has a blast radius.

18Ageless Cellar

Passive · Your bottles never spoil, never lose potency, and never go stale — age only ever moves in one direction. You carry twice as many safely (2 × PB). Brewing costs 1 less CE (minimum 1 CE). When a creature drinks one of your bottles, they gain +1 to AC and +10 feet of movement speed for 1 minute.

Not a bigger bottle — the removal of every constraint the cellar ever had. The brewmaster stops managing risk and starts managing inventory.

Pinnacle

Maximum.

Maximum, The Perfect Vintage (action, 10 CE): uncork your oldest Vintage-or-better bottle — the patience of weeks, spent in a second. Drunk: the brewmaster's apotheosis — gain the bottle's drink effect (temporary HP + cleansing), advantage on all saving throws, and +2 to all ability scores for 1 minute. Thrown instead: the bottle detonates in a doubled radius — 1d8 acid damage — and the fumes linger for 1 minute (heavily obscured; creatures that start their turn inside have disadvantage on saving throws). The setup is the limiter: the Maximum is only ever as generous as your patience made it.

Feats

Unconventional applications.

Hair of the Dog

Action · 2 CE · Brew a restorative tonic instead of a weapon: the drinker ends one poison or disease, or gains advantage on saving throws against poison for 1 hour. Brewing, but as medicine — the same craft that burns throats can also close wounds.

Spiked

When a creature drinks one of your bottles — willingly, tricked, or slipped into their own cup (Sleight of Hand vs. their Perception) — you decide at serving time whether it nourishes or harms. The same bottle, your call, their surprise. Brewing, but as a trick.

Last Call

Bonus action · 1 CE · Crack a bottle without throwing it: a 15-foot radius of clinging vapor for 1 minute (heavily obscured; 1d8 acid to creatures that enter or start their turn inside). Brewing, but as a smokescreen — the bar closes, and nobody sees who left.

Mother Culture

You keep a living starter — the mother of every bottle you will ever brew. Once per long rest, brew a bottle without spending CE (the culture provides). Your bottles never go stale from age, and the starter remembers your recipes: you always know exactly how old every bottle you brewed is. Brewing, but as self-sufficiency.
