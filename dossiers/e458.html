Properties & techniques

Exact player-facing procedures

Calamitous Strikes

Passive · +3 to attack and damage rolls with the claws; each attack deals 1d12 bludgeoning, slashing, or piercing damage (your choice per attack).

Calamity does not finesse. These claws land like a collapsed bridge, heavy, certain, and agnostic about the kind of ruin: bludgeoning, slashing, or piercing, the wielder's choice on each attack. When attacking with the Claws of Calamity you gain a +3 to attack and damage rolls, and deal 1d12 bludgeoning, slashing, or piercing damage.

Ungranted Mercy

Passive · The damage required for Limb Removal for arms increases by 20 when you are the attacker.

The mercy is ungranted to the victim: twenty more damage must be done before an arm is torn away, and the increase follows the wearer's hand. The damage required for Limb Removal for arms increases by 20.

Field notes. Subject: gauntlets shaped like disaster. Two truths. First: they are calamity made wearable, a landslide does not choose where it lands, and these claws strike with the same indiscriminate weight: +3 to strike and to wound, a d12 of ruin in whichever flavor the hand prefers, chosen blow by blow. Second, the stranger truth: the increase to the arm-severing threshold follows the wearer's hand, not the wearer's body, the claws demand twenty more damage before an arm parts from its owner, and the demand rides with the attacker. Assess them correctly: this is not a brawler's tool but a siege weapon with a handle, built to break armies at the arm joints. The disaster gives; the disaster also withholds.

Campaign canon notes

Damage type [INVENTED]: the source lists three damage types without saying who chooses. Choose bludgeoning, slashing, or piercing when you make each attack.
Limb Removal threshold [INVENTED]: the source adds "20" to "the damage required for Limb Removal for arms" without a base threshold. Read as +20 to the arm-severing damage threshold under the campaign's Limb Removal rules, the increase applies when the wearer is the attacker, not when they are attacked.
