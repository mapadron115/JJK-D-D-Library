Feature map

Level-by-level features, as the figure refined them.

L1

Laying Plans

Bonus action · 1 CE.

Tier V · Controller (strategist) · Suggested CE ability: WIS · Primary verb: Decide. Technique DC = 8 + proficiency bonus + WIS modifier. Your technique attack bonus = proficiency bonus + WIS modifier.

All warfare is deception, but this is older than deception: this is decision, made before the enemy has finished choosing. Declare a Plan: a specific, observable IF trigger — something that has not yet happened and can be witnessed — a declared response, and a designated Responder: any creature you can see within 120 feet, or yourself. The Responder does not consent, and there is no saving throw: the prediction is the restriction. If the trigger does not occur exactly as declared, the Plan simply never fires — Heaven holds only accurate declarations, and an inaccurate one is cursed energy spent on a future that did not happen. There is no limit to the number of pending Plans you may hold — Heaven's ledger is exactly as deep as your cursed energy. The only caps on your preparation are what you can pay to declare (1 CE each) and what you can pay when it all comes due. A pending Plan lasts until it triggers or you finish a long rest. A Plan's response must be something the Responder could do — Heaven enforces answers, never nullification: a Plan cannot declare that a technique, Maximum, or Domain simply fails.

When the trigger occurs, the Plan fires immediately: the Responder performs the declared response — one action, or movement up to their speed. Fire cost: when a Plan resolves, you pay 3 CE. If the Responder accepts Tempo Debt (see below), the fire cost is 1 CE instead. The fire cost is paid whether the response succeeds or fails. The perfect-prediction rule: if the declared trigger occurs exactly as specified and the response resolves as declared — the GM adjudicates — the prediction was perfect, and Heaven waives the CE: the fire cost is 0 — and this waiver takes precedence over any minimum fire cost stated elsewhere in this technique. Being right is its own economy, and the General is not charged for it. But the Tempo Debt is never waived. If the Responder borrowed against their next turn, they owe it in full, perfect prediction or not — the debt is the ledger that caps this art, and Heaven always collects it. A creature that owes Tempo Debt cannot be designated as a Responder.

The slip. Formulating a Plan manifests a slip of cursed paper in your hand, and your declaration writes itself across it in brush script — the IF, the THEN, the Responder's name. The slip then vanishes from your hand; Heaven holds the only copy. When the Plan fires, the slip reappears in front of the Responder an instant before they move, its writing legible only to them, and burns to ash as they act. It is the technique's only visible tell: by the time anyone sees the paper, the answer is already happening.

L1

Tempo Debt

Passive.

Time borrowed must be repaid. The universe keeps accounts, and it has never once been cheated. When a Responder accepts Tempo Debt to cheapen a Plan's fire cost, they owe the economy they borrowed: at the start of their next turn, the debt is repaid automatically — they lose the corresponding action (if the response used an action) or their movement (if the response was movement). A Responder can owe at most one debt at a time and cannot accept another while one is outstanding. There is no way to default, and no way to refuse repayment: the debt is not a choice the future gets to revisit. There is no exception: not a perfect prediction, not a Maximum, not a Domain forgives, reduces, or delays a Tempo Debt. A perfect prediction waives the General's price in cursed energy; it never touches the Responder's debt. The debt is what caps the art — a borrowed creature cannot be borrowed again until it has paid, and the army of the owed is always smaller than the army of the willing moment. Heaven always collects.

L3

Linked Plans

Passive.

Chain the answers so that one response becomes the next question. When one of your Plans fires, you may immediately formulate a new Plan keyed to the outcome of the response that just resolved, paying the normal 1 CE formulation cost; formulating this way requires no action. There is no limit to how many of your Plans may resolve in a single round, and no limit to how long the chain runs — each link is bought twice, once when you declare it and once when it fires, and the chain ends only when your cursed energy does. This is the whole of the art: a prediction accurate enough to cause the next event, and the next, each answer laying the board for the question after it.

L6

Revealed Strategy

Passive.

Show them the trap. Knowing the trap and avoiding the trap are different skills, and the difference has been priced. When you formulate a Plan, you may declare it openly — speaking its trigger and its response aloud so that enemies can hear. If an enemy who heard the declaration knowingly causes the trigger to occur anyway, that Plan's fire cost is reduced by 1 CE (minimum 1): Heaven holds a witnessed vow more cheaply. Also, once per round, when a creature you have already seen act in this combat declares an action, you may learn whether its declared intent is genuine (1 CE, no action) — the General has read this page before, and the page charges a coin a reading. Nothing in this feature — or in Heaven's accounting — requires a spoken declaration to correspond to a Plan you have actually formulated. You may announce Plans you have not made, in the same voice, with the same certainty, and no effect, inspection, or insight can distinguish the announcement of a true Plan from an invented one: Heaven holds the slips, and Heaven does not show its hand. A declared Plan therefore never has to be real. It only has to be believed — and disbelieving the wrong one is how armies walk into the real one with their eyes open.

L11

Distributed Command

Action · 1 CE per officer.

Lend the answers to officers you trust, until the whole army moves as one mind. Designate up to your proficiency bonus in officers among creatures you could designate as Responders; the designation lasts until you finish your next long rest. An officer holds your Plans as if they were their own for designation purposes: a Plan may designate any Responder within 1 mile of one of its officers, even if that Responder is beyond your own sight. An officer remains a valid Responder for your Plans as normal.

L18

No Plan Survives Contact

Reaction · 2 CE · once per round.

The answer does not break on contact with reality. It updates. When a Plan's trigger fails to occur as declared, or its designated Responder cannot execute the declared response, you may rewrite that Plan's trigger and response on the spot. The rewritten Plan remains pending under all its original terms. A disproved prediction is not a defeat. It is information, purchased at the price of being wrong once.

L18

Supreme Excellence

Passive.

The field itself becomes a place you are already three moves inside. Formulating a Plan no longer requires you to see the Responder — you must know where they are. And when one of your Plans proves a perfect prediction, you may immediately formulate a new Plan without spending an action, paying the normal 1 CE: perfection lays the next stone itself. Supreme excellence is not winning a hundred battles. It is having decided them all before the first.

Maximum Technique

Subdue the Enemy Without Fighting

Action · 10 CE · once per long rest.

The supreme art of war is to subdue the enemy without fighting. Every hostile creature within 120 feet makes a WIS save against your technique DC. On a failure, for 1 minute the creature cannot willingly take a hostile action against you or your allies; it repeats the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on itself on a success. The effect ends for a creature if it takes damage, and it ends for all creatures the moment you or an ally takes a hostile action against any creature — the General's peace holds only while the General keeps it. On a success, a creature is unaffected.

Domain Expansion

Victory Already Decided — The War Room Beneath Heaven

Full-turn action · 20 CE · 1,000 clash points · burnout 3 rounds · duration 3 minutes · 120-ft open radius · disclosed.

A war room beneath Heaven, open to the sky that witnesses it. The room harms no one. It requires one thing: inside it, no creature can deliberately falsify a course of action it declares aloud. A creature may stay silent — silence is always legal — but what it has declared, it declared truly, and Heaven marks the difference. At initiative 20, the sure-hit Strategic Error: choose up to your proficiency bonus in hostile creatures in the Domain; each must declare aloud the action it will attempt this round. If a chosen creature abandons its declaration before the next initiative 20, you may immediately formulate a Plan against it and fire that Plan at half its normal fire cost (rounded up, minimum 1 CE), with a Responder inside the Domain. The War Room forces nothing. It simply makes changing your mind the most dangerous thing on the field.

Level 20 · Capstone

Master of the Field

Once per long rest · action · 5 CE.

Every prepared answer whose moment has arrived, resolved in the order of your choosing. Not an attack — a conclusion. For 1 minute, formulating Plans requires no action — you may declare as many as you can pay for, in any moment, even inside the chain of their own resolutions — your Plans fire at the 1 CE Tempo Debt rate, the Responder owing the debt in full as always, and you may key a Plan's trigger to anything a creature you can perceive could observe, as if you stood where it stands. For one minute, the field is a place you have already finished deciding.
