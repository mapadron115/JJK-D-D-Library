At-the-table character reference

Recovered printed values & campaign clarifications

Vow scope and stacking:

No vow on allies without consent. Unknowing targets cannot break an unheard vow. No stacking vows on the same target.

Retrial limits:

Retrial requires a vow active and a spell you could otherwise cast (caster level available). It cannot reduce costs below what Retrial could itself pay (binding-vow-style cost limits apply). Once per long rest covers both dismissal and re-creation.

Reconsideration:

Ending a Binding Vow with Reconsideration triggers neither party's broken-vow consequences.

Theft-Proof scope:

"You cannot use your technique in any other way while it is active" covers all other technique features except ending the effect, and it still ends as a bonus action.

Vow-feat rulings:

Forcible Confession requires willing and able communication, no coercion bypass, and “truthful words” means no lies. Cunning Judicator triggers only on an actually broken vow; “1d4 per remaining hit point” means up to the creature’s HP total, with the GM assigning the final number.

Field notes. Subject: the auditor of souls. Three truths govern her. First, the contract is the weapon: a Binding Vow, forged as a bonus action on a creature within thirty feet that hears and understands, and the signature must be unwilling, or there is no point. Second, the escape clauses are the real technique: Retrial halves a spell’s cost once per rest; Reconsideration ends a vow with no consequences, once per rest; Theft-Proof forges the contract with herself, the broken-vow benefits, no target, no other features while it stands. Third, the ledger must balance: the Domain, Black Friday, full-turn, twenty CE, sixty feet, one minute, makes Recreation and Contractual Control free while it stands, and at initiative 20 she draws a random receipt: object, vehicle, reservation, or structure. Maxim: every soul keeps books. She is simply the only one who audits them in combat.

Bonus action · No CE · Forge a Binding Vow with a creature within 30 ft that can hear and understand you and is unwilling to abide by its terms (lasts until broken, Reconsidered, or 24 hrs) · Choose one mutual exchange: movement, unarmed damage dice, reactions/opportunity attacks, or advantage trade · If they break it: your reaction (once/turn) — advantage on all attacks vs them for 1 min, or an extra weapon/unarmed attack as a free action; if you break it, they get the same.

The foundation: a bonus action, a creature within thirty feet that can hear and understand, and the terms must be unwilling, or there is no contract. She does not fight her enemies. She gets them to sign. As a bonus action, you can forge a Binding Vow with your technique and target. The target must be a creature within 30 ft that can hear and understand you. The opponent must be able to understand the vow and be unwilling to abide by its terms. The vow can be broken at any time by either party, and it lasts until broken, ended by Reconsideration, or 24 hours pass. Choose one of the following effects to give your opponent:

• You give up a number of feet of movement equal to 10 × your Charisma modifier and your opponent gives up a number of feet of movement equal to 10 × your Wisdom modifier.

• You give up a number of d6s on your unarmed strikes (not including Reinforced Strikes) and the opponent gives up an equal amount of damage dice on their unarmed strikes.

• You give up your reactions and the opponent gives up the ability to make opportunity attacks.

• You give up advantage on attack rolls against your opponent and your opponent gives up disadvantage against any rolls (only the highest amount applies).

If your opponent breaks the vow, your cursed technique is strengthened. When you perceive the opponent breaking the vow, once per turn as a reaction, you can choose one of the below: • You gain advantage on all attack rolls against them for 1 minute. • You can make an additional weapon attack as a free action. • You can make an additional unarmed strike as a free action. If you break the vow, your cursed technique is weakened. They can choose the same for one minute, once per turn as a reaction.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it.

Prerequisite: Must have Binding Vow

Forcible Confession

Two CE, a bonus action: thirty feet, and the target speaks only truth for a minute, or until a Charisma save ends it. The auditor’s first instrument is the deposition. As a bonus action for 2 CE, you can force a creature within 30 ft to speak only truthful words for 1 minute, or until they succeed on a Charisma saving throw at the end of their turn.

Prerequisite: Must have Retrial

Cunning Judicator

When a creature breaks a vow with her, five CE as a reaction: a Charisma save, or d4s of force per ten hit points remaining. The penalty clause, enforced at the moment of breach. When a creature breaks a Binding Vow with you, you can spend 5 CE as a reaction to force the creature to make a Charisma saving throw. On a failure, it takes 1d4 force damage per 10 hit points it has remaining (rounded down, maximum 10d4).

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier II: a vow-exchange controller with spell-cost halving and a guilt-gated sure-hit, strong at the bargaining table and the battlefield, without calamity output.
Hold, vehicle damage dice: the source references vehicle damage dice by size without supplying a size table. Until such a table is supplied, the technique cannot target vehicles.
Retrial scope: it modifies a Sorcerer spell you are able to cast, it does not grant spell knowledge on its own. The halving applies once, rounded down.

Additional material.

Contractual Recreation
You can forge Binding Vows with your targets and enforce the terms of
the contract.

Cursed Energy
Starting at 1st level, you can harness your negative feelings into a
usable source of energy, called cursed energy. These feelings can be
harnessed into attacks or abilities. The number of cursed energy you
possess is based on your class, some classes use a modifier that
being charisma or wisdom (Player's choice)
Your cursed energy DC is 8 + user cursed energy ability modifier +
proficiency bonus.

Binding Vow
Starting at 1st level, you can forge a Binding Vow with your
technique and target. As a bonus action, you can use your technique to
give one of the below effects to your opponent. The opponent must be
able to understand the vow and be unwilling to abide by its terms.
The vow can be broken at any time by either party.
Effects:
• You give up a number of feet of movement equal to 10 × your
Charisma modifier and your opponent gives up a number of feet of
movement equal to 10 × your Wisdom modifier.
• You give up a number of d6s on your unarmed strikes (not including
Reinforced Strikes) and the opponent gives up an equal amount of
damage dice on their unarmed strikes.
• You give up your reactions and the opponent gives up the ability to
make opportunity attacks.
• You give up advantage on attack rolls against your opponent and
your opponent gives up disadvantage against any rolls (only the
highest amount applies).
If your opponent breaks the vow, your cursed technique is
strengthened. Once per turn as a reaction, you can choose one of the
below:
• You gain advantage on all attack rolls against them for 1 minute.
• You can make an additional weapon attack as a free action.
• You can make an additional unarmed strike as a free action.
If you break the vow, your cursed technique is weakened. They can
choose the same for one minute, once per turn as a reaction.

Retrial
Starting at 3rd level, you can use this vow to recreate the contract
of your technique. Choose a spell from the Sorcerer Spell List that
you are able to cast. Its cursed energy cost is reduced by half
(rounded down). You can use this feature once per long rest.

Reconsideration
At 6th level, you can renegotiate the terms of the contract. As an
action, you can end a Binding Vow with no consequences. You may only
use this feature once per long rest.

Theft-Proof
At 11th level, you can forge an unbreakable contract with yourself.
As a bonus action, you can gain the benefits of a broken Binding Vow
(you gain advantage on all attack rolls against them for 1 minute).
You do not need a target to use this feature. However, you cannot
use your technique in any other way while it is active. You can end
it as a bonus action.

Guilty
At 18th level, as an action for 12 CE you can recreate the domain's
sure hit. Choose a creature. If they are guilty of a crime under the
law of the land (see Guilt below), you suffer no consequences for
breaking a Binding Vow, and your opponent suffers the consequences of
breaking a Binding Vow as if they broke it themselves.
This feature cannot be used in conjunction with Retrial.
