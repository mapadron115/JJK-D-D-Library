Features & procedures

Properties & techniques

Exact player-facing procedures

Action

Deploy the figurine

Take an action to throw the bottle to a point on the ground within 60 feet of yourself. The bottle smashes and the figurine grows into the cursed corpse specified below (a construct). If the space is occupied or too small, the statuette does not transform.

Property

Loyalty and commands

The creature is friendly to you and your allies, understands your languages, and obeys your commands. It takes its turn immediately after you on your Initiative count. With no commands, it defends itself but takes no other actions.

Property

Reversion

The creature lasts for the duration listed on its figurine, then reverts to figurine form. It reverts early if it drops to 0 hit points or if you take an action while touching it to dismiss it. Once reverted, its property cannot be used again until the bottle has reformed around the cursed corpse, as specified in the figurine's description.

Ebony Fly

Mount of the air

The ebony horsefly statuette becomes a Giant Fly for up to 12 hours and can be ridden as a mount. Cannot be used again until 2 days have passed.

Onyx Dog

Sentient hound

The onyx dog statuette becomes a Mastiff for up to 6 hours. It has Intelligence 8, can speak Common, and has Blindsight to 60 feet. Cannot be used again until 7 days have passed.

Silver Raven

Messenger raven

The silver raven statuette becomes a Raven for up to 12 hours. Cannot be used again until 2 days have passed. While in raven form, the figurine grants you the ability to cast Animal Messenger on it.

Serpentine Owl

Far-eyed owl

The serpentine owl statuette becomes a Giant Owl for up to 8 hours. It can communicate telepathically with you at any range while you are on the same plane of existence. Cannot be used again until 2 days have passed.

Observer’s account

Field notes. Subject: a pocket regiment, corked. The bottle holds a statuette; the thrown bottle holds an army, fly, hound, raven, owl, each loyal, each temporary, each rationed by days. Three truths. First, loyalty is absolute but narrow: the corpse obeys and guards its own, yet without orders it only defends itself, a soldier awaiting the word. Second, form follows the bottle: the fly rides for twelve hours, the owl sees across planes, the dog speaks, pick the tool, and the mission is half-decided. Third, every deployment is a clock: reversion comes by duration, by death, or by your own dismissing hand. Hence the maxim: an army you can carry in a bottle is an army you can afford to lose, but never one you can afford to waste.
