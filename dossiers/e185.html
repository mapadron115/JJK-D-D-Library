Hoard-Heart Dragon

Core law

Character engine

The whole arrangement: mark valuables, tools, CE-bearing objects, trophies as the Hoard, stay within thirty feet and Greed accrues to PB, while enemies may steal or relocate the objects to starve her. Mark valuables, cursed tools, CE-bearing objects, or meaningful trophies as your Hoard. Staying near the Hoard generates Greed: gain 1 Greed at the start of your turn while within 30 feet of at least one Hoard Mark, maximum PB (invented); a Hoard Die is a d8 (invented). Enemies can steal or relocate the objects to weaken you.

Technique save DC: 8 + PB + chosen CE ability modifier. Technique attack: PB + chosen CE ability modifier.

Field notes. Subject: avarice made into architecture. Three truths govern her. First: her second body is her property. Marked valuables, cursed tools, trophies, the hoard is where her energy lives, and she stands near it because it is her; steal from it and she pursues like a body chasing a severed limb. Second: greed is her armor and her leash. Greed buys damage shrugged off, dragon's breath, wings, even a refusal to die, but it only accrues within thirty feet of the hoard, and enemies may steal or relocate the objects to starve her. Third: the dragon was never the fantasy. The fantasy was owning something death could not take, and the CE built the dragon to defend it. Consider her as a vault with teeth: peak durability requires risking objects she actually values, and every scale she wears is mortgaged against what she cannot bear to lose.

Full progression

Level progression

1Covetous Scale

The mark itself: the hoard begins with a single claimed thing — and the dragon’s scales are the hoard’s first dividend. Covetous Scale (bonus action, 1 CE): mark an eligible object as part of your Hoard. When you are hit, you may use your reaction and spend Greed to reduce the damage by one Hoard Die per Greed spent, up to PB per round.

3Dragon's Claim

Theft has a scent. The dragon always knows which way her gold went — and she is already moving. A creature that takes a Hoard Mark without your permission reveals its direction to you for 1 hour; as a reaction you may move up to half your speed toward it. Your first hit each turn against that thief deals one additional Hoard Die of damage (invented).

6Wyrm Shape

Wyrm Shape: Bonus action · 3 CE · 1 minute · Grow scales and claws: gain a climb speed, +1 AC, and +5 ft of reach · Action · 1 CE + 2 Greed: exhale a 30-ft cone (invented); each creature inside makes a Constitution save vs your CE DC, taking 3 Hoard Dice poison damage on failure and half on success (invented).

The wyrm-shape: the vault grows teeth, and the breath it exhales is the hoard’s own venom.

11Gold-Sick Immortality

Passive · Once per long rest · While near 3 or more Hoard Marks, when you are reduced to 0 HP: spend 2 CE and at least 3 Greed to remain at 1 HP and gain temp HP equal to 5 per Greed spent (invented).

Near her hoard, the dragon refuses to die: the gold will not let its keeper fall while it can still be spent.

18Domain Expansion: Hall Beneath the Glittering Heath

Domain Expansion · Full-turn action · 20 CE · Enclosed 60-ft radius centered on you · up to 1 minute · Technique Focus · When the barrier opens, every unattended object inside is catalogued · Domain Cycle begins at Claim and advances Claim → Covet → Reckon at initiative 20: Claim — choose up to PB unattended objects; they become Hoard Marks until the Domain ends; Covet — each chosen hostile carrying a Hoard Mark must drop it or become Dragon-Marked until the next initiative 20 (a creature protected from the sure-hit may make a Wisdom save to avoid the mark while keeping the object); Reckon — each Dragon-Marked hostile automatically takes 3 Hoard Dice necrotic (a protected creature makes a Constitution save, half on success), then all Dragon-Marks end and the cycle returns to Claim · Ordinary attacks still roll; Hoard objects remain stealable.

The sky goes first: moorland gold under a low aurora, every blade of heath a coin catching light that has no source. Beneath it the hall rises, vaulted and windowless, its walls built from the hoard itself, swords and crowns mortared into the stone like fossils. The air smells of old metal and cold earth, and everything that glitters is watching.

Her vault, enclosed: the Domain is a hall built from the hoard itself, and everything inside is catalogued, coveted, and reckoned — but the hoard remains stealable, even here.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Appraiser's Eye

The dragon knows her gold the way a miser knows his own heartbeat. As a bonus action, you may appraise one object you can see within 30 feet, learning its approximate value, whether it bears cursed energy, and whether it shows signs of a curse, binding, or forgery. If the object is eligible to become a Hoard Mark, you may immediately mark it with Covetous Scale as part of the same bonus action, still paying the 1 CE. Once per long rest, you may appraise a cursed tool worn or carried by a creature this way, reading its nature despite its keeper.

Jealous Wings

Jealous wings: the wyrm-shape takes to the air, and no one follows her home. While in Wyrm Shape, you may spend 2 Greed as a bonus action to unfurl jealous wings, gaining 30-foot flight until the end of your next turn.

No Thief Escapes

The hunt, shared: the whole party sees the golden thread, and the thief has nowhere left to run. Once per round when Dragon's Claim reveals a thief's direction, you may spend 1 CE as a bonus action to share the hunt: your allies perceive a glinting golden thread pointing at the thief until the start of your next turn, and each may use their reaction to move up to half their speed toward it.

Accursed Gold (11+)

The stolen gold turns traitor in the thief’s hands — too heavy to carry, too precious to drop. As a bonus action, you may spend 1 Greed to make one stolen Hoard Mark within your Claim's 1-hour window unbearably heavy: the carrier's speed is halved while it carries the mark, until it drops it.
