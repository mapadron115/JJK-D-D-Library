Ratio Technique

A precise critical-hit engine with a terrain collapse and increasingly reliable 7:3 weak-point rolls.

The fiction. Registry notes describe it as accounting made manifest: the user's cursed energy divides everything into ten equal parts and instinctively knows where the 7:3 line falls. Nothing here is inherited — it is one sorcerer's cold, managerial certainty, given teeth.

Full progression

Feature map

1Critical Focus.

Critical Focus widens your natural critical range by one and adds one extra base weapon/unarmed die on a critical. If another feature widens the range, combine them to a maximum natural range of 18–20. “Damage dice” means the attack’s base weapon or unarmed die, not every rider die.

3Ratio.

Ratio. After an attack hits but before rolling damage, pay 1 CE and roll a d10. A 3 or 7 makes that hit critical. Additional CE up to CE modifier adds one d10 each. Pay before rolling the Ratio dice; multiple qualifying dice do not create multiple criticals.

The critical bonus dice replace, rather than add to, earlier amounts: from level 3, a critical adds two extra base weapon/unarmed dice. “Damage dice” means the attack’s base weapon or unarmed die, not every rider die.

6Collapse.

Collapse. The 30-foot circle is centered on a ground point you touch, not necessarily on your body. It becomes difficult terrain until repaired. Roll unarmed damage once plus 3d10; a failed Dexterity save takes full damage, success half. This is a save effect and cannot become critical through Ratio.

From level 6, a critical adds three extra base weapon/unarmed dice. “Damage dice” means the attack’s base weapon or unarmed die, not every rider die.

11Improved Ratio.

Improved Ratio. Roll two d10 by default and the hit is critical if any die is 3 or 7. Additional CE up to CE modifier adds one d10 each. Pay before rolling the Ratio dice; multiple qualifying dice do not create multiple criticals.

From level 11, a critical adds four extra base weapon/unarmed dice. “Damage dice” means the attack’s base weapon or unarmed die, not every rider die.

18Golden Ratio.

Golden Ratio. Every attack that hits is critical without rolling Ratio or spending its CE. This does not turn a miss into a hit and does not make a save-based feature critical. A qualifying critical may interact with Black Flash under the current Black Flash rules.

Inside Golden Ratio, a critical adds five extra base weapon/unarmed dice. “Damage dice” means the attack’s base weapon or unarmed die, not every rider die.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it.

Prerequisite: Critical Focus

Measured Guard

Reaction when you are hit by a weapon attack, 1 CE: roll your Ratio d10 (two dice from level 11, as the feature). On a 3 or 7 you found the blow's weak ratio and the damage is halved. Declare before damage is rolled.

Prerequisite: Collapse

Weapon Breaker

Reaction when targeted by a weapon attack, 3 CE: wielder makes Dexterity save. On failure a mundane weapon breaks before damage; a cursed tool breaks only if your striking tool is equal or higher grade, otherwise the attack is deflected and misses. Artifacts and unique Special Grades break only when their own story permits.

Prerequisite: Improved Ratio

Fatal Ratio

Corrected prerequisite: Improved Ratio. After a Ratio critical, spend 2 CE to make it a limb-tagged hit without targeted-strike disadvantage. Resolve damage and the revised injury threshold/save normally; the feat does not automatically sever a limb.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier I: its numbers become spectacular, but the technique remains one coherent task—convert successful attacks into decisive critical hits.
