Directive Arrows

Write glowing arrows into the battlefield, ride their instructions at impossible speed, redirect weapons and enemies, and turn a carefully drawn route into one decisive strike.

Core law

An arrow is an instruction.

The arrows are placed objects of cursed information. They do not roll, accumulate, or become another point currency: their positions and directions are the resource.

Power identity: the map is the engine.

Classify this correctly: there is no hidden fuel gauge here. The inscribed positions and directions are the entire resource, the battlefield, charted, becomes the weapon.

Directive Arrows earns Tier III through repeatable scene control, party movement, reflected ranged attacks, scalable weapon pressure, and a lethal late-game Domain. It has no permanent assets, no nullification, no Evil Eye, and no separate resource meter.

Arrow statistics

A visible cursed glyph

No secrets here: each arrow occupies its five-foot space, intangible, visible to CE perception, spaced five feet apart. The enemy sees the map too, and still cannot outrun what was decided.

  • Each arrow occupies one 5-foot space.
  • Your arrows must be at least 5 feet apart.
  • They are intangible and do not block movement.
  • They can be perceived through normal CE perception.
  • They are technique effects, not creatures or damageable objects.

Active capacity

Replace, never overflow

The chart has a capacity: twice his proficiency. To inscribe beyond it, he must first choose which arrows vanish. Discipline: every new decision displaces an old one.

You may have up to twice your PB in active arrows. If an inscription would exceed that number, choose which of your existing arrows disappear before placing the new ones.

Movement limit

PB activations per turn

A creature may ride no more than his proficiency in arrows per turn, the technique’s own speed limit. Directed movement ignores difficult terrain but remains distance traveled, not teleportation.

A creature can activate no more than your PB of your arrows during one turn. Directed movement is not teleportation, ignores difficult terrain, and counts as distance traveled for Directive Arrows features.

Obstructions

Reality still has walls

The map is not the territory: solid obstruction and total cover still stop directed movement, halting him at the last legal space. Arrows command motion, not miracles.

Directed movement cannot pass through a solid obstruction or total cover. It stops in the last legal space before the obstacle. It causes no collision damage unless a feature expressly supplies it.

Loss of movement

Pin the accelerator

Speed zero means no voluntary riding: grapples, restraint, and readied steel at visible endpoints are honest counters. A charted escape is still an escape someone can block.

A creature whose speed is 0 cannot voluntarily activate an arrow. Grappling, restraint, tight terrain, readied attacks at visible endpoints, and technique suppression are therefore meaningful counters.

Vector Edge timing:

it is declared after the weapon attack hits but before damage is rolled. Its dice are part of that attack, double on a critical hit, and are included in the completed critical damage if the attack becomes a Black Flash.

Field notes. Subject: the sorcerer who fights tomorrow’s battle today. Three truths govern him. First, the map is the engine: the arrows’ positions and directions are the resource, no hidden meter, no second currency, only glowing instructions written onto the field. Second, resolve must be legible: he decides early and enforces at impossible speed, chaining ridden arrows into a single decisive Vector Edge; hesitation is the one thing the technique cannot inscribe. Third, the route delivers but does not aim: speed and position are his to guarantee, the strike itself remains the weapon’s honest work, accuracy is still his responsibility. Mark the doctrine: write the battlefield before the battle, then have the courage to walk exactly what you wrote.

Choose the weapon. Draw the angle.

The technique rewards tactical positioning and accuracy without requiring one prescribed weapon, ancestry, class, or maximum ability score.

Level 3 · Action · PB CE

Acceleration Sphere

The encirclement: PB temporary arrows conjured around one visible target, then a sequence of straight legs, each ending in a weapon attack, each leg one arrow activation for Vector Edge. An action entire, ending where the last leg leaves him.

Choose one creature you can see within 60 feet and a weapon you are wielding. Conjure PB temporary arrows around the target; they do not count against capacity and only you can activate them. Repeat the following sequence PB times:

  1. Move in a straight, unobstructed line to a different legal space within 20 feet of the target. The first leg may move up to 60 feet; every later leg may move up to your Arrow Distance. This is directed movement and does not provoke.
  2. Make one weapon attack against the chosen target from that space. Normal range, reach, ammunition, cover, and weapon requirements apply.

Each leg counts as one arrow activation for Vector Edge. Temporary arrows vanish when the action finishes, and you remain in the final space. If no different legal space or unobstructed path exists for a leg, that leg and all remaining legs are lost. Acceleration Sphere is an action, not the Attack action, so features adding attacks specifically to the Attack action do not add attacks to it.

Level 6 · On-hit · 2 CE

Imposed Directive

The route turned weapon: once per turn, a weapon hit lets him inscribe a direction onto the enemy itself, 2 CE, a Strength save, and the target is marched his Arrow Distance. Obstructions convert the unspent journey into collision.

Once per turn after you hit a creature with a weapon attack, choose a direction and spend 2 CE. The target makes a Strength save against your CE save DC. On a failure, it is moved your Arrow Distance in that direction without provoking; on a success, it is not moved.

If a failed target meets a solid obstruction before completing the distance, it stops in the last legal space, falls prone, and takes 1d6 force per 5 feet it could not travel, maximum PBd6. An unattended object takes the same damage. If the obstruction is another creature, that creature makes a Dexterity save, taking the collision damage and falling prone on failure, or taking half damage and remaining standing on success.

Level 6 · Reaction · 1 CE + one arrow

Projectile Redirection

He answers ranged attacks with inscription: reaction, 1 CE, one expended arrow, and the enemy’s shot continues along the arrow’s displayed direction, finding a new target by the original roll. The arrow decides; the attacker merely supplied the missile.

When you see a ranged attack roll made against a creature or object within 10 feet of one of your active arrows, after seeing the roll but before the result is resolved, expend that arrow and spend 1 CE. From the arrow’s space, the attack continues in the arrow’s displayed direction. The first legal creature or object in a 5-foot-wide line within the attack’s normal maximum range becomes its new target; compare the original attack roll to that target’s AC. If the line contains no legal target, the attack misses.

The original attacker remains the source of the attack and all of its damage and riders. An attack can be redirected only once. Projectile Redirection cannot affect an area, cone, line, saving-throw effect, or attack that did not make an attack roll.

Creative permission:

Projectile Redirection may bend an enemy shot away, reflect it toward its source, or turn your own ranged attack into a prepared trick shot. The arrow’s visible direction—not a new choice made during the reaction—determines the redirected line.

Rules integration

No missing procedures.

Use these rulings whenever Arrowpath intersects critical hits, Maximum Output, conditions, vertical movement, or another creature’s turn.

Weapon attacks continue using their normal weapon attack modifier. Directive Arrows does not replace Strength, Dexterity, or another weapon statistic with CE.

Vertical vectors Gravity resumes afterward

An arrow may point vertically or diagonally. A creature unsupported when directed movement ends immediately begins falling unless another arrow, feature, surface, or flying speed prevents it.

Conditions Speed 0 prevents voluntary use

Prone creatures may activate arrows. Grappled or restrained creatures normally have speed 0 and cannot voluntarily do so. Imposed Directive can still move them because that movement is forced by you.

Criticals and Black Flash Vector Edge joins the completed attack

Vector Edge and Acceleraise Boost damage are declared after a hit and before damage, so their dice double on that attack’s critical hit and are multiplied with the completed critical if Black Flash succeeds. Every rolled weapon attack in Acceleration Sphere may critically hit and attempt Black Flash independently.

Attack-action stacking Sphere is its own action

Acceleration Sphere makes PB weapon attacks as one technique action. It does not receive attacks granted specifically when taking the Attack action. Routebreaker is one separate weapon attack without an action. Distinct additional actions can still be used normally.

Maximum Output Enhance the chosen application

Maximum Output on Directive Inscription doubles its 1 CE cost. Range mode increases placement range by half; duration makes that batch last 15 rounds. Damage mode marks that batch: the next time you use Vector Edge after activating at least one marked arrow, add Arrow Dice equal to half the marked arrows activated for that hit, rounded up, then end the mark. Accuracy has no effect on a normal inscription.

For Acceleration Sphere, accuracy applies to every attack made by that activation, range increases target range to 90 feet, and damage adds Arrow Dice equal to half PB rounded up to one Sphere hit. For Dominion of Borrowed Speed, range makes the aura 60 feet, duration makes it last 15 rounds, and accuracy increases its save DC by half PB rounded up.

Focus Arrows persist independently

Ordinary arrows and their one-minute duration require no Technique Focus. Dominion of Borrowed Speed does. Opening the Domain follows the comprehensive compatibility rule for an existing Focus effect.

Suppression Glyphs can be erased

Technique Extermination and similar effects may suppress or remove arrows normally. An arrow inside a suppressed area cannot activate; if still present when suppression ends, it resumes until its original duration expires.

No Reversal Not every technique has one

Directive Arrows has no Cursed Technique Reversal. RCT functions through the campaign’s universal rules but does not automatically produce a reversed arrow feature. Dominion of Borrowed Speed is a Maximum Technique, not a Reversal.

Counterplay Read the route and break the approach
  • Arrows are visible to CE perception, including their direction and likely endpoint.
  • A creature can avoid arrow spaces, prepare attacks at endpoints, create total cover, or obstruct straight lines.
  • Speed reduction, grappling, restraint, and effects preventing movement stop voluntary activation.
  • Forced movement can push the user away from a planned starting arrow.
  • Technique suppression can disable the route; destroying ordinary terrain can remove safe landing spaces.
  • Arrow movement still enters hazards and never passes through walls merely because it is fast.
