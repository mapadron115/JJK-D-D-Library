May We Come In?

Power grows only through genuine permission. Offer useful bargains, and each accepted bargain becomes a permanent threshold — a door you may always cross, a line you may always reach through.

Combat roleSocial Controller / Curse Specialist
TierTier IV · Special Grade
Core engineMap of Open Doors
Core law

Character fantasy

Power grows only through genuine permission. Every bargain a creature knowingly accepts becomes a permanent threshold — a door you may always cross, a line you may always reach through. The technique grows as a map of open doors. Doors are never currency; they are never spent.

Combat loop

  1. Offer a target something genuinely useful.
  2. If it voluntarily accepts, record an Open Door to one Threshold it controls.
  3. Doors never close on their own — cross them, reach through them, know the welcomed.
  4. Engineer situations where accepting your help is tempting without ever forcing consent.

Core law

Open Doors & Thresholds

Offer:

once per turn as a bonus action, spend 1 CE and make a concrete offer to one creature within 30 feet: 10 feet of movement without provoking opportunity attacks, Help on one check or attack before your next turn, PB temporary HP, or allowing it to end prone for free. If it knowingly and voluntarily accepts the benefit, record 1 Open Door: choose one Threshold the creature meaningfully controls. Maximum 3 Open Doors per creature. Charm, domination, threats of immediate technique harm, or mind control cannot open Doors.

Open Door:

An Open Door is permanent. It does not fade on a rest and it is never spent. It closes only when the granting creature genuinely revokes permission — a deliberate, communicated revocation (spoken to your face, sent by messenger, or sealed in a warding rite), never an accident, a trick, or a coerced retraction. A creature that does not know you hold its Door cannot revoke it. If revocation drops a Welcomed creature below 3 Doors, the welcome ends.

Cold Knock (the fallback):

When no one will bargain, the technique does not stall. Once per short rest, when a hostile creature within 30 feet refuses or ignores your Offer, you may spend 2 CE to wedge a door by force: record 1 temporary Open Door to one Threshold it controls. It lasts until the end of your next turn, then slams shut — and the creature knows you forced it. A wedged door works like a genuine Door except it can never count toward Welcomed. Forced consent never becomes genuine; this is leverage, not permission.

Threshold:

a home, room, vehicle, barrier, camp, ward, or space that a creature meaningfully controls can count as its Threshold. Domains are not automatically bypassed by this feature.

Full progression

Level progression

1The First Invitation

Each Open Door marks its granter. You know the direction to a creature whose Door you hold while it is within 300 feet (anywhere on the same plane from level 11). Once per turn when such a creature makes a save against your technique, subtract 1 from that save. You may ask permission to cross a mundane locked or guarded Threshold; if a creature whose Door you hold explicitly allows it, you can open or cross that Threshold as though physically authorized.

3Access

Your Doors deepen. Your attacks and technique effects ignore half cover created by a granter’s own possessions, summons, or mundane fortifications. As a bonus action and 2 CE, step through a held Door: teleport to an unoccupied space just inside a non-Domain Threshold you hold a Door for, provided it is within 300 feet.

6Welcome

When a single creature has granted you 3 Open Doors — three separate accepted bargains — it is Welcomed, and the welcome is permanent until revoked below 3. Once per turn when you damage a Welcomed creature, add 1 Invitation Die psychic damage. Action, 3 CE: a Welcomed creature within 60 feet makes a Wisdom save; on failure it is charmed by you until the end of your next turn and cannot willingly cross a Threshold away from you during that time. On success it is immune to this use until your next turn.

11Once Welcomed, Never Gone

Your Doors outlast memory: they persist until genuinely revoked, and you know the direction and approximate distance to every granter on the same plane. Once per short rest, when a granter activates a barrier, Curtain, or non-Domain ward, you may use your reaction and 3 CE to make a CE ability check against its save DC; on success, you personally count as authorized for that effect for 1 round. This never defeats a Domain barrier or nullifies its sure-hit.

18Domain Expansion: Please, We’re Cold Outside

Full-turn action, 20 CE, 90-foot radius, 1 minute. A freezing night surrounds one warm house. The Domain is rules-based. Outside the house, movement costs +5 feet per 5 feet and creatures cannot regain temporary HP. Anyone may enter the house by willingly crossing its open doorway; doing so immediately removes frightened, grants 2 Invitation Dice temporary HP, and counts as voluntarily accepting your hospitality, you record 1 Open Door to that creature (maximum 3 per creature). Inside, violence is allowed normally. At initiative 20 the exterior cold intensifies: each creature outside gains one Cold stack; at 2 stacks speed −10 feet, at 3 disadvantage on Constitution saves, at 4 it cannot take reactions. Entering the house removes all Cold. Anti-domain protection prevents gaining Cold but does not remove the house or its voluntary bargain. The Domain never forces anyone to enter. Clash bookkeeping: 800 clash points (Tier IV). A 100+ point advantage collapses the weaker Domain; collapse deals no damage. Barrier AC 12 + PB; barrier HP = 10 × max(1, your CE modifier) + 1,600. Technique Burnout: 5 rounds.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Polite Stranger

You may deliver Offer through a phone, speaker, intercom, or other live two-way communication within 1 mile. The target must hear and voluntarily accept.

Take My Hand

Add one Offer option: the target may stand from prone and move 10 feet toward you without provoking. If accepted, you may move 10 feet toward it as well.

Guest Right

While inside a Threshold whose controller has invited you, gain +1 AC and advantage on checks to notice traps, hidden doors, or deliberate attempts to expel you.

The Eyes Are Wrong

A creature that has granted you 2+ Open Doors has disadvantage on Insight checks against you and cannot benefit from being invisible against your technique senses.

Rules integration

Building the character

Charisma-heavy Support Specialist or Strategist is natural, but a martial character can weaponize the “help first, curse later” loop. This technique is intentionally strongest when the player is socially clever rather than when the GM makes NPCs foolish.
Technique die: Invitation Die follows the shared d6 → d8 → d10 → d12 progression.
Rules integration

Counterplay

Refuse offers, communicate the danger, isolate the user from controlled spaces, deliberately revoke Doors you granted, and avoid granting explicit access. Forced or manipulated consent never opens a Door.
