White Flag

1d6 piercing spear. Plant as action to create 15-ft Truce Zone for 1 minute, 2 CE, Focus.

FormSpear-banner A truce that outlived its makers, planted like a claim, carried like a warning.
RoleParley / battlefield support It does not stop violence; it prices betrayal, a truce, not a cage.
EngineTruce A fifteen-foot zone where the first striker pays, symmetric, visible, and voluntary.
Attunement1 hour* An hour's ritual, the banner must believe you mean the peace you plant.
Features & procedures

Properties & techniques

Exact player-facing procedures

Feature

Passive (zone) · The first creature each round to deal damage inside loses reactions and has its speed halved until the end of its next turn.

The rule, known to all who enter: the first creature each round to deal damage inside loses reactions and half its speed until its next turn ends. Planting the spear is asking everyone present to keep a promise no one present made. Creatures entering know the rule: the first creature each round to deal damage while inside the zone (invented) loses reactions and has its speed halved until the end of its next turn.

Feature

Passive (zone) · Aid, healing, movement, harmless grappling, and talking remain legal.

Mark what remains permitted: aid, healing, movement, harmless grappling, talking. The zone forbids nothing but punishes the first blow, parley needs room to breathe. Aid, healing, movement, grappling without damage, and talking remain legal.

Break Parley

Bonus action · End the zone; if you were first to deal damage that round, you suffer the penalty too.

The wielder may end the zone as a bonus action, but if they struck first that round, they suffer the penalty too. The flag's law is symmetric; its planter is not exempt. wielder may end zone as bonus action; if they were first to deal damage that round they suffer the penalty too.

Feature

The final honesty: nothing here prevents violence outright. It makes betrayal costly, not impossible, a truce, not a cage. No effect prevents violence outright; it makes betrayal costly.

Field notes. Subject: a promise, planted. Three truths govern it. First, the banner was raised over a battlefield where enemies agreed, for a time, not to bleed, and the agreement soaked into the cloth. Second, the zone's rule is symmetric because the fear that forged it was betrayal, not violence: the first striker each round pays in reactions and speed, wielder included. Third, it never forbids, only prices: aid, healing, and speech remain legal; only the first blow is taxed. Consider the doctrine. A tool that cannot stop a single act of violence, yet has ended more fights than any blade on this registry. The flag does not disarm hands. It disarms excuses.

Rules integration

Cursed-tool attunement

Campaign canon

Cursed-tool attunement: you have PB attunement slots (2–6). This Grade 2 tool functions as a mundane tool while unattuned, or with its full properties for one slot and a one-hour ritual at GM discretion. Attunement ends voluntarily over a short rest, on death, or when you replace it while over capacity. Distance does not break attunement. Cursed-tool attunement is separate from magic-item attunement.
Balance & counterplay. Rule is symmetric and visible, so it creates negotiations without mind control.
