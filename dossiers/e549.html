Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the falling sand. When you take the Attack action, you may replace one attack with Sandfall: 1 CE — a lash of cursed sand, a ranged CE attack within 60 feet dealing 1d8 slashing damage on a hit. Sandsense (passive): you always know the exact time, and you can sense the remaining duration of any timed effect on a creature you can see — spells, conditions, transformations, the rounds left on anything that is counting down. The hourglass does not guess. It reads the glass.

Field notes. Three truths from the arbiter. First: every fight is already an hourglass — it begins full and ends empty, and the only question has ever been who noticed the sand; the technique simply makes the glass visible, and visibility is nine-tenths of judgment. Second: a condition is a promise with teeth — name it clearly, name it fairly, and never name one you cannot keep yourself, because the glass does not know whose mouth spoke it; I have watched masters of this technique punish themselves with their own cleverness, and the sand fell on schedule. Third: the final accounting is not cruelty, it is arithmetic — grains counted, verdicts rendered, no appeals; the universe keeps books, and this technique merely asks to see the ledger. Mark the economy: the sand is free. The verdicts are not.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1Sandfall

When you take the Attack action, you may replace one attack with Sandfall: 1 CE · ranged CE attack within 60 feet, 1d8 slashing damage on a hit. Passive · Sandsense: you always know the exact time and can sense the remaining duration of timed effects on creatures you can see.

The bread and butter, folded into the attacks you were making anyway — and the passive is the technique's real first gift. Knowing exactly how many rounds the enemy's transformation has left, when the poison ends, when the Domain falls: that is the hourglass teaching you to read every fight as a countdown before you ever turn the glass.

3The Hourglass

You learn your Domain Expansion (see Pinnacle expression).

The Domain is not the capstone. It is the thesis — like Gambit’s pachinko and Judgeman’s courtroom, the game arrives early and everything after is learning to play it better. From here the technique is never really about the lash — it is about everything the lash was practicing for.

6Terms

Passive · Sandfall now deals 2d8 slashing damage. Bonus action · 2 CE · Name one creature within 60 feet and one trigger — attack, move more than half their speed, or spend CE. Until the end of your next turn, the first time they do it, the sand judges them: 1d8 force damage, no attack roll.

The new system pairs with a sharper foundation: harder sand, and the condition game in miniature — one creature, one trigger, one verdict — for the fights where the glass stays unopened. Ask what the technique was blind to: it could wound the body but not the choices. Now the choices have a price, named aloud, in advance.

11Turn Again

Once per short rest · When your Domain's sand runs out and the Final Accounting resolves, you may pay 10 CE to flip the glass: the sand resets to 3 rounds, and you name 2 new conditions. Grains reset to zero.

High power, not a finisher: the limiter is the short rest and the price of admission, not the CE — and the second game is shorter, sharper, and played on fresh terms. It doesn't end the fight. It deals a second hand to a table that thought the game was over.

18The Glass Remembers

Passive · Your Domain costs 15 CE instead of 20, and you name 3 conditions instead of 2. When the Final Accounting awards the boon, you always count as holding the most Grains, ties or not.

Enablement, not a nuke: the technique stops taxing its own existence and starts dealing from the bottom of the deck. Three conditions instead of two is not a bigger number — it is a different game, one with no safe moves, played in a glass you can now afford to turn twice.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

The sky becomes glass. Above, a vast reservoir of sand dark as old blood; below, the battlefield for the lower bulb — and the sand is already falling, each grain ticking like a tiny bell as it lands. There are no walls, only the curve of the glass and the certainty of the count. Somewhere, impossibly distant and impossibly close, something is keeping time.

Domain Expansion, The Hourglass (action, 20 CE, until the sand runs out — 5 rounds, 90-foot radius): when the Domain opens, name 2 conditions from the list below — choose them carefully, because the glass tests everyone inside, including you. At the end of each creature's turn, evaluate each condition: met — the creature gains 1 Grain (a simple tally, not a pool); failed — the sand judges them for 2d8 force damage, no attack roll, no save (this is the sure-hit). The Final Accounting — when the sand runs out: the creature holding the most Grains is favored by the glass, regaining 10 CE and 3d8 HP; the creature holding the fewest suffers the verdict, 8d10 force damage, no attack roll (Dexterity save for half). You win all ties.

The conditions (choose 2): Blood — deal damage to another creature on your turn. Motion — move at least 15 feet on your turn. Stillness — end your turn in the space where you started it. Focus — spend at least 1 CE on your turn. Restraint — spend no CE and make no attack roll on your turn. Risk — take damage from another creature's effect on your turn. Note the oppositions honestly: Motion and Stillness cannot both be kept; Blood and Restraint cannot both be kept; Focus and Restraint cannot both be kept. The rigging is the game — name the pair that splits the table in your favor, and pray you can keep your own terms.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Read the Grains

Action · 1 CE · Touch a creature, or study one you can see for 1 minute: learn their current CE total and the remaining duration of every timed effect on them — buffs, poisons, transformations, countdowns. The glass shows you their sand. The hourglass, but as diagnosis.

Borrowed Time

Bonus action · 2 CE · Once per short rest · One willing creature within 30 feet gains an additional bonus action on their next turn — sand lent from your own glass, to be repaid never. The hourglass, but as generosity.

Against the Grain

Once per long rest · When you fail a saving throw, turn the glass: reroll it. For one breath the sand runs backward, and the verdict is unmade. Time, but as a second chance.

Pocket Glass

Action · 4 CE · Trap one creature within 60 feet in a personal hourglass for 1 minute (Wisdom save negates; they may repeat the save as an action on their turn): name 1 condition from the Domain list — at the end of each of their turns they fail it, they take 2d8 force damage. The Domain, but as a snare.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: Direct rolled technique attacks (Sandfall) can use the campaign's Black Flash procedure when otherwise eligible. Save-effect damage (Terms, failed-condition judgments, the Final Accounting verdict) cannot critically hit or Black Flash.
Generic Maximum Output applies only to ordinary features, never to The Hourglass.
Grains are a simple per-creature tally kept by the table — not a resource, not a pool, not spendable. Teleportation counts as movement for Motion and breaks Stillness. A creature that takes no turn (stunned, unconscious) automatically fails every condition except Stillness and Restraint.
Turn Again's flipped glass is a new game: new conditions, zeroed Grains, 3 rounds of sand. The Final Accounting of the flipped game resolves normally. Domain Burnout applies to The Hourglass as normal; Turn Again does not bypass it.
