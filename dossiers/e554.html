Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the keeper of keys. Turnkey (bonus action · 1 CE): touch a lock — or impose one. Lock: a door, chest, container, or set of manacles seals shut; it cannot be opened without your key-copy or forced entry (DC equal to your CE save DC). Lock a wound: a bleeding wound seals — ongoing bleed damage ends, and a creature making death saves from blood loss stabilizes. Unlock: open any mundane lock instantly, no check, no tools. The key-copy law: every lock you lock, unlock, or pick, you keep a metaphysical copy of its key — a ring of remembered keys, not a currency. Key-copies do not expire, cannot be stolen or dispelled, and work wherever the original lock stands.

Field notes. Three truths from the workbench. First: every lock ever made is a confession — someone had something worth keeping, and was afraid; learn to read the fear and you never need to force the door. Second: the key is the honest part of the transaction — anyone can break a lock, but only the keyholder was invited; be the one who decides who gets invited. Third: a locksmith never truly closes anything — every lock you touch stays half-open to you forever, because you kept the key; the world is full of doors you have already opened. Mark the trade: nothing is ever fully shut again. Not to you.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1Turnkey

Bonus action · 1 CE · Touch a lock — or impose one. Lock a door, chest, container, or manacles (sealed; forced entry DC = your CE save DC). Lock a wound (ongoing bleed ends; a creature dying from blood loss stabilizes). Unlock any mundane lock instantly, no check. You keep a metaphysical copy of every key you touch.

The bread and butter: access and denial, folded into a bonus action so you never choose between fighting and locking. You don't pick the lock — you simply decide it is open, or decide it is shut, and the world agrees. The key-copy is the quiet part: every door you have ever touched stays half-open to you forever.

3Pick

Action · 2 CE · Pick a supernatural lock: a magical barrier, ward, seal, or a technique held in suspension. Make a CE ability check against the creator's CE save DC (or DC 15 + the effect's level for environmental magic). On a success, the lock opens — or is suppressed for 1 minute, your choice. You keep a key-copy of anything you pick.

The new verb: L1 works any lock it can touch; L3 opens locks that were never meant for hands. Ask what the technique was blind to: other people's magic was a locked door it couldn't open. Now it can. A ward that took a sorcerer a week to raise falls to a thought and two cursed breaths — and you keep the key, so you can close it behind you.

6Rekey

Passive · Turnkey no longer requires touch — range 30 feet. Action · 2 CE · Rekey: target a lock within 30 feet you hold the key-copy for and change who it answers to. Name the keyholders — only they (or you) can open it with a key; all other keys, copies, and picks fail until you rekey it again.

The system deepens and the foundation sharpens with it: your hands get longer at the same level your authority gets absolute. Rekeying is the quiet tyranny of the trade — you don't break into the vault, you simply decide the vault was always yours, and everyone else's key stops working. The technique doesn't abandon its first key; it just learns to change the locks.

11Deadbolt

Reaction · 8 CE · Once per short rest · When a technique, spell, or magical effect manifests within 60 feet, lock it shut mid-cast: make a CE ability check against the originator's CE save DC. On a success, the effect is negated — the technique's lock jams it before it finishes forming. You keep no key-copy of a deadbolted technique; some doors are only ever closed once.

High power, not a finisher: the limiter is the short rest, the reaction timing, and the contest — you can only slam the bolt so often, and the other locksmith might be better. It doesn't end the fight. It ends one moment, absolutely, and everyone on the field learns that your hands are faster than their incantations.

18Master of Keys

Passive · Your key-copies work at 120 feet, through barriers and walls — a locked door on the far side of stone is still yours to open. Turnkey becomes a free action once per turn (still 1 CE). Locks you impose cannot be picked by mundane tools, only by magic that beats your CE save DC.

Enablement, not a nuke: the technique stops asking permission and starts assuming it. Distance was the last honest limit on the trade — a lock had to be within reach. Now reach is wherever your keys remember, and your keys remember everything. You are very difficult to contain now — not because you hit harder, but because no door on the field is truly closed to you.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

You hold up your hand, and the air goes quiet the way a vault goes quiet. Every lock within sight — doors, wards, barriers, vows, the sealed places behind people's eyes — shivers once, like a held breath. In your palm there is a key. There was never a lock it didn't fit.

Maximum, The Skeleton Key (action, 10 CE, 1 minute): for 1 minute, your Turnkey and Pick require no check against any lock of mundane or magical origin — they simply open, or seal, at your word. Once during the minute, you may attempt the impossible: lock or unlock something that should not have a lock — a Domain's barrier (seal it from outside, or open it from within), a binding vow (release a creature from its terms), a creature's technique (they cannot use it until the end of your next turn). Make a CE ability check against DC 25; on a success, the impossible opens. On a failure, the minute ends early — some doors stay shut.

Feats

Build-defining options.

The Lock Remembers

Action · 1 CE · Touch any lock — mundane or magical — and read its history: who made it, who last opened it, and what it was made to guard. Locks are confessions; you just learned to take them. Locking, but as memory.

Sealed Room

Action · 3 CE · Lock a creature inside a sealed space: a 10-foot cube barrier snaps shut around a creature within 60 feet (Dexterity save negates, DC = your CE save DC). They cannot leave until the lock is picked, broken (DC = your CE save DC), or you dismiss it. Lasts 1 minute. Locking, but as imprisonment.

Spare Key

Bonus action · 1 CE · Press a copy of one of your key-copies into an ally's hand. For 1 hour, they may open or seal that lock as if they were you — your authority, lent. The copy fades after the hour; the trust doesn't have to. Keys, but for others.

Deadlock

Bonus action · 2 CE · Lock yourself down until the start of your next turn: you have resistance to all damage and cannot be moved against your will — but you cannot move, take actions, or be willingly teleported either. You are the vault. Nothing gets in. Nothing gets out. Locking, but as defense.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: This technique makes no attack rolls; Black Flash does not apply to its features. Save-effect features cannot critically hit.
Generic Maximum Output applies only to ordinary features, never to The Skeleton Key.
Key-copies are metaphysical impressions, not objects: they cannot be stolen, dispelled, or destroyed. A lock you impose can be forced (Athletics/Strength check vs your CE save DC) or picked by mundane tools (Thieves' Tools check vs your CE save DC) unless Master of Keys applies. Rekeyed locks answer only to their named keyholders.
Pick cannot open a Domain's barrier from outside under normal circumstances — that is the province of The Skeleton Key's impossible attempt. Deadbolt's negation is not a counterspell and cannot be countered in turn; it is a lock, and it holds.
