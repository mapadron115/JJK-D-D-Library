Blind Spot

Tax enemy reactions to maintain observation, then explode through any lapse in line of sight with impossible movement and a punishing follow-up strike.

Combat roleAssassin / movement controller

Research log. Subject: the thing that exists between blinks. The subject was recovered after the east wing went dark for nine seconds, nine seconds in which every camera agreed nothing had moved, and every witness agreed something had. Two truths govern this file. First: observation is the cage, while any hostile observer maintains watch, the subject cannot Step and cannot Reflex; the interval between looks is the only country it owns. Second: the blink is the border crossing, it may force that interval at will, and every arrival is an attack on the watcher's neurology, not their body. We staff four observers minimum, in rotation. We have stopped asking what it looks like when no one is watching. The question answers itself, and we did not like the answer.

Full progression

Progression

1Observation Protocol

Observe.

Observation protocol: the watcher must commit a reaction at the start of every turn, or the interval goes unguarded. No exceptions have been recorded. At the start of its turn, a hostile creature within 60 feet that can see you may spend its reaction to Observe you until the start of its next turn. Observation ends early if it loses line of sight, becomes blinded or incapacitated, or an effect suppresses its senses. A creature cannot Observe if it has no reaction available.

Source Rule.

Confirmed across all trials: under even one watchful eye, the Step and the Reflex do not occur. Hypothesis: the subject's movement requires a true gap in observation, not mere inattention. Test personnel have stood motionless for minutes awaiting a single blink. The hypothesis holds. While at least one Observer has direct line of sight to you, you cannot use Blind Spot Step, Blink Reflex, or another feature in this technique that says it requires an unobserved interval. Your ordinary movement and class features still work; this is the playable translation of SCP-173 being immobile under direct observation.

Blind Spot Step.

Twenty feet through the unobserved interval. Note: no opportunity attacks are possible, there is no observer present to take them. Unobserved movement cannot be punished by the unobserving; the logic is airtight and, frankly, rude. Bonus action, 1 CE. If no hostile Observer can currently see you, teleport up to 20 feet to an unoccupied space you can perceive. This movement provokes no opportunity attacks.

Once per round · no action

Blink Advance

Unexpected result: when an observer's reaction is spent or lost for any reason, the subject advances fifteen feet, even while otherwise watched. The expenditure itself appears to constitute a micro-lapse. We are revising the observation protocol. When a creature Observing you spends or loses its reaction for any reason, you may move up to 15 feet without provoking. This works while observed.

Snap Strike.

Arrival-event analysis: the strike carries no bonus force. The effect is neurological, subjects who resist lose their reactions; subjects who fail are knocked prone and likewise cannot react. The subject does not need to wound. It needs you still. Once per turn, the first melee weapon or unarmed hit you land after a Blind Spot Step deals no bonus damage. The target makes a Constitution save: on a success it cannot take reactions until the end of its next turn; on a failure it is knocked prone and cannot take reactions until the end of its next turn.

3Blink Reflex

Reflex event: when the final observer stops watching, the subject Steps as a reflex, half range, no energy expended, no apparent decision made. Hypothesis: the Step is triggered, not willed. Like a knee-jerk. This disturbs me more than the teleportation does. Once per round when the final Observer currently watching you stops Observing, you may use your reaction to teleport up to half your Blind Spot Step range without spending CE.

6Peripheral Failure

Action · 2 CE · Up to PB Observers within 60 ft: Constitution saves · On failure, their Observation ends and they cannot Observe you again until the end of their next turn, and they are blinded until the end of their next turn (Snap Strike upgrade).

Induced-lapse event: the subject can force the blink, observers' watch ends involuntarily and cannot resume until their next turn ends, and the arrival strike now blinds as well. Note: the subject is learning to manufacture the condition it needs. Or it always could, and we are only now observing it. Action, 2 CE: up to PB Observers within 60 feet make Constitution saves. On a failure their Observation ends and they cannot Observe you again until the end of their next turn. Snap Strike upgrade: a failed save also blinds the target until the end of its next turn.

Range extension confirmed: sixty feet, and the Step can now precede a strike within the attack sequence itself. Failed arrival strikes blind until the subject's next turn. The containment problem is scaling faster than our protocols. Blind Spot Step range becomes 60 feet. Once on your turn you may pay its CE cost and use it immediately before one attack in your Attack action instead of using a bonus action. Snap Strike upgrade: the blind lasts until the end of your next turn, and you may move 10 feet without provoking after the Step.

Terminal observation: failed arrival strikes now deny reactions and blind until the subject's next turn. The subject has additionally manifested the Chamber (see Domain entry). Pinnacle expression

Domain Expansion — Containment Chamber: DON’T BLINK

Research log. The chamber is a white room with no visible light source and one door that was not there when we entered. The walls are featureless except for a single stenciled instruction, repeated at eye level on every surface: DON'T BLINK. The air pressure changes when anyone's eyes close. We are measuring the interval. We do not like the interval.

ExpansionDomain event, designated Containment Chamber: for one minute, the barrier itself enforces the lapse, at each twenty-count, all hostile observation ends simultaneously, and each granted interval becomes a free Step. The subject has built a room in which its condition is always true. This is the most elegant and the most alarming entry in the file. Full-turn action, 20 CE, enclosed 60-foot-radius Domain for 1 minute. Every hostile creature knows exactly what Observation means when the barrier forms.

Biological Blink, sole sure-hit At initiative count 20, every chosen hostile creature’s Observation immediately ends and it cannot Observe you until the start of its next turn. A protected creature instead makes a Constitution save; on a success it may continue Observing if it already was.

Blink Windows Gain one Blink Window for each hostile affected by Biological Blink, maximum PB. Before the next initiative count 20, spend one Window instead of CE when using Blind Spot Step. You may spend no more than one Window per turn or reaction.

Accuracy remains yours Neck attacks, unarmed strikes, weapon attacks, and Snap Strike still require their normal attack rolls. The Domain guarantees the lapse in observation, not the kill.

Clash bookkeeping 500 clash points (Tier III). In a Domain clash, a 100+ point advantage collapses the weaker Domain; collapse deals no damage. Barrier: AC 12 + PB; HP = 10 × max(1, your CE modifier) + 500. Burnout: 10 rounds.

Rules integration

Table Rulings

Observation is a reaction commitment, not passive 360-degree D&D vision. This is what makes the SCP rule tactical instead of shutting the user off whenever an enemy exists.
Invisible creatures can Observe only if they can actually perceive you. Blindsight, truesight, cameras, familiars, and similar senses count if they provide direct perception.
Blind Spot Step is teleportation for rules purposes. It does not pass through anti-teleportation effects.
The technique never grants a literal instant neck snap against a healthy combatant; high-level lockdown is represented by Snap Strike riders and the Domain.
Snap Strike deals no bonus damage.

Subject file

Containment & observation record.

Containment Procedures

The cell is a featureless white sphere, ten meters across, with no corners, no fixtures, and a single painted mark at its center. The subject is the mark's only furnishing.

Four observers minimum, in rotation, on staggered blink schedules. No observer looks away without announcing "breaking" and receiving "covered" from the rotation. At least two observers maintain direct line of sight at all times. No fewer.

Cameras record; cameras do not contain. A camera has no reaction to spend, and the interval does not respect it.

Relief observers enter and establish watch before outgoing observers break theirs. There is no handoff gap. There has never been a handoff gap, and the file intends to keep it that way.

Prohibited: mirrors or polished surfaces as observer substitutes, solitary entry, and unscheduled blink drills. See Incident Report.

Research Log

Research log. 09/2024 — Recovery: the east wing went dark for nine seconds. Every camera agreed nothing had moved. Every witness agreed something had. The subject was found at the center of the dark, occupying a position no one saw it take. We staffed four observers that same night and have not been without them since.

Research log. 02/2025 — The reaction tax is confirmed across all trials: the watcher must commit a reaction at the start of every turn or the interval goes unguarded. The subject cannot Step and cannot Reflex under even one watchful eye. The hypothesis holds: the interval between looks is the only country it owns, and we are very good at looking.

Research log. 08/2025 — It forced the blink. Up to ██ observers within sixty feet had their Observation ended involuntarily, and the arrival strike now blinds. The subject is learning to manufacture the condition it needs. Or it always could, and we are only now observing it. Both readings are in the file. Neither is comforting.

Research log. 03/2026 — The subject has manifested the Chamber: a Domain in which the barrier itself enforces the lapse. It has built a room in which its condition is always true. Staffing is raised to six. The containment problem is scaling faster than our protocols, and the protocols know it.

Incident Report

Filed: mirror-substitution test, Wing C. During a staffing shortage, a polished steel panel was angled to substitute for a fourth observer during rotation change. The panel reflected the cell faithfully. The subject was nevertheless recorded standing at the door seam, and the outgoing observer's reaction had been spent — startled, by her own report, at nothing she could name.

No one was harmed. The panel has been removed and melted down, which was perhaps excessive, and perhaps not. Finding: mirrors are not observers. The file now lists "reflective surfaces" under prohibited substitutes, and the staffing minimum is absolute even during shortages. Especially during shortages.

Incident log

Breach report, declassified.

On ██/██, during a four-second lapse in observation, the object was recorded in Corridor C-9 and found standing beside Researcher Ellis, whose assignment had been to maintain unbroken eye contact.

The survivor's debrief was stricken from the record on the grounds of excessive screaming. Margin note, in a different hand: “It doesn't blink. We do.”
