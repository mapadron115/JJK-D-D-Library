Observer’s account

Field notes. Subject: the unbroken seat. Seated in this saddle, the mount is warded, attacks against it suffer disadvantage, and the rider cannot be dragged from the seat against their will, short of incapacitation. Two truths. First, cavalry dies from separation: the saddle solves the oldest failure of mounted war by binding rider and beast into one defended unit. Second, the ward is a shield, not a wall: disadvantage still permits a lucky strike, and incapacitation still unseats, the saddle buys the mount’s survival and the rider’s position, not their invulnerability. Classify this correctly: it is not a weapon but a formation, worn as leather. Hence the maxim: he who cannot be unhorsed has already won the ground his enemy must cross.
