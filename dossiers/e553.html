Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the student's dread. This technique is not a teacher's authority — it is a student's panic, the cold sweat of the unprepared, pushed outward through cursed energy until it becomes a weapon. Its wielder lived inside exam dread long enough that the dread became fluent, and now it speaks through other people's hands. Pop Quiz is a reaction: when a creature within 60 feet makes an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, you may spend 1 CE to spring a quiz on the subject of that action — name it aloud ("your footwork," "that incantation," "my name"). The target makes an Intelligence saving throw against your CE save DC. On a failure, the triggering roll is made with disadvantage and the target takes 1d8 psychic damage — the answer was wrong and everyone saw it. On a success, nothing happens; they knew the answer. There is no pool, no tracker, no hand of banked tests — the quiz exists only in the moment it is sprung, like every pop quiz that ever mattered.

Field notes. Three truths from the desk at the back of the room. First: the technique does not test what you know — it tests what you were doing, mid-swing, mid-word, and that is why it lands; nobody is ready to be examined on the thing their hands were already doing, which is the whole cruelty of the pop quiz and the whole point. Second: the wielder is not a teacher and never was one — teachers test from above, from confidence, from the answer key; this technique tests from below, from the seat where you sat, and it remembers exactly what it felt like to go blank, which is why it knows precisely where to press. Third: failing is not the punishment. Being watched while failing is the punishment — the damage is psychic because the wound is not in the body but in the moment the target realizes everyone just saw them not know. Mark the economy: dread is free, answers cost.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1Pop Quiz

Reaction · 1 CE · When a creature within 60 feet makes an attack roll, ability check, or saving throw, spring a Pop Quiz on the subject of that action (name it aloud). The target makes an INT save vs your CE save DC. On a failure: the triggering roll has disadvantage and the target takes 1d8 psychic damage. On a success: nothing.

The bread and butter, and it never competes with your own actions — it interrupts theirs. The surprise is the mechanic: a reaction, not an attack replacement, because a pop quiz does not wait for your turn. Every swing, every incantation, every desperate save is a potential exam, and the enemy learns quickly that doing anything in your sight is doing it under observation.

3Cram

Bonus action · 1 CE · When a creature within 60 feet uses a technique feature, make an INT check contested by their CE save DC. On a success, you learn what the feature does (effect, cost, range — the GM tells you truthfully) and your Pop Quizzes against that creature impose disadvantage on their INT save for 1 minute. You studied them like a textbook.

The new verb: L1 punishes the unprepared, L3 prepares — the student flips the desk. The technique was blind to what it was testing; it could spring quizzes all day and never know the subject. Now it studies. Ask what it couldn't do: understand the enemy. Now every technique you witness becomes a chapter you've read, and the next quiz is on material you've already covered.

6Midterms

Passive · Your Pop Quiz now deals 2d8 psychic damage on a failed save. Additionally, a creature that fails its INT save against your Pop Quiz becomes Tested until the end of your next turn: it has disadvantage on ability checks and cannot take reactions — frozen mid-answer, hand half-raised.

The system deepens and the foundation sharpens with it: harder quizzes, and a condition that lingers past the moment. Tested is the exam-hall freeze made mechanical — the target isn't hurt worse, it's stuck, second-guessing every choice while the clock runs. The bread and butter now leaves a mark, and the mark is hesitation.

11Finals Week

Action · 8 CE · Once per short rest · For 1 minute, exam conditions fall on every hostile creature within 60 feet of you. At the start of each of their turns, each hostile makes an INT save vs your CE save DC. On a failure: they take 2d8 psychic damage and become Tested until the start of their next turn. On a success: nothing — they studied.

High power, not a finisher: the limiter is the short-rest gate, not the CE — one finals week per rest, and it doesn't end anyone by itself. It changes the terms: the whole room is suddenly taking the same test at the same time, every turn is an exam, and the ones who didn't prepare start failing in public. The technique stops picking targets and starts administering.

18Honor Roll

Passive · Your Pop Quiz can now spring on any d20 roll a creature within 60 feet makes — including initiative and death saving throws. Tested now lasts 1 minute instead of one round. You can maintain Cram's studied-enemy benefit against up to PB creatures at once.

Enablement, not a nuke: the technique stops missing moments and starts owning them. Every roll is a potential quiz, every failure lingers, every enemy is a textbook you've already read — you are very difficult to act against now, not because any single test is the hardest on the field, but because there is no longer any moment that isn't being graded.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

The room goes quiet in the specific way it does when the papers are being handed out face-down. You look at one creature — just one — and you can see the exact moment they understand: this is not a quiz. This is the final. There are three sections. There is no partial credit. Pencils down was a warning; this is the consequence.

Maximum Technique, The Final Exam (action, 10 CE): target one creature within 60 feet. They sit the exam: three consecutive INT saving throws against your CE save DC (three sections, taken in order, no pause between). Each failed section deals 3d10 psychic damage. If they fail all three sections, they are stunned for 1 minute (they may repeat the save at the end of each of their turns) — the blank-page freeze, total. A creature that passes all three sections takes no damage and cannot be targeted by The Final Exam again until they finish a long rest — they earned the grade.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Test Anxiety

Bonus action · 2 CE · For 1 minute, dread radiates 15 feet from you: hostile creatures in the aura have disadvantage on concentration saving throws. The room feels like an exam hall and everyone's hands are shaking. Dread, but as disruption.

Cliff Notes

Passive · When you roll initiative, you may immediately Cram one creature you can see, no action or CE required — you skimmed their file before class started. Cramming, but in advance.

The Curve

Reaction · 2 CE · When an ally within 30 feet fails a saving throw, grade on a curve: they reroll the save and take the better result. Everybody fails sometimes; the generous teacher pretends not to notice. Testing, but as protection.

Senioritis

Passive · You have advantage on Intelligence saving throws and immunity to the frightened condition. You have survived so many exams that fear itself reads as a Tuesday. Dread, but as armor.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: Pop Quiz's INT save is a saving throw the target makes — features that grant advantage on saves against being frightened or charmed do not apply unless they apply to all INT saves. The "subject" named aloud is flavor; it has no mechanical restriction.
Tested does not stack with itself — reapplying refreshes the duration. A creature immune to the frightened condition is not immune to Tested (it is not fear, it is performance anxiety — the rules are crueler than that).
Cram's learned information is truthful but limited to the observed feature's mechanics, not the creature's full dossier. The GM decides how much a single observation reveals.
The Final Exam's three sections are resolved immediately in order; a creature that drops to 0 HP mid-exam stops taking the remaining sections. Stun from failing all three follows standard stun rules.
