Core law

What the technique actually does.

Core law, the lunar phase. Your cursed energy is moonlight borrowed and returned. The technique's power and character are set by the current lunar phase, determined by the in-game calendar — the GM tracks the date, and the moon does the rest. There is no pool, no tracker, no engine: the sky is the mechanic. Moon-Touched (passive): you are attuned to the lunar cycle — you always know the current phase, the date, and the nights remaining until it changes. Moon-Touched (trigger) · 1 CE · When you hit a creature with an attack, you may infuse the blow with moonlight: the current phase's rider applies to the hit. New Moon (the dark, ~3 nights): the attack is silent and leaves no visible flash; your CE cannot be sensed — you register as a non-sorcerer to detection — and you have advantage on Stealth checks. Half Moon (the threshold, ~7 nights): after the hit, you may move 10 feet without provoking opportunity attacks. Gibbous Moon (the tide's pull, ~7 nights): the target makes a Strength save or is pulled 10 feet toward you or pushed 10 feet away (your choice). Full Moon (the catastrophe, ~3 nights): the attack deals an additional 1d8 force damage.

Field notes. Three truths from the observatory. First: the technique has no opinion about you — the moon was turning long before your birth and will turn long after your death, and your cursed energy is merely the current thing it happens to be shining through; practitioners who forget this start believing they command the cycle, and the cycle has a way of correcting them publicly. Second: every phase is a complete fighting style, and the masters are the ones who stopped resenting the new moon — the dark is not the absence of the technique, it is the technique at its quietest, and the sorcerer who can win silently on the moonless nights is the one the full moon cannot embarrass. Third: your enemies can read a calendar. The clever ones will schedule the ambush for the new moon and the parley for the full. Let them. The moon rewards those who plan around it and punishes those who plan against it, and there is no third category.

Full progression

Level 1 through 20.

1Moon-Touched

Passive · You are attuned to the lunar cycle: you always know the current phase, the date, and the nights until it changes. Trigger · 1 CE · When you hit a creature with an attack, infuse the blow with moonlight — the current phase's rider applies (New: silent, unsensed, advantage on Stealth; Half: 10 ft free move after the hit; Gibbous: pull/push 10 ft on failed STR save; Full: +1d8 force damage).

The bread and butter, riding the hits you were landing anyway — Amber's trigger, not Shrine's replacement: four techniques wearing one body, and the calendar picks which one shows up. The new-moon infiltrator and the full-moon calamity are the same person on different nights, and the dossier insists you learn to love both — the sorcerer who only fights at full moon is a tourist in their own technique.

3Borrowed Light

Action · 3 CE · Once per long rest · Perform a brief lunar rite: for 1 minute, you gain the passive and strike rider of a lunar phase other than the current one. You cannot borrow the Full Moon — the true full belongs to the calendar alone, and the rite knows it.

The new verb: L1 is the moon as it is, L3 is the moon as you need it to be. Not bigger — different. The technique was blind to the wrong night; now it can cheat, a little, once, at a price the calendar sets. Note the honesty of the restriction: the rite refuses the full moon the way a lock refuses the wrong key, and that refusal is what keeps the calendar meaningful.

6The Tide's Pull

Passive · Your Moon-Touched trigger sharpens with the cycle: New — your infused attacks leave no CE residue (zan'e); Half — the free movement becomes 15 feet; Gibbous — the pull/push becomes 15 feet; Full — the additional damage becomes 2d8 force. Action · 3 CE · Tidecall: tidal force surges through a 30-foot radius within 60 feet — each creature makes a Strength save or falls prone, and the area becomes difficult terrain for 1 minute. During the Gibbous Moon, the radius is 45 feet and the save is made with disadvantage; during the New Moon, the surge is silent.

The first system arrives and the foundation sharpens with it: harder strikes in every phase, and the moon's oldest trick — the tide — made into battlefield control. The gibbous practitioner finally gets the ocean they were promised; the new-moon practitioner gets a prone enemy who never heard it coming. The technique grows wider without abandoning a single night of the month.

11Phase Eclipse

Action · 10 CE · Once per long rest · For 1 minute, you impose an artificial eclipse on the battlefield: treat the current lunar phase as one step stronger (New → Half → Gibbous → Full). You cannot reach the Full Moon this way — the calendar's catastrophe is not yours to counterfeit. During the eclipse, attacks carrying your Moon-Touched trigger ignore resistance.

High power, not a finisher: the limiter is the sky, not the CE — you can cheat the moon upward but never all the way, and the long-rest gate means the eclipse is a decision, not a habit. It doesn't end the fight. It moves the month forward by force, and dares the enemy to fight you on a night they didn't plan for.

18Lunar Constant

Passive · You carry the moon with you: underground, indoors, or during daylight, you always count as being under at least a Half Moon for your technique (the calendar still governs stronger phases). Borrowed Light can now borrow the Full Moon — but doing so costs 5 CE instead of 3, and leaves you with one level of exhaustion when the minute ends.

Enablement, not a nuke: the technique stops abandoning you when the sky is wrong. The new-moon nights are still the new-moon nights — the dossier refuses to erase the cycle's weakest face, because the weakness was always the point — but you are never again without at least the threshold. And the full moon, borrowed at last, collects its toll in exhaustion the way the real one collects it in consequences.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

The light goes wrong first — not dark, but eclipsed, a ring of fire around a black disc where the moon should be, and it is the wrong moon, it is every moon at once. The shadows sharpen into blades. You stand in the center of the ring, and for one minute, the calendar stops arguing with you.

Maximum Technique, Totality (action, 10 CE, once per long rest): for 1 minute, you gain the Full Moon's rider and the New Moon's untraceability simultaneously — your Moon-Touched trigger's Full Moon damage becomes 3d8 force, your CE cannot be sensed, you have advantage on Stealth checks, and attacks against you have disadvantage. Once during Totality, as an action, you may call down the dark of the moon: each hostile creature within 120 feet makes a Dexterity save, taking 8d10 force damage on a failure (half on a success). When Totality ends, you suffer one level of exhaustion — the moon exacts its price.

Feats

Build-defining options.

Lunacy

When you use a Full Moon feature or Totality, each hostile creature within 30 feet that can see you makes a Wisdom save or is frightened of you until the end of your next turn. The moon has always made people strange; you merely weaponized it. Moonlight, but as madness.

Moonrest

Bonus action · 2 CE · At night, under the visible moon, bathe up to PB willing creatures in moonlight: each regains 2d8 HP. The same light that breaks bodies at full moon knits them in its gentler phases — the technique never distinguished between the two, only you did. Destruction, but as restoration.

Tidefinder

You always know true north, the current lunar phase, and the nights until the next full moon and the next eclipse. While traveling at night, you and your party cannot become lost by non-magical means. The moon, but as a compass.

The Dark Side

During the New Moon, divination magic fails to perceive you, scrying shows only darkness, and you leave no CE residue for trackers to follow. There is a side of the moon no one ever sees; for three nights a month, it is you. Stealth, but as erasure.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Codex rulings: Attacks carrying your Moon-Touched trigger use the normal Black Flash procedure for the underlying attack when otherwise eligible. The trigger's additional force damage cannot critically hit or trigger Black Flash independently — it rides the underlying attack's roll. Save-effect damage (the dark of the moon) cannot critically hit or Black Flash.
Generic Maximum Output applies only to ordinary features, never to Totality.
The lunar phase is set by the in-game calendar and cannot be changed by the practitioner except through Borrowed Light, Phase Eclipse, and Totality. If the campaign does not track dates, the GM assigns the phase at the start of each session (d8: 1 = New, 2-3 = Half, 4-5 = Gibbous, 6-8 = Full, weighted toward the longer phases).
Borrowed Light and Phase Eclipse cannot stack — the stronger effect applies. Totality's simultaneous Full/New benefits override any borrowed or eclipsed phase for its duration.
