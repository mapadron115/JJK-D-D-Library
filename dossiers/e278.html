Paralyzing Gaze

Your technique allows you to paralyze others with a mere gaze.

Power tierTier III · Apex
Combat roleController
Core engineEye-HP gaze debuff engine
DomainChamber of Fractured Reflections (18)

Field notes. Subject: the winter that looks back. Three truths govern this technique. First, every gaze is paid in the currency of sight: the eyes carry their own hit points — a third of the user's maximum, a half at the Maximum — and every resister wounds them, one damage for every point their roll clears the save DC. Second, the cold deepens in stages: slowed and weakened at 1st level, spread to many by peripheral vision at 3rd, then the Maximum's true paralysis at 11th — concentration-held, and the struggling victim's Athletics check wounds the eyes twice over. Third, the Domain is the blizzard's logic perfected: mirrors on every side make line of sight unbreakable, and at initiative 20 three chosen creatures simply suffer the gaze. Mark this: a sorcerer who stops the world with a look has agreed to let the looking cost him. It is one person's refusal to blink, priced in blood.

Full progression

Feature map

1Immobilizing Eyes.

The founding gaze: two energy as an action, and the looked-upon is halved in speed and diminished in strike — while the eyes begin their slow bleeding. Starting at 1st level, you gain the ability to slow anyone you have within line of sight. As an action for 2 CE, you force a creature within 60 ft to make a Dexterity saving throw. On a failure, their movement speed is halved and their attack rolls and AC are reduced by your Cursed Energy modifier until the beginning of your next turn. At the beginning of your next turn, you can spend 1 CE as a bonus action to maintain the technique. If you lose line of sight, the technique automatically ends. Using your technique places extreme strain on your eyes. Your eyes have a number of hit points equal to 1/3rd of your maximum hit points(rounded up). Your eyes take 1 damage for every 1 a creature rolls above your save DC to resist your technique. Your eyes regain 1 hit point at the beginning of your turn, unless at 0 hit points. Any HP you regain affects your eyes as well. If your eyes reach 0 hit points, you are blinded and they can't regain hit points unless by Reverse Cursed Technique or long rest.

3Peripheral Vision.

The gaze widened: a full-turn action to hold many at once, an energy per extra soul, and a per-creature price to keep them held. At 3rd level, you have trained your peripheral vision to lock down multiple targets at once. When you use Immobilizing Eyes, you instead spend a Full-Turn Action (Action + Bonus Action + Movement) to affect a number of creatures up to your Cursed Energy modifier, spending 1 CE for every creature beyond the first. You must spend 1 CE for each targeted creature to maintain your technique against them.

6Reactive Eye Movement.

The gaze as reflex: two energy to meet an incoming attack with a look, and the already-held fail without rolling. At 6th level, whenever a target you can see makes an attack roll while within 60 ft or the attack is against you, you can spend 2 CE as a reaction to force that creature to make a Dexterity saving throw. On a failure, they have disadvantage on the attack roll. A creature under the effects of Immobilizing Eyes automatically fails this saving throw.

11Maximum: Paralyzing Gaze.

The true paralysis: six energy for the unmoving, unmovable victim — held by concentration and a per-turn tithe of energy. At 11th level, you have learned to completely paralyze targets with your technique. Your eyes' hit points increase to 1/2 of your maximum hit points (rounded up). As an action for 6 cursed energy, you force a creature you can see within 120 ft to make a Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, they are paralyzed and have their AC reduced by your Cursed Energy modifier. You must maintain concentration on the technique as if concentrating on a spell and at the beginning of your turn by spending 2 CE as a bonus action. However, focusing your power to this extent leaves you vulnerable. The target can make an Strength (Athletics) check at the end of their turn. On a success, your eyes take 2 damage for every 1 they rolled above your Cursed Energy save DC (damage to your eyes does not force a concentration check). A creature paralyzed by this technique cannot be physically moved, even remaining suspended in the air.

18Domain Expansion: Chamber of Fractured Reflections.

The mirrors: line of sight that cannot break, and three chosen creatures held by the Domain's own gaze at initiative 20. At 18th level, you have learned to expand your domain. At initiative 20, three creatures of your choice within the domain suffer the effects of Immobilizing Eyes. At each initiative 20, you can change the target for your domain's guaranteed hit effect. The domain takes the appearance of several mirrors that reflect your gaze from every angle, making line of sight impossible to break within the domain.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it.

Prerequisite: Must have Immobilizing Eyes

Immobilized Limbs

The gaze given anatomy: legs to halve and topple, arms to disarm, torso to strip of armor — one energy for the focus. When you use Immobilizing Eyes, you can spend 1 additional CE as part of activating the technique concentrate your eyes on a specific part of the creature, also causing one of the following restrictions while you maintain your technique: ● Legs. The target's speed is halved. If the target tries to move more than their movement speed, they must make a Dexterity saving throw or fall prone. ● Arms. The target gains an additional -3 penalty to attack rolls and cannot take object interactions. ● Torso. You reduce the target's AC by 3.

Prerequisite: Must have Peripheral Vision

Measured Gaze

The gaze learned soft: speed halved and nothing more, no strain on the eyes, and one soul releasable without freeing the rest. You have learned to measure your gaze. When you use Immobilizing Eyes, you can soften it: the target's speed is halved but its attack rolls and AC are unaffected, and your eyes take no strain damage from that target resisting. You can also end the technique on one creature you are maintaining it against without ending it on the others.

Prerequisite: Must have Reactive Eye Movement

Eye Drops

The eyes taught to rest: a bonus action, an energy, closed lids — and the bleeding staunched at the price of a turn's blindness. Through constant exposure, your eyes have learned to rest between strains. As a bonus action for 1 CE, you can close your eyes and let them recover — your eyes regain hit points equal to your CE modifier, but you are blinded until the start of your next turn.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Flag — Maximum naming: the 11th-level Maximum is named "Paralyzing Gaze," identical to the technique itself. A rename (e.g. "Maximum: Total Paralysis") is proposed but not adopted without approval.
Eye damage on resist: eye damage from a resister's save applies even when the resist succeeds, as printed.
Speed-halving overlap: the Legs restriction's speed-halving overlaps the base Immobilizing Eyes speed-halving — run both as printed (they do not double-halve; the campaign's standard rule for multiple speed reductions applies).
Destructive Gaze dice: the die progression (2d8 → 2d10 → 4d8 → 6d8) is irregular but printed; keep as-is, do not smooth it.
Domain conversion: initiative-20 timing already matches; clashes use the deterministic 1–1,000 point system; barrier per D007; burnout per D010. The sure-hit applies Immobilizing Eyes to the three chosen creatures with the retargeting procedure as printed.

Prerequisite: Must have Reactive Eye Movement

 Through constant exposure, your eyes have become surprisingly tough.

bonus each long rest.
