Ice Formation

Repeatable restraint and cold zones that escalate into mass targeting and a lethal icefall.

Core law

Completed rules

The posted physics of the technique’s ice: restraining ice is an object, AC 15, HP 5 × PB, immune to cold and poison, vulnerable to fire, broken by Strength saves or by any blade. The law is precise because the ice is honest: it holds exactly as well as it holds, and no better. Ice restraining a creature is an object occupying its space with AC 15, HP equal to 5 × PB, immunity to cold and poison, and vulnerability to fire. The creature repeats a Strength save at the end of each turn; any creature may also attack the ice. Destroying it ends restrained.

Free-standing technique ice persists until dismissed, destroyed, or you become poisoned, paralyzed, stunned, petrified, or unconscious. It is difficult terrain. Restraining ice cannot stack on one creature.

Field notes. Subject: a refusal that froze over, grief that stopped asking targets to surrender and simply kept them. Three truths govern the winter. First, the ice is honest: restraining ice is an object, AC 15, HP 5 × PB, immune to cold and poison, vulnerable to fire, broken by a Strength save or any blade. Second, the held pay a tax: Extreme Cold adds an exhaustion level that vanishes when the restraint ends, and the unresisting move at half speed near persistent ice. Third, the refusal escalates: a 10-foot cone at 1 CE, a 30-foot-radius mist at 4 CE for 7d8, and Ice Fall’s gambit, the sorcerer prone, one attack on every creature within 30 feet. And the Domain declares winter as law: at every initiative 20, White Abyss deals 10d8 cold and restraint to every chosen creature, no roll, no save. The held breath, made a room.

Ice Fall

Action · 8 CE · You fall prone until the start of your next turn · One CE attack compared against every creature within 30 feet: on a hit, 1d10 cold damage and restrained (Strength save at the beginning of its turns to break free) · Hit or miss, each targeted creature also makes a Dexterity save: 6d8 cold + 6d8 piercing damage, half on success; roll the spike damage once.

The sorcerer falls prone until his next turn, and one CE attack is compared against every creature within 30 feet: 1d10 cold and restraint on each hit. He spends his own footing to take everyone else’s. As an action for 8 CE, you fall prone until the start of your next turn. Choose any number of creatures within 30 feet. Make one CE attack and compare it with each target’s AC: on a hit a target takes 1d10 cold damage and becomes restrained (it may make a Strength save at the beginning of its turns to break the ice and end the restrained condition). Hit or miss, giant spikes of ice fall on the targeted creatures: each makes a Dexterity save, taking 6d8 cold damage and 6d8 piercing damage on failure or half as much on success. Roll the spike damage once.

Pinnacle expression

Maximum, Domain, or equivalent.

Domain Expansion, White Abyss:

Color drains from the world, leaving only white, the white of snowblindness, of bone, of a page with nothing written on it. The ground is ice so clear it shows a depth that cannot exist, an abyss beneath your feet that goes down forever, and the air itself seems frozen into crystalline stillness. It is not cold. Cold would be a mercy. It is the absence of warmth as a physical place.

At every initiative count 20, every chosen creature except the sorcerer takes 10d8 cold and is restrained, no attack roll, no initial save. The Domain is winter declared as law: the held breath, made a room. 18: full-turn action, 20 CE; 60-foot-radius enclosed Domain centered on you; 1 minute; Burnout 10 rounds. Sure-Hit Event, White Abyss: at initiative count 20 each round, every chosen creature except you takes 10d8 cold damage and is restrained, with no attack roll and no initial save. A restrained creature may make a Strength (Athletics) check against your CE save DC at the beginning of its turn to break free (DC invented). A creature protected by an anti-domain defense makes a Constitution saving throw vs your CE save DC when the event resolves (invented); on a success it takes half damage and is not restrained.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Rules priority: the reviewed rulings, explicit codex clarifications, and the level progression always control when original wording conflicts. Legacy Domain auto-hit language, action terminology, event timing, missing saves, and older scaling never override revised player rules.
Why Tier II: it reliably controls space and actions, but the ice is tangible, destructible, and governed by repeated saves.
