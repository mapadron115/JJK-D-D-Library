Clone

Your technique allows you to create perfect clones of yourself.

Power tierTier III · Apex
Combat roleStriker
Core engineCursed-energy self-duplication
DomainDivide and Conquer (L18)

Field notes. Subject: a parliament of one. Three truths govern it. First: the duplicates are perfect in every way except the ones that matter, a quarter of the hit points, no innate technique features, no items, and every coin of cursed energy they spend comes out of the same purse. Second: the clone economy is honest. When one falls, a single CE returns to the pool, the death is priced, not mourned, and the standing army is capped at the proficiency bonus. Third: at the eleventh level the question the technique has been dodging finally arrives, which one is you? Body-swap trades hit points for a new body; death itself can be transferred into a clone. Mark this as the founding paradox: it was built by someone who refused to accept there was only one of them, and it never once asks which of you gets to stop.

Full progression

Feature map

1One Man Army.

Action · 3 CE · Create a clone within 5 ft for 8 hrs: indistinguishable; 1/4 your HP max (rounded up), your AC; has all your class features and cursed techniques (not innate technique features); its CE spends come from your pool.

Three coins and an action open the debate, a clone within five feet, indistinguishable, a quarter of your hit points, your class features and cursed techniques but never the innate ones, spending from your pool. Starting at 1st level, you can spend 3 CE as an action to create a clone within 5 feet of yourself for 8 hours. Clones are completely indistinguishable from you in every way. A clone has 1/4 of your hit point maximum (rounded up), your AC, and access to all your class features and cursed techniques, but not innate technique features. Any cursed energy a clone spends is treated as if you spent it.

Clone rules · Clones act on your turn: one attack each (even with Extra Attack); you can replace your attacks with attacks from a clone's space · No items · Max = PB at once; a dead clone reduces your cap by 1 for 1 min.

Your clones act on your turn, but can only make one attack on their turn even if you have Extra Attack or bonus action attacks. You can replace any of your attacks on your turn with an attack from the clone's space. Your clones do not have any of your items. You can have a number of clones equal to your proficiency bonus at once, and when a clone dies, the number of clones you can have is reduced by 1 for 1 minute.

3Twice the Fun.

Upgrade · Action: create multiple clones at once (3 CE each) · Melee attacks vs a creature with a clone within 5 ft of it: advantage.

At the third level the argument multiplies in one action, three coins per clone, and any melee strike made with a clone beside the target lands at advantage. At 3rd level, you can now create multiple clones as an action by spending 3 CE for each clone. When you make a melee attack against a creature while a clone is within 5 feet of the target, you gain advantage on the attack roll.

6Three is a Crowd.

Upgrade · Clones make as many attacks as you; 1/2 your HP · A clone reduced to 0 HP: you regain 1 CE.

The sixth level promotes the duplicates, full attack routines, half your hit points, and a single coin of cursed energy refunded when one dies. At 6th level you have become more skillful and efficient with the creation of your clones. Your clones can now make as many attacks as you and have 1/2 of your total hit points. When one of your clones is reduced to 0 hit points, you regain 1 cursed energy.

11Four is a Party.

Upgrade · Clones have your full HP · Bonus action · 1 CE: swap bodies with a clone (trade HP; it becomes your body) · Reduced to 0 HP or killed with a clone active: transfer into a clone with its current HP.

The eleventh level collapses the question of identity, clones at your full hit points, body-swaps by bonus action, death transferable into a spare, and clones that can spend your energy to make clones of their own. At 11th level, you have mastered the ability to choose which of your clones is your actual body. Your clones have hit points equal to yours and you can swap bodies with one of your clones as a bonus action for 1 CE, trading hit points and causing that clone to become your new body. When you are reduced to 0 hit points or killed outright while you have a clone active, you can instead transfer yourself into one of your clones, gaining the clone's current hit points.

Upgrade · Your clones can each create one clone per turn by spending your CE (counts against your limit).

Your clones can now create one clone by spending your cursed energy, with each clone counting against your limit. Each clone can create one clone per turn.

18Domain Expansion: Divide and Conquer.

Domain (18th level) · Once per turn: create one clone as a bonus action for no CE (your clones don't get this) · Unlimited clones inside; clones' attacks are guaranteed hits · Initiative 20: one more clone appears · Domain-created clones vanish when the domain ends.

The domain opens onto a parliament hall with no ceiling, rows of empty seats rising in tiers into darkness, each one waiting. At the chamber's heart stands a single podium, and behind it the user's banner hangs impossibly large, snapping in a wind that touches nothing else. It is a government of one, about to be a government of many.

The Domain is the parliament in permanent session, a free clone every turn by bonus action, no cap on the standing army, guaranteed hits inside the barrier, and at initiative twenty the house gains another member. At 18th level you have perfected your technique, allowing you to expand your domain. You can now create one clone as a bonus action without spending cursed energy once per turn (your clones do not gain this benefit). Within your domain, you can have an unlimited number of clones, and your clones' attacks are guaranteed hits within your domain. At initiative 20, an additional clone appears. All clones created with your domain disappear when the domain ends.

Innate Technique Feats

Build-defining options.

These are optional character-building feats. You must meet each listed prerequisite before selecting it. V.6 prints no feats for Clone; the four below are codex-authored fills grounded in the printed kit (see appendix).

Prerequisite: Must have One Man Army

Many Hands

The clones learn to deliver as well as strike, touch-range cursed techniques may originate from any clone’s space within sixty feet. Your clones can deliver your techniques as well as your strikes. When you use a cursed technique with a range of touch, you can originate it from the space of one of your clones within 60 feet of you instead of your own space.

Prerequisite: Must have Twice the Fun

Shell Game

The enemy can never be sure which body is you, a bonus action trades places with any clone within sixty feet. Your enemies can never be sure which body is you. As a bonus action, you can swap positions with one of your clones within 60 feet of you.

Prerequisite: Must have Four is a Party

Shared Burden

The duplicates learn to suffer in your place, a bonus action transfers a condition to a clone within thirty feet, and the clone bears it instead. You have learned to trade more than bodies and hit points. As a bonus action, you can transfer one condition affecting you, blinded, charmed, deafened, frightened, poisoned, or restrained, to one of your clones within 30 feet of you; the clone suffers the condition instead.

Prerequisite: Must have One Man Army

Long Leash

The duplication outgrows arm’s reach, clones may now be created within thirty feet instead of five. Your duplication is no longer confined to arm's reach. You can create clones within 30 feet of yourself instead of 5 feet.

Prerequisite: Must have One Man Army

Meant to be Expendable

Four coins and a reaction summon a clone mid-blow, it shoves you five feet aside and takes the damage in your place. As a reaction to damage, you can spend 4 CE to summon a Clone, which shoves you 5 feet and takes the damage in your place.

Prerequisite: Must have Four is a Party

Maximum: Conga Line

The Maximum is the whole parliament moving as one, eight coins as a free action, and every body moves thirty feet and takes the attack action. You can spend 8 CE as a free action to tag team an enemy with your Clones, making a Conga Line on your way to stomp them. You and all clones can immediately move 30 feet and take the attack action.

Rules integration

Edge cases and shared systems.

Not Body Clone: this is the standalone V.6 Clone technique, a full 1–18 progression. It is not the "Body Clone" feature of V.3 Body Manipulation. The two are different sources with different mechanics and do not stack or convert; a character selects one or the other.
Shared cursed energy: clones draw from your cursed energy pool, any CE a clone spends is treated as if you spent it. Clone-spawned clones likewise spend your CE and count against your proficiency-bonus cap.
Clone cap: you can maintain a number of clones equal to your proficiency bonus at once; when a clone dies, your cap drops by 1 for 1 minute. The domain's unlimited clones are a domain-granted exception and vanish when the domain ends.
Domain conversion: Divide and Conquer is adopted with campaign conversion: the initiative-20 clone arrival stands as the sure-hit event; clones' guaranteed hits apply to attacks made inside the domain; clash via the point system; the bonus-action free clone costs the user's bonus action once per turn.
Rules priority: the printed V.6 values and the clarifications above always control when interpretations conflict. The four Innate Technique Feats are codex-authored fills, marked as such, and may be removed or replaced by the GM without affecting the printed kit.
