Section I

Cursed Energy & the CE Reserve

Cursed energy is the negative emotion you weaponize. It lives in a reserve that behaves like hit points: it grows with every level, it empties as you spend it, and it is the whole character's — not any one class's.

The reserve formula

Maximum CE Starting die maximum + every later level's Reserve gain + CE ability modifier (once) + permanent bonuses

When you first gain cursed energy, choose Wisdom or Charisma as your CE ability, unless your class names another score. The choice applies to every class, technique, feat, and item you use. At character level 1, take the maximum of your starting class's Reserve Die and add your CE ability modifier. At every later level, roll that class's Reserve Die or take its fixed gain. The CE ability modifier is added only once to the whole reserve — never per level. If the modifier changes, adjust maximum and current CE by the same amount. Your maximum CE is always at least 1 if you possess cursed energy.

Fixed-gain milestones (assuming a +5 CE modifier and no permanent bonuses): a d4 class holds 9 CE at level 1, 39 at level 11, 66 at level 20; d6 holds 11 / 51 / 87; d8 holds 13 / 63 / 108; d10 holds 15 / 75 / 129.

One pool, one ability, one modifier

Multiclassing never creates several independent CE pools. Use the maximum Reserve Die of your starting class at character level 1; every later character level adds the Reserve roll or fixed gain of the class taken at that level. Add the CE ability modifier once to the combined reserve — a new class never adds it again, and never grants a second CE ability. Total character level sets your proficiency bonus; each class's features still advance on that class's levels.

Example — Warrior 5 / Support Specialist 3, +4 CE modifier, fixed gains: 6 (starting d6) + 16 (four later Warrior levels × 4) + 15 (three Support levels × 5) + 4 (modifier once) = 41 maximum CE.

Attack, save DC, and spending

  • CE attack modifier = proficiency bonus + CE ability modifier.
  • CE save DC = 8 + proficiency bonus + CE ability modifier.
  • Recovery: you regain all spent CE when you finish a long rest — not during combat or on a short rest, unless a feature expressly says so.
  • Pay the listed cost. If a feature costs 6 CE, pay 6 CE. Availability is not gated by level: once a class, technique, feat, ritual, or breakthrough grants an ability, you may use it whenever you can pay its cost and meet its trigger — even if that creates exceptional output earlier than expected.
  • Variable input (X). If the feature gives no ceiling, X may be any positive amount you can currently pay. A printed limit (CE modifier, PB, 10 CE) still applies.
  • No universal Output Rank. A large reserve is allowed to create a large moment. There is no level-based fixed-cost gate, proficiency-bonus overcharge cap, or universal accuracy ceiling — the feature's own cost and printed limits are the procedure. Named limits that stop a feature from triggering itself remain binding.
  • Cost arithmetic: base cost → add optional CE and extra targets → apply multiplier (e.g. Maximum Output) → apply cost reduction. Halved costs round up; CE is spent on activation and is not refunded by a miss, a saved-against effect, or a broken Focus. If you cannot pay the full calculated cost, the activation does not begin — you may choose a lower legal X first.

Temporary cursed energy

Borrowed output, not a permanent expansion. Temporary CE can raise current CE above maximum but never changes the maximum; it is spent before normal CE; it cannot be restored and vanishes when its source ends; repeated uses of the same source do not stack. Temporary CE may power techniques but cannot pay a permanent creation cost, a permanent Binding Vow cost, or any cost explicitly requiring your natural CE.

❖ Canon note

This formula replaces every older fixed class-table CE total (and old formulas like level + modifier or 20 + modifier). The old guide's fixed CE milestones above are recomputed from the same formula — reserve dice now scale like hit points across levels.

Section II

Innate Techniques

Every sorcerer is born with one innate technique — the shape their cursed energy takes. It arrives at level 1 and deepens across your whole career.

Milestones

Every sorcerer class gains its Innate Technique at level 1, with major technique feature slots at class levels 3, 6, 11, and 18 — each slot uses the selected technique's own feature text. A completed technique framework may also print automatic internal unlocks at other levels; those belong to that technique's progression and do not consume a class feature slot. Above that lie the level 20 capstone, the Maximum technique, and the Domain.

Technique grades — descriptive, not a ban list

Every technique is catalogued on the jujutsu grade scale, from Grade 4 up through Special Grade and beyond. The grade measures the technique's normal encounter influence, narrative reach, rules load, and late-game volatility — it never gates selection or weakens output.

  • Reality Warping. Reserved for techniques that edit the resolution layer itself (dice faces, turns, dimensions, local physics). Not a promotion for an optimized Calamity.
  • Calamity. A campaign-defining endpoint whose full release can invalidate normal encounter assumptions. Still a standard player choice — the label sets expectations.
  • Special Grade. Campaign-warping: permanent acquisition, armies, copying, nullification, body or soul rewriting, bespoke rules engines.
  • Grade 1. Strong but bounded — the ceiling of disciplined sorcery. No rewritten rules, no warped campaign; just excellence, perfected.
  • Grade 2. Consistent scene control, unusually high output, early Domain access, or party-wide swing potential.
  • Grade 3. Multiple tactical vectors, mobility, control, support, or a contained summon package.
  • Grade 4. A narrow, dependable combat identity. Strong when used well, easy to prepare around.
  • Unrated. Frameworks and toolkits rather than finished techniques — unranked by design, not by weakness.

Technique Focus

Technique Focus works like spell concentration: you maintain only one Focus effect at a time. Taking damage forces a Constitution save, DC 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher. Focus ends if you are incapacitated, die, end it voluntarily, or begin another Focus effect. When old technique text is silent about sustaining, apply this rule of thumb:

  • Self-buff or transformation — 1 minute; requires Focus if it grants major combat benefits.
  • Hostile condition with no repeat save — until the end of the source's next turn.
  • Hostile condition with repeat saves — up to 1 minute; repeat at the end of each affected turn.
  • Persistent hazard, zone, or movable effect — 1 minute and Focus.

Up to two techniques per sorcerer

Every character may have up to two innate techniques — this is a campaign rule, not a variant, and multiclassing is neither required nor sufficient to gain one: multiclassing alone never grants a second cursed technique. Technique milestones always use total character level (multiclass levels are never summed or split for technique progression). With two techniques, both advance by total character level, but both still share one CE Reserve, one CE ability, one action economy, one Focus slot, one reaction pool, and the normal Domain and Burnout limits. Gaining a second technique never creates duplicate CE, extra turns, a second Focus slot, or simultaneous Domains.

Full technique records — every grade, the complete progression map, and binding mechanical rulings for each — live in the Technique Codex.

Section III

Binding Vows

A binding vow is a contract written on your own cursed energy. Heaven — the world itself — enforces it. That enforcement is what makes a vow powerful, and it is also what makes one dangerous to make.

The iron law: every vow has a price

No vow grants anything for nothing. A vow trades restriction for power (limit yourself, gain something), or power for consequence (declare something, pay if you break it). The price must be real: lost options, lost resources, a permanent cost, a genuine vulnerability. A vow whose "restriction" never actually restricts you grants nothing — Heaven can tell.

The structure every vow needs

  • The restriction or declaration, stated plainly. What you cannot do, must do, or have declared — in words every creature at the table can understand.
  • The gain. What the vow returns: more output, a new option, a sharper edge. Sized to the price.
  • The penalty for breaking. What you lose if you violate it — paid automatically, without appeal. Breaking a vow always costs more than keeping it.

Compulsory effects need an out

A vow that compels another creature's behavior — forcing them to act, speak, or be bound — must give the target a fair procedure: a saving throw against the vow-maker's CE save DC, or a willing-decline valve (the target may refuse and suffer a stated, bounded consequence instead of being compelled outright). No vow removes another creature's agency without giving them a roll or a choice.

No precognition cheese

Vows cannot be used to extract information the vow-maker could not otherwise know — no vows that reveal the future, read hidden truths, or turn the contract into a divination engine. A vow binds conduct and cost; it is not a window.

Working with vows at the table

  • Permanent vow costs must be paid with your natural CE or a real permanent sacrifice — temporary CE can never fund one.
  • A vow's restriction and its gain are both active at all times unless the vow names a window.
  • Maximum Output cannot enhance Binding Vows, and vows do not bypass Technique Burnout.
  • The GM adjudicates whether a proposed vow's price is genuine. Err on the side of a steeper price — the game will be better for it.
❖ Canon note

The older guides carry no standing vow engine — scattered feats reference Binding Vows but define no procedure. The rules above are the campaign's table canon: real price, saves or a willing-decline valve for compulsion, and no precognition.

Section IV

Domains

Domain Expansion is the pinnacle of jujutsu: your innate domain given form, your technique written onto the world itself. Expanding one is a fight-defining commitment — and ending one leaves a mark.

The complete Domain — baseline

StatisticValue
ActivationFull-turn action (your action, bonus action, object interaction, and all movement for the turn) plus the required hands or signs.
Cost20 CE.
Area60-foot-radius enclosed space centered on you.
TargetsChoose perceived creatures in the area when it opens. Every other creature is moved harmlessly to the nearest space outside.

A technique-specific Domain may change its action, cost, shape, range, target rule, or duration — its explicit wording wins. Opening a Domain ends your current Technique Focus, and the Domain occupies that Focus slot.

Refinement — a milestone, not a currency

Refinement tiers are awarded by the GM after a meaningful breakthrough, a decisive clash, sustained narrative training, or technique evolution — never for defeating trivial creatures, dumping unused CE before bed, or staging friendly clashes. Each tier converts into clash points, and clash points set both duration and Burnout.

Sure-hits — two philosophies

Modern philosophy

The sure-hit event — initiative count 20

Every complete lethal Domain names one self-contained Sure-Hit Event. At initiative count 20 each round — after any clash comparison — the event resolves once against every eligible chosen target still inside. It is a manifestation of the Domain itself, not an activation of your ordinary attack or technique.

  • An unprotected chosen target receives the event's complete printed result with no attack roll and no initial saving throw.
  • A creature protected by an anti-domain defense uses the fallback attack or save printed in the Domain. If none is printed, use the most relevant save — success halves damage and prevents movement, conditions, and secondary riders.
  • The event resolves once per round and loses ties to other initiative-20 effects. A creature entering after that count waits until the next round. The event is not a critical hit and cannot trigger Black Flash unless its own entry creates a rolled critical.
  • Everything else still rolls: ordinary attacks, summon attacks, and saving-throw features used inside the Domain resolve normally.
Heian philosophy

The Domain as law — continuous, not an event

Some Domains do not fire a discrete pulse. The Domain itself is a place — a truth, a terrain, a law — and the sure-hit is continuous: there is no count because there is no event. Under this philosophy the Domain's rules apply constantly to everyone inside (a court that always judges, a terrain that always punishes, a muster that always answers). These Domains name their continuous rules instead of an initiative-20 event; the rules are understood by every trapped creature when the Domain opens.

Anti-domain defenses

Simple Domain, Hollow Wicker Basket, and similar defenses suppress sure-hit against protected creatures. They do not end the Domain, erase its terrain, prevent ordinary attacks, or automatically negate its separate environmental rules. A refined Simple Domain — the New Shadow Style discipline — is achievable around level 6: a 20-foot radius you can raise as a bonus action or as a reaction to a Domain or sure-hit effect, without CE a number of times per long rest equal to your proficiency bonus.

Non-lethal Domains

A non-lethal Domain replaces sure-hit with a specific rule, trigger, consequence, and completion condition, disclosed to every trapped creature when the Domain opens. A creature that knowingly violates it makes the listed save; success avoids the consequence until the start of its next turn. The procedure must offer meaningful choices — without explicit text it cannot cause instant death, permanent resource loss, or unavoidable violence. It clashes and burns out normally.

Barriers, stability, and collapse

  • Barrier: AC 12 + PB; HP = 10 × max(1, actual CE modifier) + clash points (doubled clash-point contribution at 800+ points). Immune to psychic and poison damage. By default resists damage from inside and is vulnerable from outside; automatically fails saves; immune to conditions. From inside, a creature must use an action and succeed on Wisdom (Perception) against your CE save DC to expose a weak point until the end of its next turn.
  • Tier 3 barrier choice: standard (resistance inside, vulnerability outside), inverted (vulnerability inside, resistance outside), or balanced (+5 CE: neither).
  • Domain Stability: if one damage source deals at least 20% of your hit point maximum, make a Constitution save (DC 10 or half the damage taken, whichever is higher). On a failure, the Domain collapses. It also collapses if you are incapacitated, die, dismiss it (no action on your turn), its barrier or anchor reaches 0 HP, or another rule breaks it.
  • A closed barrier blocks physical passage and teleportation across it unless an effect expressly crosses planar barriers. Destroying it collapses the Domain.

Open-barrier Domains — tier 5

A divine technique with an escape route. Omit the outer barrier and choose a visible point on the ground as the anchor: 120-foot radius, 1 minute. Creatures can enter or leave normally, but eligible creatures inside remain subject to its effect. The anchor has AC equal to your CE save DC, HP equal to 5 × your character level, immunity to psychic and poison damage, and no vulnerability. Destroying it collapses the Domain.

0.2-second expansion — tier 4

As an action, spend 10 CE to expand until the end of the current turn and immediately resolve your Domain's Sure-Hit Event once against every eligible creature in its area, including allies. Reactions cannot be used after the expansion begins, though they may still be used against its announced activation if their trigger permits. When it ends, suffer your normal Tier 4 Burnout. It cannot be extended or made open-barrier.

Technique Burnout

A Domain ending for any reason causes Technique Burnout. During Burnout you cannot activate an Innate Technique feature, Cursed Technique Reversal, Maximum Output, Maximum Technique, or Domain Expansion. You can still move, attack with weapons, use class features independent of your Innate Technique, reinforce yourself with generic CE, use anti-domain techniques, and use generic RCT. Existing manifestations remain and take ordinary commands. Burnout is not exhaustion, cannot be removed by ordinary condition cleansing, and Black Flash does not end it.

Burnout Recovery (tier 4 with RCT): as an action, spend 8 CE and make a DC 15 Constitution save. Success ends Burnout; failure deals 6d6 irreducible psychic damage, leaves Burnout intact, and prevents actions and reactions until the start of your next turn. Tier 4 permits one attempt per long rest; tier 5 may retry until a failure, with each prior success since your long rest raising the DC by 5.

Section V

Reverse Cursed Technique

Cursed energy is negative; multiply it by itself and it turns positive. Reverse cursed technique converts CE into flesh — powerful emergency healing, with a visible limit.

Unlocking RCT

You gain RCT from a class or technique feature, a feat, or a GM-awarded breakthrough after a near-death experience or exceptional CE insight. Surviving danger can create the opportunity, but never guarantees the ability.

The Healing Reserve

Your Healing Reserve maximum = 10 × total character level. It is a separate pool from HP and CE, and it returns to maximum only on a long rest — not a short rest. There is no automatic healing pulse. Reserve spent on healing or repairs does not return until the long rest, even if CE is restored sooner. CE governs burst speed; the Reserve governs rebuildable tissue.

Self-healing

Once per round while conscious, use a bonus action — or a reaction immediately after taking damage — to spend from 1 CE up to your CE ability modifier. Heal up to 10 HP per CE spent, limited by your missing HP and your remaining Healing Reserve. Deduct only the HP actually restored from the reserve. If the triggering damage reduced you to 0 HP, you cannot use the reaction unless a feature expressly permits RCT while unconscious or at 0 HP. Unspent healing from an activation is lost; CE is not refunded.

Healing others — Positive Energy Output

Base RCT targets only you. A feature granting RCT Output lets you touch another creature as an action, spending up to your CE ability modifier at a rate of 2 CE per 10 HP restored, deducting the healing from your reserve. A class feature may improve this rate or action.

What RCT cannot do

RCT cannot restore CE, revive a dead creature, recreate a destroyed brain or torso, remove Technique Burnout, repair soul damage, or end a curse or supernatural condition — unless a feature expressly says it can. You do not automatically weaponize positive energy; if an RCT Output feature lets you harm cursed spirits or undead, use that feature's damage.

Section VI

Black Flash & Output

Within 2.5 microseconds of a physical hit, cursed energy distorts space and strikes black. It cannot be aimed for — only earned — and when it lands, it changes the math of the fight.

Black Flash — the procedure

Whenever a creature capable of using cursed energy lands a critical hit with an attack, roll a second unmodified d20 before damage. On a natural 20, the attack becomes a Black Flash.

  • There is no universal once-per-turn or once-per-round limit on attempts or successes. Every qualifying critical may attempt the confirmation roll.
  • A saving throw, automatic hit, passive effect, initiative-20 Domain event, or manifestation attack does not qualify unless a specific feature explicitly creates a qualifying critical hit. Forced or expanded critical ranges qualify if the triggering feature permits Black Flash; an explicit restriction (such as Great Luck's non-qualifying critical) still wins.
  • Choose and pay any damage rider that must be declared before damage is rolled, then resolve the complete critical hit. Effects that trigger only after damage is dealt remain separate and are not multiplied.
  • Damage: roll the qualifying critical normally — critical dice, flat modifiers, eligible declared riders — then triple the completed critical-hit damage. This is multiplication of the final number, not one extra set of dice.
  • Defense: the complete Black Flash hit ignores resistance to its damage types. Immunity still applies unless another feature separately ignores immunity.

The feat Blessed by Black Sparks confirms on 18–20 after any qualifying critical; the Zone cannot improve it beyond 18–20.

What the Zone never does: it never restores Healing Reserve, never ends Technique Burnout, never removes exhaustion or injuries by itself, and never creates a second Technique Focus slot. A separate explicit feature may still create its own exception.

Maximum Output — overcharge one dimension

Maximum Output is the universal act of forcing an ordinary technique above its safe operating level. It is not a named Maximum Technique, and it no longer turns an attack bonus into an accuracy-breaking number.

  • Prerequisite: CE ability score of 20, and a feature or breakthrough unlocking Maximum Output; normally level 11+.
  • Activation: as a bonus action, declare one eligible technique you will activate before the end of the current turn and pay its Output surcharge, then pay and activate the technique normally afterward. If you do not activate it before the turn ends, the surcharge is lost.
  • Surcharge: double the technique's printed fixed base CE cost (minimum 2 CE). A variable-cost technique uses the chosen X as its surcharge. Optional overcharge, extra targets, or other add-on CE is paid once and is not doubled. A normally free technique has a 2 CE surcharge. Cost reduction applies to the base activation only, not the surcharge.

Section VII

Clashes

When two Domains meet, there is no contest of dice and no negotiation. The stronger domain stands; the weaker one breaks. This is the table rule.

Table rule · deterministic clashes

Points decide. Dice do not.

Every Domain converts its refinement tier into clash points: Tier 1 = 1, Tier 2 = 200, Tier 3 = 500, Tier 4 = 800, Tier 5 = 1,000. Compare the points. A gap of 100 or more collapses the weaker Domain immediately — before its sure-hit resolves. Equal points, or a gap under 100, means neither collapses automatically: there is no countdown and no clash roll. That is the entire decision procedure.

The clash procedure

  • Answer the expansion. When a perceived Domain opens within 120 feet, an eligible creature may use its reaction and pay its own Domain cost to expand.
  • Compare points. Each Domain's clash points come from its refinement tier.
  • Break on a 100-point gap. Compare each pair of clashing Domains. If one leads by 100 or more, the weaker collapses immediately — before its periodic sure-hit. Guarantees stay suppressed in the contested space while the clash continues, and a clash does not extend either Domain's expiry.
  • Equal refinement — no automatic winner. Resolve the clash through barrier damage, Domain Stability failures, or expiry.
  • Collapse deals no damage. The collapse itself deals nothing. The defeated creator suffers Burnout: 10 rounds below 700 points, 5 rounds at 700–999, 3 rounds at 1,000.
  • Breaking or withdrawing. A defeated or voluntarily dismissed Domain collapses immediately and its creator burns out. The winner's sure-hit resumes at the next initiative count 20, not the round the rival broke.
  • Keep fighting. Creators act normally during the clash. Damage can trigger Domain Stability, barriers and open anchors can be attacked, and anti-domain techniques still suppress sure-hit locally.
  • Three or more Domains. Compare every pair. A Domain can outmatch one rival while losing to another. When a Domain collapses, remove it from the comparison; the rest keep clashing.
  • Non-lethal and open Domains use the same point comparison. An open Domain also makes its barrier attack at initiative 20 during the clash — 4d10 + CE modifier force damage to the closed barrier, before outside vulnerability is applied; two open Domains do not make this attack. An open Domain trailing by 100+ points collapses even if its anchor survives.
Section VIII

Attunement

Cursed tools are not ordinary magic items, and they do not share ordinary attunement. A sorcerer can only truly master as many tools as their experience allows.

Cursed-tool attunement slots

You have cursed-tool attunement slots equal to your proficiency bonus — 2 at 1st level, up to 6 at 20th. This is a separate pool from ordinary magic-item attunement; the two systems never share slots.

GradeRarityAttunement
Grade 4CommonNormally none. Counts as cursed; utility only, no numerical bonus.
Grade 3UncommonTechnique items only.
Grade 2RareOptional, at the GM's discretion: usable unattuned (mundane weapon stats only) or attuned for one slot to gain full properties.
Grade 1Very RareYes — one slot, through a one-hour ritual.
Special GradeLegendary+Yes — one slot, through a one-hour ritual.

Suppression and repair

A suppressed cursed tool remains a mundane weapon of its type until repaired during appropriate downtime. A destroyed named Special Grade tool returns only through a campaign event — ordinary crafting cannot reproduce it.

Technique-bearing tools (including items created by techniques such as Construction) still require their normal attunement before their technique properties function — a tool's text controls.

Section IX

Ten Shadows & Special Systems

Some techniques are entire subsystems unto themselves. The rules below are the load-bearing pillars; the full dossiers live in the library.

Ten Shadows Technique

  • Subjugation from level 1. Every subjugation ritual may be attempted from level 1 — including the taming of the strongest shadows — provided you can pay the listed CE cost. There is no artificial roster gate.
  • Mahoraga stays unreasonable. It uses the complete CR 25 profile before and after taming, with legendary actions and no reduced player version. Its taming ritual is the balancing event: outside help may keep you alive but invalidates the taming result.
  • Manifestation capacity: one at level 1, two at level 3, three at level 11. The Divine Dogs together count as one; all active Gama count as one; a fusion counts as one; Mahoraga consumes the entire normal capacity.
  • Manifest as an action at the listed cost. A manifestation acts immediately after you; one bonus action commands one manifestation, and uncommanded ones Dodge. A shadow reduced to 0 disperses until the next long rest.
  • Fusions use the fifteen prebuilt Wells Unknown Abyss recipes (or Chimera rules: one intact base plus up to three donors). Shadow Wearing (level 8) and the Wheel Wearing capstone (level 17, once per long rest) follow their own entries.

Full dossiers: Technique Guide · Shikigami Roster · Fusion Catalog · Shadow Wearing.

❖ Canon note

The old guide's Ten Shadows entry lists the ritual under its level-3 feature ("challenge any untamed shadow"). The campaign canon is that every subjugation ritual is attemptable from level 1 — Mahoraga included — with the ritual's own danger, not a level gate, as the price of ambition.

Other special systems

  • Nine Tails transformation exists as a standalone subsystem — a once-ever transformation with an existential cost. See its technique record in the Technique Codex.
  • Two innate techniques — a campaign rule, not a variant — both advance by total character level while sharing one CE Reserve, one CE ability, one action economy, one Focus slot, and the normal Domain/Burnout limits (Section II).
  • Cursed Spirits as player characters follow their own ancestry rules; cursed-spirit techniques are catalogued with every other technique in the Technique Codex.

Named tools are intentionally stronger than generic equipment: the Inverted Spear of Heaven suppresses spells and CE features on a hit, the Soul Split Katana wounds the soul itself, Dragon Bone banks a third of the damage it deals into the next strike, and the Sword of Extermination exorcises cursed spirits and undead outright — no save, HP irrelevant. Every named tool has exact rules text; use it as written.

Heavenly Restriction users may wield cursed tools and benefit from passive weapon properties, but cannot fuel a tool with CE or activate a property whose cost or prerequisite requires CE unless the tool supplies its own independent charge.

The full arsenal — every named tool with complete rules — is catalogued in library/cursed-tool-arsenal.html.

❖ Quick Reference

Cut this out. Tape it to your character sheet.

Cursed Energy

  • Max CE = die max (L1) + later gains + CE mod once + permanent bonuses
  • CE attack = PB + CE mod · DC = 8 + PB + CE mod
  • All CE returns on a long rest
  • Temp CE: spent first, can't fund permanent costs, vanishes when its source ends

Spending

  • Pay the listed cost — cost is not a level gate
  • Order: base → additions → multiplier → reduction; halves round up
  • CE is committed on activation; misses and saves don't refund
  • No universal Output Rank; the feature's own caps are the law

Techniques

  • Milestones: 1 / 3 / 6 / 11 / 18, then L20, Maximum, Domain
  • One Focus at a time; damage forces a CON save (DC 10 or half)
  • Multiclassing never grants a second technique

Binding Vows

  • Every vow has a real price — restriction for power, or power with a penalty
  • Compulsion needs a save or a willing-decline valve
  • No precognition; temp CE can't pay permanent vow costs

Domains

  • Full-turn action · 20 CE · 60-ft enclosed · 1 min (2 min at 500+ refinement pts, 3 min at 1000+)
  • Sure-hit: one event at initiative 20 (or continuous law, per the Domain)
  • Simple Domain / Hollow Wicker Basket suppress sure-hit locally
  • Any end → Technique Burnout (technique dark; weapons, CE reinforcement, RCT still work)

Clashes

  • Points: 1 / 200 / 500 / 800 / 1000 by refinement tier
  • Gap of 100+ → weaker collapses immediately; equal/under → no auto-winner, no clash roll
  • Burnout: 10 / 5 / 3 rounds by points (<700 / 700–999 / 1000)
  • Winner's sure-hit resumes at the next initiative 20

RCT

  • Healing Reserve = 10 × level; long rest only
  • Once/round while conscious: bonus action or reaction, 1–CE-mod CE → 10 HP per CE
  • Repairs: 20/30/40/60/80 reserve — poison, exhaustion, limbs, organs
  • Can't: restore CE, revive, regrow a brain, end Burnout, heal soul damage

Black Flash

  • Critical → second unmodified d20 → nat 20 = triple the completed critical
  • Ignores resistance, not immunity; no per-round cap
  • Zone: nat 20 → Stage 1 (19–20); → Stage 2 (18–20) · once/turn +1d4 to a CE roll
  • Black Flash never restores Healing Reserve or ends Burnout

Maximum Output

  • CE ability 20 + feature/breakthrough, L11+; bonus-action declare
  • Surcharge = base cost (min 2); pick one: Power · Reach · Duration · Pressure
  • Can't touch Domains, RCT, vows, named Maximums, or Black Flash

Attunement

  • Cursed-tool slots = PB (2 → 6); separate from ordinary attunement
  • Grade 1 & Special Grade: 1 slot each, 1-hour ritual
  • Grade 2: GM's call — unattuned (mundane only) or attuned
  • Destroyed Special Grade tools return only by campaign event
